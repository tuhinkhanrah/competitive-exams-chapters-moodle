English

1.1 Electronic Structure & Nature of the Carbonyl Group

The carbonyl group (\(>\mathrm{C=O}\)) consists of a carbon atom bonded to an oxygen atom by a double bond. The carbonyl carbon atom is \(sp^2\)-hybridised and forms three coplanar \(\sigma\)-bonds separated by approximately \(120^\circ\). The unhybridised \(2p\)-orbital of carbon overlaps laterally with a parallel \(2p\)-orbital of oxygen to form a \(\pi\)-bond whose electron cloud lies above and below the trigonal planar frame. The oxygen atom also possesses two non-bonding electron lone pairs residing in its remaining orbitals.

Oxygen has a significantly higher electronegativity (\(3.44\)) than carbon (\(2.55\)). Consequently, the \(\pi\)-electron density is strongly shifted toward oxygen, rendering the carbon-oxygen double bond highly polar. Carbonyl compounds exhibit substantial dipole moments (\(\mu \approx 2.3 - 2.8\,\mathrm{D}\)), distinctly higher than isomeric ethers. As represented by resonance structures (neutral form A and dipolar zwitterionic form B), the carbonyl carbon functions as an electrophilic centre (Lewis acid), susceptible to attack by electron-rich nucleophiles, while the carbonyl oxygen behaves as a nucleophilic centre (Lewis base).

1.1 कार्बोनिल समूह की इलेक्ट्रॉनिक संरचना एवं प्रकृति

कार्बोनिल समूह (\(>\mathrm{C=O}\)) में एक कार्बन परमाणु, ऑक्सीजन परमाणु से द्वि-आबंध द्वारा जुड़ा होता है। कार्बोनिल कार्बन परमाणु \(sp^2\)-संकरित होता है तथा तीन सिग्मा (\(\sigma\)) आबंध बनाता है जो एक ही तल में स्थित होते हैं और इनके बीच का कोण लगभग \(120^\circ\) होता है। कार्बन का असंकरित \(2p\)-कक्षक ऑक्सीजन के समानांतर \(2p\)-कक्षक के साथ पार्श्वीय अतिव्यापन (lateral overlap) करके एक पाई (\(\pi\)) आबंध का निर्माण करता है, जिसका इलेक्ट्रॉन अभ्र इस त्रिकोणीय समतल के ऊपर और नीचे फैला होता है। ऑक्सीजन परमाणु के पास दो अनाबंधी एकाकी इलेक्ट्रॉन युग्म (lone pairs) भी उपस्थित होते हैं।

ऑक्सीजन की विद्युत-ऋणात्मकता (\(3.44\)) कार्बन (\(2.55\)) की तुलना में काफी अधिक होती है। इस कारण पाई-इलेक्ट्रॉन घनत्व प्रबलता से ऑक्सीजन की ओर आकर्षित रहता है, जिससे कार्बन-ऑक्सीजन द्वि-आबंध अत्यधिक ध्रुवीय हो जाता है। कार्बोनिल यौगिकों का द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण काफी उच्च (\(\mu \approx 2.3 - 2.8\,\mathrm{D}\)) होता है, जो ईथरों की तुलना में बहुत अधिक है। अनुनादी संरचनाओं (उदासीन संरचना A एवं द्विध्रुवीय संरचना B) के अनुसार, कार्बोनिल कार्बन एक इलेक्ट्रॉनरागी केन्द्र (लुईस अम्ल) की तरह व्यवहार करता है जिस पर नाभिकरागी आक्रमण करते हैं, जबकि कार्बोनिल ऑक्सीजन एक नाभिकरागी केन्द्र (लुईस क्षार) की तरह कार्य करता है।

1.2 Nomenclature Rules for Aldehydes and Ketones

Common System: Trivial names of aldehydes stem from the historical common names of the corresponding carboxylic acids by replacing the suffix -ic acid with aldehyde (e.g., formic acid \(\rightarrow\) formaldehyde; acetic acid \(\rightarrow\) acetaldehyde; butyric acid \(\rightarrow\) butyraldehyde). Positions of substituents are designated by Greek letters (\(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta\)), where the \(\alpha\)-carbon is directly adjacent to the carbonyl carbon. For ketones, common names specify the two alkyl/aryl substituents followed by the word "ketone" (e.g., methyl n-propyl ketone). Alkyl phenyl ketones are named as acylphenones (e.g., acetophenone, propiophenone, benzophenone).

IUPAC System:

  • Acyclic Aldehydes: The longest chain including the \(-\mathrm{CHO}\) carbon is selected. The terminal "-e" of the alkane name is replaced by "-al". The aldehyde carbon is unconditionally designated as \(\mathrm{C}\text{-}1\).
  • Acyclic Ketones: The longest chain containing the carbonyl carbon is numbered from the end nearest to the \(>\mathrm{C=O}\) group, and the suffix "-one" replaces "-e".
  • Ring Systems (Carbaldehydes): When a \(-\mathrm{CHO}\) group is attached to a carbocyclic or heterocyclic ring, the suffix "carbaldehyde" is appended to the full name of the cycloalkane (e.g., cyclohexanecarbaldehyde, benzenecarbaldehyde). The ring carbon bonded to \(-\mathrm{CHO}\) is numbered \(1\).
  • Special Rule for Three or More Formyl Groups: If three or more \(-\mathrm{CHO}\) groups are linked to an unbranched carbon chain, none of the carbonyl carbons are included in the parent chain, treating all aldehyde groups symmetrically: \(\mathrm{OHC-CH_2-CH(CHO)-CH_2-CHO}\) is systematically named propane-1,2,3-tricarbaldehyde.

1.3 Nomenclature and Structure of the Carboxyl Group

The carboxyl group (\(-\mathrm{COOH}\)) derives its name from carbonyl (\(>\mathrm{C=O}\)) and hydroxyl (\(-\mathrm{OH}\)). All three bonds attached to the carboxyl carbon atom are coplanar and separated by approximately \(120^\circ\). The carboxylic carbon is markedly less electrophilic than a typical aldehyde or ketone carbonyl carbon. This reduced electrophilicity is attributable to the resonance interaction with the adjacent hydroxyl oxygen lone pair: the unshared electron pair on \(-\mathrm{OH}\) is delocalised into the carbonyl \(\pi^*\)-antibonding orbital, diminishing the positive charge density (\(\delta^+\)) on the carboxyl carbon.

IUPAC Nomenclature for Carboxylic Acids: Aliphatic carboxylic acids replace the ending "-e" of the corresponding alkane with "-oic acid". The carboxyl carbon is always assigned number \(1\). Compounds with two carboxyl groups are named as alkanedioic acids (e.g., ethanedioic acid, butanedioic acid). When three or more \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) groups are attached to an unbranched carbon chain, the prefix tricarboxylic acid is added to the full alkane chain: \(\mathrm{HOOC-CH_2-CH(COOH)-CH_2-COOH}\) is systematically designated propane-1,2,3-tricarboxylic acid.

कार्बोक्सिल समूह (\(-\mathrm{COOH}\)) का नाम कार्बोनिल (\(>\mathrm{C=O}\)) एवं हाइड्रऑक्सिल (\(-\mathrm{OH}\)) समूहों के संयोग से बना है। कार्बोक्सिल कार्बन से जुड़े तीनों आबंध एक ही तल में स्थित होते हैं तथा इनके मध्य का कोण लगभग \(120^\circ\) होता है। कार्बोक्सिल कार्बन साधारण एल्डिहाइड या कीटोन के कार्बोनिल कार्बन की तुलना में काफी कम इलेक्ट्रॉनरागी (less electrophilic) होता है। इसका कारण समीपवर्ती हाइड्रॉक्सिल समूह के ऑक्सीजन पर उपस्थित एकाकी इलेक्ट्रॉन युग्म का अनुनाद है: \(-\mathrm{OH}\) का एकाकी युग्म कार्बोनिल समूह के साथ विस्थानीकृत होकर कार्बोनिल कार्बन के धनावेश घनत्व (\(\delta^+\)) को अत्यधिक कम कर देता है।

2.1 Methods Common to Aldehydes and Ketones

1. Controlled Oxidation of Alcohols: Primary alcohols undergo controlled oxidation to aldehydes when treated with pyridinium chlorochromate (PCC, \(\mathrm{C_5H_5NH^+CrO_3Cl^-}\)) in anhydrous dichloromethane (\(\mathrm{CH_2Cl_2}\)), preventing over-oxidation to carboxylic acids. 3.1 Physical State, Odour, and Intermolecular Associations

Methanal (formaldehyde) is a pungent gas at room temperature, while ethanal is a volatile liquid (b.p. \(294\,\mathrm{K}\)). Other aldehydes and ketones containing up to eleven carbon atoms are liquids, and higher analogues are wax-like solids. Lower aldehydes possess sharp, irritating, and pungent odours, but as molar mass increases, the fragrance becomes more pleasant, floral, and fruity (e.g., vanillin, cinnamaldehyde, camphor).

Boiling Point Trends:

Carbonyl compounds exhibit boiling points higher than those of non-polar hydrocarbons and weakly polar ethers of comparable molecular masses due to strong dipole-dipole attractions arising from the polarized \(>\mathrm{C^{\delta+}=O^{\delta-}}\) bond. However, their boiling points remain significantly lower than isomeric alcohols and carboxylic acids because aldehydes and ketones lack a hydrogen atom bonded directly to oxygen, precluding intermolecular hydrogen bonding between their own molecules.

3.2 Dimerization of Carboxylic Acids & Water Solubility Trends

Carboxylic acids possess remarkably high boiling points—even surpassing alcohols of matching molar masses. This exceptional physical stability results from extensive association via two strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds, forming stable cyclic dimers. Remarkably, these hydrogen bonds are so robust that carboxylic acids exist predominantly as dimers even in the vapor phase and when dissolved in non-polar aprotic solvents such as benzene.

Solubility Characteristics: Lower aliphatic carbonyl compounds (methanal, ethanal, propanone) and lower carboxylic acids (formic, acetic, propionic) are miscible with water in all proportions because their carbonyl oxygen atom forms effective hydrogen bonds with water molecules. However, water solubility diminishes precipitously with increasing carbon chain length because the non-polar, hydrophobic hydrocarbon chain becomes increasingly bulky and disrupts the hydrogen-bonding hydration network. Benzoic acid is nearly insoluble in cold water.

4.1 Nucleophilic Addition Reactions of Aldehydes and Ketones

Unlike alkenes (which undergo electrophilic additions across non-polar \(\mathrm{C=C}\) bonds), aldehydes and ketones characteristically undergo nucleophilic addition reactions across the strongly polarized \(>\mathrm{C^{\delta+}=O^{\delta-}}\) double bond.

Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition:

  1. Step 1 (Slow, Rate-determining step): A nucleophile (\(\mathrm{Nu^-}\)) attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon atom from a trajectory approximately perpendicular (\(\approx 107^\circ\), Bürgi-Dunitz angle) to the plane of the \(sp^2\)-hybridized orbitals. The hybridisation of carbon changes from planar \(sp^2\) to tetrahedral \(sp^3\), producing a tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate.
  2. Step 2 (Fast step): The tetrahedral alkoxide intermediate captures a proton (\(\mathrm{H^+}\)) from the reaction medium or solvent to yield an electrically neutral addition product.

Factors Governing Relative Reactivity:

  • Steric Hindrance: Ketones possess two relatively bulky alkyl/aryl substituents that crowd the carbonyl carbon, hindering the approach of the incoming nucleophile and destabilizing the crowded \(sp^3\) transition state much more than in aldehydes (which have only one alkyl substituent or none).
  • Electronic Effects: Alkyl groups exert an electron-donating inductive effect (\(+I\)), dispersing the partial positive charge on the carbonyl carbon and reducing its electrophilicity. Hence, formaldehyde is the most reactive, followed by higher aldehydes, and ketones are least reactive: \[ \mathrm{HCHO > CH_3CHO > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > (CH_3)_2CHCOCH(CH_3)_2} \]
  • Aromatic Aldehydes: Benzaldehyde is less reactive than aliphatic aldehydes (e.g., propanal) toward nucleophilic attack because resonance donation (\(+R\)) from the benzene ring delocalises electron density into the carbonyl group, reducing the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon: \[ \mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO > C_6H_5CHO > C_6H_5COCH_3 > C_6H_5COC_6H_5} \]

4.2 Key Examples of Nucleophilic Addition and Addition-Elimination

1. Addition of Hydrogen Cyanide (\(\mathrm{HCN}\)): Reaction with pure \(\mathrm{HCN}\) is sluggish. A base catalyst (such as \(\mathrm{OH^-}\)) is required to generate the powerful nucleophile \(\mathrm{CN^-}\), which readily attacks the carbonyl carbon to yield cyanohydrins. Cyanohydrins are invaluable synthetic intermediates; acidic hydrolysis converts the nitrile group to an \(\alpha\)-hydroxy carboxylic acid.

\[ \mathrm{HCN + OH^- \rightleftharpoons :CN^- + H_2O} \] \[ \mathrm{>\!C=O + :CN^- \rightleftharpoons >\!C(O^-)(CN) \xrightarrow{H^+} >\!C(OH)(CN) \quad (\text{Cyanohydrin})} \]

2. Addition of Sodium Hydrogensulphite (\(\mathrm{NaHSO_3}\)): Nucleophilic attack by bisulphite yields water-soluble crystalline bisulphite addition compounds. The position of equilibrium lies far to the right for almost all aldehydes, but to the left for bulky ketones due to steric crowding. Since the addition product reverts to the parent carbonyl upon treatment with dilute mineral acid or alkali, this reaction is utilized for the separation and purification of aldehydes.

3. Addition of Alcohols:

  • Aldehydes react reversibly with one equivalent of monohydric alcohol in the presence of dry \(\mathrm{HCl}\) gas to form an unstable alkoxyalcohol known as a hemiacetal. Treatment with a second equivalent yields a gem-dialkoxy compound termed an acetal.
  • Ketones do not readily form acyclic ketals with simple alcohols. However, they react smoothly with dihydric alcohols (ethylene glycol) in the presence of dry \(\mathrm{HCl}\) (or p-toluenesulfonic acid) to produce five-membered cyclic ethylene glycol ketals (used to protect carbonyl groups during synthesis).
  • Dry \(\mathrm{HCl}\) gas serves to protonate the carbonyl oxygen, strongly elevating the electrophilicity of the carbonyl carbon. Aqueous mineral acids hydrolyze acetals and ketals back to parent aldehydes and ketones.

4. Addition of Ammonia Derivatives (\(\mathrm{H_2N-Z}\)): Nitrogen nucleophiles add reversibly across the carbonyl double bond, followed by rapid dehydration to form imine derivatives containing a \(\mathrm{>C=N-Z}\) linkage. The reaction requires mild acid catalysis (\(\mathrm{pH \approx 3.5 - 4.5}\))—strong acid protonates the amine, destroying nucleophilicity, whereas neutral/basic conditions fail to activate the carbonyl oxygen.

Regioselectivity of Semicarbazide (\(\mathrm{H_2N-CO-NH-NH_2}\)): Semicarbazide possesses two distinct \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) groups. The amide \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) group attached directly to the carbonyl is strongly involved in resonance delocalisation (\(\mathrm{H_2N-C(=O)- \leftrightarrow H_2N^+=C(-O^-)-}\)), rendering its lone pair unavailable. Only the hydrazine-terminus \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) has a localized, nucleophilic lone pair and selectively attacks the carbonyl group to furnish a semicarbazone.

4.3 Reduction and Oxidation Transformations

Reduction to Alcohols vs Hydrocarbons:

  • To Alcohols: \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) (mild, selective for aldehydes/ketones in protic solvents) and \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) reduce aldehydes to primary alcohols and ketones to secondary alcohols.
  • Clemmensen Reduction: Heating with zinc amalgam and concentrated hydrochloric acid (\(\mathrm{Zn-Hg / conc.\ HCl}\)) deoxygenates carbonyl groups into methylene (\(>\mathrm{CH_2}\)) units. Excellent for base-sensitive molecules; incompatible with acid-sensitive moieties.
  • Wolff-Kishner Reduction: Refluxing with hydrazine (\(\mathrm{NH_2NH_2}\)) followed by heating with potassium hydroxide in high-boiling ethylene glycol (\(453-473\,\mathrm{K}\)) converts \(>\mathrm{C=O}\) to \(>\mathrm{CH_2}\) with expulsion of nitrogen gas (\(\mathrm{N_2}\)). Ideal for acid-sensitive substrates.

Oxidation & Distinguishing Chemical Tests:

  • Tollens' Test (Silver Mirror Test): Warming an aldehyde with freshly prepared ammoniacal silver nitrate solution (\([\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]^+\)) in alkaline medium oxidises the aldehyde to a carboxylate while depositing metallic silver as a luminous specular mirror: \[ \mathrm{RCHO + 2[Ag(NH_3)_2]^+ + 3OH^- \longrightarrow RCOO^- + 2Ag\downarrow + 2H_2O + 4NH_3} \] Both aliphatic and aromatic aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent. Ketones do not react.
  • Fehling's Test: Comprises Fehling A (aqueous \(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\)) and Fehling B (alkaline sodium potassium tartrate / Rochelle salt). 4.4 Enolate Reactions & Reactions of \(\alpha\)-Hydrogen

Acidity of \(\alpha\)-Hydrogens: Hydrogens on the carbon directly adjacent to the carbonyl group (\(\alpha\)-hydrogens) are remarkably acidic (\(\mathrm{pK_a \approx 19 - 20}\)). Deprotonation by base yields a resonance-stabilized enolate anion where the negative charge is delocalized onto the electronegative carbonyl oxygen.

1. Aldol Condensation: Aldehydes and ketones with at least one \(\alpha\)-hydrogen react in the presence of dilute alkali (e.g., dilute \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), \(\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2}\)) to yield \(\beta\)-hydroxyaldehydes (aldols) or \(\beta\)-hydroxyketones (ketols). Upon gentle heating, they undergo facile dehydration to form conjugated \(\alpha,\beta\)-unsaturated carbonyl compounds:

\[ \mathrm{2CH_3CHO \xrightarrow{dil.\ NaOH} CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CHO \xrightarrow[\Delta]{-H_2O} CH_3-CH=CH-CHO \quad (\text{But-2-enal})} \]

2. Cross-Aldol Condensation: When carried out between two different carbonyl partners, both having \(\alpha\)-hydrogens, a mixture of four structural products is obtained (two self-condensation products and two cross-condensation products). For example, condensation of ethanal with propanal yields: but-2-enal, 2-methylpent-2-enal, 2-methylbut-2-enal, and pent-2-enal.

When an aldehyde lacking \(\alpha\)-hydrogen (e.g., benzaldehyde) reacts with a ketone possessing \(\alpha\)-hydrogen (e.g., acetophenone) in base, cross-aldol condensation gives 1,3-diphenylprop-2-en-1-one (benzalacetophenone) as the major product (Claisen-Schmidt reaction).

3. Cannizzaro Reaction: Aldehydes that completely lack \(\alpha\)-hydrogen atoms (e.g., \(\mathrm{HCHO, C_6H_5CHO, (CH_3)_3CCHO}\)) cannot form enolates. Instead, when heated with concentrated alkali (\(50\%\,\mathrm{NaOH}\) or \(\mathrm{KOH}\)), they undergo self-redox (disproportionation): one molecule is reduced to an alcohol while the second is oxidised to a carboxylic acid salt.

\[ \mathrm{2HCHO + conc.\ KOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3OH + HCOOK} \] \[ \mathrm{2C_6H_5CHO + conc.\ NaOH \xrightarrow{\Delta} C_6H_5CH_2OH + C_6H_5COONa} \]

Cross-Cannizzaro: In a reaction between benzaldehyde and formaldehyde with concentrated alkali, formaldehyde is much more susceptible to initial nucleophilic attack by hydroxide ion due to sterics; hence, formaldehyde is preferentially oxidized to sodium formate, while benzaldehyde is reduced to benzyl alcohol:

\[ \mathrm{C_6H_5CHO + HCHO + conc.\ NaOH \longrightarrow C_6H_5CH_2OH + HCOONa} \]

4. Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution: In benzaldehyde and acetophenone, the carbonyl group is strongly electron-withdrawing by resonance (\(-R\)), deactivating the benzene ring and directing incoming electrophiles exclusively to the meta-position. Nitration of benzaldehyde yields m-nitrobenzaldehyde.

4.5 Chemical Reactions of Carboxylic Acids

1. Acidity & Salt Formation: Carboxylic acids react with active electropositive metals (evolving \(\mathrm{H_2}\)), alkalies (forming salts), and crucially, with weaker bases like sodium hydrogencarbonate (\(\mathrm{NaHCO_3}\)) and sodium carbonate to liberate effervescence of carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO_2}\)). This distinguishes carboxylic acids from phenols (which do not liberate \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) with \(\mathrm{NaHCO_3}\), except poly-nitrophenols like picric acid).

Why Carboxylic Acids are Stronger Acids than Phenols: The conjugate base of a carboxylic acid (carboxylate ion) is stabilized by two equivalent resonance structures where the negative charge resides exclusively on two highly electronegative oxygen atoms. Conversely, the phenoxide ion possesses non-equivalent resonance structures where the negative charge is distributed onto less electronegative carbon atoms of the ring. Thus, carboxylate is thermodynamically far more stable than phenoxide, rendering carboxylic acids substantially more acidic (\(\mathrm{pK_a \approx 4 - 5}\) vs phenol \(\mathrm{pK_a \approx 10}\)).

Substituent Effects on Acid Strength:

  • Electron-Withdrawing Groups (EWG): Stabilize the carboxylate anion via \(-I\) and \(-M\) effects, dispersing negative charge and increasing acidity. Group order: \[ \mathrm{-CF_3 > -NO_2 > -CN > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I > -Ph} \]
  • Electron-Donating Groups (EDG): Destabilize the carboxylate anion via \(+I\) and \(+M\) effects, intensifying negative charge and weakening the acid (\(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOH < CH_3COOH < HCOOH}\)).
  • Comprehensive NEET Acidity Ranking: \[ \mathrm{CF_3COOH > CCl_3COOH > CHCl_2COOH > NO_2CH_2COOH > NC-CH_2COOH >} \] \[ \mathrm{FCH_2COOH > ClCH_2COOH > BrCH_2COOH > HCOOH > ClCH_2CH_2COOH >} \] \[ \mathrm{C_6H_5COOH > C_6H_5CH_2COOH > CH_3COOH > CH_3CH_2COOH} \]
  • Substituted Benzoic Acids: \(p\)-Nitrobenzoic acid (\(\mathrm{pK_a = 3.41}\)) > Benzoic acid (\(\mathrm{pK_a = 4.19}\)) > \(p\)-Methoxybenzoic acid (\(\mathrm{pK_a = 4.46}\)).

2. Cleavage of the \(\mathrm{C-OH}\) Bond (Formation of Acid Derivatives):

  • Anhydride Formation: Dehydration on heating with \(\mathrm{P_2O_5}\) or concentrated \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) yields acid anhydrides (e.g., ethanoic acid \(\rightarrow\) ethanoic anhydride).
  • Esterification: Heating with alcohols in the presence of concentrated \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) or dry \(\mathrm{HCl}\) catalyst proceeds via nucleophilic acyl substitution through a tetrahedral intermediate. Water or ester must be removed continuously to shift the equilibrium.
  • Formation of Acyl Chlorides: Reaction with \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\), \(\mathrm{PCl_3}\), or \(\mathrm{SOCl_2}\). Thionyl chloride (\(\mathrm{SOCl_2}\)) is the preferred laboratory reagent because both co-products (\(\mathrm{SO_2}\) and \(\mathrm{HCl}\)) are gases that escape automatically, yielding pure acid chloride without complex purification: \[ \mathrm{RCOOH + SOCl_2 \longrightarrow RCOCl + SO_2\uparrow + HCl\uparrow} \]
  • Reaction with Ammonia: Carboxylic acids react with ammonia to form ammonium carboxylate salts, which upon thermal dehydration give primary amides. Phthalic acid reacts with ammonia to give ammonium phthalate, which upon heating yields phthalamide, and upon intense heating undergoes imide cyclization with loss of \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) to form phthalimide.

3. Reactions of the \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) Group:

  • Selective Reduction: Reduced to primary alcohols by \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) or diborane (\(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\) in ether/THF). Diborane is superior because it selectively reduces \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) without affecting functional groups like esters, nitro, or halo. Note: \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) does not reduce the carboxyl group!
  • Decarboxylation: Heating sodium salts of carboxylic acids with soda-lime (\(\mathrm{NaOH + CaO}\) in a \(3:1\) mass ratio) eliminates \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) to yield an alkane with one less carbon atom: \[ \mathrm{R-COONa \xrightarrow[\Delta]{NaOH + CaO} R-H + Na_2CO_3} \]

4. Substitution in the Hydrocarbon Moiety:

  • Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky (HVZ) Reaction: Aliphatic carboxylic acids possessing an \(\alpha\)-hydrogen undergo selective halogenation at the \(\alpha\)-carbon upon treatment with chlorine or bromine in the presence of a catalytic amount of red phosphorus, followed by aqueous workup: \[ \mathrm{R-CH_2-COOH \xrightarrow[2.\ H_2O]{1.\ X_2/\text{red } P} R-CH(X)-COOH \quad (X = Cl, Br)} \] Formic acid, trimethylacetic acid, and benzoic acid do not undergo the HVZ reaction because they lack \(\alpha\)-hydrogens.
  • Ring Electrophilic Substitution: Aromatic carboxylic acids undergo meta-substitution (e.g., nitration with \(\mathrm{HNO_3/H_2SO_4}\) yields \(m\)-nitrobenzoic acid; bromination with \(\mathrm{Br_2/FeBr_3}\) yields \(m\)-bromobenzoic acid). Carboxylic acids DO NOT undergo Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation because the strongly deactivating carboxyl group deactivates the ring and coordinates directly as a Lewis base to the \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\) catalyst.

\[ \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 + 2,4-(NO_2)_2C_6H_3NHNH_2 \xrightarrow{H^+} C_6H_5C(CH_3)=N-NH-C_6H_3(NO_2)_2\downarrow (orange-red) + H_2O} \] \[ \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 + 3I_2 + 4NaOH \longrightarrow C_6H_5COONa + CHI_3\downarrow (yellow) + 3NaI + 3H_2O} \] \[ \mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 \xrightarrow{H_2CrO_4\text{ / drastic } [O]} C_6H_5COOH \quad (\text{Compound B})} \]

Worked Example 2: Multistep Functional Group Interconversions

Problem: Outline efficient chemical pathways to accomplish each of the following organic transformations:

  1. Benzyl alcohol to 2-phenylethanoic acid (ascending the series by one carbon).
  2. 3-Nitrobromobenzene to 3-nitrobenzoic acid.
  3. Cyclohexene to hexane-1,6-dioic acid (adipic acid).

Solution:

1. Benzyl alcohol to 2-phenylethanoic acid:

\[ \mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2OH \xrightarrow{HBr} C_6H_5CH_2Br \xrightarrow{KCN} C_6H_5CH_2CN \xrightarrow{H_3O^+ / \Delta} C_6H_5CH_2COOH} \]

Conversion of \(-\mathrm{OH}\) to a good leaving group (\(-\mathrm{Br}\)) permits nucleophilic displacement by cyanide (\(\mathrm{CN^-}\)), introducing the required additional carbon, which upon aqueous acidic hydrolysis furnishes phenylethanoic acid.

2. 3-Nitrobromobenzene to 3-nitrobenzoic acid:

\[ \mathrm{m-NO_2-C_6H_4-Br \xrightarrow{Mg / dry\ ether} m-NO_2-C_6H_4-MgBr \xrightarrow{1.\ CO_2\ (dry\ ice)} \xrightarrow{2.\ H_3O^+} m-NO_2-C_6H_4-COOH} \]

Grignard carbonation with solid carbon dioxide (dry ice) directly carboxylates the aromatic ring.

3. Cyclohexene to hexane-1,6-dioic acid (adipic acid):

\[ \mathrm{Cyclohexene \xrightarrow{KMnO_4 / H_2SO_4, \Delta} HOOC-(CH_2)_4-COOH \quad (\text{Hexane-1,6-dioic acid})} \]

Vigorous oxidative cleavage of the endocyclic olefinic double bond oxidatively cleaves both \(sp^2\) carbons into terminal carboxylic acid groups.

Practice Question 2 (अभ्यास प्रश्न 2)
English

Which of the following represents the correct decreasing order of reactivity toward nucleophilic addition reactions (e.g., addition of \(\mathrm{HCN}\))?

(A) \(\mathrm{CH_3CHO > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > C_6H_5COCH_3}\)
(B) \(\mathrm{CH_3COCH_3 > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3CHO > C_6H_5COCH_3}\)
(C) \(\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3 > CH_3COCH_3 > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3CHO}\)
(D) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3CHO > C_6H_5COCH_3 > CH_3COCH_3}\)
Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Reasoning: Nucleophilic addition reactivity is governed by two factors: 1. Steric hindrance: Aldehydes (one alkyl group) are less crowded than ketones (two alkyl groups). Electronic factors: Ethanal has only one \(+I\) methyl group, whereas propanal has a larger ethyl group (\(+I\)), making ethanal's carbonyl carbon more electrophilic (\(\mathrm{CH_3CHO > CH_3CH_2CHO}\)). In acetophenone (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5COCH_3}\)), strong \(+R\) resonance from the benzene ring donates electron density into the carbonyl group, drastically reducing electrophilicity compared to acetone. Thus, the order is: \(\mathrm{CH_3CHO > CH_3CH_2CHO > CH_3COCH_3 > C_6H_5COCH_3}\).

English

A bifunctional organic compound, 4-oxopentanoic acid (\(\mathrm{CH_3-CO-CH_2CH_2-COOH}\)), contains both a keto group and a carboxyl group. Predict the principal organic products when this compound is treated separately with (i) \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) in methanol, and (ii) Diborane (\(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\)) in ether followed by acidification:

(A) (i) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2COOH}\);   (ii) \(\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\)
(B) (i) \(\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\);   (ii) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2COOH}\)
(C) (i) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\);   (ii) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\)
(D) Both reagents reduce only the carboxyl group, leaving the keto group unaffected.
Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Reasoning: 1. Action of \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\): Sodium borohydride readily reduces aldehydes and ketones to alcohols, but it completely fails to reduce carboxylic acids (due to electrostatic repulsion from the carboxylate salt and resonance attenuation of the carbonyl). Hence, \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) selectively reduces the keto group to a secondary alcohol, affording 4-hydroxypentanoic acid (\(\mathrm{CH_3CH(OH)CH_2CH_2COOH}\)). 2. Action of Diborane (\(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\)): Diborane is an electrophilic reducing agent that coordinates rapidly with the electron-rich carboxyl group, reducing \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) to a primary alcohol with extraordinary selectivity (faster than ketones and without reducing esters, nitro, or halo groups). Thus, \(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\) selectively reduces the carboxyl group to give 5-hydroxypentan-2-one (\(\mathrm{CH_3COCH_2CH_2CH_2OH}\)).

7. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist | सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावलोकन चेकलिस्ट

English

High-Yield Traps & Examiner Pitfalls in NEET UG

  • Trap 1 (Fehling's with Benzaldehyde): Students frequently generalize that all aldehydes reduce both Tollens' and Fehling's reagents. Benzaldehyde reduces Tollens' reagent but FAILS to reduce Fehling's solution. This distinction is one of the most repeatedly tested concepts in NEET.
  • Trap 2 (Selective Reduction of Carboxylic Acids): Never choose \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) to reduce a carboxylic acid. \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) is inert toward \(-\mathrm{COOH}\). To reduce \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) to \(-\mathrm{CH_2OH}\) in the presence of an ester, nitro, or halide, use Diborane (\(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\)).
  • Trap 3 (Friedel-Crafts Reaction on Benzoic Acid): Benzoic acid never undergoes Friedel-Crafts alkylation or acylation. The \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) group is strongly deactivating (\(-M\)) and acts as a Lewis base to coordinate with the catalyst \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\), completely halting electrophilic attack.
  • Trap 4 (Semicarbazide Regioselectivity): Semicarbazide (\(\mathrm{H_2N-CO-NH-NH_2}\)) has two amino groups. Only the hydrazine \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) acts as a nucleophile. The amide \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) lone pair is delocalised into the adjacent carbonyl group.
  • Trap 5 (Clemmensen vs. Wolff-Kishner Choice):
    • If the substrate contains an acid-sensitive group (e.g., an acetal, ketal, or \(\mathrm{-OH}\) prone to dehydration), use Wolff-Kishner (\(\mathrm{NH_2NH_2 / KOH}\)).
    • If the substrate contains a base-sensitive group (e.g., an alkyl halide or ester), use Clemmensen (\(\mathrm{Zn-Hg / HCl}\)).
  • Trap 6 (Side-chain Oxidation of Alkylbenzenes): Tertiary alkylbenzenes (such as tert-butylbenzene, \(\mathrm{C_6H_5-C(CH_3)_3}\)) do NOT yield benzoic acid with alkaline \(\mathrm{KMnO_4}\) because they lack benzylic hydrogen atoms.

Quick Reference Revision Matrix

  • Rosenmund Reduction: \(\mathrm{RCOCl + H_2 \xrightarrow{Pd-BaSO_4} RCHO}\) (Selective for aldehydes).
