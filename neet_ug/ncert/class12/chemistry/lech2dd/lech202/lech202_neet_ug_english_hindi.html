1. Learning Objectives & Overview / अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं अवलोकन

English

Alcohols, phenols, and ethers are oxygen-containing organic compounds that serve as foundational building blocks for pharmaceuticals, antiseptics, detergents, solvents, and fragrances. For NEET UG, this chapter carries high weightage because it integrates functional group geometry, acid-base equilibria, carbocation rearrangements, nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2), and electrophilic aromatic substitution.

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Classify alcohols, phenols, and ethers and assign accurate IUPAC and common names.
  • Compare bond angles and bond lengths in alcohols, phenols, and ethers using hybridization and resonance.
  • Predict products and stereochemical/regiochemical outcomes for alcohol and phenol syntheses.
  • Explain boiling point and water-solubility trends using intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding.
  • Order the acidic strengths of alcohols and substituted phenols using inductive and resonance effects.
  • Master key named reactions (Lucas test, Hydroboration-oxidation, Kolbe's reaction, Reimer-Tiemann reaction, Williamson synthesis) and ether cleavage with HI.

2. Classification & IUPAC Nomenclature / वर्गीकरण एवं IUPAC नामपद्धति

English

Replacing one or more hydrogen atoms of an aliphatic hydrocarbon with a hydroxyl (\(–\mathrm{OH}\)) group produces alcohols, whereas replacing a hydrogen atom directly on an aromatic ring with \(–\mathrm{OH}\) produces phenols. Replacing the hydroxyl hydrogen of an alcohol or phenol with an alkyl or aryl group yields ethers (\(\mathrm{R–O–R'}\) or \(\mathrm{Ar–O–R}\)).

Classification Highlights

  • Number of \(–\mathrm{OH}\) groups: Monohydric (one \(–\mathrm{OH}\), e.g., ethanol), dihydric (two \(–\mathrm{OH}\), e.g., ethane-1,2-diol), and trihydric (three \(–\mathrm{OH}\), e.g., propane-1,2,3-triol / glycerol).
  • \(\mathrm{C}_{sp^3}–\mathrm{OH}\) Alcohols:
    • Alkyl alcohols: Primary (\(1°\), \(–\mathrm{CH_2OH}\)), Secondary (\(2°\), \(>\mathrm{CH–OH}\)), Tertiary (\(3°\), \(-\mathrm{C(R)_2OH}\)).
    • Allylic alcohols: \(–\mathrm{OH}\) is bonded to an \(sp^3\) carbon directly adjacent to a \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bond (e.g., prop-2-en-1-ol, \(\mathrm{CH_2=CH–CH_2OH}\)).
    • Benzylic alcohols: \(–\mathrm{OH}\) is bonded to an \(sp^3\) carbon directly attached to a benzene ring (e.g., phenylmethanol, \(\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_2OH}\)).
  • \(\mathrm{C}_{sp^2}–\mathrm{OH}\) Compounds: Vinylic alcohols (\(\mathrm{CH_2=CH–OH}\)) and phenols, where \(–\mathrm{OH}\) is attached directly to an \(sp^2\)-hybridized carbon.
  • Ethers: Symmetrical (simple) if both groups on oxygen are identical (\(\mathrm{C_2H_5OC_2H_5}\)), and unsymmetrical (mixed) if they differ (\(\mathrm{CH_3OC_2H_5}\) or \(\mathrm{C_6H_5OCH_3}\)).

3. Structure & Bonding of Functional Groups / क्रियात्मक समूहों की संरचना एवं आबंधन

English

In alcohols, phenols, and ethers, oxygen is \(sp^3\)-hybridized with two bond pairs and two lone pairs in an approximately tetrahedral arrangement. However, bond angles and \(\mathrm{C–O}\) bond lengths vary systematically:

  • Methanol (\(\mathrm{CH_3OH}\)): The \(\mathrm{C–O–H}\) bond angle is \(108.9°\)—slightly smaller than the ideal tetrahedral angle (\(109°28'\)) because lone-pair–lone-pair repulsion on oxygen compresses the bonding orbitals. The \(\mathrm{C–O}\) bond length is \(142\,\mathrm{pm}\).
  • Phenol (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}\)): The \(\mathrm{C–O}\) bond length is noticeably shorter (\(136\,\mathrm{pm}\)) than in methanol. Two factors cause this contraction: (i) partial double-bond character arising from resonance delocalization of oxygen's lone pair into the benzene ring, and (ii) bonding to a more electronegative, smaller \(sp^2\)-hybridized aromatic carbon.
  • Methoxymethane (\(\mathrm{CH_3OCH_3}\)): The \(\mathrm{C–O–C}\) bond angle opens up to \(111.7°\) (greater than \(109°28'\)) due to steric repulsion between the two bulky methyl groups. The \(\mathrm{C–O}\) bond length (\(141\,\mathrm{pm}\)) remains nearly identical to that of alcohols.

  • मेथेनॉल (\(\mathrm{CH_3OH}\)): इसमें \(\mathrm{C–O–H}\) आबंध कोण \(108.9°\) होता है, जो आदर्श चतुष्फलकीय कोण (\(109°28'\)) से थोड़ा कम है क्योंकि ऑक्सीजन के एकाकी युग्मों के बीच प्रतिकर्षण आबंध युग्मों को दबाता है। \(\mathrm{C–O}\) आबंध लंबाई \(142\,\mathrm{pm}\) है।
  • फीनॉल (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5OH}\)): इसमें \(\mathrm{C–O}\) आबंध लंबाई (\(136\,\mathrm{pm}\)) मेथेनॉल से कम होती है। इसके दो कारण हैं: (i) ऑक्सीजन के एकाकी युग्म के बेन्जीन वलय के साथ संयुग्मन के कारण आंशिक द्वि-आबंध गुण, और (ii) ऑक्सीजन का अधिक विद्युतऋणात्मक \(sp^2\)-संकरित कार्बन से जुड़ा होना।
  • मेथॉक्सीमेथेन (\(\mathrm{CH_3OCH_3}\)): दो स्थूल मेथिल समूहों के बीच प्रतिकर्षण के कारण \(\mathrm{C–O–C}\) आबंध कोण बढ़कर \(111.7°\) हो जाता है। \(\mathrm{C–O}\) आबंध लंबाई (\(141\,\mathrm{pm}\)) ऐल्कोहॉल के लगभग समान रहती है।

4. Preparation of Alcohols & Phenols / ऐल्कोहॉल एवं फीनॉल का विरचन

English

A. Synthesis of Alcohols

  1. Acid-Catalyzed Hydration of Alkenes: Addition of water in the presence of dilute \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) follows Markovnikov's rule via a carbocation intermediate (watch out for carbocation rearrangements!).
    \[\mathrm{CH_3–CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H^+}} CH_3–CH(OH)–CH_3}\]
  2. Hydroboration–Oxidation: Diborane \((\mathrm{BH_3})_2\) adds to an alkene to form a trialkylborane, which is oxidized by \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}/\mathrm{OH^-}\) to yield an alcohol with anti-Markovnikov regioselectivity (and syn-addition, without carbocation rearrangement).
    \[\mathrm{3\,CH_3–CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{(i)\ (\mathrm{BH_3})_2\ \ (ii)\ 3\,\mathrm{H_2O_2},\ \mathrm{OH^-}} 3\,CH_3–CH_2–CH_2OH + B(OH)_3}\]
  3. Reduction of Carbonyls & Acids:
    • Aldehydes give \(1°\) alcohols and ketones give \(2°\) alcohols using \(\mathrm{H_2/Pd}\), \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\), or \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\). Note that \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) is mild and chemoselective: it reduces aldehydes/ketones without reducing esters or carboxylic acids.
    • Carboxylic acids require strong \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) (followed by \(\mathrm{H_2O}\)) to form \(1°\) alcohols, or commercial esterification followed by catalytic hydrogenation.
  4. From Grignard Reagents (\(\mathrm{RMgX}\)): Nucleophilic addition to carbonyls followed by acid hydrolysis produces:
    • Methanal (\(\mathrm{HCHO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow \mathrm{RCH_2OH}\) (\(1°\) alcohol)
    • Other aldehydes (\(\mathrm{R'CHO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow \mathrm{R'CH(OH)R}\) (\(2°\) alcohol)
    • Ketones (\(\mathrm{R'_2CO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow \mathrm{R'_2C(OH)R}\) (\(3°\) alcohol)

A. ऐल्कोहॉल का संश्लेषण

  1. ऐल्कीनों का अम्ल-उत्प्रेरित जलयोजन: तनु \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) की उपस्थिति में जल का योग कार्बधनायन मध्यवर्ती के माध्यम से मार्कोनीकॉफ नियम के अनुसार होता है (इसमें कार्बधनायन पुनर्विन्यास संभव है)।
    \[\mathrm{CH_3–CH=CH_2 + H_2O \xrightleftharpoons{\mathrm{H^+}} CH_3–CH(OH)–CH_3}\]
  2. हाइड्रोबोरॉनन–ऑक्सीकरण: डाइबोरेन \((\mathrm{BH_3})_2\) ऐल्कीन से जुड़कर ट्राइऐल्किलबोरेन बनाता है, जिसका \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}/\mathrm{OH^-}\) द्वारा ऑक्सीकरण करने पर प्रति-मार्कोनीकॉफ (anti-Markovnikov) अभिविन्यास से ऐल्कोहॉल प्राप्त होता है (इसमें पुनर्विन्यास नहीं होता)।
    \[\mathrm{3\,CH_3–CH=CH_2 \xrightarrow{(i)\ (\mathrm{BH_3})_2\ \ (ii)\ 3\,\mathrm{H_2O_2},\ \mathrm{OH^-}} 3\,CH_3–CH_2–CH_2OH + B(OH)_3}\]
  3. कार्बोनिल यौगिकों एवं अम्लों का अपचयन:
    • \(\mathrm{H_2/Pd}\), \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\), या \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) द्वारा ऐल्डिहाइड से \(1°\) ऐल्कोहॉल तथा कीटोन से \(2°\) ऐल्कोहॉल बनते हैं। \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) दुर्बल व चयनात्मक अपचायक है जो एस्टर या कार्बोक्सिलिक अम्ल को अपचयित किए बिना ऐल्डिहाइड/कीटोन को अपचयित करता है।
    • कार्बोक्सिलिक अम्लों के सीधे अपचयन के लिए प्रबल \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) (तत्पश्चात \(\mathrm{H_2O}\)) का उपयोग किया जाता है।
  4. ग्रिन्यार अभिकर्मक (\(\mathrm{RMgX}\)) से: नाभिकरागी योग और जल-अपघटन द्वारा:
    • मेथेनैल (\(\mathrm{HCHO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow 1°\) ऐल्कोहॉल
    • अन्य ऐल्डिहाइड (\(\mathrm{R'CHO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow 2°\) ऐल्कोहॉल
    • कीटोन (\(\mathrm{R'_2CO}\)) + \(\mathrm{RMgX} \rightarrow 3°\) ऐल्कोहॉल

5. Physical Properties & Acidic Strength / भौतिक गुणधर्म एवं अम्लीय प्रबलता

English

Boiling Points & Solubility

  • Intermolecular H-bonding: Alcohols and phenols form strong intermolecular hydrogen bonds, giving them much higher boiling points than ethers, haloalkanes, and hydrocarbons of comparable molar mass (e.g., ethanol \(351\,\mathrm{K}\) vs. methoxymethane \(248\,\mathrm{K}\) vs. propane \(231\,\mathrm{K}\)).
  • Chain Length & Branching: Boiling point increases with carbon chain length (greater van der Waals forces) and decreases with branching (smaller surface area).
  • Steam Volatility of Nitrophenols: o-Nitrophenol exhibits intramolecular H-bonding (chelation) and is steam-volatile, whereas p-nitrophenol forms intermolecular H-bonding (molecular association) and is less volatile. They are separated by steam distillation.
  • Water Solubility: Lower alcohols and ethers dissolve in water by forming H-bonds with water molecules; solubility drops as the hydrophobic alkyl/aryl group grows larger.

Acidity of Alcohols and Phenols

  • Alcohols vs. Water: Electron-donating alkyl groups (\(+I\) effect) increase electron density on oxygen, reducing \(\mathrm{O–H}\) polarity. Hence, acid strength follows: \(\mathrm{H_2O > 1°\text{ alcohol} > 2°\text{ alcohol} > 3°\text{ alcohol}}\). Consequently, alkoxides (\(\mathrm{RO^-}\)) are stronger bases than \(\mathrm{OH^-}\).
  • Why Phenol is Much More Acidic than Alcohols: Phenol (\(\mathrm{pK_a} = 10.0\)) is roughly a million times more acidic than ethanol (\(\mathrm{pK_a} = 15.9\)) because: (i) the \(sp^2\) ring carbon withdraws electrons, increasing \(\mathrm{O–H}\) polarity, and (ii) the phenoxide ion is strongly stabilized by resonance delocalization of its negative charge over the ortho and para carbons without charge separation (unlike neutral phenol, whose resonance forms have charge separation).
  • Substituent Effects on Phenol: Electron-withdrawing groups like \(–\mathrm{NO_2}\) stabilize the phenoxide ion (especially at ortho and para positions, lowering \(\mathrm{pK_a}\) to \(7.1\text{–}7.2\)), whereas electron-releasing groups like \(–\mathrm{CH_3}\) (cresols, \(\mathrm{pK_a} \approx 10.1\text{–}10.2\)) destabilize it and weaken acidity.

6. Key Chemical Reactions of Alcohols & Phenols / ऐल्कोहॉल एवं फीनॉल की प्रमुख रासायनिक अभिक्रियाएँ

English

A. Reactions of Alcohols

  • Esterification & Acetylation: Alcohols/phenols react with carboxylic acids or anhydrides (catalytic \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)) or acid chlorides (with pyridine to neutralize \(\mathrm{HCl}\)) to form esters. Acetylation of salicylic acid with acetic anhydride yields aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid).
  • Lucas Test (Conc. \(\mathrm{HCl}\) + anhydrous \(\mathrm{ZnCl_2}\)): Distinguishes \(1°, 2°, 3°\) alcohols by the rate of \(\mathrm{C–O}\) cleavage to form insoluble alkyl chlorides (turbidity):
    • \(3°\) alcohol: Immediate turbidity at room temperature.
    • \(2°\) alcohol: Turbidity after ~5 minutes.
    • \(1°\) alcohol: No turbidity at room temperature (requires heating).
  • Acid-Catalyzed Dehydration: Forms alkenes via a carbocation intermediate (rate-determining step). Ease of dehydration: \(3° > 2° > 1°\). Carbocation rearrangements (hydride/methyl shifts) occur whenever a more stable carbocation can form.
  • Oxidation & Catalytic Dehydrogenation (\(\mathrm{Cu}\) at \(573\,\mathrm{K}\)):
    • \(1°\) alcohol \(\xrightarrow{\mathrm{PCC}\text{ or }\mathrm{CrO_3}\text{ (anhyd.)}}\) Aldehyde; \(\xrightarrow{\mathrm{KMnO_4}}\) Carboxylic acid; \(\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Cu}/573\,\mathrm{K}}\) Aldehyde.
    • \(2°\) alcohol \(\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CrO_3}\text{ or }\mathrm{Cu}/573\,\mathrm{K}}\) Ketone.
    • \(3°\) alcohol: Resists mild oxidation; with \(\mathrm{Cu}/573\,\mathrm{K}\) undergoes dehydration to an alkene.

B. Reactions Specific to Phenols

  • Nitration: Dilute \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) (\(298\,\mathrm{K}\)) gives o- and p-nitrophenol; conc. \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) gives 2,4,6-trinitrophenol (picric acid).
  • Bromination: \(\mathrm{Br_2}\) in low-polarity solvent (\(\mathrm{CS_2}\) or \(\mathrm{CHCl_3}\) at \(273\,\mathrm{K}\)) yields p-bromophenol (major) + o-bromophenol (no Lewis acid needed). \(\mathrm{Br_2}\) in water gives a white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
  • Kolbe's Reaction: Phenol + \(\mathrm{NaOH} \rightarrow\) Sodium phenoxide \(\xrightarrow{\text{(i) }\mathrm{CO_2},\ \text{(ii) }\mathrm{H^+}}\) 2-Hydroxybenzoic acid (Salicylic acid).
  • Reimer–Tiemann Reaction: Phenol + \(\mathrm{CHCl_3}\) + aq. \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), followed by acidification \(\rightarrow\) Salicylaldehyde (o-hydroxybenzaldehyde).
  • Reduction with Zinc Dust: Phenol + \(\mathrm{Zn}\) (heat) \(\rightarrow\) Benzene + \(\mathrm{ZnO}\).
  • Oxidation: Phenol + \(\mathrm{Na_2Cr_2O_7 / H_2SO_4} \rightarrow\) Benzoquinone (conjugated diketone).

7. Ethers: Preparation, Cleavage with HI & Ring Substitution / ईथर: विरचन, HI द्वारा विदलन एवं वलय प्रतिस्थापन

English
  • Dehydration of Alcohols: Ethanol with conc. \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) gives ethoxyethane at \(413\,\mathrm{K}\) (via \(\mathrm{S_N2}\)), whereas at \(443\,\mathrm{K}\) elimination dominates to give ethene. Suitable only for unhindered \(1°\) alcohols.
  • Williamson Synthesis: Reaction of an alkoxide/phenoxide (\(\mathrm{R'ONa}\)) with a primary alkyl halide (\(\mathrm{R–X}\)) via \(\mathrm{S_N2}\).
    Crucial Limitation: Because alkoxides are strong bases as well as nucleophiles, using a \(3°\) alkyl halide causes elimination (E2) to form an alkene instead of an ether. To make tert-butyl ethyl ether, use sodium tert-butoxide + ethyl bromide (NOT sodium ethoxide + tert-butyl bromide).
  • Cleavage of Ethers with \(\mathrm{HI}\) (Reactivity: \(\mathrm{HI > HBr > HCl}\)):
    • \(1°\) / \(2°\) mixed alkyl ethers: \(\mathrm{I^-}\) attacks the less sterically hindered (smaller) alkyl group via \(\mathrm{S_N2}\) to form the smaller alkyl iodide + larger alcohol.
    • Ethers with a \(3°\) alkyl group: Protonation followed by \(\mathrm{C–O}\) cleavage generates a stable \(3°\) carbocation (\(\mathrm{S_N1}\)), giving the tertiary alkyl iodide + smaller alcohol.
    • Alkyl aryl ethers (e.g., anisole): Always cleave at the \(\mathrm{O–R}\) bond to give phenol + \(\mathrm{R–I}\), because the \(\mathrm{C_{sp^2}–O}\) bond has partial double-bond character and aryl carbons resist nucleophilic substitution.
  • Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution in Anisole: The \(–\mathrm{OCH_3}\) group activates the ring and is ortho, para-directing (para isomer is major due to less steric hindrance) in bromination (in \(\mathrm{CH_3COOH}\) without \(\mathrm{FeBr_3}\)), Friedel–Crafts alkylation/acylation, and nitration.

English
Worked Example 1: Regioselectivity in Alkene Hydration vs. Hydroboration–Oxidation

Problem: Consider 1-methylcyclopentene as a starting alkene. Predict the major alcohol formed when it is treated separately with: (a) dilute \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}/\mathrm{H_2O}\), and (b) \(\mathrm{(BH_3)_2/THF}\) followed by \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}/\mathrm{OH^-}\).

Explanation: In alkyl aryl ethers, resonance delocalization gives the \(\mathrm{C_{aryl}–O}\) bond partial double-bond character, and the \(sp^2\)-hybridized aryl carbon cannot undergo \(\mathrm{S_N2}\) attack. Hence, \(\mathrm{I^-}\) attacks the ethyl group to cleave the weaker \(\mathrm{O–C_2H_5}\) bond, forming phenol and \(\mathrm{C_2H_5I}\).
अभ्यास प्रश्न 8 (अभिकथन–कारण: ईथर विदलन)

अभिकथन (A): एथॉक्सीबेन्जीन (फेनीटॉल) को सांद्र \(\mathrm{HI}\) के साथ गर्म करने पर उत्पाद के रूप में फीनॉल तथा आयोडोएथेन प्राप्त होते हैं।

कारण (R): आंशिक द्वि-आबंध गुण एवं \(sp^2\) संकरण के कारण फेनिल कार्बन और ऑक्सीजन के बीच का \(\mathrm{C_{sp^2}–O}\) आबंध, \(\mathrm{O–C_2H_5}\) आबंध की तुलना में अधिक प्रबल होता है।

  • (A) (A) और (R) दोनों सत्य हैं तथा (R), (A) की सही व्याख्या है।
  • (B) (A) और (R) दोनों सत्य हैं, परन्तु (R), (A) की सही व्याख्या नहीं है।
  • (C) (A) सत्य है, परन्तु (R) असत्य है।
  • (D) (A) असत्य है, परन्तु (R) सत्य है।
उत्तर: (A)
व्याख्या: ऐल्किल ऐरिल ईथर में अनुनाद के कारण \(\mathrm{C_{aryl}–O}\) आबंध में आंशिक द्वि-आबंध गुण होता है और \(sp^2\) कार्बन पर \(\mathrm{S_N2}\) आक्रमण नहीं होता। अतः \(\mathrm{I^-}\) दुर्बल \(\mathrm{O–C_2H_5}\) आबंध को तोड़कर फीनॉल व \(\mathrm{C_2H_5I}\) बनाता है।

10. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावृत्ति जाँच-सूची

English
  • Pitfall 1 — Tertiary Halide in Williamson Synthesis: Never pair a \(3°\) alkyl halide with an alkoxide; E2 elimination gives an alkene. Always keep the halide \(1°\).
  • Pitfall 2 — Carbocation Rearrangement in Hydration/Dehydration: Unlike hydroboration–oxidation, acid-catalyzed hydration and dehydration involve carbocations—always check for 1,2-hydride or 1,2-methyl shifts.
  • Pitfall 3 — \(\mathrm{HI}\) Cleavage of \(3°\) Ethers: Remember that \(1°\)/\(2°\) mixed ethers cleave by \(\mathrm{S_N2}\) (smaller alkyl iodide), but \(3°\) alkyl ethers cleave by \(\mathrm{S_N1}\) (giving the \(3°\) alkyl iodide!).
  • Pitfall 4 — \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) Selectivity: \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) reduces aldehydes and ketones, NOT carboxylic acids or esters; use \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) for acids and esters.
  • Pitfall 5 — Bromination Solvent for Phenol: \(\mathrm{Br_2/CS_2}\) gives monobromophenol (p-major), while \(\mathrm{Br_2/H_2O}\) gives 2,4,6-tribromophenol (white ppt).
हिन्दी
  • त्रुटि 1 — विलियमसन संश्लेषण में तृतीयक हैलाइड: कभी भी \(3°\) ऐल्किल हैलाइड को ऐल्कॉक्साइड के साथ न लें; E2 विलोपन से ऐल्कीन बन जाएगी। हैलाइड सदैव \(1°\) होना चाहिए।
  • त्रुटि 2 — जलयोजन/निर्जलन में पुनर्विन्यास: हाइड्रोबोरॉनन–ऑक्सीकरण के विपरीत, अम्ल-उत्प्रेरित जलयोजन व निर्जलन में कार्बधनायन बनता है—अतः 1,2-हाइड्राइड या मेथिल विस्थापन की जाँच अवश्य करें।
  • त्रुटि 3 — \(3°\) ईथर का \(\mathrm{HI}\) विदलन: \(1°\)/\(2°\) मिश्रित ईथर \(\mathrm{S_N2}\) द्वारा छोटा आयोडाइड देते हैं, किन्तु \(3°\) ऐल्किल ईथर \(\mathrm{S_N1}\) द्वारा \(3°\) ऐल्किल आयोडाइड देते हैं।
  • त्रुटि 4 — \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) की चयनात्मकता: \(\mathrm{NaBH_4}\) केवल ऐल्डिहाइड व कीटोन को अपचयित करता है, अम्ल या एस्टर को नहीं; अम्ल/एस्टर के लिए \(\mathrm{LiAlH_4}\) का उपयोग करें।
  • त्रुटि 5 — फीनॉल ब्रोमीनन का विलायक: \(\mathrm{Br_2/CS_2}\) मोनोब्रोमोफीनॉल (पैरा मुख्य) देता है, जबकि ब्रोमीन जल (\(\mathrm{Br_2/H_2O}\)) 2,4,6-ट्राइब्रोमोफीनॉल का श्वेत अवक्षेप देता है।

11. Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम पुनरावृत्ति

English

Alcohols, phenols, and ethers share an \(sp^3\)-hybridized oxygen atom, yet their physical and chemical behaviors diverge sharply due to hydrogen bonding and resonance. Alcohols exhibit high boiling points and undergo \(\mathrm{O–H}\) cleavage (weak acids, weaker than water) as well as \(\mathrm{C–O}\) cleavage (Lucas test, dehydration, oxidation). Phenols are ~\(10^6\) times more acidic than aliphatic alcohols because the phenoxide ion is resonance-stabilized, and their strongly activated aromatic ring undergoes rapid electrophilic substitution, Kolbe's carboxylation, and Reimer–Tiemann formylation. Ethers lack \(\mathrm{O–H}\) bonds (low boiling points like alkanes) but accept H-bonds from water (comparable solubility to alcohols); they are best prepared by Williamson synthesis (\(\mathrm{S_N2}\)) and cleaved only under vigorous conditions by \(\mathrm{HI}\).

हिन्दी

ऐल्कोहॉल, फीनॉल और ईथर तीनों में \(sp^3\)-संकरित ऑक्सीजन परमाणु होता है, फिर भी हाइड्रोजन आबंधन और अनुनाद के कारण इनके गुणधर्म भिन्न होते हैं। ऐल्कोहॉल के क्वथनांक उच्च होते हैं और ये \(\mathrm{O–H}\) विदलन (जल से दुर्बल अम्ल) तथा \(\mathrm{C–O}\) विदलन (ल्यूकास परीक्षण, निर्जलन, ऑक्सीकरण) दर्शाते हैं। फीनॉक्साइड आयन के अनुनाद स्थायित्व के कारण फीनॉल ऐल्कोहॉल से लगभग \(10^6\) गुना अधिक अम्लीय होते हैं तथा सक्रिय बेन्जीन वलय होने से तीव्र इलेक्ट्रॉनरागी प्रतिस्थापन, कोल्बे अभिक्रिया और राइमर–टीमान अभिक्रिया देते हैं। ईथरों में \(\mathrm{O–H}\) आबंध न होने से इनके क्वथनांक ऐल्केनों के समान कम होते हैं, किन्तु जल से H-आबंध बनाने के कारण ये विलेय होते हैं; इन्हें विलियमसन संश्लेषण (\(\mathrm{S_N2}\)) द्वारा बनाया जाता है और केवल प्रबल परिस्थितियों में \(\mathrm{HI}\) द्वारा विदलित किया जाता है।
