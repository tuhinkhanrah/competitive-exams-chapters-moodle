Exam Blueprint & Core Learning Objectives

Electrostatics forms the foundational pillar of NEET UG Physics, contributing typically 2 to 3 direct questions (8 to 12 marks) every year. The conceptual focus spans charge conservation, Coulomb force in vector and scalar forms, the principle of superposition, field trajectories of charged particles, dipole moments, and rigorous calculation of flux using Gauss's Law.

  • Concept Master 1: Understand quantization (\(q = ne\)), conservation, and additivity of charge at macroscopic vs. microscopic scales.
  • Concept Master 2: Apply Coulomb's inverse-square law, vector notation, medium permittivity (\(\varepsilon = \varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0\)), and the principle of superposition to discrete systems.
  • Concept Master 3: Calculate electric field strengths, field lines properties, and kinematics of charged particles (\(a = qE/m\), \(t = \sqrt{2hm/qE}\)) in uniform fields.
  • Concept Master 4: Analyze electric dipoles: axial field (\(E \propto 1/r^3\)), equatorial field, external field torque (\(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{E}\)), and potential energy orientations.
  • Concept Master 5: Master Gauss's Law (\(\oint \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = q_{\text{encl}}/\varepsilon_0\)) and its primary symmetries: infinite straight line, infinite planar sheet, and conducting/non-conducting spherical shells.

1. Electric Charge, Electrification, and Fundamental Properties

1.1 What is Electric Charge?

Electrostatics deals with forces, fields, and potentials arising from static charges. Historically, rubbing amber with wool or silk was shown to attract light objects. The property that differentiates the two manifestations of charge is termed the polarity of charge. By convention established by Benjamin Franklin:

  • Charge acquired by a glass rod rubbed with silk or cat's fur is designated positive (+).
  • Charge acquired by a plastic (or ebonite) rod rubbed with fur or silk is designated negative (-).
  • Fundamental Law: Like charges repel each other, whereas unlike charges attract each other.
Interaction between charged rods: Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.

1.2 Conductors and Insulators

Substances that readily permit the flow of electric charges due to abundant free electrons are called conductors (e.g., metals, human and animal bodies, Earth). When charge is given to an isolated conductor, mutual repulsion causes it to immediately distribute over the outer surface. Substances offering exceptionally high resistance with virtually no mobile charges are called insulators (e.g., glass, plastic, nylon, porcelain, dry wood). Any charge placed on an insulator remains localized at the contact site.

1.3 Charge Detection: The Gold-Leaf Electroscope

A gold-leaf electroscope consists of a vertical brass rod housed inside a glass-windowed grounded box, with two thin, flexible gold leaves suspended at the bottom. When a charged body touches the top brass knob, identical charges conduct down the rod into both leaves. The resulting electrostatic repulsion forces the leaves apart; the angle of divergence serves as a direct indicator of the quantity of transferred charge.

Gold-Leaf Electroscope: Charge transferred to the leaves causes divergence due to electrostatic repulsion.

1.4 Three Fundamental Properties of Electric Charge

  1. Additivity of Charge: Electric charge is a scalar quantity. If a system contains discrete charges \(q_1, q_2, \ldots, q_n\), the total net charge is the algebraic sum taking algebraic signs into account:
    \[ q_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i = q_1 + q_2 + q_3 + \dots + q_n \]
  2. Conservation of Charge: The total electric charge of an isolated system remains strictly constant over time. When bodies are electrified by friction, electrons are merely transferred from one body to another; no new charges are created or destroyed. In nuclear reactions (e.g., neutron \(\beta\)-decay: \(n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e\)), the total charge before (0) and after (\(+e - e + 0 = 0\)) is identically preserved.
  3. Quantization of Charge: Experimentally demonstrated by Millikan, all observable free charges in nature exist as integral multiples of a basic fundamental elementary charge \(e\):
    \[ q = \pm n e \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots) \]
    where \(e = 1.602192 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}\). At the microscopic level (subatomic particles), quantization is essential. However, at the macroscopic level (where typical charges are of the order of \(\mu\text{C}\), representing \(\approx 10^{13}\) electrons), charge increments appear continuous, and the grainy nature can be safely ignored.

১. তড়িৎ আধান, তড়িতাধানীকরণ এবং মৌলিক ধর্মাবলী

১.১ তড়িৎ আধান কী?

স্থির আধান দ্বারা সৃষ্ট বল, ক্ষেত্র এবং বিভবের চর্চাকে স্থিরতড়িৎবিজ্ঞান বলা হয়। প্রাচীনকালে দেখা যায় রেশম বা পশম দ্বারা অ্যাম্বার ঘষলে তা হালকা বস্তুকে আকর্ষণ করে। আধানের এই দ্বিমুখী আচরণকে আধানের ধ্রুবত্ব (Polarity) বলে। বেঞ্জামিন ফ্র্যাঙ্কলিনের প্রচলিত নিয়ম অনুযায়ী:

  • রেশম বা সিল্ক দিয়ে ঘষা কাচদণ্ডের আধানকে ধনাত্মক (+) ধরা হয়।
  • পশম দিয়ে ঘষা প্লাস্টিক বা এবোনাইট দণ্ডের আধানকে ঋণাত্মক (-) ধরা হয়।
  • মৌলিক সূত্র: সমজাতীয় আধান পরস্পরকে বিকর্ষণ করে এবং বিপরীত জাতীয় আধান পরস্পরকে আকর্ষণ করে।

১.২ পরিবাহী এবং অন্তরক

যেসব পদার্থের মধ্য দিয়ে প্রচুর মুক্ত ইলেকট্রন থাকার কারণে খুব সহজে তড়িৎ আধান চলাচল করতে পারে, তাদের পরিবাহী (Conductors) বলা হয় (যেমন ধাতু, মানবদেহ, পৃথিবী)। পরিবাহীতে আধান প্রদান করলে পারস্পরিক বিকর্ষণের জন্য তা অবিলম্বে পরিবাহীর বাইরের পৃষ্ঠে ছড়িয়ে পড়ে। যেসব পদার্থের মধ্য দিয়ে আধান চলাচল করতে পারে না, তাদের অন্তরক (Insulators) বলে (যেমন কাচ, প্লাস্টিক, চিনামাটি, শুকনো কাঠ)। অন্তরকের কোনো অংশে আধান প্রদান করলে তা সেই নির্দিষ্ট স্থানেই আবদ্ধ থাকে।

১.৩ আধান শনাক্তকরণ: স্বর্ণপত্র তড়িৎবীক্ষণ যন্ত্র

স্বর্ণপত্র তড়িৎবীক্ষণ যন্ত্রে একটি ধাতব রডের মাথায় একটি ধাতব গোলক বা নব এবং নিচের প্রান্তে দুটি অত্যন্ত পাতলা সোনার পাত ঝোলানো থাকে, যা একটি কাচের পাত্রে সুরক্ষিত থাকে। কোনো আহিত বস্তু নব স্পর্শ করলে সমজাতীয় আধান দণ্ড বেয়ে পাত দুটিতে পৌঁছায়। পাত দুটি পারস্পরিক তড়িৎ বিকর্ষণের কারণে অপসারিত (বিস্ফারিত) হয়। এই প্রসারণ বা অপসরণের পরিমাণ আধানের পরিমাণের সমানুপাতিক।

১.৪ তড়িৎ আধানের তিনটি মৌলিক ধর্ম

  1. আধানের যোজনীয়তা (Additivity of Charge): আধান একটি স্কেলার রাশি। কোনো সংস্থায় একাধিক আধান \(q_1, q_2, \ldots, q_n\) থাকলে সংস্থার মোট আধান হয় তাদের বীজগাণিতিক সমষ্টি (চিহ্নসহ যোগফল):
    \[ q_{\text{net}} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i = q_1 + q_2 + q_3 + \dots + q_n \]
  2. আধানের সংরক্ষণশীলতা (Conservation of Charge): একটি বিচ্ছিন্ন সংস্থার মোট আধান সর্বদাই ধ্রুবক থাকে। ঘর্ষণের ফলে আধান স্থানান্তর ঘটে মাত্র, কোনো নতুন আধানের সৃষ্টি বা ধ্বংস হয় না। যেমন নিউট্রনের \(\beta\)-ক্ষয়ে (\(n \to p + e^- + \bar{\nu}_e\)), বিক্রিয়ার পূর্বের মোট আধান (0) এবং পরের মোট আধান (\(+e - e + 0 = 0\)) সমান থাকে।
  3. আধানের কোয়ান্টায়ন (Quantization of Charge): প্রকৃতিতে যেকোনো মুক্ত আধানের মান সর্বদা একটি মৌলিক একক আধান \(e\)-এর পূর্ণসংখ্যার গুণিতক হয়:
    \[ q = \pm n e \quad (n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots) \]
    যেখানে \(e = 1.602192 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}\)। আণুবীক্ষণিক স্তরে কোয়ান্টায়ন অত্যন্ত তাৎপর্যপূর্ণ হলেও স্থূল বা ম্যাক্রোস্কোপিক স্তরে (\(\mu\text{C}\) মানের আধান, যা প্রায় \(10^{13}\) টি ইলেকট্রনের সমান) আধানকে প্রায় অবিচ্ছিন্ন ধরে নেওয়া যায়।

2. Coulomb's Law and Electrostatic Force

2.1 Mathematical Formulation

Coulomb's Law states that the electrostatic force of attraction or repulsion between two stationary point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them, acting along the line joining them:

\[ F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} \]

where in SI units:

  • \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854187 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2\text{ N}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-2}\) is the permittivity of free space.
  • \(k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.987 \times 10^9 \approx 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}\).
  • Definition of 1 Coulomb: One Coulomb is the quantity of charge that, when placed in vacuum at a separation of \(1\text{ m}\) from an identical equal charge, experiences a repulsive electrostatic force of \(9 \times 10^9\text{ N}\).
  • Medium Effect: In a material medium of relative permittivity (dielectric constant) \(\varepsilon_r = K\), the force reduces to: \[ F_m = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} = \frac{F_{\text{vacuum}}}{K} \quad (\text{where } \varepsilon = K\varepsilon_0) \]

2.2 Vector Form and Newton's Third Law

Let position vectors of charges \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) be \(\mathbf{r}_1\) and \(\mathbf{r}_2\). The displacement vector directed from \(q_1\) to \(q_2\) is \(\mathbf{r}_{21} = \mathbf{r}_2 - \mathbf{r}_1\), with unit vector \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{21} = \mathbf{r}_{21}/r_{21}\). The force exerted on \(q_2\) by \(q_1\) is:

\[ \mathbf{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{21}^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{21} \]

Similarly, the force on \(q_1\) by \(q_2\) is \(\mathbf{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12}\). Since \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{12} = -\hat{\mathbf{r}}_{21}\), it follows strictly that:

\[ \mathbf{F}_{12} = -\mathbf{F}_{21} \]

Thus, Coulomb forces between two charges constitute an action-reaction pair in perfect agreement with Newton's Third Law and act along the central line joining them (central forces).

Coulomb's Law in vector notation: Like charges repel along the line joining them (F21 = -F12).

2.3 Comparison with Newton's Law of Gravitation

Feature Coulomb's Electrostatic Force (\(F_e\)) Newton's Gravitational Force (\(F_g\))
Nature Attractive (unlike charges) or Repulsive (like charges) Always Attractive
Intervening Medium Strongly dependent on medium dielectric constant (\(1/K\)) Strictly independent of intervening medium
Magnitude Ratio (for \(e^-\) and \(p^+\)) \[ \frac{F_e}{F_g} = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 G m_e m_p} \approx 2.4 \times 10^{39} \]
Macro-dominance Neutralizes because large bodies possess equal \(+\) and \(-\) charges Dominates astronomical structures because mass has only one sign

2.4 Principle of Superposition

Coulomb's law defines forces between pairs of charges. When multiple charges are present, experimental verification shows that the net electrostatic force acting on any charge is the vector sum of the forces exerted by all other individual charges taken one at a time, where each pairwise interaction is completely unaffected by the presence of any other charge:

\[ \mathbf{F}_1 = \mathbf{F}_{12} + \mathbf{F}_{13} + \dots + \mathbf{F}_{1n} = \frac{q_1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \sum_{i=2}^{n} \frac{q_i}{r_{1i}^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}}_{1i} \]

২.১ কুলম্বের সূত্রের গাণিতিক রূপ

কুলম্বের সূত্রানুযায়ী, দুটি স্থির বিন্দু আধানের মধ্যবর্তী পারস্পরিক আকর্ষণ বা বিকর্ষণ বল আধান দুটির পরিমাণের গুণফলের সমানুপাতিক এবং তাদের মধ্যবর্তী দূরত্বের বর্গের ব্যস্তানুপাতিক। এই বল সর্বদা আধানদ্বয়ের সংযোগকারী সরলরেখা বরাবর ক্রিয়া করে:

\[ F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} \]

যেখানে SI এককে:

  • \(\varepsilon_0 = 8.854187 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C}^2\text{ N}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-2}\) হলো শূন্যস্থানের তড়িৎভেদ্যতা (Permittivity of Free Space)।
  • \(k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}\)।
  • ১ কুলম্বের সংজ্ঞা: শূন্যস্থানে ১ মিটার দূরত্বে অবস্থিত দুটি সমমানের বিন্দু আধানের মধ্যে যদি \(9 \times 10^9\text{ N}\) বিকর্ষণ বল ক্রিয়া করে, তবে প্রতিটি আধানের মানকে ১ কুলম্ব (\(1\text{ C}\)) বলে।
  • মাধ্যমের প্রভাব: \(K\) পরাবৈদ্যুতিক ধ্রুবকবিশিষ্ট কোনো মাধ্যমে বলের মান শূন্যমাধ্যমের বলের তুলনায় \(K\) গুণ হ্রাস পায়: \[ F_m = \frac{F_{\text{vacuum}}}{K} \]

অনুরূপভাবে, \(q_2\) দ্বারা \(q_1\)-এর ওপর বল \(\mathbf{F}_{12} = -\mathbf{F}_{21}\)। সুতরাং, স্থিরতড়িৎ বল সর্বদা সমান ও বিপরীতমুখী ক্রিয়া-প্রতিক্রিয়া জোড় গঠন করে, যা নিউটনের তৃতীয় গতিসূত্র মেনে চলে এবং এটি একটি কেন্দ্রকীয় বল (Central Force)।

3. Electric Field and Charged Particle Dynamics

3.1 Concept and Definition

To eliminate action-at-a-distance, physics introduces the electric field. A source charge \(Q\) establishes an electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) in the surrounding space. When another charge \(q\) is brought into this region, it experiences a force exerted directly by the local field:

\[ \mathbf{E}(\mathbf{r}) = \lim_{q_0 \to 0} \left( \frac{\mathbf{F}}{q_0} \right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Q}{r^2} \hat{\mathbf{r}} \]

Key properties:

  • Test charge condition: The limiting process \(q_0 \to 0\) guarantees that the test charge does not disturb or shift the source charges.
  • Force on charge: Any charge \(q\) situated in an electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) experiences a force \(\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{E}\). If \(q > 0\), \(\mathbf{F}\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{E}\); if \(q < 0\), \(\mathbf{F}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{E}\).
  • SI Unit and Dimensions: \(\text{N C}^{-1}\) or \(\text{V m}^{-1}\); Dimensions: \([\text{M L T}^{-3}\text{A}^{-1}]\).
  • Physical reality: Time-dependent electromagnetic fields transport momentum and energy through space at the speed of light \(c\), existing as objective physical entities.

3.2 Motion of Charged Particles in a Uniform Electric Field

Consider a particle of mass \(m\) and charge \(q\) entering or released in a uniform electric field \(\mathbf{E}\). The electrostatic acceleration experienced is:

\[ \mathbf{a} = \frac{q\mathbf{E}}{m} \]

Crucial contrast with gravitational free-fall:

  • In gravitational free fall, acceleration \(g = GM/R^2\) is strictly independent of the falling body's mass.
  • In an electric field, acceleration depends directly on the charge-to-mass ratio (specific charge \(q/m\)).
  • If dropped from rest through distance \(h\), time of fall is:
    \[ t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{a}} = \sqrt{\frac{2hm}{qE}} \implies t \propto \sqrt{m} \]
    Since \(m_e \ll m_p\), an electron accelerates much faster and takes vastly less time to fall through a given distance than a proton (\(t_e \ll t_p\)).

4. Electric Field Lines

4.1 Visualizing the Field: Concept and Physical Meaning

An electric field line is an imaginary continuous space curve drawn in an electric field such that the tangent at any point indicates the direction of the resultant electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) at that location. Introduced by Michael Faraday, field lines visually map both direction and relative magnitude.

Electric field line configurations for isolated charges, dipole, and like charge pair.

4.2 Four Invariant Properties of Electrostatic Field Lines

  1. Start and End Points: Field lines emanate from positive charges and terminate at negative charges. For an isolated single charge, they extend to or from infinity.
  2. Continuity: In a charge-free region, field lines are smooth, continuous curves without sudden breaks or gaps.
  3. Non-crossing Rule: Two field lines can never intersect. If they did, two distinct tangents could be drawn at the intersection point, implying two distinct directions for the net electric field at a single point in space, which is physically impossible.
  4. Absence of Closed Loops: Electrostatic field lines never form closed loops. This is a direct consequence of the conservative nature of the electrostatic field (\(\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0\)).
  5. Density Proportional to Intensity: The number of field lines crossing unit cross-sectional area perpendicular to the lines is proportional to the local field strength (\(E \propto \text{density of lines}\)).

5. Electric Flux

5.1 Area Vector and Flux Definition

A planar surface element possesses orientation in space and is treated as an area vector \(\mathbf{\Delta S} = \Delta S\,\hat{\mathbf{n}}\), where \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) is a unit normal vector perpendicular to the surface. For a closed surface, \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\) points strictly along the outward normal.

The electric flux \(\Delta \Phi\) through an area element \(\mathbf{\Delta S}\) represents the total measure of electric field lines passing through it:

\[ \Delta \Phi = \mathbf{E} \cdot \mathbf{\Delta S} = E \Delta S \cos\theta \]

where \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\mathbf{E}\) and the normal vector \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}\).

Electric Flux through an area element tilted at angle θ with the uniform electric field: ΔΦ = E ΔS cosθ.
  • Maximum Flux (\(\theta = 0^\circ\)): Field is perpendicular to surface; \(\Delta\Phi = E\Delta S\).
  • Zero Flux (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)): Field is parallel (grazing) to surface; \(\Delta\Phi = 0\).
  • Negative Flux (\(\theta > 90^\circ\)): Field lines enter the closed surface.
  • Total Surface Flux: \(\Phi = \int_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S}\); Unit: \(\text{N m}^2 \text{ C}^{-1}\) or \(\text{V m}\). Dimensions: \([\text{M L}^3 \text{T}^{-3} \text{A}^{-1}]\).

6. Electric Dipole

6.1 Dipole Moment Definition

An electric dipole is a system of two equal and opposite point charges \(q\) and \(-q\) separated by a small distance \(2a\). The electric dipole moment vector \(\mathbf{p}\) is defined as:

\[ \mathbf{p} = q (2a) \hat{\mathbf{p}} \]

By international convention, the direction of \(\mathbf{p}\) points along the dipole axis from the negative charge \(-q\) to the positive charge \(+q\). SI Unit: \(\text{C m}\); Dimensions: \([\text{L T A}]\).

Electric field due to an electric dipole at: (a) axial point P, (b) equatorial point P.

6.2 Electric Field of a Dipole

Although the total net charge of a dipole is zero (\(q - q = 0\)), its electric field is non-zero because the charges are spatially separated. At large distances (\(r \gg a\)), the fields due to \(+q\) and \(-q\) nearly cancel, causing the dipole field to fall off as \(1/r^3\) rather than \(1/r^2\):

(a) On the Dipole Axis (End-on or Axial Position)

At an axial point located distance \(r\) from the dipole center:

\[ \mathbf{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{4qa r}{(r^2 - a^2)^2} \hat{\mathbf{p}} \xrightarrow{r \gg a} \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2\mathbf{p}}{r^3} \]

Notice that \(\mathbf{E}_{\text{axial}}\) is directed parallel to \(\mathbf{p}\) (along \(\hat{\mathbf{p}}\)).

(b) On the Equatorial Plane (Broadside-on Position)

At an equatorial point on the perpendicular bisector at distance \(r\) from center:

\[ \mathbf{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2qa}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}} \hat{\mathbf{p}} \xrightarrow{r \gg a} -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\mathbf{p}}{r^3} \]

Notice that \(\mathbf{E}_{\text{eq}}\) is directed antiparallel (opposite) to \(\mathbf{p}\).

Vital NEET Ratio: For an ideal or short point dipole (\(r \gg a\)) at the same radial distance \(r\): \[ \frac{|\mathbf{E}_{\text{axial}}|}{|\mathbf{E}_{\text{eq}}|} = 2 \]

6.3 Physical Significance: Polar vs. Non-Polar Molecules

  • Non-polar Molecules: Centers of positive and negative charges coincide (e.g., \(\text{CO}_2\), \(\text{CH}_4\)); permanent dipole moment is zero. An external field induces an induced dipole moment.
  • Polar Molecules: Centers of positive and negative charge distribution are permanently separated by bond angles (e.g., \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\), dipole moment \(\approx 6 \times 10^{-30}\text{ C m}\)); possessing a permanent dipole moment.

7. Dipole in an External Electric Field

7.1 Uniform Electric Field: Net Force vs. Torque

When an electric dipole is placed in a uniform electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) making an angle \(\theta\) with it:

  • Net Force: The forces on charges \(+q\) and \(-q\) are \(\mathbf{F}_+ = +q\mathbf{E}\) and \(\mathbf{F}_- = -q\mathbf{E}\). Therefore,
    \[ \mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{F}_+ + \mathbf{F}_- = \mathbf{0} \]
    The dipole undergoes no translational acceleration.
  • Torque (Couple): Since the two antiparallel forces act along distinct lines of action separated by arm length \(2a\sin\theta\), they form a torque:
    \[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{E} \implies \tau = p E \sin\theta \]
    This torque tends to rotate and align \(\mathbf{p}\) with \(\mathbf{E}\).
Dipole in a uniform electric field: Net force is zero, experiencing a restoring torque τ = p × E.

7.2 Equilibrium States

  • Stable Equilibrium (\(\theta = 0^\circ\)): \(\mathbf{p}\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{E}\). \(\tau = 0\), potential energy is minimum.
  • Unstable Equilibrium (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)): \(\mathbf{p}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{E}\). \(\tau = 0\), potential energy is maximum.
  • Maximum Torque (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)): \(\tau_{\text{max}} = pE\).

7.3 Non-Uniform Electric Field

If \(\mathbf{E}\) is non-uniform, the forces on \(+q\) and \(-q\) do not balance, producing a non-zero net translational force in addition to a torque:

  • When \(\mathbf{p} \parallel \mathbf{E}\): Net force acts in the direction of increasing field.
  • When \(\mathbf{p}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{E}\): Net force acts in the direction of decreasing field.
  • Frictional Attraction: A charged comb polarizes neutral paper bits, inducing dipoles parallel to the comb's non-uniform divergent field. Consequently, the paper bits experience a net attractive force toward the comb.

9. Gauss's Law and Its Key Applications

9.1 Statement and Characteristics

Gauss's Law states that the total electric flux through any arbitrary closed Gaussian surface \(S\) is equal to \(1/\varepsilon_0\) times the total net charge enclosed inside the surface:

\[ \Phi = \oint_S \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \frac{q_{\text{enclosed}}}{\varepsilon_0} \]
Core NEET Principles of Gauss's Law:
  1. Gauss's Law is valid for any closed surface, regardless of its shape or size.
  2. The term \(q_{\text{enclosed}}\) represents only the algebraic sum of charges located strictly inside the closed surface. Outside charges do not contribute to net flux.
  3. The electric field \(\mathbf{E}\) appearing in the flux integral is the resultant field produced by all charges (both inside and outside the Gaussian surface).
  4. A Gaussian surface must never pass through a discrete point charge (where field diverges), but it can cross continuous charge distributions.
Applications of Gauss's Law: (1) Long straight wire, (2) Infinite plane sheet, (3) Thin spherical shell profile and field curve.

9.2 Three Standard Symmetry Applications

1. Infinitely Long Straight Wire of Linear Charge Density \(\lambda\)

Taking a coaxial cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius \(r\) and length \(l\): flux through flat ends is zero (field is radial). Flux through curved area \(2\pi r l\) gives:

\[ E (2\pi r l) = \frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} \quad \left( E \propto \frac{1}{r} \right) \]

2. Uniformly Charged Infinite Plane Sheet of Surface Charge Density \(\sigma\)

Taking a Gaussian cylinder/pillbox of cross-sectional area \(A\) perpendicular to the sheet: flux only emerges from the two end caps (\(2EA\)), enclosing charge \(\sigma A\):

\[ 2 E A = \frac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} \]

Crucially, \(E\) is independent of distance from the sheet.

3. Uniformly Charged Thin Spherical Shell of Radius \(R\) and Total Charge \(q\)

  • Outside the Shell (\(r \ge R\)): Enclosed charge is \(q = 4\pi R^2 \sigma\). Gaussian sphere of radius \(r\) yields:
    \[ E (4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2} \quad \left( E \propto \frac{1}{r^2} \right) \]
    The shell behaves for all outside points as if its entire charge were concentrated at the center.
  • Inside the Shell (\(r < R\)): Enclosed charge is zero (\(q_{\text{enclosed}} = 0\)). Therefore:
    \[ E (4\pi r^2) = 0 \implies E = 0 \]
    The electric field vanishes identically at all points inside a charged conducting/thin spherical shell!

10. Worked Examples with Step-by-Step Solutions

Worked Example 1: Charge Transfer and Quantization
Problem: If \(10^9\) electrons move out of a body to another body every second, how much time is required to accumulate a total negative charge of \(1\text{ C}\) on the recipient body?
Solution:
  1. Charge transferred per second: \[ \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = 10^9 \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}) = 1.602 \times 10^{-10}\text{ C s}^{-1} \]
  2. Time required to accumulate \(q = 1\text{ C}\): \[ t = \frac{q}{\Delta q/\Delta t} = \frac{1\text{ C}}{1.602 \times 10^{-10}\text{ C s}^{-1}} \approx 6.24 \times 10^9\text{ s} \]
  3. Converting into years: \[ t = \frac{6.24 \times 10^9}{365 \times 24 \times 3600} \approx 198\text{ years} \]
Conclusion: One Coulomb is an extraordinarily large practical unit of electrostatic charge.
Worked Example 2: Superposition of Electrostatic Forces
Problem: Three point charges \(+q\), \(+q\), and \(-q\) are placed at vertices A, B, and C of an equilateral triangle of side \(l\). Determine the net electrostatic force acting on each charge.
Solution:
  1. Magnitude of pairwise Coulomb force between any two vertices: \[ F_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q^2}{l^2} \]
  2. Force on charge \(+q\) at A: It experiences repulsive force \(\mathbf{F}_{AB}\) of magnitude \(F_0\) away from B, and attractive force \(\mathbf{F}_{AC}\) of magnitude \(F_0\) towards C. The angle between \(\mathbf{F}_{AB}\) and \(\mathbf{F}_{AC}\) is \(120^\circ\). \[ F_A = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0 F_0 \cos 120^\circ} = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 - F_0^2} = F_0 \] The resultant \(\mathbf{F}_A\) is directed parallel to side BC (from B towards C).
  3. Force on charge \(+q\) at B: By exact symmetry, \(F_B = F_0\), directed parallel to side AC (from A towards C).
  4. Force on charge \(-q\) at C: Attracted towards A with force \(F_0\) and towards B with force \(F_0\). The angle between them is \(60^\circ\): \[ F_C = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0^2 \cos 60^\circ} = \sqrt{3} F_0 \] Directed along the internal angle bisector of \(\angle C\).
  5. System check: \(\mathbf{F}_A + \mathbf{F}_B + \mathbf{F}_C = \mathbf{0}\), perfectly consistent with Newton's Third Law.
Worked Example 3: Kinematics of Electron vs. Proton in Uniform Electric Field
Problem: An electron falls from rest through a vertical distance \(h = 1.5\text{ cm}\) in a uniform upward electric field of magnitude \(E = 2.0 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}\). The field direction is then reversed (pointing downward), and a proton falls from rest through the same distance. Calculate the time of fall for each particle. (Take \(m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}\), \(m_p = 1.67 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}\)).
Solution:
  1. Downwards electric force on charge \(e\) is \(F = eE\). The acceleration is \(a = eE/m\).
  2. For the electron: \[ a_e = \frac{eE}{m_e} = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19})(2.0 \times 10^4)}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}} \approx 3.51 \times 10^{15}\text{ m s}^{-2} \] Since \(a_e \gg g\) (\(9.8\text{ m s}^{-2}\)), gravity is negligible. \[ t_e = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{a_e}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.015}{3.51 \times 10^{15}}} \approx 2.92 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s} = 2.92\text{ ns} \]
  3. For the proton: \[ a_p = \frac{eE}{m_p} = \frac{(1.6 \times 10^{-19})(2.0 \times 10^4)}{1.67 \times 10^{-27}} \approx 1.92 \times 10^{12}\text{ m s}^{-2} \] \[ t_p = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{a_p}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.015}{1.92 \times 10^{12}}} \approx 1.25 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s} = 125\text{ ns} \]
  4. Conclusion: Unlike gravitational free fall where time is identical for all masses, in electrostatics the lighter particle falls much faster (\(t \propto \sqrt{m}\)).
Worked Example 4: Gauss's Law with Non-Uniform Electric Field
Problem: An electric field is given by \(\mathbf{E} = \alpha x^{1/2} \hat{\mathbf{i}}\), with \(\alpha = 800\text{ N C}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1/2}\), and \(E_y = E_z = 0\). Consider a cube of edge length \(a = 0.1\text{ m}\) with its left face located at \(x = a\) and right face at \(x = 2a\). Find (a) the net electric flux through the cube, and (b) the net charge enclosed within the cube.
Solution:
  1. Since \(\mathbf{E}\) has only an \(x\)-component, flux through the top, bottom, front, and back faces is identically zero (\(\mathbf{E} \perp \mathbf{\Delta S}\)).
  2. Left Face (\(x = a\)): Normal vector \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_L = -\hat{\mathbf{i}}\). \[ \Phi_L = \mathbf{E}_L \cdot \mathbf{\Delta S}_L = (\alpha a^{1/2} \hat{\mathbf{i}}) \cdot (-a^2 \hat{\mathbf{i}}) = -\alpha a^{5/2} \]
  3. Right Face (\(x = 2a\)): Normal vector \(\hat{\mathbf{n}}_R = +\hat{\mathbf{i}}\). \[ \Phi_R = \mathbf{E}_R \cdot \mathbf{\Delta S}_R = (\alpha (2a)^{1/2} \hat{\mathbf{i}}) \cdot (a^2 \hat{\mathbf{i}}) = \alpha \sqrt{2} a^{5/2} \]
  4. Net Flux: \[ \Phi_{\text{net}} = \Phi_R + \Phi_L = \alpha a^{5/2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) = 800 \times (0.1)^{5/2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) \approx 1.05\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1} \]
  5. Enclosed Charge: By Gauss's Law: \[ q_{\text{encl}} = \varepsilon_0 \Phi_{\text{net}} = (8.854 \times 10^{-12}) \times 1.05 \approx 9.27 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C} = 9.27\text{ pC} \]

১০. বিস্তারিত সমাধানসহ গুরুত্বপূর্ণ উদাহরণ

উদাহরণ ১: আধানের স্থানান্তর এবং কোয়ান্টায়ন
সমস্যা: যদি একটি বস্তু থেকে প্রতি সেকেন্ডে \(10^9\) টি ইলেকট্রন অন্য বস্তুতে স্থানান্তরিত হয়, তবে অন্য বস্তুটিতে মোট \(1\text{ C}\) ঋণাত্মক আধান জমা হতে কত সময় লাগবে?
সমাধান:
  1. প্রতি সেকেন্ডে স্থানান্তরিত আধান: \[ \frac{\Delta q}{\Delta t} = 10^9 \times (1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}) = 1.602 \times 10^{-10}\text{ C s}^{-1} \]
  2. \(1\text{ C}\) আধান সঞ্চয়ের জন্য প্রয়োজনীয় সময়: \[ t = \frac{1\text{ C}}{1.602 \times 10^{-10}\text{ C s}^{-1}} \approx 6.24 \times 10^9\text{ s} \approx 198\text{ বছর} \]
সিদ্ধান্ত: ১ কুলম্ব ব্যবহারিক ক্ষেত্রে অত্যন্ত বৃহৎ একটি আধানের একক।
উদাহরণ ২: স্থিরতড়িৎ বলের উপরিপাত নীতি
সমস্যা: \(l\) বাহুবিশিষ্ট একটি সমবাহু ত্রিভুজের A, B এবং C শীর্ষবিন্দুতে যথাক্রমে \(+q\), \(+q\) এবং \(-q\) আধান রাখা আছে। প্রতিটি আধানের ওপর প্রযুক্ত লব্ধি বল নির্ণয় করো।
সমাধান:
  1. যেকোনো দুটি আধানের মধ্যবর্তী বলের মান: \(F_0 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q^2}{l^2}\)।
  2. A বিন্দুতে \(+q\) আধানের ওপর বল: B-এর বিকর্ষণ বল এবং C-এর আকর্ষণ বলের মধ্যবর্তী কোণ \(120^\circ\)। \[ F_A = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0^2 \cos 120^\circ} = F_0 \] এই বলের অভিমুখ BC বাহুর সমান্তরাল (B থেকে C অভিমুখে)।
  3. B বিন্দুতে \(+q\) আধানের ওপর বল: প্রতিসাম্যের কারণে \(F_B = F_0\), অভিমুখ AC বাহুর সমান্তরাল।
  4. C বিন্দুতে \(-q\) আধানের ওপর বল: A ও B দ্বারা আকর্ষণ বলের মধ্যবর্তী কোণ \(60^\circ\)। \[ F_C = \sqrt{F_0^2 + F_0^2 + 2F_0^2 \cos 60^\circ} = \sqrt{3} F_0 \] অভিমুখ \(\angle C\)-এর সমদ্বিখণ্ডক বরাবর।
  5. সংস্থার মোট বলের সমষ্টি \(\mathbf{F}_A + \mathbf{F}_B + \mathbf{F}_C = \mathbf{0}\)।
উদাহরণ ৩: সুষম তড়িৎক্ষেত্রে ইলেকট্রন ও প্রোটনের পতন
সমস্যা: একটি ইলেকট্রন \(E = 2.0 \times 10^4\text{ N C}^{-1}\) মানের ঊর্ধ্বমুখী সুষম তড়িৎক্ষেত্রে স্থিরাবস্থা থেকে \(h = 1.5\text{ cm}\) নিচে পড়ে। এরপর ক্ষেত্রটির দিক বিপরীত (নিম্নমুখী) করে একই দূরত্বে একটি প্রোটনকে মুক্ত করা হলো। উভয়ের পতনের সময় গণনা করো।
সমাধান:
  1. তড়িৎ বল \(F = eE\); ত্বরণ \(a = eE/m\)। অভিকর্ষজ ত্বরণ \(g\) স্থিরতড়িৎ ত্বরণের তুলনায় নগণ্য।
  2. ইলেকট্রনের পতনের সময়: \[ t_e = \sqrt{\frac{2h m_e}{eE}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.015 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^4}} \approx 2.92 \times 10^{-9}\text{ s} = 2.92\text{ ns} \]
  3. প্রোটনের পতনের সময়: \[ t_p = \sqrt{\frac{2h m_p}{eE}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 0.015 \times 1.67 \times 10^{-27}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^4}} \approx 1.25 \times 10^{-7}\text{ s} = 125\text{ ns} \]
  4. উপসংহার: অভিকর্ষীয় পতনে সময় ভরের ওপর নির্ভর না করলেও স্থিরতড়িৎ ক্ষেত্রে হালকা কণার (ইলেকট্রন) সময় অনেক কম লাগে (\(t \propto \sqrt{m}\))।
উদাহরণ ৪: অসুষম তড়িৎক্ষেত্রে গাউসের সূত্র প্রয়োগ
সমস্যা: তড়িৎক্ষেত্র \(\mathbf{E} = \alpha x^{1/2} \hat{\mathbf{i}}\), যেখানে \(\alpha = 800\text{ N C}^{-1}\text{ m}^{-1/2}\)। \(a = 0.1\text{ m}\) বাহুবিশিষ্ট একটি ঘনকের বাম তল \(x = a\) এবং ডান তল \(x = 2a\)-তে অবস্থিত। ঘনকের মধ্য দিয়ে মোট ফ্লাক্স এবং আবদ্ধ আধান নির্ণয় করো।
সমাধান:
  1. যেহেতু ক্ষেত্র কেবল \(x\)-অক্ষ বরাবর, তাই অন্য চারটি তলের ফ্লাক্স শূন্য।
  2. বাম তল (\(x = a\)): \(\Phi_L = -\alpha a^{5/2}\); ডান তল (\(x = 2a\)): \(\Phi_R = +\alpha \sqrt{2} a^{5/2}\)।
  3. মোট ফ্লাক্স: \[ \Phi_{\text{net}} = \alpha a^{5/2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) = 800 \times (0.1)^{5/2} (\sqrt{2} - 1) \approx 1.05\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-1} \]
  4. গাউসের সূত্রানুযায়ী আবদ্ধ আধান: \[ q_{\text{encl}} = \varepsilon_0 \Phi_{\text{net}} \approx 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 1.05 \approx 9.27 \times 10^{-12}\text{ C} \]

Practice Question 2 • অনুশীলন প্রশ্ন ২ Coulomb's Law & Charge Sharing
English Version

Question: Two identical small conducting spheres A and B have charges \(+q\) and \(-3q\) respectively and repel/attract each other with an electrostatic force \(F\) when separated by distance \(r\). If they are brought into contact and then returned to their initial separation \(r\), the new electrostatic force between them is:

(A) \(\frac{F}{3}\), repulsive
(B) \(\frac{F}{3}\), attractive
(C) \(\frac{4F}{3}\), repulsive
(D) \(3F\), repulsive
Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Solution:

  1. Initial force magnitude: \[ F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{|(+q)(-3q)|}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{3q^2}{r^2} \] (This force was attractive because charges had opposite signs).
  2. When identical conducting spheres touch, total charge divides equally by symmetry: \[ q' = \frac{(+q) + (-3q)}{2} = \frac{-2q}{2} = -q \] Each sphere now carries charge \(-q\).
  3. New force at separation \(r\): \[ F' = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(-q)(-q)}{r^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q^2}{r^2} \]
  4. Ratio: \[ \frac{F'}{F} = \frac{\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q^2}{r^2}}{\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{3q^2}{r^2}} = \frac{1}{3} \implies F' = \frac{F}{3} \] Since both charges now have the same sign (\(-q\)), the new force is repulsive.

Hence, option (A) is correct.

Practice Question 3 • অনুশীলন প্রশ্ন ৩ Equilibrium of Collinear Charges
English Version

Question: Two positive point charges \(+4q\) and \(+q\) are fixed at a distance \(L\) apart. A third point charge \(Q\) is placed on the line joining them such that the entire system is in electrostatic equilibrium. The magnitude, sign, and position of \(Q\) are:

(A) \(Q = -\frac{4}{9}q\), located at distance \(\frac{2L}{3}\) from \(+4q\)
(B) \(Q = +\frac{4}{9}q\), located at distance \(\frac{L}{3}\) from \(+4q\)
(C) \(Q = -\frac{2}{3}q\), located at distance \(\frac{L}{2}\) from \(+4q\)
(D) \(Q = -q\), located at distance \(\frac{2L}{3}\) from \(+4q\)
Correct Answer: (A)

Worked Solution:

  1. Let \(Q\) be placed at distance \(x\) from \(+4q\), and thus distance \((L - x)\) from \(+q\).
  2. For \(Q\) to be in equilibrium, net force on \(Q\) must vanish: \[ \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{4q Q}{x^2} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q Q}{(L - x)^2} \implies \frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{1}{(L - x)^2} \] Taking square roots: \[ \frac{2}{x} = \frac{1}{L - x} \implies 2L - 2x = x \implies 3x = 2L \implies x = \frac{2L}{3} \]
  3. For the entire system to be in equilibrium, the force on charge \(+q\) must also be zero: \[ \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(4q)(q)}{L^2} + \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{Q q}{(L - x)^2} = 0 \] Substitute \(L - x = L/3\): \[ \frac{4q}{L^2} + \frac{Q}{(L/3)^2} = 0 \implies \frac{4q}{L^2} + \frac{9Q}{L^2} = 0 \implies Q = -\frac{4}{9}q \]

Hence, option (A) is correct.

বাংলা সংস্করণ

(A) \(Q = -\frac{4}{9}q\), \(+4q\) থেকে \(\frac{2L}{3}\) দূরত্বে
(B) \(Q = +\frac{4}{9}q\), \(+4q\) থেকে \(\frac{L}{3}\) দূরত্বে
(C) \(Q = -\frac{2}{3}q\), \(+4q\) থেকে \(\frac{L}{2}\) দূরত্বে
(D) \(Q = -q\), \(+4q\) থেকে \(\frac{2L}{3}\) দূরত্বে

বিস্তারিত ব্যাখ্যা:

  1. ধরি, \(+4q\) থেকে \(x\) দূরত্বে \(Q\) অবস্থিত। \(Q\)-এর ওপর লব্ধি বল শূন্য হলে: \[ \frac{4q}{x^2} = \frac{q}{(L - x)^2} \implies \frac{2}{x} = \frac{1}{L - x} \implies x = \frac{2L}{3} \]
  2. সমগ্র সংস্থা সাম্যে থাকার জন্য প্রান্তীয় আধান \(+q\)-এর ওপর মোট বলও শূন্য হতে হবে: \[ \frac{(4q)(q)}{L^2} + \frac{Q q}{(L/3)^2} = 0 \implies 4q + 9Q = 0 \implies Q = -\frac{4}{9}q \]

অতএব, সঠিক বিকল্প হলো (A)।

Practice Question 4 • অনুশীলন প্রশ্ন ৪ Electric Field Lines Properties
English Version

Question: Which of the following statements regarding electrostatic field lines is INCORRECT?

(A) The tangent to a field line at any point gives the direction of electric field at that point.
(B) Electrostatic field lines can form continuous closed loops in empty space.
(C) Two field lines can never cross each other because that would imply two directions of electric field at the intersection point.
(D) The electric field is stronger in regions where field lines are crowded together and weaker where they are spaced apart.
Correct Answer: (B)

Worked Solution:

Electrostatic field lines never form closed loops. This follows directly from the conservative nature of the electrostatic field (\(\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0\)). Therefore, statement (B) is incorrect and the correct choice.

Which particle has the highest specific charge (charge-to-mass ratio, \(|q|/m\))?

(A) Particle 1
(B) Particle 2
(C) Particle 3
(D) Particles 1 and 2 have identical and highest ratio
Correct Answer: (C)

Worked Solution:

  1. Time spent in electric field of length \(x\): \(t = x / v_x\).
  2. Vertical acceleration: \(a_y = \frac{|q|E}{m}\).
  3. Vertical deflection: \[ y = \frac{1}{2} a_y t^2 = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{|q|}{m} \right) \frac{E x^2}{v_x^2} \]

Hence, option (C) is correct.

The electric field in the region between the plates and in the outer region to the right of the second plate are respectively:

12. Common Pitfalls and High-Yield Revision Checklist

Top 5 NEET Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Pitfall 1 (Dipole Moment Direction): By physics convention, the dipole moment \(\mathbf{p}\) points from \(-q\) to \(+q\). In chemistry, it is often drawn from positive to negative. In NEET Physics, strictly follow \(-q \to +q\)!
  • Pitfall 2 (Distance Dependences): Do not mix up the powers of \(r\):
    • Point charge: \(E \propto 1/r^2\)
    • Electric dipole: \(E \propto 1/r^3\)
    • Infinite line charge: \(E \propto 1/r\)
    • Infinite plane sheet: \(E \propto r^0\) (independent of distance)
  • Pitfall 3 (Gauss's Law Interpretation): In \(\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = q_{\text{in}}/\varepsilon_0\), \(\mathbf{E}\) is the total field caused by all existing charges (inside and outside), while \(q_{\text{in}}\) includes only interior charges. Outside charges produce zero net flux, but they distort the local field lines!
  • Pitfall 4 (Stable vs. Unstable Dipole Orientation): When \(\mathbf{p} \parallel \mathbf{E}\) (\(\theta = 0^\circ\)), the dipole is in stable equilibrium (\(U = -pE\)). When \(\mathbf{p}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{E}\) (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)), it is in unstable equilibrium (\(U = +pE\)). Both have \(\tau = 0\).
  • Pitfall 5 (Field vs. Force in Medium): Inserting a dielectric of constant \(K\) decreases Coulomb force and field by factor \(K\): \(F = F_0 / K\). Permittivity increases: \(\varepsilon = K \varepsilon_0\).

High-Yield NEET Revision Checklist

  • ☑ Quantization formula: \(q = \pm ne\) (\(e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}\)).
  • ☑ Coulomb's Law: \(F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\); \(k = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N m}^2\text{ C}^{-2}\).
  • ☑ Electric field of point charge: \(E = \frac{kq}{r^2}\); Force on charge: \(\mathbf{F} = q\mathbf{E}\).
  • ☑ Dipole axial field: \(E_{\text{axial}} = \frac{2kp}{r^3}\); Equatorial field: \(E_{\text{eq}} = \frac{kp}{r^3}\); Ratio: \(E_{\text{axial}} = 2 E_{\text{eq}}\).
  • ☑ Torque on dipole in uniform field: \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{p} \times \mathbf{E}\); Net force: \(\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{0}\).
  • ☑ Gauss's Law: \(\Phi = \oint \mathbf{E} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = \frac{q_{\text{enclosed}}}{\varepsilon_0}\).
  • ☑ Field of long straight wire: \(E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\).
  • ☑ Field of infinite flat sheet: \(E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\).
  • ☑ Spherical shell of radius \(R\): \(E = \frac{kq}{r^2}\) for \(r \ge R\); \(E = 0\) for \(r < R\).
