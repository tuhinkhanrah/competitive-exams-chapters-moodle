3. Core Concepts & Explanations / मूल संकल्पनाएं एवं विस्तृत व्याख्या

3.1 Basic Properties of Magnets and Magnetic Poles / चुंबक के मूल गुण एवं चुंबकीय ध्रुव

English

Magnetism is an intrinsic physical phenomenon historically traced back to naturally occurring iron ores (magnetite, \(\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}\)) discovered in Magnesia, Greece. Modern physics formulates magnetism through moving electrical charges and circulating atomic currents (Ampere's hypothesis).

Fundamental Properties of Magnets:

  1. Directive Property: When freely suspended, a bar magnet aligns approximately along the geographic North-South direction. The end pointing toward geographic north is designated the North-seeking pole (N-pole), and the end pointing toward geographic south is the South-seeking pole (S-pole). The Earth acts as a giant magnetic dipole with its magnetic field directed approximately from geographic south to geographic north.
  2. Attractive and Repulsive Forces: Like magnetic poles repel each other (\(\mathrm{N-N}\) or \(\mathrm{S-S}\)), while unlike magnetic poles attract each other (\(\mathrm{N-S}\)).
  3. Non-Existence of Magnetic Monopoles: Unlike electric charges, where isolated positive and negative charges freely exist, magnetic poles cannot be isolated. Slicing a bar magnet in half creates two complete, smaller bar magnets, each possessing an intact north and south pole. Isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist in nature.
NEET High-Yield: Effect of Cutting a Bar Magnet on Magnetic Moment (\(m\))
Let a bar magnet have pole strength \(q_m\), length \(2l\), and magnetic dipole moment \(m = q_m \times 2l\).
  • Case I: Cut transverse to length (perpendicular to axis): The length of each half becomes \(l\), while the cross-sectional pole strength \(q_m\) remains unchanged. The new magnetic moment of each piece is: \[ m' = q_m \times l = \frac{m}{2} \]
  • Case II: Cut longitudinal to length (parallel to axis): The length remains \(2l\), but the pole strength of each sliced half is halved to \(q_m/2\). The new magnetic moment of each piece is: \[ m' = \left(\frac{q_m}{2}\right) \times 2l = \frac{m}{2} \]
Crucial Result: In both transverse and longitudinal cuts, the new magnetic dipole moment of each resulting half is exactly half the original value (\(m/2\)), though the pole strengths change differently!

  2. आकर्षण एवं प्रतिकर्षण: समान चुंबकीय ध्रुव (\(\mathrm{N-N}\) अथवा \(\mathrm{S-S}\)) एक-दूसरे को प्रतिकर्षित करते हैं, जबकि असमान ध्रुव (\(\mathrm{N-S}\)) परस्पर आकर्षित करते हैं।
  3. चुंबकीय एकल ध्रुवों का अस्तित्व न होना: विद्युत आवेशों के विपरीत, जहाँ स्वतंत्र धन एवं ऋण आवेश पाए जाते हैं, चुंबकीय ध्रुवों को कभी भी अलग नहीं किया जा सकता। यदि किसी चुंबक को दो टुकड़ों में विभाजित किया जाए, तो प्रत्येक टुकड़ा अपने आप में एक पूर्ण चुंबक बन जाता है जिसमें उत्तरी व दक्षिणी ध्रुव विद्यमान होते हैं। प्रकृति में एकल चुंबकीय ध्रुव (मोनोपोल) नहीं पाए जाते।

  • स्थिति I: लंबाई के लंबवत (अनुप्रस्थ) काटना: प्रत्येक आधे भाग की लंबाई \(l\) रह जाती है, जबकि ध्रुव सामर्थ्य \(q_m\) अपरिवर्तित रहती है। प्रत्येक टुकड़े का नया चुंबकीय आघूर्ण: \[ m' = q_m \times l = \frac{m}{2} \]
  • स्थिति II: लंबाई के समांतर (अनुदैर्ध्य) काटना: प्रत्येक भाग की लंबाई \(2l\) ही रहती है, परंतु अनुप्रस्थ काट आधी होने से ध्रुव सामर्थ्य \(q_m/2\) हो जाती है। प्रत्येक टुकड़े का नया चुंबकीय आघूर्ण: \[ m' = \left(\frac{q_m}{2}\right) \times 2l = \frac{m}{2} \]
महत्वपूर्ण निष्कर्ष: चाहे दंड चुंबक को लंबाई के लंबवत काटा जाए अथवा लंबाई के अनुदिश, प्रत्येक नए भाग का चुंबकीय आघूर्ण मूल मान का आधा (\(m/2\)) ही होता है!

3.2 Magnetic Field Lines and Bar Magnet as an Equivalent Solenoid / चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाएं एवं परिनालिका सदृशता

English

Magnetic field lines provide a visual representation of the magnetic field vector \(\mathbf{B}\) in space. Sprinkling iron filings around a bar magnet reveals clear curves emerging from one end and entering the other, mirroring the field around a current-carrying finite solenoid.

Core Properties of Magnetic Field Lines:

  • Continuous Closed Loops: Magnetic field lines form uninterrupted closed loops. Outside the magnet, they emerge from the North pole and enter the South pole. Inside the magnetic material, they travel from the South pole back to the North pole. Contrasting note: Electrostatic field lines are discontinuous; they originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges.
  • Direction of Field: The tangent drawn to a magnetic field line at any given point indicates the direction of the net magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\) at that point.
  • Density Represents Field Strength: The spatial density of field lines (number of lines passing per unit normal area) is directly proportional to the magnitude of \(\mathbf{B}\). Closer spacing indicates a stronger field (e.g., near the poles).
  • No Intersection: Two magnetic field lines can never cross each other. If they did, a magnetic compass needle placed at the intersection point would point in two different directions simultaneously, which is physically impossible.
  • Why not "Lines of Force"? The term "magnetic lines of force" is historically obsolete and technically misleading. In electrostatics, the electric force on a stationary charge is tangential to the electric field line (\(\mathbf{F}_e = q\mathbf{E}\)). In magnetism, the magnetic Lorentz force on a moving charge is \(\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\), which is always perpendicular to the field line, never along it!

Bar Magnet as an Equivalent Finite Solenoid:

Ampere suggested that all magnetic phenomena stem from circulating electric currents. A finite solenoid of length \(2l\), radius \(a\), carrying current \(I\), and having \(n\) turns per unit length contains a continuous series of circular current loops. At a distant axial point \(P\) at distance \(r\) from its center (\(r \gg a\) and \(r \gg l\)), the axial magnetic field is:

\[ B_{\text{axial}} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2m}{r^3} \]

where \(m = N I A = (n \times 2l) I (\pi a^2)\) is the total magnetic dipole moment of the solenoid. Because this field equation is identical to the far axial field of a bar magnet, a bar magnet is equivalent to a closely wound current-carrying solenoid possessing the identical magnetic moment.

चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाएं दिक्स्थान में चुंबकीय क्षेत्र सदिश \(\mathbf{B}\) का दृश्य निरूपण प्रदान करती हैं। किसी दंड चुंबक के चारों ओर लोहे का बुरादा छिड़कने पर सुंदर वक्राकार रेखाएं प्राप्त होती हैं, जो धारावाही परिनालिका के चारों ओर उत्पन्न क्षेत्र रेखाओं के सर्वथा समरूप होती हैं।

चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाओं के प्रमुख गुणधर्म:

  • संतत बंद लूप: चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाएं अविरल बंद वक्र बनाती हैं। चुंबक के बाहर ये उत्तरी ध्रुव (N) से निकलकर दक्षिणी ध्रुव (S) में प्रवेश करती हैं, जबकि चुंबक के भीतर ये दक्षिणी ध्रुव (S) से उत्तरी ध्रुव (N) की ओर गमन करती हैं। तुलनात्मक अंतर: स्थिरवैद्युत क्षेत्र रेखाएं खुले वक्र होती हैं; वे धन आवेश से प्रारंभ होकर ऋण आवेश पर समाप्त होती हैं।
  • क्षेत्र की दिशा: किसी भी बिंदु पर क्षेत्र रेखा पर खींची गई स्पर्श रेखा उस बिंदु पर कुल चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) की दिशा को प्रदर्शित करती है।
  • रेखाओं का घनत्व क्षेत्र की तीव्रता दर्शाता है: एकांक लंबवत क्षेत्रफल से गुजरने वाली रेखाओं की संख्या चुंबकीय क्षेत्र के परिमाण के समानुपाती होती है। ध्रुवों के समीप रेखाएं अत्यधिक सघन होती हैं, जो प्रबल चुंबकीय क्षेत्र को व्यक्त करती हैं।
  • प्रतिच्छेदन का अभाव: दो चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाएं कभी भी एक-दूसरे को नहीं काट सकतीं। यदि वे काटतीं, तो प्रतिच्छेद बिंदु पर चुंबकीय क्षेत्र की दो दिशाएं होतीं, जो भौतिक रूप से सर्वथा असंभव है।
  • इन्हें "बल रेखाएं" क्यों नहीं कहा जाता? चुंबकत्व में "चुंबकीय बल रेखाएं" पद का प्रयोग अनुचित माना जाता है। स्थिरवैद्युतिकी में स्थिर आवेश पर बल क्षेत्र रेखा के अनुदिश होता है (\(\mathbf{F}_e = q\mathbf{E}\)), जबकि चुंबकत्व में गतिशील आवेश पर चुंबकीय लॉरेंट्ज़ बल \(\mathbf{F}_m = q(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\) सदैव चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) के लंबवत कार्य करता है, उसके अनुदिश नहीं!

तुल्य परिनालिका के रूप में दंड चुंबक:

ऐम्पीयर के अनुसार समस्त चुंबकीय प्रभाव परिभ्रामी विद्युत धाराओं के कारण होते हैं। एक परिमित परिनालिका (लंबाई \(2l\), त्रिज्या \(a\), धारा \(I\), प्रति एकांक लंबाई फेरे \(n\)) वृत्ताकार धारा लूपों की एक श्रेणी होती है। इसके केंद्र से अत्यधिक दूरी \(r\) पर अक्षीय बिंदु पर चुंबकीय क्षेत्र का मान निम्न होता है:

\[ B_{\text{axial}} = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2m}{r^3} \]

3.3 Magnetic Dipole in a Uniform Magnetic Field / एकसमान चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव

English

Consider a magnetic needle or small bar magnet of magnetic dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\) placed in a uniform external magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\) at an angle \(\theta\). The magnetic poles experience equal and opposite magnetic forces (\(\mathbf{F}_N = +q_m\mathbf{B}\) and \(\mathbf{F}_S = -q_m\mathbf{B}\)).

Net Force and Restoring Torque:

  • Translational Equilibrium: The net force is strictly zero: \[ \mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{F}_N + \mathbf{F}_S = \mathbf{0} \] A magnetic dipole placed in a uniform magnetic field experiences zero net translational acceleration.
  • Torque: Because the two equal and opposite forces act along different lines of action, they form a couple that exerts a deflecting torque: \[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m} \times \mathbf{B} \] In magnitude: \[ \tau = m B \sin\theta \] The direction of \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\) (given by the right-hand thumb rule) acts to rotate the magnetic dipole into alignment with \(\mathbf{B}\) (\(\theta \to 0^\circ\)).

Magnetic Potential Energy (\(U_m\)):

The work done by an external torque to rotate the dipole against the restoring magnetic torque is stored as potential energy:

\[ U_m = \int \tau(\theta) \, d\theta = \int m B \sin\theta \, d\theta = -m B \cos\theta = -\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B} \]

By standard convention, the zero of potential energy is chosen at \(\theta = 90^\circ\) (\(\mathbf{m} \perp \mathbf{B}\)).

Equilibrium States of a Magnetic Dipole:
  1. Stable Equilibrium (\(\theta = 0^\circ\)): Dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\) is parallel to \(\mathbf{B}\). \[ \tau = mB\sin 0^\circ = 0, \quad U_{\text{min}} = -mB \] Potential energy is at an absolute minimum. If slightly perturbed, the restoring torque pulls the dipole back to \(\theta = 0^\circ\).
  2. Unstable Equilibrium (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)): Dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{B}\). \[ \tau = mB\sin 180^\circ = 0, \quad U_{\text{max}} = +mB \] Potential energy is maximum. Any minor perturbation results in a torque that drives the dipole away from this orientation.
  3. Zero Reference State (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)): Dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\) is perpendicular to \(\mathbf{B}\). \[ \tau = mB \quad (\text{Maximum torque}), \quad U = 0 \]

Work Done in Rotating the Dipole:

The external work \(W_{\text{ext}}\) required to rotate the dipole from an initial orientation \(\theta_1\) to a final orientation \(\theta_2\) is:

\[ W_{\text{ext}} = \Delta U = U(\theta_2) - U(\theta_1) = -mB\cos\theta_2 - (-mB\cos\theta_1) = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2) \]

For example, rotating a dipole from stable equilibrium (\(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\)) to unstable equilibrium (\(\theta_2 = 180^\circ\)) requires:

\[ W = mB(\cos 0^\circ - \cos 180^\circ) = mB(1 - (-1)) = 2mB \]

चुंबकीय स्थितिज ऊर्जा (\(U_m\)):

प्रत्यानयन चुंबकीय बल आघूर्ण के विरुद्ध द्विध्रुव को घुमाने में बाह्य स्रोत द्वारा किया गया कार्य स्थितिज ऊर्जा के रूप में संचित होता है:

चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव की संतुलन अवस्थाएं:

  2. अस्थायी संतुलन (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)): द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण \(\mathbf{m}\) चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) के प्रति-समांतर होता है। \[ \tau = mB\sin 180^\circ = 0, \quad U_{\text{max}} = +mB \] स्थितिज ऊर्जा अधिकतम होती है। थोड़ा सा भी विक्षेपित करने पर यह इस अवस्था से दूर घूम जाता है।
  3. शून्य संदर्भ अवस्था (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)): जब \(\mathbf{m}\) क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) के लंबवत हो। \[ \tau = mB \quad (\text{अधिकतम बल आघूर्ण}), \quad U = 0 \]

द्विध्रुव को घुमाने में किया गया कार्य:

द्विध्रुव को प्रारंभिक कोण \(\theta_1\) से अंतिम कोण \(\theta_2\) तक घुमाने में बाह्य बल आघूर्ण द्वारा किया गया कार्य:

\[ W_{\text{ext}} = \Delta U = U(\theta_2) - U(\theta_1) = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2) \]

उदाहरणार्थ, स्थायी संतुलन (\(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\)) से अस्थायी संतुलन (\(\theta_2 = 180^\circ\)) तक घुमाने में आवश्यक कार्य:

\[ W = mB(\cos 0^\circ - \cos 180^\circ) = 2mB \]

3.4 The Electrostatic Analogy for Magnetic Dipoles / स्थिरवैद्युतिकी समरूपता

English

The mathematical formulations for a magnetic dipole of moment \(\mathbf{m}\) are directly obtained from electric dipole equations of moment \(\mathbf{p}\) by substituting the corresponding electrostatic quantities with their magnetic analogs:

\[ \mathbf{E} \longrightarrow \mathbf{B}, \quad \mathbf{p} \longrightarrow \mathbf{m}, \quad \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \longrightarrow \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \]

Magnetic Field of a Short Bar Magnet (\(r \gg l\)):

  • Axial Field (\(\mathbf{B}_A\)): At distance \(r\) along the magnetic axis (pointing from S to N): \[ \mathbf{B}_A = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2\mathbf{m}}{r^3} \] Notice that \(\mathbf{B}_A\) is directed parallel to the magnetic dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\).
  • Equatorial Field (\(\mathbf{B}_E\)): At distance \(r\) along the normal bisector (equatorial plane): \[ \mathbf{B}_E = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{\mathbf{m}}{r^3} \] Notice that \(\mathbf{B}_E\) is directed antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}\).
  • Ratio of Magnitudes: For equal distant points on the axis and equator (\(r_{\text{axial}} = r_{\text{equatorial}} = r\)): \[ \frac{|\mathbf{B}_A|}{|\mathbf{B}_E|} = 2 \] The axial field magnitude is exactly twice the equatorial field magnitude, and their directions are strictly opposite!

3.5 Magnetism and Gauss's Law / चुंबकत्व एवं गाउस का नियम

English

In electrostatics, Gauss's law establishes that the net electric flux through any closed surface is proportional to the net enclosed electric charge: \( \oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = q/\varepsilon_0 \). For an isolated positive charge, field lines diverge outward, creating a non-zero outward flux.

In stark contrast, magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops without beginning or end. Every field line that enters a closed surface must inevitably exit it. Consequently, Gauss's Law for Magnetism states:

\[ \phi_B = \oint_S \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0 \]

Physical Significance and Fundamental Conclusions:

  • Absence of Magnetic Monopoles: There are no isolated magnetic charges (sources or sinks of \(\mathbf{B}\)). The simplest magnetic element is inherently a dipole or a current loop.
  • Theoretical Extension: If isolated magnetic monopoles of magnetic charge \(q_m\) were ever discovered in nature, Gauss's law for magnetism would be modified to: \[ \oint_S \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \mu_0 q_m \] analogous to Gauss's law for electrostatics.
NEET Conceptual Analysis: Evaluating Magnetic Field Line Configurations
Physics examinations frequently ask students to identify invalid magnetic field line patterns. The essential criteria derived from Gauss's and Ampere's laws are:
  1. Cannot radiate from a point: Field lines diverging radially outward from a single point would mean a net non-zero magnetic flux across a sphere enclosing that point, violating \(\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0\). (Such radial lines belong strictly to isolated electric charges).
  2. Cannot cross each other: An intersection point implies two distinct directions for \(\mathbf{B}\) at the same point, which is physically impossible.
  3. Closed loops cannot exist around empty space: By Ampere's circuital law (\(\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = \mu_0 I_{\text{enclosed}}\)), magnetostatic field lines can form closed loops only if they enclose a region through which an electric current or moving charge passes. They cannot form loops encircling empty space devoid of current.
  4. End-effect Fringing is Mandatory: Magnetic field lines between two magnetic pole faces or at the open ends of a solenoid cannot be perfectly straight and abruptly terminate; they must flare outwards (fringe) and loop back. Perfectly straight, confined lines at the boundary would violate Ampere's circuital law.

3.6 Magnetisation (\(\mathbf{M}\)), Magnetic Intensity (\(\mathbf{H}\)), and Permeability (\(\mu, \mu_r, \chi\)) / चुंबकन, चुंबकीय तीव्रता एवं पारगम्यता

English

Inside matter, atomic electrons orbiting nuclei and possessing intrinsic spin act as microscopic circulating current loops. In macroscopic bulk materials, these atomic magnetic dipole moments sum vectorially.

1. Magnetisation Vector (\(\mathbf{M}\)): Defined as the net magnetic dipole moment per unit volume of the material:

\[ \mathbf{M} = \frac{\mathbf{m}_{\text{net}}}{V} \]
  • SI Unit: \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\) (ampere per metre) or \(\mathrm{J\,T^{-1}\,m^{-3}}\).
  • Dimensional Formula: \([\mathrm{L^{-1} A}]\).

2. Magnetic Intensity (\(\mathbf{H}\)): When an external magnetising field is applied (such as an electric current \(I\) in a solenoid of \(n\) turns per unit length), the external magnetising agent is quantified by the magnetic intensity vector \(\mathbf{H}\):

\[ \mathbf{H} = nI \]

The vector \(\mathbf{H}\) depends purely on external driving currents and geometry, completely independent of the core material placed inside. It has the same dimensions and SI units as magnetisation: \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\) (\([\mathrm{L^{-1} A}]\)).

3. Total Magnetic Induction (\(\mathbf{B}\)) in Material: Inside a material core placed inside a solenoid, the total magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\) is the superposition of the vacuum field \(\mathbf{B}_0 = \mu_0 \mathbf{H}\) and the internal field produced by material magnetisation \(\mathbf{B}_m = \mu_0 \mathbf{M}\):

\[ \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B}_0 + \mathbf{B}_m = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}) \]

4. Magnetic Susceptibility (\(\chi\)): For linear, isotropic magnetic materials, the induced magnetisation \(\mathbf{M}\) is directly proportional to the magnetic intensity \(\mathbf{H}\):

\[ \mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H} \implies \chi = \frac{M}{H} \]

\(\chi\) is a dimensionless scalar quantity that indicates how easily and in which direction a material magnetises when subjected to an external magnetic field.

5. Relative Permeability (\(\mu_r\)) and Magnetic Permeability (\(\mu\)):

\[ \mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \chi \mathbf{H}) = \mu_0(1 + \chi)\mathbf{H} = \mu_0 \mu_r \mathbf{H} = \mu \mathbf{H} \]

Hence, the fundamental interrelationships are:

\[ \mu_r = 1 + \chi, \quad \mu = \mu_0 \mu_r = \mu_0 (1 + \chi) \]
  • \(\mu_r\) is dimensionless and analogous to the dielectric constant \(K\) in electrostatics.
  • \(\mu\) (permeability of the material) has SI units of \(\mathrm{T\,m\,A^{-1}}\) or \(\mathrm{N\,A^{-2}}\), with dimensions \([\mathrm{M L T^{-2} A^{-2}}]\).
  • \(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{\,T\,m\,A^{-1}}\) is the permeability of free space.

6. Magnetising Current (\(I_m\)): The equivalent additional current that must pass through the solenoid windings in the absence of a magnetic core to produce the same total magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}\) achieved with the core:

\[ B = \mu_0 n (I + I_m) \implies I_m = (\mu_r - 1)I = \chi I \]

पदार्थ के भीतर नाभिक के परितः परिक्रमण करते तथा चक्रण करते इलेक्ट्रॉन सूक्ष्म धारा लूपों की भांति कार्य करते हैं। स्थूल पदार्थ में इन सभी परमाण्वीय चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव आघूर्णों का सदिश योग होता है।

1. चुंबकन सदिश (\(\mathbf{M}\)): पदार्थ के प्रति एकांक आयतन में उत्पन्न कुल चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण को चुंबकन कहते हैं:

\[ \mathbf{M} = \frac{\mathbf{m}_{\text{net}}}{V} \]
  • SI मात्रक: \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\) (ऐम्पीयर प्रति मीटर)।
  • विमीय सूत्र: \([\mathrm{L^{-1} A}]\)।

2. चुंबकीय तीव्रता (\(\mathbf{H}\)): जब किसी बाह्य चुंबकीय क्षेत्र (जैसे \(n\) फेरे प्रति एकांक लंबाई वाली परिनालिका में धारा \(I\)) को लागू किया जाता है, तो बाह्य चुंबकन कारक को चुंबकीय तीव्रता \(\mathbf{H}\) द्वारा व्यक्त किया जाता है:

\[ \mathbf{H} = nI \]

सदिश \(\mathbf{H}\) केवल बाह्य चालन धाराओं एवं ज्यामिति पर निर्भर करता है, पदार्थ की प्रकृति पर नहीं। इसका SI मात्रक भी \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\) तथा विमीय सूत्र \([\mathrm{L^{-1} A}]\) है।

3. पदार्थ में कुल चुंबकीय प्रेरण (\(\mathbf{B}\)): परिनालिका में रखे पदार्थ के भीतर कुल चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\), निर्वात के क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}_0 = \mu_0 \mathbf{H}\) तथा पदार्थ के चुंबकन द्वारा उत्पन्न क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}_m = \mu_0 \mathbf{M}\) का सदिश योग होता है:

\[ \mathbf{B} = \mathbf{B}_0 + \mathbf{B}_m = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M}) \]

4. चुंबकीय प्रवृत्ति (\(\chi\)): रेखीय एवं समदैशिक पदार्थों के लिए चुंबकन \(\mathbf{M}\), चुंबकीय तीव्रता \(\mathbf{H}\) के समानुपाती होता है:

\[ \mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H} \implies \chi = \frac{M}{H} \]

\(\chi\) एक विमाहीन अदिश राशि है, जो यह दर्शाती है कि बाह्य चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में रखने पर पदार्थ कितनी सुगमता से और किस दिशा में चुंबकित होता है।

5. आपेक्षिक पारगम्यता (\(\mu_r\)) एवं चुंबकशीलता (\(\mu\)):

\[ \mathbf{B} = \mu_0(1 + \chi)\mathbf{H} = \mu_0 \mu_r \mathbf{H} = \mu \mathbf{H} \]

अतः इनके मध्य आधारभूत संबंध निम्न हैं:

\[ \mu_r = 1 + \chi, \quad \mu = \mu_0 \mu_r = \mu_0 (1 + \chi) \]
  • \(\mu_r\) विमाहीन राशि है, जो स्थिरवैद्युतिकी के परावैद्युतांक \(K\) के तुल्य है।
  • \(\mu\) का SI मात्रक \(\mathrm{T\,m\,A^{-1}}\) अथवा \(\mathrm{N\,A^{-2}}\) है, तथा विमीय सूत्र \([\mathrm{M L T^{-2} A^{-2}}]\) है।
  • निर्वात की चुंबकशीलता \(\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \mathrm{\,T\,m\,A^{-1}}\) होती है।

6. चुंबकन धारा (\(I_m\)): क्रोड की अनुपस्थिति में परिनालिका में प्रवाहित की जाने वाली वह अतिरिक्त धारा, जो क्रोड युक्त परिनालिका के समान कुल चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) उत्पन्न करे:

\[ B = \mu_0 n (I + I_m) \implies I_m = (\mu_r - 1)I = \chi I \]

3.7 Classification of Magnetic Materials / चुंबकीय पदार्थों का वर्गीकरण

English

Based on their response to external magnetic fields, magnetic susceptibility \(\chi\), and permeability \(\mu_r\), all materials are classified into three primary categories:

1. Diamagnetic Materials (प्रतिचुंबकीय पदार्थ)

  • Behavior in Magnetic Fields: Feebly repelled by magnets. When placed in a non-uniform magnetic field, they tend to migrate slowly from regions of stronger field to weaker field.
  • Field Line Pattern: Magnetic field lines are expelled or repelled from the interior of the material. The magnetic field inside the material (\(B\)) is slightly less than the external field (\(B_0\)).
  • Microscopic Origin: Found in atoms/molecules with fully paired electrons having zero net intrinsic magnetic moment (\(m_{\text{atom}} = 0\)). When an external field \(\mathbf{B}\) is applied, according to Faraday-Lenz's law, circulating electrons experience electromagnetic induction: electrons with orbital magnetic moment parallel to \(\mathbf{B}\) decelerate, while those antiparallel accelerate. This induces a net magnetic moment strictly opposite to \(\mathbf{B}\).
  • Susceptibility & Permeability: \[ -1 \le \chi < 0 \quad (\text{small and negative, independent of temperature}), \quad 0 \le \mu_r < 1, \quad \mu < \mu_0 \]
  • Universality: Diamagnetism is present in all substances; however, because it is extremely weak (\(\chi \sim -10^{-5}\)), it is completely masked in substances exhibiting paramagnetism or ferromagnetism.
  • Examples: Bismuth (\(\mathrm{Bi}\), strongest element), Copper (\(\mathrm{Cu}\)), Lead (\(\mathrm{Pb}\)), Silicon (\(\mathrm{Si}\)), Nitrogen at STP, Water (\(\mathrm{H_2O}\)), Sodium Chloride (\(\mathrm{NaCl}\)).
  • Superconductors and Meissner Effect: Superconductors cooled below their critical transition temperature exhibit both zero electrical resistivity and perfect diamagnetism: \[ \chi = -1, \quad \mu_r = 0, \quad \mathbf{B}_{\text{inside}} = \mathbf{0} \] The complete expulsion of magnetic flux lines from the interior of a superconductor is known as the Meissner Effect, which enables frictionless magnetic levitation (e.g., Maglev bullet trains).

2. Paramagnetic Materials (अनुचुंबकीय पदार्थ)

  • Behavior in Magnetic Fields: Feebly attracted by magnets. In a non-uniform field, they migrate from regions of weaker field to stronger field.
  • Field Line Pattern: Field lines are pulled inward and slightly concentrated inside the material. The interior field \(B\) is slightly greater than \(B_0\).
  • Microscopic Origin: Individual atoms or ions possess a permanent magnetic dipole moment (\(m_{\text{atom}} \neq 0\)) due to unpaired electrons. In the absence of an external field, ceaseless thermal agitation randomizes the dipole orientations, yielding zero net bulk magnetisation (\(\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}\)). When an external field is applied, the atomic dipoles experience an aligning torque, resulting in weak net magnetisation parallel to \(\mathbf{B}\).
  • Susceptibility & Permeability: \[ 0 < \chi < \varepsilon \quad (\text{small and positive, } \sim 10^{-5}), \quad 1 < \mu_r < 1 + \varepsilon, \quad \mu > \mu_0 \]
  • Temperature Dependence: Highly temperature-dependent. Elevated temperatures increase random thermal collisions, disrupting dipole alignment. Magnetisation increases with increasing magnetic field or decreasing temperature until saturation is reached (where all atomic dipoles are fully aligned).
  • Examples: Aluminium (\(\mathrm{Al}\)), Sodium (\(\mathrm{Na}\)), Calcium (\(\mathrm{Ca}\)), Oxygen gas at STP (\(\mathrm{O_2}\)), Copper Chloride (\(\mathrm{CuCl_2}\)).

3. Ferromagnetic Materials (लौहचुंबकीय पदार्थ)

  • Behavior in Magnetic Fields: Strongly attracted to magnets. In non-uniform magnetic fields, they move rapidly toward regions of highest field strength.
  • Field Line Pattern: Field lines crowd intensely into the material. The internal magnetic field is hundreds to thousands of times stronger than the applied external field.
  • Microscopic Origin (Domain Theory): Individual atoms have permanent dipole moments that interact via quantum mechanical exchange forces. This causes millions of adjacent atomic dipoles to spontaneously align parallel to one another across microscopic macroscopic regions called domains (typical volume \(\sim 1\,\mathrm{mm^3}\), containing \(\sim 10^{11}\) atoms).
    • Unmagnetised State: The magnetisation vectors of individual domains point in random directions, producing zero net bulk magnetisation.
    • In Applied Field: Domains aligned favorably with \(\mathbf{B}_0\) grow in volume by domain wall displacement, while unfavorably aligned domains rotate their magnetisation toward \(\mathbf{B}_0\). In strong fields, domains coalesce into a single giant domain pointing along \(\mathbf{B}_0\).
  • Susceptibility & Permeability: \[ \chi \gg 1 \quad (\text{very large and positive, } \sim 10^3\text{ to }10^5), \quad \mu_r \gg 1 \quad (\mu_r > 1000), \quad \mu \gg \mu_0 \]
  • Temperature Dependence: Domain structure is disrupted by thermal energy. As temperature rises, ferromagnetic order gradually decreases until, at sufficiently high temperatures, the domain structure completely disintegrates and the ferromagnet transitions into a paramagnet.
  • Hard vs Soft Ferromagnets:
    • Hard Ferromagnets: Retain substantial magnetisation even after the external field is removed (high retentivity and coercivity). Used to fabricate permanent magnets (e.g., compass needles, loudspeaker magnets). Examples: Alnico (alloy of \(\mathrm{Fe, Al, Ni, Co, Cu}\)) and natural lodestone.
    • Soft Ferromagnets: Magnetisation vanishes rapidly upon removal of the external magnetising field (low retentivity and coercivity). Used in transformer cores, electromagnets, and chokes to minimize hysteretic energy loss. Prime example: Soft Iron.
  • Ferromagnetic Elements: Iron (\(\mathrm{Fe}\)), Cobalt (\(\mathrm{Co}\)), Nickel (\(\mathrm{Ni}\)), Gadolinium (\(\mathrm{Gd}\)).

2. अनुचुंबकीय पदार्थ (Paramagnetic Materials)

  • चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में व्यवहार: चुंबक द्वारा दुर्बल रूप से आकर्षित होते हैं। असमान चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में ये कम तीव्रता वाले भाग से अधिक तीव्रता वाले भाग की ओर आकर्षित होते हैं।
  • क्षेत्र रेखाओं का स्वरूप: क्षेत्र रेखाएं पदार्थ के भीतर संकेंद्रित हो जाती हैं। पदार्थ के भीतर चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(B\) बाह्य क्षेत्र \(B_0\) से थोड़ा सा अधिक होता है।
  • परमाण्वीय मूल: अयुग्मित इलेक्ट्रॉनों के कारण प्रत्येक परमाणु अथवा आयन का अपना स्थायी चुंबकीय द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण होता है (\(m_{\text{atom}} \neq 0\))। बाह्य क्षेत्र की अनुपस्थिति में परमाणुओं के सतत यादृच्छिक ऊष्मीय विक्षोभ के कारण नेट चुंबकन शून्य रहता है। बाह्य क्षेत्र लगाने पर द्विध्रुवों पर संरेखक बल आघूर्ण कार्य करता है, जिससे क्षेत्र की दिशा में अल्प नेट चुंबकन उत्पन्न होता है।

  • तापमान पर निर्भरता: तापमान बढ़ने पर ऊष्मीय विक्षोभ बढ़ता है जिससे द्विध्रुवों का संरेखण बाधित होता है। चुंबकीय क्षेत्र बढ़ाने अथवा तापमान घटाने पर चुंबकन बढ़ता है, जब तक कि संतृप्ति अवस्था प्राप्त न हो जाए।

3. लौहचुंबकीय पदार्थ (Ferromagnetic Materials)

  • चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में व्यवहार: चुंबक द्वारा अत्यधिक प्रबलता से आकर्षित होते हैं। असमान चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में ये दुर्बल क्षेत्र से अति तीव्र क्षेत्र की ओर तीव्र गति से आकर्षित होते हैं।
  • क्षेत्र रेखाओं का स्वरूप: चुंबकीय क्षेत्र रेखाएं अत्यधिक सघनता से पदार्थ के भीतर केंद्रित होती हैं। पदार्थ के भीतर चुंबकीय क्षेत्र बाह्य क्षेत्र की तुलना में सैकड़ों से हजारों गुना अधिक हो जाता है।
  • डोमेन सिद्धांत: इनमें परमाण्वीय द्विध्रुव क्वांटम यांत्रिक विनिमय अन्योन्यक्रिया द्वारा परस्पर अंतःक्रिया करते हैं, जिससे सूक्ष्म स्थूल क्षेत्रों में स्वतः एक ही दिशा में संरेखित हो जाते हैं। इन क्षेत्रों को डोमेन कहते हैं (विशिष्ट आयतन \(\sim 1\,\mathrm{mm^3}\), परमाणुओं की संख्या \(\sim 10^{11}\))।
    • अचुंबकित अवस्था: विभिन्न डोमेनों का चुंबकन यादृच्छिक दिशाओं में होता है, जिससे कुल स्थूल चुंबकन शून्य होता है।
    • बाह्य क्षेत्र में: क्षेत्र की दिशा में संरेखित डोमेन भित्ति-विस्थापन द्वारा आकार में बढ़ते हैं, तथा अन्य डोमेन घूमकर क्षेत्र की दिशा में संरेखित होते हैं। प्रबल क्षेत्र में पूरा पदार्थ एक विशाल एकल डोमेन बन जाता है।
  • प्रवृत्ति एवं पारगम्यता: \[ \chi \gg 1 \quad (\text{अत्यधिक मान, } \sim 10^3\text{ से }10^5), \quad \mu_r \gg 1 \quad (\mu_r > 1000), \quad \mu \gg \mu_0 \]
  • तापमान पर निर्भरता: तापमान वृद्धि से डोमेन संरचना धीरे-धीरे विघटित होने लगती है। उच्च तापमान पर लौहचुंबकत्व समाप्त हो जाता है और पदार्थ अनुचुंबकीय पदार्थ में रूपांतरित हो जाता है।
  • कठोर एवं नर्म लौहचुंबकीय पदार्थ:
    • कठोर लौहचुंबक: बाह्य क्षेत्र हटाने के पश्चात भी दीर्घकाल तक प्रबल चुंबकन बनाए रखते हैं (उच्च धारणशीलता व निग्राहिता)। इनका उपयोग स्थायी चुंबक (दिकसूचक सुई आदि) बनाने में होता है। उदाहरण: ऐल्निको (Alnico) (\(\mathrm{Fe, Al, Ni, Co, Cu}\) की मिश्रधातु) एवं प्राकृतिक लोडस्टोन।
    • नर्म लौहचुंबक: बाह्य क्षेत्र हटाते ही इनका चुंबकन शीघ्रता से समाप्त हो जाता है। इनका उपयोग ट्रांसफार्मर क्रोड तथा विद्युत चुंबक बनाने में होता है ताकि ऊर्जा हानि न्यूनतम हो। उदाहरण: नर्म लोहा (Soft Iron)।

5. Conceptual Connections & NEET Traps / वैचारिक संबंध एवं परीक्षा की प्रमुख भ्रांतियां

English

1. Uniform vs. Non-Uniform Magnetic Fields on a Dipole:

  • In a uniform field, the net force on a dipole is strictly zero (\(\mathbf{F}_{\text{net}} = \mathbf{0}\)), but a torque acts (\(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{B}\)).
  • In a non-uniform field, because the field strength differs at the north and south poles, both a net force and a net torque act simultaneously! This explains why an unmagnetised iron nail placed near a bar magnet is drawn towards it: the bar magnet's non-uniform field induces magnetic poles in the nail, and the closer induced opposite pole experiences a stronger attractive force than the farther repelling pole, resulting in net attraction.

2. Magnetic Monopoles vs Electric Charge Quantisation:

  • Electric charges exist as isolated entities and are quantised in integer multiples of the fundamental charge \(e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\mathrm{\,C}\).
  • In contrast, isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist. Even an individual electron possesses an intrinsic magnetic dipole moment due to quantum mechanical spin. Slicing a magnet repeatedly always yields dipoles.

3. Universality of Diamagnetism:

  • Diamagnetism is an intrinsic property of all matter because all atoms possess orbiting electrons that undergo electromagnetic induction in an external field. However, in substances with permanent atomic dipoles (paramagnets and ferromagnets), the aligning paramagnetic/ferromagnetic effect is much stronger and completely overwhelms the faint diamagnetic response.

Worked Example 1: Dipole Torque, Work Done, and Potential Energy Standard NEET
English

Problem: A short bar magnet of magnetic moment \(m = 1.5\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\) is placed aligned with a uniform horizontal magnetic field of \(B = 0.22\mathrm{\,T}\).

  1. What is the torque on the magnet in this initial orientation?
  2. What is the external work required to rotate the magnet so as to align its magnetic moment: (i) normal to the field direction (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)), (ii) opposite to the field direction (\(\theta = 180^\circ\))?
  3. What is the magnitude of torque in cases (i) and (ii)?
Solution:

(a) Initial Torque (\(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\)):

\[ \tau_1 = m B \sin(0^\circ) = 1.5 \times 0.22 \times 0 = 0\mathrm{\,N\,m} \]

(b) External Work Done \(W = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\):

  • (i) From \(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\) to \(\theta_2 = 90^\circ\): \[ W = mB(\cos 0^\circ - \cos 90^\circ) = (1.5 \times 0.22)(1 - 0) = 0.33\mathrm{\,J} \]
  • (ii) From \(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\) to \(\theta_2 = 180^\circ\): \[ W = mB(\cos 0^\circ - \cos 180^\circ) = (1.5 \times 0.22)(1 - (-1)) = 2 \times 0.33 = 0.66\mathrm{\,J} \]

(c) Torque in Final Orientations:

  • For case (i) (\(\theta = 90^\circ\)): \[ \tau = m B \sin(90^\circ) = 1.5 \times 0.22 \times 1 = 0.33\mathrm{\,N\,m} \quad (\text{directed to align with field}) \]
  • For case (ii) (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)): \[ \tau = m B \sin(180^\circ) = 1.5 \times 0.22 \times 0 = 0\mathrm{\,N\,m} \]

Worked Example 2: Solenoid with Core — H, B, M, and Magnetising Current High-Yield NEET
English

Problem: A long solenoid has \(1000\mathrm{\,turns\,m^{-1}}\) and carries a current of \(2.0\mathrm{\,A}\). It is filled with an iron core of relative permeability \(\mu_r = 400\). Calculate:

  1. Magnetic intensity \(H\) inside the core
  2. Total magnetic field \(B\) inside the core
  3. Magnetisation \(M\) of the core material
  4. The additional magnetising current \(I_m\) required without a core to achieve the same magnetic field
Solution:

(a) Magnetic Intensity \(H\):

\[ H = nI = 1000\mathrm{\,m^{-1}} \times 2.0\mathrm{\,A} = 2.0 \times 10^3\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} \]

(b) Total Magnetic Induction \(B\):

\[ B = \mu H = \mu_r \mu_0 H = 400 \times (4\pi \times 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,T\,m\,A^{-1}}) \times 2000\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} \] \[ B = 400 \times 8\pi \times 10^{-4} \approx 3200\pi \times 10^{-4} = 0.32\pi \approx 1.005\mathrm{\,T} \approx 1.0\mathrm{\,T} \]

(c) Magnetisation \(M\):

\[ M = \chi H = (\mu_r - 1) H = (400 - 1) \times 2000\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} = 399 \times 2000 = 7.98 \times 10^5\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} \approx 8.0 \times 10^5\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} \]

(d) Additional Magnetising Current \(I_m\):

\[ B = \mu_0 n (I + I_m) \implies I + I_m = \frac{B}{\mu_0 n} = \mu_r I \] \[ I_m = (\mu_r - 1) I = (400 - 1) \times 2.0\mathrm{\,A} = 399 \times 2.0 = 798\mathrm{\,A} \]

Insight: An enormous current of \(798\mathrm{\,A}\) would be required to produce a \(1.0\mathrm{\,T}\) field in an air-core solenoid, demonstrating the profound practical value of a ferromagnetic core in electromagnets!

  3. क्रोड पदार्थ का चुंबकन \(M\)

\[ H = nI = 1000 \times 2.0 = 2.0 \times 10^3\mathrm{\,A\,m^{-1}} \]

(b) कुल चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(B\):

\[ B = \mu_r \mu_0 H = 400 \times (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times (2.0 \times 10^3) \approx 1.0\mathrm{\,T} \]

English

Problem: A short bar magnet has a magnetic dipole moment of \(m = 0.48\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\). Determine the magnitude and direction of the magnetic field produced by the magnet at a distance of \(10\mathrm{\,cm}\) from its center:

  1. On its magnetic axis
  2. On its normal bisector (equatorial line)
Solution:

Given: \(m = 0.48\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\), \(r = 10\mathrm{\,cm} = 0.1\mathrm{\,m}\), \(\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} = 10^{-7}\mathrm{\,T\,m\,A^{-1}}\).

\[ B_A = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{2m}{r^3} = 10^{-7} \times \frac{2 \times 0.48}{(0.1)^3} = 10^{-7} \times \frac{0.96}{10^{-3}} = 0.96 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,T} = 0.96\mathrm{\,G} \]

Direction: Directed along the axis from the South pole to the North pole (parallel to \(\mathbf{m}\)).

(b) Equatorial Field Magnitude and Direction:

\[ B_E = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{m}{r^3} = \frac{B_A}{2} = \frac{0.96 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,T}}{2} = 0.48 \times 10^{-4}\mathrm{\,T} = 0.48\mathrm{\,G} \]

Direction: Directed parallel to the magnet's axis from the North pole to the South pole (antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}\)).

Question: A short bar magnet placed with its magnetic axis at an angle of \(30^\circ\) to a uniform external magnetic field of \(0.25\mathrm{\,T}\) experiences a torque of magnitude \(4.5 \times 10^{-2}\mathrm{\,J}\). What is the magnitude of the magnetic dipole moment of the magnet?

(A) \(0.18\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\)
(B) \(0.36\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\)
(C) \(0.72\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\)
(D) \(0.09\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\)
Correct Answer: Option (B)
Worked Reasoning & Calculation:

The torque on a magnetic dipole in a uniform magnetic field is given by:

\[ \tau = m B \sin\theta \]

Given values:

  • \(\tau = 4.5 \times 10^{-2}\mathrm{\,J}\)
  • \(B = 0.25\mathrm{\,T} = \frac{1}{4}\mathrm{\,T}\)
  • \(\theta = 30^\circ \implies \sin 30^\circ = 0.5 = \frac{1}{2}\)

Substituting into the equation:

\[ 4.5 \times 10^{-2} = m \times 0.25 \times 0.5 = m \times 0.125 = m \times \frac{1}{8} \] \[ m = 8 \times (4.5 \times 10^{-2}) = 36.0 \times 10^{-2} = 0.36\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}} \]
NEET Speed Tip: Remember \(\sin 30^\circ = 1/2\) and \(0.25 = 1/4\). Their product is \(1/8\). Simply multiply torque by 8!

Question: A bar magnet of magnetic moment \(m = 0.4\mathrm{\,J\,T^{-1}}\) is initially placed in stable equilibrium in a uniform horizontal magnetic field of \(B = 0.5\mathrm{\,T}\). How much work must be done by an external agent to rotate it into the position of unstable equilibrium?

(C) \(0.4\mathrm{\,J}\)

1. Stable equilibrium corresponds to \(\theta_1 = 0^\circ\).

Practice Question 5 / अभ्यास प्रश्न 5 NEET Assertion-Reason Format

Directions: In the following question, a statement of Assertion (A) is followed by a statement of Reason (R).

Assertion (A): The net magnetic flux passing through any arbitrary closed Gaussian surface is always zero.

Reason (R): Isolated magnetic monopoles do not exist in nature, and magnetic field lines form continuous closed loops without beginning or end.

(A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
(C) (A) is true but (R) is false.
(D) (A) is false but (R) is true.
Correct Answer: Option (A)
Worked Reasoning & Explanation:

According to Gauss's law for magnetism, \(\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0\) for any closed surface. The fundamental physical reason for this mathematical condition is the non-existence of isolated magnetic charges (magnetic monopoles). Because magnetic poles always exist in pairs (dipoles), magnetic field lines never originate or terminate on points; instead, they circulate in continuous, unbroken closed loops. Hence, every field line that enters a closed surface must also exit it, ensuring that net flux is identically zero. Thus, Assertion is true, Reason is true, and Reason correctly explains Assertion.

Practice Question 7 / अभ्यास प्रश्न 7 NEET Cutting of Magnet Problem
English

Question: A uniform bar magnet of magnetic dipole moment \(M\) and pole strength \(q_m\) is cut into two identical halves perpendicular to its length. One of these halves is then cut into two identical pieces along its length. What is the magnetic dipole moment and pole strength of each of the smallest resulting pieces?

(A) Moment \(= M/4\), Pole strength \(= q_m/2\)
(B) Moment \(= M/4\), Pole strength \(= q_m/4\)
(C) Moment \(= M/2\), Pole strength \(= q_m/2\)
(D) Moment \(= M/8\), Pole strength \(= q_m/2\)
Correct Answer: Option (A)
Worked Reasoning & Calculation:

Let the original magnet have length \(2L\), pole strength \(q_m\), and dipole moment \(M = q_m \times 2L\).

Step 1: First cut perpendicular to length (transverse cut):

  • Length of each piece: \(L' = L\) (halved)
  • Pole strength of each piece: \(q_m' = q_m\) (unchanged)
  • Magnetic moment: \(M_1 = q_m \times L = M/2\)

Step 2: Second cut along the length (longitudinal cut) of one half:

  • Length remains: \(L'' = L\)
  • Cross-sectional area is halved, so pole strength becomes: \(q_m'' = q_m/2\)
  • New magnetic moment: \(M'' = q_m'' \times L = \left(\frac{q_m}{2}\right) \times L = \frac{M_1}{2} = \frac{M}{4}\)

Therefore, each resulting smallest piece has magnetic moment \(M/4\) and pole strength \(q_m/2\).

NEET Rule of Thumb: Transverse cut halves length (pole strength invariant). Longitudinal cut halves pole strength (length invariant). Each cut halves the dipole moment!

(A) Parallel to \(\mathbf{m}_0\); \(U = -\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3}\)
(B) Antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}_0\); \(U = -\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3}\)

(D) Antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}_0\); \(U = +\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3}\)
Correct Answer: Option (B)
Worked Reasoning & Calculation:

1. The magnetic field produced by the fixed bar magnet of moment \(\mathbf{m}_0\) on its equatorial line at distance \(r\) is given by:

\[ \mathbf{B}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{\mathbf{m}_0}{r^3} \]

2. A free magnetic needle of dipole moment \(\mathbf{m}\) attains stable equilibrium when its dipole moment aligns parallel to the local magnetic field: \(\mathbf{m} \parallel \mathbf{B}_{\text{eq}}\).

Because \(\mathbf{B}_{\text{eq}}\) is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}_0\), the needle's magnetic moment \(\mathbf{m}\) in stable equilibrium must be directed antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}_0\)!

3. The potential energy in this stable orientation is:

\[ U_{\text{min}} = -m B_{\text{eq}} \cos(0^\circ) = -m \left(\frac{\mu_0 m_0}{4\pi r^3}\right) (1) = -\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3} \]

(C) \(\mathbf{m}_0\) के लंबवत; \(U = 0\)
(D) \(\mathbf{m}_0\) के प्रति-समांतर; \(U = +\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3}\)
सही उत्तर: विकल्प (B)
चरणबद्ध हल एवं व्याख्या:

1. स्थिर चुंबक (आघूर्ण \(\mathbf{m}_0\)) द्वारा निरक्षीय बिंदु पर उत्पन्न चुंबकीय क्षेत्र:

\[ \mathbf{B}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \frac{\mathbf{m}_0}{r^3} \]

इस क्षेत्र की दिशा मूल आघूर्ण \(\mathbf{m}_0\) के प्रति-समांतर (विपरीत) होती है।

2. चुंबकीय सुई का स्थायी संतुलन तब होता है जब उसका आघूर्ण \(\mathbf{m}\) स्थानीय चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}_{\text{eq}}\) के समांतर संरेखित हो जाए।

चूंकि स्थानीय क्षेत्र स्वयं \(\mathbf{m}_0\) के प्रति-समांतर है, अतः सुई का आघूर्ण \(\mathbf{m}\) भी \(\mathbf{m}_0\) के प्रति-समांतर (antiparallel) संरेखित होगा।

3. इस स्थायी संतुलन अवस्था में स्थितिज ऊर्जा:

\[ U_{\text{min}} = -m B_{\text{eq}} \cos(0^\circ) = -\frac{\mu_0 m_0 m}{4\pi r^3} \]
NEET परीक्षा संकेत: स्थायी संतुलन का अर्थ है स्थानीय चुंबकीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{B}\) के समांतर होना। निरक्ष पर \(\mathbf{B}\) स्वयं स्रोत चुंबक के विपरीत होता है!

8. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist / सामान्य गलतियां एवं दोहराव चेकलिस्ट

English
Frequent NEET Mistakes to Avoid:
  1. Confusing \(\mathbf{B}\) and \(\mathbf{H}\): Remember that \(\mathbf{H}\) is determined strictly by external conducting currents (\(H = nI\)), whereas \(\mathbf{B}\) is the total field inside the medium including atomic magnetisation (\(\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})\)). \(\mathbf{H}\) is measured in \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\), while \(\mathbf{B}\) is measured in Tesla (\(\mathrm{T}\)).
  2. Potential Energy Sign Errors: Potential energy is \(U = -mB\cos\theta\). At \(\theta = 0^\circ\), \(U = -mB\) (minimum, most stable). At \(\theta = 180^\circ\), \(U = +mB\) (maximum, most unstable). Do not forget the leading negative sign!
  3. Axial vs Equatorial Vector Direction: In magnitude, \(B_A = 2B_E\). However, in vector form, \(\mathbf{B}_A = -2\mathbf{B}_E\). Axial field is parallel to \(\mathbf{m}\), while equatorial field is antiparallel to \(\mathbf{m}\).
  4. Force vs Torque in Uniform Fields: In a uniform \(\mathbf{B}\)-field, net translational force is zero (\(\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\)), only torque exists. A dipole experiences a net translational force only in a non-uniform field.
  5. Diamagnetism Universality: Diamagnetism is present in all materials. Do not claim that an aluminium or iron atom possesses no diamagnetic effect; it is merely overshadowed by dominant paramagnetic or ferromagnetic ordering.
NEET 10-Minute Rapid Revision Checklist:
  • ☑ Magnetic dipole moment: \(m = NIA = q_m(2l)\), units \(\mathrm{A\,m^2}\) or \(\mathrm{J\,T^{-1}}\).
  • ☑ Cutting a magnet: transverse or longitudinal cut both yield \(m' = m/2\).
  • ☑ Torque: \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{B}\); Potential energy: \(U = -\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B}\).
  • ☑ Work from \(\theta_1\) to \(\theta_2\): \(W = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\).
  • ☑ Far axial field: \(B_A = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{2m}{r^3}\); Far equatorial field: \(B_E = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{m}{r^3}\).
  • ☑ Gauss's law for magnetism: \(\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0\) (No monopoles, closed field lines).
  • ☑ Relations: \(\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})\), \(\mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H}\), \(\mu_r = 1 + \chi\), \(\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r\).
  • ☑ Diamagnetic: \(-1 \le \chi < 0\), \(\mu_r < 1\), moves to weaker field, temperature-independent.
  • ☑ Paramagnetic: \(0 < \chi < \varepsilon\), \(\mu_r > 1\), moves to stronger field, \(\chi\) decreases with \(T\).
  • ☑ Ferromagnetic: \(\chi \gg 1\), \(\mu_r > 1000\), domains, transitions to paramagnet at high \(T\).
  • ☑ Superconductors: \(\chi = -1\), \(\mu_r = 0\), \(B = 0\) (Meissner effect).

NEET परीक्षा में होने वाली प्रमुख गलतियों से बचें:
  1. \(\mathbf{B}\) एवं \(\mathbf{H}\) में भ्रम: \(\mathbf{H}\) केवल बाह्य चालक धाराओं पर निर्भर करता है (\(H = nI\)), जबकि \(\mathbf{B}\) माध्यम के भीतर परमाण्वीय चुंबकन सहित कुल क्षेत्र है (\(\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})\))। \(\mathbf{H}\) का मात्रक \(\mathrm{A\,m^{-1}}\) तथा \(\mathbf{B}\) का मात्रक टेस्ला (\(\mathrm{T}\)) है।
  2. स्थितिज ऊर्जा में ऋण चिह्न की त्रुटि: स्थितिज ऊर्जा \(U = -mB\cos\theta\) होती है। \(\theta = 0^\circ\) पर \(U = -mB\) (न्यूनतम, स्थायी संतुलन) तथा \(\theta = 180^\circ\) पर \(U = +mB\) (अधिकतम, अस्थायी संतुलन) होता है। ऋण चिह्न कभी न भूलें!
  3. अक्षीय व निरक्षीय क्षेत्र की सदिश दिशा: परिमाण में \(B_A = 2B_E\) होता है, किंतु सदिश रूप में \(\mathbf{B}_A = -2\mathbf{B}_E\) होता है। अक्षीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{m}\) के समांतर तथा निरक्षीय क्षेत्र \(\mathbf{m}\) के विपरीत होता है।
  4. एकसमान क्षेत्र में बल बनाम टॉर्क: एकसमान चुंबकीय क्षेत्र में कुल स्थानांतरीय बल शून्य होता है (\(\mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\)), केवल बल आघूर्ण कार्य करता है। द्विध्रुव पर नेट बल केवल असमान क्षेत्र में लगता है।
  5. प्रतिचुंबकत्व की सार्वत्रिकता: प्रतिचुंबकत्व सभी पदार्थों में उपस्थित रहता है। यह कहना गलत है कि लोहे में प्रतिचुंबकीय प्रभाव नहीं होता; वास्तव में प्रबल लौहचुंबकत्व के कारण वह दब जाता है।

  • ☑ चुंबक को काटना: लंबवत अथवा समांतर काटने पर प्रत्येक नए भाग का आघूर्ण \(m' = m/2\) होता है।
  • ☑ बल आघूर्ण: \(\boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{B}\); स्थितिज ऊर्जा: \(U = -\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{B}\)।
  • ☑ \(\theta_1\) से \(\theta_2\) तक घुमाने में कार्य: \(W = mB(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\)।
  • ☑ अक्षीय क्षेत्र: \(B_A = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{2m}{r^3}\); निरक्षीय क्षेत्र: \(B_E = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\frac{m}{r^3}\)।
  • ☑ गाउस का नियम: \(\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0\) (एकल ध्रुव अनुपस्थित, रेखाएं बंद लूप बनाती हैं)।
  • ☑ सूत्र संबंध: \(\mathbf{B} = \mu_0(\mathbf{H} + \mathbf{M})\), \(\mathbf{M} = \chi \mathbf{H}\), \(\mu_r = 1 + \chi\), \(\mu = \mu_0 \mu_r\)।
  • ☑ प्रतिचुंबकीय: \(-1 \le \chi < 0\), \(\mu_r < 1\), दुर्बल क्षेत्र की ओर गति, ताप से स्वतंत्र।
  • ☑ अनुचुंबकीय: \(0 < \chi < \varepsilon\), \(\mu_r > 1\), तीव्र क्षेत्र की ओर गति, ताप बढ़ने पर \(\chi\) घटती है।
  • ☑ लौहचुंबकीय: \(\chi \gg 1\), \(\mu_r > 1000\), डोमेन संरचना, उच्च ताप पर अनुचुंबकीय में रूपांतरण।
  • ☑ अतिचालक: \(\chi = -1\), \(\mu_r = 0\), \(B = 0\) (माइस्नर प्रभाव)।
