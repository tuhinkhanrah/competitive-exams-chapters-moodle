2.1 Strategies to Enhance Crop Productivity

Global food demand can be addressed through three main agricultural approaches: (i) agrochemical-based farming, (ii) organic farming, and (iii) genetically engineered crop-based farming. While the Green Revolution tripled food output using improved varieties, better management, and agrochemicals, chemical fertilizers and pesticides remain costly for developing-world farmers and conventional breeding has reached a yield plateau.

2.2 Plant Tissue Culture and Somatic Hybridisation

  • Totipotency and Explants: Any excised plant part (an explant) grown aseptically in a nutrient medium containing a carbon source (sucrose), inorganic salts, vitamins, amino acids, and growth regulators (auxins, cytokinins) can regenerate a complete plant. This inherent cellular capacity is totipotency.
  • Micropropagation and Somaclones: Rapid in vitro multiplication yields thousands of plants that are genetically identical to the parent plant; these clones are termed somaclones (widely used for banana, tomato, and apple).
  • Meristem Culture: Even in virus-infected plants, the apical and axillary meristems remain virus-free because cell division outpaces viral movement. Culturing these meristems recovers healthy, virus-free plants (e.g., sugarcane, banana, potato).
  • Somatic Hybridisation: Enzymatic digestion removes plant cell walls to isolate naked protoplasts. Fusing protoplasts from two distinct varieties combines desirable traits into a somatic hybrid. For example, fusing potato and tomato protoplasts produced pomato, though it lacked the commercial trait balance needed for farming.

2.3 Genetically Modified (GM) Crops and Bt Cotton

Genetically Modified Organisms (GMOs) carry deliberately altered genes. In agriculture, GM crops offer five key advantages: (1) tolerance to abiotic stresses (cold, drought, salinity, heat), (2) reduced chemical pesticide dependency, (3) lower post-harvest losses, (4) improved mineral-use efficiency that prevents premature soil exhaustion, and (5) enhanced nutrition, exemplified by Golden Rice (enriched with Vitamin A precursor).

Mechanism of Bt Toxin: The soil bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis synthesizes crystalline insecticidal proteins (Cry proteins) during a specific growth phase. These crystals do not harm the bacterium because they exist as inactive protoxins. When an insect ingests the crop tissue, the alkaline pH of the insect midgut solubilizes the crystals and converts the protoxin into an active toxin. The active toxin binds to receptors on midgut epithelial cells, creating membrane pores that cause osmotic swelling, cell lysis, and insect death.

Gene Specificity: Bt toxins target specific insect groups—Lepidopterans (tobacco budworm, armyworm), Coleopterans (beetles), and Dipterans (flies, mosquitoes). In transgenic crops, cryIAc and cryIIAb control cotton bollworms, whereas cryIAb controls the corn borer.

2.4 Nematode-Resistant Tobacco via RNA Interference (RNAi)

The nematode Meloidogyne incognita parasitizes tobacco roots, severely lowering crop yield. Researchers countered this using RNA interference (RNAi), a natural cellular defense mechanism found in all eukaryotes that silences specific mRNA molecules by preventing their translation via a complementary double-stranded RNA (dsRNA). Naturally, such dsRNA arises from RNA virus infections or mobile genetic elements (transposons) that replicate through an RNA intermediate.

Using Agrobacterium vectors, nematode-specific DNA was inserted into tobacco plants so that host cells transcribed both sense and anti-sense RNA. Because these strands are complementary, they paired to form dsRNA, triggering RNAi. When the nematode feeds on the transgenic root, the specific parasite mRNA is silenced, preventing nematode survival.

3.1 Genetically Engineered Human Insulin

Historically, insulin for diabetic patients was extracted from the pancreas of slaughtered cattle and pigs, which often provoked immune allergies. Structurally, mature insulin consists of two short polypeptide chains—Chain A and Chain B—linked by disulphide bridges. In mammals, insulin is first synthesized as an inactive precursor called pro-insulin (a pro-hormone), which contains an extra stretch known as the C-peptide. During maturation, the C-peptide is cleaved off.

Because bacteria lack the eukaryotic processing machinery to cleave C-peptide, producing mature insulin in E. coli posed a challenge. In 1983, Eli Lilly solved this by synthesizing separate DNA sequences for human A and B chains and inserting them into distinct E. coli plasmids. The two chains were produced separately, extracted, and chemically joined via disulphide bonds to yield functional human insulin. (Note: Insulin cannot be taken orally because stomach and intestinal proteases would digest the polypeptide before absorption.)

3.2 Gene Therapy: ADA Deficiency

Gene therapy corrects a diagnosed hereditary defect by delivering a normal, functional gene into a patient's cells or embryo to compensate for the non-functional allele. The first clinical gene therapy was administered in 1990 to a 4-year-old girl suffering from Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency, an immune disorder caused by deletion of the ADA gene.

Conventional options—bone marrow transplantation and enzyme replacement therapy (periodic ADA injections)—are not permanently curative. In gene therapy, lymphocytes from the patient's blood are cultured in vitro, transduced with a functional ADA cDNA using a retroviral vector, and re-infused into the patient. Because mature lymphocytes have a finite lifespan (are not immortal), periodic infusions are required. A permanent cure is achievable only if the functional ADA gene isolated from marrow cells is introduced into cells at early embryonic stages.

3.3 Molecular Diagnosis: Early Detection

Conventional serum or urine tests only detect pathogens after symptoms appear, when pathogen concentration is already high. Molecular techniques detect infections or genetic defects much earlier:

  • PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction): Amplifies trace nucleic acids from low pathogen concentrations (e.g., HIV in suspected AIDS patients) or detects mutations in suspected cancer and genetic disorder cases. After \(n\) amplification cycles, a single target DNA copy theoretically yields \(2^{n}\) copies.
  • Radioactive Probe & Autoradiography: A single-stranded DNA or RNA tagged with a radioactive molecule (probe) hybridizes to complementary DNA in a cell clone. A clone carrying a mutated gene lacks complementarity, so the probe fails to bind and the clone does not appear on the photographic film.
  • ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay): Operates on the principle of antigen–antibody interaction, detecting either pathogen antigens (proteins, glycoproteins) or host antibodies synthesized against the pathogen.

৩.৩ আণবিক রোগ নির্ণয় (Molecular Diagnosis)

  • PCR (পলিমারেজ চেইন রিঅ্যাকশন): রোগের লক্ষণ প্রকাশের আগেই অত্যন্ত স্বল্প পরিমাণ ব্যাকটেরিয়া বা ভাইরাসের নিউক্লিক অ্যাসিডকে বিবর্ধিত (amplify) করে শনাক্ত করে (যেমন—সন্দেহভাজন AIDS রোগীর HIV শনাক্তকরণ এবং ক্যানসার রোগীর জিন মিউটেশন নির্ণয়)। \(n\) সংখ্যক চক্রের পর একটি DNA অণু থেকে তাত্ত্বিকভাবে \(2^{n}\) সংখ্যক অনুলিপি তৈরি হয়।
  • তেজস্ক্রিয় প্রোব ও অটোরেডিওগ্রাফি: তেজস্ক্রিয় অণু যুক্ত একতন্ত্রী DNA বা RNA (প্রোব)-কে কোষের ক্লোনে পরিপূরক DNA-এর সাথে সংকরায়িত (hybridise) করা হয়। মিউটেটেড জিন যুক্ত ক্লোনে পরিপূরকতা না থাকায় প্রোব যুক্ত হয় না, ফলে ফটোগ্রাফিক ফিল্মে সেই ক্লোনটি দেখা যায় না।
  • ELISA (এনজাইম-লিঙ্কড ইমিউনোসরবেন্ট অ্যাসেই): এটি অ্যান্টিজেন–অ্যান্টিবডি বিক্রিয়ার নীতির ওপর ভিত্তি করে কাজ করে। এর মাধ্যমে জীবাণুর অ্যান্টিজেন (প্রোটিন, গ্লাইকোপ্রোটিন) অথবা জীবাণুর বিরুদ্ধে তৈরি হওয়া অ্যান্টিবডি শনাক্ত করা হয়।

4.1 Transgenic Animals

Animals whose DNA has been manipulated to possess and express a foreign gene are called transgenic animals. Over 95% of all existing transgenic animals are mice (others include rats, rabbits, pigs, sheep, cows, and fish). Their five primary applications are:

  1. Normal Physiology & Development: Studying gene regulation and biological factors (e.g., insulin-like growth factor).
  2. Study of Disease: Serving as models for human diseases such as cancer, cystic fibrosis, rheumatoid arthritis, and Alzheimer's disease.
  3. Biological Products: Producing expensive therapeutic proteins—for example, human α-1-antitrypsin to treat emphysema, and research into phenylketonuria (PKU) and cystic fibrosis. In 1997, the first transgenic cow, Rosie, produced milk enriched with human α-lactalbumin (\(2.4\,\mathrm{g/L}\)), nutritionally superior for human infants.
  4. Vaccine Safety: Transgenic mice are used to test polio vaccine safety before human trials, potentially replacing monkeys.
  5. Chemical Safety / Toxicity Testing: Transgenic animals engineered to be hypersensitive to toxins yield faster toxicity results.

4.2 Ethical Issues, GEAC, and Biopiracy

Unregulated genetic manipulation risks unpredictable ecological consequences. To oversee GM research and evaluate the safety of introducing GMOs for public use, the Indian Government established the GEAC (Genetic Engineering Approval Committee).

Biopiracy is the unauthorized use of bio-resources and traditional knowledge by multinational companies or organizations without proper permission or compensatory payment to the source country and indigenous communities.

  • Basmati Rice Case: India possesses an estimated 200,000 rice varieties, including 27 documented varieties of aromatic Basmati. In 1997, an American company obtained a US patent on a "new" Basmati variety created by crossing Indian Basmati with semi-dwarf varieties, threatening Indian farmers' rights.
  • Herbal Patents & Legislation: Similar patent attempts targeted turmeric and neem. To safeguard indigenous bio-resources, the Indian Parliament passed the second amendment of the Indian Patents Bill, incorporating emergency provisions, patent term rules, and R&D protections.

৪.১ ট্রান্সজেনিক প্রাণী

যে সকল প্রাণীর DNA-তে পরিবর্তন ঘটিয়ে কোনো বহিরাগত (foreign) জিন প্রবেশ করানো ও প্রকাশ করা হয়, তাদের ট্রান্সজেনিক প্রাণী বলে। বিদ্যমান সমস্ত ট্রান্সজেনিক প্রাণীর ৯৫%-এরও বেশি হলো ইঁদুর (mice); এছাড়াও খরগোশ, শূকর, ভেড়া, গরু ও মাছ তৈরি করা হয়েছে। এদের পাঁচটি প্রধান ব্যবহার হলো:

  4. টিকার নিরাপত্তা পরীক্ষা: মানুষের ওপর প্রয়োগের আগে পোলিও টিকার নিরাপত্তা পরীক্ষায় বানরের পরিবর্তে ট্রান্সজেনিক ইঁদুর ব্যবহৃত হচ্ছে।
  5. রাসায়নিক নিরাপত্তা / বিষক্রিয়া পরীক্ষা: বিষাক্ত পদার্থের প্রতি অধিক সংবেদনশীল ট্রান্সজেনিক প্রাণীর সাহায্যে কম সময়ে বিষক্রিয়ার ফলাফল পাওয়া যায়।

Worked Example 1: Estimating Early Pathogen Detection via PCR

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): Suppose a blood sample from an asymptomatic patient contains only \(5\) copies of a viral DNA sequence, whereas a standard diagnostic gel requires at least \(5.0 \times 10^{6}\) copies for clear visualization. Calculate the minimum number of PCR cycles (\(n\)) needed to exceed this detection threshold, assuming \(100\%\) amplification efficiency per cycle.

Solution:

After \(n\) cycles, the total number of DNA copies \(N\) starting from \(N_0 = 5\) copies is:

\[N = N_0 \times 2^{n}\]

Setting \(5 \times 2^{n} \ge 5.0 \times 10^{6}\), we get \(2^{n} \ge 10^{6}\). Since \(2^{19} = 524,288\) and \(2^{20} = 1,048,576\), a minimum of \(20\) PCR cycles is required to cross the threshold.

Worked Example 2: Interpreting Radioactive Probe Autoradiography

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): A researcher screens four bacterial clones (P, Q, R, S) for a suspected proto-oncogene mutation using a single-stranded radioactive DNA probe complementary to the normal (wild-type) sequence. On the developed X-ray film, clones P, Q, and S produce dark spots, while clone R shows no spot. Identify which clone carries the mutated gene and explain why.

Solution:

Clone R carries the mutated gene. Because the radioactive probe is complementary to the normal gene sequence, it hybridizes tightly with clones P, Q, and S, exposing the photographic film. In clone R, the mutation alters the base sequence so the probe cannot hybridize, leaving no signal on the film.

8. Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist | সাধারণ ভুল ও পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

English
Common NEET UG Traps to Avoid:
  • Bt Toxin pH Trap: The insect midgut is alkaline, not acidic. Acidic pH does not activate Cry protoxin.
  • C-peptide Trap: Mature insulin has no C-peptide; C-peptide is cleaved off from pro-insulin.
  • ADA Cure Trap: Lymphocyte-based ADA gene therapy is not a one-time permanent cure; only early embryonic marrow gene transfer offers a permanent cure.
  • Transgenic Animal Share: Over 95% of transgenic animals are mice, not cows or pigs.

9. Concise Final Recap | সংক্ষিপ্ত চূড়ান্ত পুনরালোচনা

English

Biotechnology bridges molecular biology and industrial application. In agriculture, totipotency, meristem culture, Bt crops (cryIAc/cryIIAb for bollworm; cryIAb for corn borer), and RNAi protect yields sustainably. In medicine, recombinant insulin (A+B chains without C-peptide), retroviral ADA gene therapy, and early diagnostics (PCR, radioactive probes, ELISA) transform healthcare. Finally, transgenic mice (>95%) and cows like Rosie advance disease modeling and biopharmaceuticals, while GEAC and patent reforms guard against ecological risk and biopiracy.
