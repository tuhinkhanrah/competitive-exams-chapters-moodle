1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

English

Humans reproduce sexually and are viviparous (giving birth to live young). Reproductive competence begins after puberty and follows an integrated sequence: gametogenesis (formation of haploid gametes), insemination (transfer of sperms into the female genital tract), fertilisation (fusion of male and female gametes to form a diploid zygote), implantation (attachment of the blastocyst to the uterine endometrium), gestation (embryonic and foetal development), and parturition (childbirth), followed by lactation.

A key biological contrast governs human reproduction: spermatogenesis in males continues even into old age, whereas oogenesis in females ceases around 50 years of age (menopause).

  • Trace chromosome numbers and hormonal regulation across spermatogenesis and oogenesis.
  • Correlate pituitary gonadotropins (LH, FSH) and ovarian steroids (estrogen, progesterone) with ovarian and uterine events of the menstrual cycle.
  • Explain fertilisation, polyspermy block, chromosomal sex determination, cleavage, implantation, placental endocrinology, foetal milestones, parturition, and lactation.

2. Anatomical Architecture of Human Reproductive Systems

2.1 The Male Reproductive System

English

Located in the pelvic region, the male reproductive system comprises a pair of testes (primary sex organs), accessory ducts, accessory glands, and the external genitalia (penis).

  • Scrotum & Testes: The testes sit outside the abdominal cavity inside the scrotum, which maintains a temperature 2–2.5 °C lower than normal internal body temperature—an essential condition for spermatogenesis. Each adult testis is oval (about 4–5 cm long, 2–3 cm wide), enclosed by a dense covering, and divided into roughly 250 compartments called testicular lobules.
  • Seminiferous Tubules & Interstitial Spaces: Each lobule houses 1 to 3 highly coiled seminiferous tubules lined internally by two cell types: male germ cells (spermatogonia) that undergo meiosis to form sperms, and Sertoli cells that nourish the developing germ cells. Outside the tubules, interstitial spaces contain blood vessels, immunologically competent cells, and Leydig cells (interstitial cells), which synthesise and secrete testicular hormones called androgens.
  • Accessory Duct Pathway: Sperms travel sequentially from the seminiferous tubules → rete testis → vasa efferentia (leaving the testis) → epididymis (along the posterior surface of each testis) → vas deferens (ascends to the abdomen, loops over the urinary bladder) → receives a duct from the seminal vesicle to form the ejaculatory duct → opens into the urethra → exits at the urethral meatus.
  • Accessory Glands & External Genitalia: Paired seminal vesicles, a single prostate, and paired bulbourethral glands secrete seminal plasma, rich in fructose, calcium, and certain enzymes. Bulbourethral secretions also lubricate the penis. The penis contains special erectile tissue to facilitate insemination; its enlarged tip, the glans penis, is covered by a loose fold of skin called the foreskin.

2.2 The Female Reproductive System & Mammary Glands

English

The female reproductive system is structurally and functionally integrated to support ovulation, fertilisation, pregnancy, childbirth, and infant care.

  • Ovaries: Paired primary female sex organs (2–4 cm long) located on each side of the lower abdomen and anchored to the pelvic wall and uterus by ligaments. A thin epithelium encloses the ovarian stroma, which is divided into a peripheral cortex and an inner medulla. Ovaries produce ova and steroid ovarian hormones.
  • Oviducts (Fallopian Tubes): Each tube is 10–12 cm long and has three regions: the funnel-shaped infundibulum near the ovary (bearing finger-like fimbriae that collect the ovulated egg), the wider ampulla (site of fertilisation), and the narrow-lumened isthmus joining the uterus.
  • Uterus & Birth Canal: The single, inverted pear-shaped uterus (womb) opens into the vagina through a narrow cervix. The cervical canal together with the vagina forms the birth canal. The uterine wall has three layers: outer thin membranous perimetrium, middle thick smooth-muscle myometrium (contracts strongly during parturition), and inner glandular endometrium (undergoes cyclical menstrual changes).
  • External Genitalia: Includes the mons pubis (fatty cushion with pubic hair), labia majora (outer fleshy folds), labia minora (paired inner folds), hymen (membrane partially covering the vaginal opening—may tear during sports, tampon use, or first coitus, or persist after coitus, so it is not a reliable indicator of virginity), and clitoris (tiny finger-like structure at the upper junction of labia minora above the urethral opening).
  • Mammary Glands: Paired breasts characteristic of female mammals, containing glandular tissue and variable fat. Each breast has 15–20 mammary lobes with clusters of milk-secreting alveoli. Milk flows sequentially: alveoli lumen → mammary tubules → mammary duct → wider mammary ampulla → lactiferous duct → nipple.

3. Gametogenesis: Spermatogenesis, Oogenesis & Sperm Architecture

English

3.1 Spermatogenesis & Hormonal Control

Spermatogenesis begins at puberty due to a marked rise in hypothalamic GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone), which stimulates the anterior pituitary to secrete LH and FSH:

  • LH acts on Leydig cells to stimulate synthesis and secretion of androgens, which drive spermatogenesis.
  • FSH acts on Sertoli cells to stimulate secretion of factors assisting spermiogenesis.

Inside seminiferous tubules, diploid spermatogonia (\(2n = 46\)) multiply by mitosis. Some differentiate into diploid primary spermatocytes (\(2n = 46\)), which complete Meiosis I (reductional division) to yield two equal haploid secondary spermatocytes (\(n = 23\)). Each secondary spermatocyte undergoes Meiosis II to form four equal haploid spermatids (\(n = 23\)). Transformation of spermatids into spermatozoa is spermiogenesis; release of mature sperms from Sertoli cells into the tubule lumen is spermiation.

3.2 Structure of a Human Sperm & Semen Fertility Criteria

A mature sperm is enveloped by a plasma membrane and consists of four parts: head (elongated haploid nucleus capped anteriorly by the enzyme-rich acrosome for ovum penetration), neck, middle piece (packed with spirally arranged mitochondria generating ATP for tail movement), and tail (flagellar motility). A human male ejaculates 200–300 million sperms per coitus; for normal fertility, at least 60% must have normal shape and size, and at least 40% of them must show vigorous motility.

3.3 Oogenesis & Follicular Maturation

Unlike spermatogenesis, oogenesis begins during embryonic fetal development when millions of diploid oogonia (\(2n = 46\)) form in each fetal ovary (no new oogonia are added after birth). Oogonia enter prophase-I of Meiosis I and arrest temporarily as primary oocytes. Each primary oocyte surrounded by a granulosa layer forms a primary follicle. Extensive follicular degeneration (atresia) leaves only 60,000–80,000 primary follicles per ovary at puberty.

Primary follicles acquire more granulosa layers and a theca layer to become secondary follicles, then transform into tertiary follicles characterised by a fluid-filled cavity called the antrum and a two-layered theca (inner theca interna, outer theca externa). Within the tertiary follicle, the primary oocyte grows and completes Meiosis I—an unequal division producing a large haploid secondary oocyte (\(n = 23\), retaining nutrient-rich cytoplasm) and a tiny first polar body. The tertiary follicle matures into a Graafian follicle, and the secondary oocyte forms a surrounding zona pellucida. Rupture of the Graafian follicle releases the secondary oocyte during ovulation.

4. The Menstrual Cycle & Endocrine Coordination

English

The reproductive cycle in female primates (monkeys, apes, and humans) is the menstrual cycle, averaging 28/29 days. First menstruation at puberty is menarche, and permanent cessation around age 50 is menopause.

  1. Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5): Menstrual flow occurs for 3–5 days due to breakdown of the endometrial lining and its blood vessels when the ovulated egg is not fertilised. Absence of menstruation may indicate pregnancy, though stress or poor health can also delay it.
  2. Follicular (Proliferative) Phase: Primary follicles grow into a mature Graafian follicle while the uterine endometrium regenerates. Gradual increases in pituitary FSH and LH stimulate follicular growth and rising estrogen secretion from the growing follicles.
  3. Ovulatory Phase (~Day 14): Both LH and FSH peak mid-cycle. Rapid LH secretion—the LH surge—induces rupture of the Graafian follicle and release of the secondary oocyte (ovulation).
  4. Luteal (Secretory) Phase: The ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, which secretes large amounts of progesterone to maintain the thickened, secretory endometrium required for implantation. If fertilisation does not occur, the corpus luteum degenerates, progesterone drops, and the endometrium disintegrates, initiating the next cycle. During pregnancy, all menstrual events cease.

5. Fertilisation, Sex Determination, Cleavage & Implantation

English
  • Site & Synchrony: Following insemination, motile sperms swim through the cervix and uterus to the ampullary region of the fallopian tube. Fertilisation occurs only when the ovum and sperms reach the ampulla simultaneously.
  • Acrosomal Action & Polyspermy Block: Acrosomal enzymes help the sperm penetrate the corona radiata and zona pellucida. Contact of a sperm with the zona pellucida induces membrane changes that block entry of additional sperms, ensuring monospermy.
  • Meiosis II & Syngamy: Sperm entry triggers the secondary oocyte to complete Meiosis II—an unequal division forming a haploid ovum (ootid) and a second polar body. Fusion of the haploid sperm and ovum nuclei produces a diploid zygote (\(2n = 46\)).
  • Sex Determination: Females are homogametic (\(XX\)), so all ova carry \(X\). Males are heterogametic (\(XY\)): 50% of sperms carry \(X\) and 50% carry \(Y\). Thus, the genetic sex of the baby (\(XX\) female or \(XY\) male) is determined at fertilisation by the father's sperm.
  • Cleavage & Implantation: As the zygote moves through the isthmus toward the uterus, repeated mitotic divisions (cleavage) form 2, 4, 8, and 16 daughter cells called blastomeres. An embryo with 8–16 blastomeres is a morula, which transforms into a blastocyst. Blastomeres arrange into an outer trophoblast (attaches to endometrium) and an inner cell mass (differentiates into the embryo). Rapidly dividing uterine cells cover the blastocyst, embedding it in the endometrium (implantation).

6. Pregnancy, Placental Endocrinology, Parturition & Lactation

English
  • Placenta & Umbilical Cord: After implantation, finger-like chorionic villi on the trophoblast interdigitate with uterine tissue to form the placenta, connected to the embryo via the umbilical cord. It supplies oxygen and nutrients and removes \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) and metabolic wastes.
  • Endocrine Role: The placenta secretes hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin), hPL (human placental lactogen), estrogens, and progestogens. Later in pregnancy, the ovary secretes relaxin. Notably, hCG, hPL, and relaxin are produced in women only during pregnancy. Maternal blood levels of cortisol, prolactin, thyroxine, estrogens, and progestogens also rise several-fold.
  • Germ Layers & Stem Cells: Soon after implantation, the inner cell mass differentiates into outer ectoderm, inner endoderm, and middle mesoderm—which together give rise to all adult tissues and organs. The inner cell mass contains pluripotent stem cells.
  • Gestation Milestones (9 Months): Month 1: heart forms (audible via stethoscope); Month 2: limbs and digits develop; End of 12 weeks (1st trimester): most major organ systems, limbs, and external genitalia are well-developed; Month 5: first foetal movements and head hair; End of 24 weeks (2nd trimester): fine body hair, eyelids separate, eyelashes form; End of 9 months: full development ready for delivery.
  • Parturition: Delivery of the foetus is driven by a neuroendocrine foetal ejection reflex: signals from the fully developed foetus and placenta trigger mild uterine contractions → stimulates maternal posterior pituitary to release oxytocin → oxytocin causes stronger myometrial contractions → stimulates further oxytocin release (positive feedback) until expulsion of the baby and placenta.
  • Lactation & Colostrum: Mammary glands differentiate during pregnancy and secrete milk near the end of gestation. The yellowish milk produced during the initial few days, colostrum, is rich in antibodies vital for newborn immunity.

7. Worked Examples (NEET UG Analytical Applications)

English • Worked Example 1 (Assumed Scenario)

Problem: Suppose a hypothetical segment of a human seminiferous tubule has \(120\) primary spermatocytes entering meiosis simultaneously, and a parallel hypothetical ovarian sample has \(120\) primary oocytes completing Meiosis I and Meiosis II (assuming all are fertilised). Calculate the ratio of functional male gametes (sperms) produced to functional female gametes (ova) produced.

Solution:

  • Each primary spermatocyte (\(2n = 46\)) undergoes Meiosis I and Meiosis II to yield \(4\) haploid spermatids, all \(4\) of which differentiate into functional sperms. Therefore, \(120 \times 4 = 480\) sperms are formed.
  • Each primary oocyte (\(2n = 46\)) undergoes unequal Meiosis I and Meiosis II to yield \(1\) functional ovum and polar bodies. Therefore, \(120 \times 1 = 120\) ova are formed.
  • Required ratio: \(\frac{480}{120} = 4:1\).

সমস্যা: ধরা যাক একটি কাল্পনিক সেমিনিফেরাস নালিকার অংশে \(120\)টি প্রাইমারি স্পার্মাটোসাইট একসাথে মায়োসিস শুরু করেছে এবং একটি কাল্পনিক ডিম্বাশয় নমুনায় \(120\)টি প্রাইমারি উওসাইট প্রথম ও দ্বিতীয় মায়োসিস সম্পন্ন করেছে (ধরা যাক সবকটি নিষিক্ত হয়েছে)। উৎপন্ন কার্যকর পুং গ্যামেট (শুক্রাণু) ও কার্যকর স্ত্রী গ্যামেটের (ডিম্বাণু) সংখ্যার অনুপাত নির্ণয় করো।

  • প্রতিটি প্রাইমারি স্পার্মাটোসাইট (\(2n = 46\)) থেকে প্রথম ও দ্বিতীয় মায়োসিসের মাধ্যমে \(4\)টি হ্যাপ্লয়েড স্পার্মাটিড এবং তা থেকে \(4\)টি কার্যকর শুক্রাণু তৈরি হয়। সুতরাং, \(120 \times 4 = 480\)টি শুক্রাণু উৎপন্ন হয়।
  • প্রতিটি প্রাইমারি উওসাইট (\(2n = 46\)) থেকে অসম প্রথম ও দ্বিতীয় মায়োসিসের মাধ্যমে \(1\)টি কার্যকর ডিম্বাণু এবং পোলার বডি তৈরি হয়। সুতরাং, \(120 \times 1 = 120\)টি ডিম্বাণু উৎপন্ন হয়।
  • নির্ণেয় অনুপাত: \(\frac{480}{120} = 4:1\)।
English • Worked Example 2 (Assumed Scenario)

Problem: In a hypothetical semen analysis, an adult male ejaculates \(2.5 \times 10^8\) sperms. Exactly \(64\%\) of the total sperms exhibit normal shape and size, and \(45\%\) of those morphologically normal sperms show vigorous motility. Does this sample meet the standard fertility criteria?

Solution:

  • Total ejaculate = \(2.5 \times 10^8\) sperms (lies within the normal range of \(200\text{–}300\) million).
  • Morphologically normal sperms = \(64\%\) (exceeds the minimum \(60\%\) threshold).
  • Vigorously motile sperms among normal ones = \(45\%\) (exceeds the minimum \(40\%\) threshold).
  • Since all three criteria are satisfied (\(2.5 \times 10^8\) count, \(64\% \ge 60\%\) normal morphology, \(45\% \ge 40\%\) vigorous motility), the sample meets the normal fertility criteria.

সমাধান:

  • মোট শুক্রাণু সংখ্যা = \(2.5 \times 10^8\) (যা স্বাভাবিক ২০০–৩০০ মিলিয়ন সীমার মধ্যে)।
  • স্বাভাবিক আকৃতি ও আয়তনযুক্ত শুক্রাণু = \(64\%\) (ন্যূনতম \(60\%\) শর্তের চেয়ে বেশি)।
  • স্বাভাবিক শুক্রাণুদের মধ্যে সক্রিয় সচল শুক্রাণু = \(45\%\) (ন্যূনতম \(40\%\) শর্তের চেয়ে বেশি)।
  • যেহেতু তিনটি শর্তই পূরণ হয়েছে, তাই নমুনাটি স্বাভাবিক উর্বরতার মানদণ্ড পূরণ করে।

Practice Question 1 (Single-Correct MCQ)

Which sequence accurately represents the path of spermatozoa from their site of synthesis to the exterior in a human male?

  • (A) Seminiferous tubules → Vasa efferentia → Rete testis → Epididymis → Vas deferens → Urethra
  • (B) Seminiferous tubules → Rete testis → Vasa efferentia → Epididymis → Vas deferens → Ejaculatory duct → Urethra
  • (C) Seminiferous tubules → Rete testis → Epididymis → Vasa efferentia → Vas deferens → Urethra
  • (D) Seminiferous tubules → Epididymis → Rete testis → Vasa efferentia → Ejaculatory duct → Urethra
Answer: (B)
Explanation: Sperms produced in the seminiferous tubules enter the rete testis, pass through the vasa efferentia to exit the testis into the epididymis, ascend via the vas deferens, join the seminal vesicle duct to form the ejaculatory duct, and exit through the urethra.

Which of the following correctly describes the neuroendocrine mechanism of parturition?

  • (B) Signals from the foetus and placenta induce mild contractions, triggering maternal oxytocin release in a positive feedback loop.

9. Common NEET Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

Common Pitfalls to Avoid (English)
  • Meiosis I vs. Meiosis II in Oogenesis: Meiosis I finishes before ovulation inside the tertiary follicle; Meiosis II finishes only upon sperm entry in the ampulla.
  • Sertoli vs. Leydig Cells: Sertoli cells are inside seminiferous tubules (nutrition + spermiogenesis factors via FSH); Leydig cells are outside in interstitial spaces (androgens via LH).
  • Spermiogenesis vs. Spermiation: Spermiogenesis is morphological transformation of spermatids into sperms; spermiation is the release of sperms from Sertoli cells.
  • Hymen Misconception: Presence or absence of the hymen is not a reliable indicator of virginity or sexual experience.
সতর্কতা ও সাধারণ ভুল (বাংলা)
  • উওজেনেসিসে প্রথম বনাম দ্বিতীয় মায়োসিস: প্রথম মায়োসিস ডিম্বপাতের পূর্বেই টারশিয়ারি ফলিকলে শেষ হয়; দ্বিতীয় মায়োসিস কেবল অ্যাম্পুলায় শুক্রাণু প্রবেশের পর সম্পন্ন হয়।
  • সার্টোলি বনাম লেডিগ কোষ: সার্টোলি কোষ সেমিনিফেরাস নালিকার ভেতরে থাকে (পুষ্টি ও FSH-এর প্রভাবে স্পার্মিওজেনেসিস সহায়তা); লেডিগ কোষ নালিকার বাইরে থাকে (LH-এর প্রভাবে অ্যান্ড্রোজেন ক্ষরণ)।
  • স্পার্মিওজেনেসিস বনাম স্পার্মিয়েশন: স্পার্মাটিডের শুক্রাণুতে রূপান্তর হলো স্পার্মিওজেনেসিস; সার্টোলি কোষ থেকে শুক্রাণু মুক্ত হওয়া হলো স্পার্মিয়েশন।
  • হাইমেন সংক্রান্ত ভুল ধারণা: হাইমেনের উপস্থিতি বা অনুপস্থিতি কৌমার্য বা যৌন অভিজ্ঞতার নির্ভরযোগ্য সূচক নয়।

10. Concise Final Recap

English

Human reproduction integrates diploid-to-haploid gametogenesis (\(2n=46 \to n=23\)), cyclic endocrine regulation of the 28-day menstrual cycle (LH surge driving ovulation, corpus luteum progesterone maintaining the endometrium), ampullary fertilisation with monospermy block and paternal \(X/Y\) sex determination, blastocyst implantation, placental support (hCG, hPL, relaxin), 9-month gestation milestones, oxytocin-driven parturition, and antibody-rich colostrum during lactation.
