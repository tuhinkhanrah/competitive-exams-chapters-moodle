2.1 Structure of a Polynucleotide Chain

2.1 पॉलीन्यूक्लियोटाइड शृंखला की संरचना

A monomeric nucleotide is composed of three distinct chemical parts:

  1. Nitrogenous Base: Heterocyclic aromatic rings classified into:
    • Purines (Bicyclic): Adenine (A) and Guanine (G).
    • Pyrimidines (Monocyclic): Cytosine (C), Uracil (U in RNA), and Thymine (5-methyl uracil in DNA).
  2. Pentose Sugar: Deoxyribose (\(\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O_4}\)) in DNA, lacking an oxygen at the \(2'\) position; Ribose (\(\mathrm{C_5H_{10}O_5}\)) in RNA, possessing a reactive \(-OH\) group at the \(2'\) position.
  3. Phosphate Group: Phosphoric acid moiety attached to the \(5'\)-hydroxyl group.
प्रत्येक न्यूक्लियोटाइड तीन घटकों से निर्मित होता है: एक नाइट्रोजनी क्षार (प्यूरीन: एडेनिन, ग्वानिन; पिरिमिडीन: साइटोसिन, थायमिन/यूरेसिल), एक पेंटोस शर्करा (डीएनए में डीऑक्सीराइबोज तथा आरएनए में राइबोज), एवं एक फॉस्फेट समूह।
Chemical Linkages of the Backbone:
  • N-glycosidic Linkage: Formed between the nitrogenous base and the \(1'\)-carbon of the pentose sugar via removal of a water molecule, yielding a nucleoside (e.g., Deoxyadenosine, Deoxyguanosine, Deoxycytidine, Deoxythymidine).
  • Phosphoester Bond: Formed when a phosphate group is esterified to the \(5'\)-OH of the nucleoside sugar to produce a nucleotide.
  • \(3'\text{–}5'\) Phosphodiester Linkage: Joins two successive nucleotides between the \(3'\)-OH of one sugar and the \(5'\)-phosphate of the next, establishing a directional backbone with a free \(5'\)-phosphate end and a free \(3'\)-hydroxyl end.

2.2 Salient Features of the Watson-Crick Double Helix Model

2.2 वाटसन एवं क्रिक के द्विकुंडली मॉडल के प्रमुख लक्षण

Proposed in 1953 by James Watson and Francis Crick, based on Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins' X-ray diffraction data and Erwin Chargaff's rules:

  • Two Polynucleotide Chains: Formed with a sugar-phosphate backbone on the outside and nitrogenous bases projecting inwards.
  • Antiparallel Polarity: One chain runs \(5'\rightarrow 3'\), and the other runs in the inverted direction \(3'\rightarrow 5'\).
  • Complementary Base Pairing: Adenine always pairs with Thymine via two hydrogen bonds (\(\mathrm{A = T}\)); Guanine pairs with Cytosine via three hydrogen bonds (\(\mathrm{G \equiv C}\)). Consequently, a purine always faces a pyrimidine, maintaining a strictly uniform diameter of \(2.0\text{ nm}\) throughout the helix.
  • Helical Metrics: Right-handed coiling; pitch is \(3.4\text{ nm}\) with \(\approx 10\text{ bp}\) per turn, resulting in a distance of \(0.34\text{ nm}\) (\(3.4\times 10^{-10}\text{ m}\)) between adjacent base pairs.
  • Dual Stability: Maintained by (1) complementary hydrogen bonding, and (2) stacking of hydrophobic aromatic planar base pairs over one another.
Chargaff's Equivalence Rule (Applicable ONLY to Double-Stranded DNA):
\[ \frac{[\mathrm{A}]}{[\mathrm{T}]} = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{[\mathrm{G}]}{[\mathrm{C}]} = 1 \implies [\mathrm{A}] + [\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{T}] + [\mathrm{C}] \quad (\text{Purines} = \text{Pyrimidines}) \] NEET Warning: The base ratio \(\frac{[\mathrm{A}] + [\mathrm{T}]}{[\mathrm{G}] + [\mathrm{C}]}\) is not necessarily 1; it is species-specific (variable). Furthermore, Chargaff's rule fails for single-stranded DNA (\(\phi \times 174\)) and all RNA molecules!

2.3 Francis Crick's Central Dogma

2.3 फ्रांसिस क्रिक का सेंट्रल डोग्मा (मूल सिद्धांत)

States that unidirectional genetic information flows from nucleic acids to functional proteins:

\[ \text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Replication}} \text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Transcription}} \text{mRNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Translation}} \text{Protein} \]

Exception: In retroviruses (e.g., Tobacco Mosaic Virus, HIV), reverse flow occurs via reverse transcription (Teminism): \(\text{RNA} \rightarrow \text{DNA}\).

2.4 Packaging of DNA Helix in Cells

2.4 डीएनए कुंडली का संवेष्टन

The total length of double-helical DNA in a human diploid somatic cell (\(6.6 \times 10^9\text{ bp}\)) is calculated as:

\[ \text{Length} = 6.6 \times 10^9\text{ bp} \times 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m/bp} \approx 2.24\text{ metres} \]

Packaging a \(2.2\text{ m}\) macromolecule into a microscopic nucleus of dimension \(\approx 10^{-6}\text{ m}\) represents an extraordinary condensing feat achieved differently across domains:

Prokaryotic Packaging (e.g., E. coli):

Lacking a defined nuclear membrane, negatively charged DNA is organized with positively charged non-histone polyamines into large looped domains anchored by proteins within a clear cytoplasmic sector termed the nucleoid.

Eukaryotic Packaging — The Nucleosome:

Histones are basic proteins enriched in positively charged basic amino acid residues: Lysine and Arginine.

  • Eight histone molecules (\(2\) each of \(\mathrm{H2A, H2B, H3, H4}\)) assemble into a cylindrical histone octamer core.
  • Negatively charged DNA wraps roughly \(1.75\) turns (\(\approx 200\text{ bp}\) of DNA helix) around the octamer to form the fundamental repeating unit: the nucleosome.
  • \(\mathrm{H1}\) Histone: Binds at the entry/exit site of DNA, sealing the nucleosome structure.
  • Under electron microscopy, chromatin resembles a 'beads-on-a-string' architecture.
  • Higher-order folding beyond nucleosomes requires non-histone chromosomal (NHC) proteins to yield condensed metaphase chromosomes.

3. The Search for Genetic Material

3. आनुवंशिक पदार्थ की खोज

3.1 Griffith's Transformation Experiment (1928)

3.1 ग्रिफ़िथ का रूपांतरण प्रयोग (1928)

Frederick Griffith experimented with Streptococcus pneumoniae (Pneumococcus):

  • S Strain (Smooth): Produces a smooth mucous (polysaccharide) capsule; virulent (causes lethal pneumonia in mice).
  • R Strain (Rough): Non-capsulated; produces rough colonies; non-virulent (mice survive).
Griffith's Experimental Series:
1. \(\text{Live S strain} \rightarrow \text{Inject into mice} \rightarrow \text{Mice Die}\)
2. \(\text{Live R strain} \rightarrow \text{Inject into mice} \rightarrow \text{Mice Live}\)
3. \(\text{Heat-killed S strain} \rightarrow \text{Inject into mice} \rightarrow \text{Mice Live}\)
4. \(\text{Heat-killed S strain} + \text{Live R strain} \rightarrow \text{Inject into mice} \rightarrow \text{Mice Die!}\)
Post-mortem recovery: Living, virulent S-strain bacteria were harvested from the dead mice. Griffith deduced that a non-living "transforming principle" was transferred from the heat-killed S cells into live R cells, conferring upon them the enzymatic capacity to synthesize a polysaccharide capsule and become virulent. However, the biochemical nature remained unidentified.

3.2 Biochemical Characterization (Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty, 1933–44)

3.2 रूपांतरण सिद्धांत का जैव-रासायनिक लक्षण-वर्णन

Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty purified biomolecules from heat-killed S-cells to determine what converted R to S:

  • Heat-killed extract treated with Proteases \(\rightarrow\) Transformation still occurred (Protein is NOT the transforming principle).
  • Heat-killed extract treated with RNase \(\rightarrow\) Transformation still occurred (RNA is NOT the transforming principle).
  • Heat-killed extract treated with DNase \(\rightarrow\) Transformation was completely inhibited!

Conclusion: DNA is the substance responsible for hereditary transformation. However, many biologists remained unconvinced until 1952.

3.3 Hershey-Chase Unequivocal Proof (1952)

3.3 हर्षे एवं चेस का असंदिग्ध प्रमाण (1952)

Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase provided unequivocal proof that DNA is the genetic material using Bacteriophage \(\mathrm{T2}\) infecting E. coli:

Batch Radioactive Isotope Added Biomolecule Specifically Labeled Reason for Specificity
Batch 1 Radioactive Phosphorus (\(^{32}\mathrm{P}\)) Viral DNA DNA contains phosphorus, but protein capsules lack phosphorus.
Batch 2 Radioactive Sulfur (\(^{35}\mathrm{S}\)) Viral Protein Coat Proteins contain sulfur (in methionine/cysteine), but DNA contains zero sulfur.

3.4 Criteria for Genetic Material (DNA versus RNA)

3.4 आनुवंशिक पदार्थ की कसौटियां (डीएनए बनाम आरएनए)

To act as a genetic material, a molecule must fulfill four fundamental evolutionary criteria:

Criterion DNA Property RNA Property
1. Self-Replication Capable via exact complementary base pairing. Capable via complementary base pairing.
2. Chemical & Structural Stability Highly stable: Double-stranded; lacks \(2'\)-OH; possesses thymine instead of uracil; resisted heat in Griffith's experiment. Labile & degradable: \(2'\)-OH group present at every nucleotide makes it reactive; acts as a catalytic enzyme (ribozyme).
3. Mutational Scope for Evolution Mutates slowly; possesses robust enzymatic repair mechanisms. Mutates at a faster rate; RNA viruses (e.g., retroviruses) evolve rapidly.
4. Mendelian Expression Indirect expression: dependent on RNA intermediates to synthesize proteins. Direct expression: codes directly for polypeptide synthesis.

Conclusion: DNA is structurally superior for stable storage of genetic information; RNA is preferred for transmission of information.

4. DNA Replication

4. डीएनए प्रतिकृतियन

4.1 Semiconservative Replication Scheme

4.1 अर्ध-संरक्षी प्रतिकृतियन योजना

Watson and Crick famously deduced: "It has not escaped our notice that the specific pairing we have postulated immediately suggests a possible copying mechanism for the genetic material." The two strands unwind, each acting as a template for synthesizing a brand-new complementary strand. Every daughter duplex contains one parental strand and one newly synthesized strand.

4.2 Experimental Proof — Meselson and Stahl (1958)

4.2 मेसेल्सन एवं स्टाल का प्रायोगिक प्रमाण

  • Cultured E. coli in \(^{15}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) is a heavy stable isotope of nitrogen, not radioactive) for many generations until all cellular DNA contained \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\).
  • Transferred cells into normal \(^{14}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) medium; sampled DNA at definite time intervals (E. coli generation time \(= 20\text{ minutes}\)).
  • Separated DNA using cesium chloride (\(\mathrm{CsCl}\)) density gradient centrifugation:
    • Generation 0 (0 min): \(100\%\) Heavy DNA (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)), sedimenting at the bottom.
    • Generation 1 (20 min): \(100\%\) Hybrid intermediate density DNA (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
    • Generation 2 (40 min): \(50\%\) Hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) and \(50\%\) Light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
    • Generation 3 (60 min): \(25\%\) Hybrid and \(75\%\) Light.
    • Generation 4 (80 min): \(12.5\%\) Hybrid (\(2/16\)) and \(87.5\%\) Light (\(14/16\)).
Taylor's Experiment on Higher Plants (1958):
J. Herbert Taylor and colleagues proved semiconservative replication in chromosomes of Vicia faba (broad bean / faba bean) using radioactive thymidine to track newly synthesized chromatids during root-tip cell division.

4.3 The Replication Machinery and Enzymes

4.3 प्रतिकृतियन तंत्र एवं एंजाइम

  • DNA-Dependent DNA Polymerase: Highly efficient enzyme that synthesizes new DNA along a template. In E. coli (\(4.6 \times 10^6\text{ bp}\)), replication finishes within \(18\text{ minutes}\), requiring an average polymerisation rate of \(\approx 2000\text{ bp/second}\) with near-zero error!
  • Dual Role of Deoxyribonucleoside Triphosphates (\(\mathrm{dNTPs}\)):
    1. Act as monomeric substrates (\(\mathrm{dATP, dGTP, dCTP, dTTP}\)).
    2. Supply energy for polymerization via high-energy pyrophosphate bonds (\(\mathrm{P \sim P}\)), analogous to \(\mathrm{ATP}\).
  • Directional Constraint: DNA polymerases strictly catalyse synthesis in the \(5'\rightarrow 3'\) direction only. They cannot initiate synthesis de novo and require an RNA primer and a designated Origin of Replication (ori).
  • Replication Fork Dynamics: Because unwinding the whole chromosome requires prohibitive energy, replication occurs within a small localized Y-shaped unzipped window called the replication fork:
    • Continuous / Leading Strand: Synthesized continuously on the template with \(3'\rightarrow 5'\) polarity.
    • Discontinuous / Lagging Strand: Synthesized discontinuously as short segments (Okazaki fragments) on the template with \(5'\rightarrow 3'\) polarity. The fragments are covalently joined by DNA ligase.

5. Transcription

5. अनुलेखन

Transcription is the enzymatic process of copying genetic information from one strand of DNA into an RNA polymer. Governed by the rule of complementarity, Uracil pairs with Adenine in place of Thymine.

Why is Only One DNA Strand Transcribed?
  1. If both strands were copied, they would code for two distinct RNA molecules with differing nucleotide sequences, yielding two different proteins from one DNA segment and confounding genetic regulation.
  2. The two simultaneously produced complementary RNA strands would hybridize into a double-stranded RNA (\(\mathrm{dsRNA}\)), terminating translation and rendering transcription completely futile.

5.1 The Transcription Unit and Strand Conventions

5.1 अनुलेखन एकक एवं रज्जुक परंपरा

A transcription unit in DNA contains three structural landmarks:

  • Promoter: Binding site for RNA polymerase located at the \(5'\)-end (upstream) relative to the coding strand.
  • Structural Gene: The flanked region coding for functional RNA or polypeptide.
  • Terminator: Defines the termination boundary located at the \(3'\)-end (downstream) relative to the coding strand.
Strand Name Polarity Role in Transcription Sequence Comparison with Transcribed RNA
Template Strand (Non-coding / Antisense) \(3'\rightarrow 5'\) Serves as physical template for RNA polymerase. Complementary and antiparallel to RNA.
Coding Strand (Non-template / Sense) \(5'\rightarrow 3'\) Does NOT code; displaced during elongation. All reference landmarks (promoter upstream, terminator downstream) are named after this strand! Identical to transcribed RNA, except Thymine (\(\mathrm{T}\)) is replaced by Uracil (\(\mathrm{U}\)).

5.2 Transcription in Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes

5.2 प्रोकैरियोट्स बनाम यूकैरियोट्स में अनुलेखन

Bacterial Transcription:
  • A single DNA-dependent RNA polymerase transcribes all three major functional RNAs: \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) (informational template), \(\mathrm{tRNA}\) (adapter), and \(\mathrm{rRNA}\) (structural and catalytic).
  • The core RNA polymerase catalyzes only elongation. It transiently associates with sigma factor (\(\sigma\)) for initiation and with rho factor (\(\rho\)) for termination.
  • Because prokaryotes lack compartmentalized nuclear membranes and \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) requires no post-transcriptional processing, coupled transcription-translation commonly occurs!
Eukaryotic Complexities:
  1. Division of Labor Among Nuclear RNA Polymerases:
    • RNA Polymerase I: Transcribes \(\mathrm{rRNAs}\) (\(28\mathrm{S}, 18\mathrm{S}, 5.8\mathrm{S}\)).
    • RNA Polymerase II: Transcribes precursor of \(\mathrm{mRNA}\), designated as heterogeneous nuclear RNA (hnRNA).
    • RNA Polymerase III: Transcribes \(\mathrm{tRNA}\), \(5\mathrm{S\ rRNA}\), and \(\mathrm{snRNAs}\) (small nuclear RNAs).
  2. Split-Gene Architecture & Processing of hnRNA: Primary eukaryotic transcripts possess coding expressed sequences (exons) interrupted by non-coding intervening sequences (introns). Maturation requires three post-transcriptional modifications:
    • Splicing: Introns are excised and exons are ligated in a defined order by spliceosomes.
    • Capping: Addition of an unusual nucleotide — methyl guanosine triphosphate (\(\mathrm{m^7G_{ppp}}\)) — to the \(5'\)-end of hnRNA.
    • Tailing (Polyadenylation): Addition of \(200\text{–}300\) adenylate residues to the \(3'\)-end in a template-independent manner.

6. Genetic Code, Mutations, and Translation

6. आनुवंशिक कूट, उत्परिवर्तन एवं रूपांतरण (अनुवादन)

6.1 Deciphering the Triplet Code

6.1 त्रिक कूट का कूटवाचन

Physicist George Gamow calculated that since only \(4\) nitrogenous bases exist to code for \(20\) primary amino acids, a single base (\(4^1=4\)) or doublet code (\(4^2=16\)) is insufficient. A triplet code generates \(4^3 = 64\) possible codons, providing ample capacity.

  • Har Gobind Khorana: Chemically synthesized defined homopolymers and copolymers (e.g., \(\mathrm{UGUGUG...}\)).
  • Marshall Nirenberg: Developed cell-free protein-synthesizing systems, deciphering that \(\mathrm{UUU}\) codes for Phenylalanine.
  • Severo Ochoa: Discovered polynucleotide phosphorylase, enabling template-independent enzymatic synthesis of RNA with defined base sequences.

6.2 Six Salient Features of the Genetic Code

6.2 आनुवंशिक कूट की छह प्रमुख विशेषताएं

  1. Triplet Nature: \(61\) codons code for amino acids; \(3\) codons — \(\mathrm{UAA, UAG, UGA}\) — do not code for amino acids and function as stop/nonsense codons.
  2. Degeneracy: Most amino acids are specified by more than one codon (e.g., Leucine, Serine, and Arginine each have \(6\) codons).
  3. Contiguous Reading: Codons are read on \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) continuously from \(5'\rightarrow 3'\) without commas, pauses, or punctuation.
  4. Near Universality: From bacteria to humans, \(\mathrm{UUU}\) specifies Phenylalanine (rare exceptions exist in human mitochondrial codons and ciliate protozoans).
  5. Dual Function of \(\mathrm{AUG}\): Codes for Methionine (\(\mathrm{Met}\)) and serves universally as the Initiator (Start) Codon.
  6. Unambiguous & Specific: One particular codon codes for one and only one amino acid.

6.3 tRNA — The Adapter Molecule

6.3 टी-आरएनए — अनुकूलक अणु

Hypothesized by Francis Crick because amino acids lack intrinsic structural capability to directly read nucleotide sequences on \(\mathrm{mRNA}\):

  • Anticodon Loop: Contains three complementary bases that hydrogen-bond antiparallelly to the \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) codon.
  • Amino Acid Acceptor Stem: The unlooped \(3'\)-terminal with sequence \(\mathrm{-CCA\text{–}3'}\) that covalently binds an activated amino acid.
  • Conformation: Secondary structure resembles a cloverleaf; true tertiary \(3\mathrm{D}\) crystal structure resembles an inverted L.
  • Specificity: Distinct tRNAs exist for each amino acid, plus a unique initiator tRNA for start codon \(\mathrm{AUG}\). No tRNAs exist for stop codons (\(\mathrm{UAA, UAG, UGA}\)).

6.4 Translation Machinery & Stepwise Kinetics

6.4 रूपांतरण तंत्र एवं चरणबद्ध कार्यप्रणाली

  1. Aminoacylation (Charging) of tRNA: Amino acids are activated in the presence of \(\mathrm{ATP}\) and covalently coupled to their cognate tRNA via aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase: \[ \text{Amino Acid} + \text{ATP} \rightarrow \text{Aminoacyl-AMP} + \mathrm{PP_i} \] \[ \text{Aminoacyl-AMP} + \text{tRNA} \rightarrow \text{Aminoacyl-tRNA} + \text{AMP} \]
  2. Ribosome Factory: Consists of structural rRNAs and \(\approx 80\) distinct proteins. In prokaryotes (\(70\mathrm{S}\)): \(50\mathrm{S}\) large subunit and \(30\mathrm{S}\) small subunit. Crucial Ribozyme: \(23\mathrm{S}\) rRNA in bacteria acts as a catalytic peptidyl transferase enzyme forming peptide bonds!
  3. Initiation: Small ribosomal subunit binds \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) at \(5'\)-leader sequence; initiator tRNA recognizes \(\mathrm{AUG}\); large subunit attaches.
  4. Elongation: Ribosome moves from codon to codon along \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) in the \(5'\rightarrow 3'\) direction, forming sequential peptide bonds between adjacent charged tRNAs.
  5. Termination: When a stop codon (\(\mathrm{UAA, UAG, UGA}\)) enters the A-site, a Release Factor binds, hydrolyzing the finished polypeptide chain.
  6. Untranslated Regions (UTRs): Sequences of \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) located before the start codon (at \(5'\)-end) and after the stop codon (at \(3'\)-end) that are not translated into protein, but are essential for efficient translation.

7. Regulation of Gene Expression • The Lac Operon

7. जीन अभिव्यक्ति का नियमन • लैक प्रचालेक (लैक ऑपेरॉन)

In eukaryotes, gene expression can be regulated at four levels:

  • Transcriptional level (formation of primary transcript).
  • Processing level (regulation of splicing/capping/tailing).
  • Transport level (transport of mature \(\mathrm{mRNA}\) from nucleus to cytoplasm).
  • Translational level (protein synthesis initiation and efficiency).

In prokaryotes, predominant control is exerted at the initiation of transcription.

7.1 लैक ऑपेरॉन मॉडल (जैकोब एवं मोनोड)

Elucidated by François Jacob (geneticist) and Jacques Monod (biochemist) in E. coli as the prototype inducible operon:

\(p\) Element Promoter Binding site for DNA-dependent RNA polymerase.
\(o\) Element Operator Binding site for active repressor protein; controls transcription initiation.
\(z\) Gene Structural gene Codes for \(\beta\)-galactosidase; hydrolyzes lactose into Glucose + Galactose.
\(y\) Gene Structural gene Codes for \(\beta\)-galactoside permease; increases membrane permeability to lactose.
\(a\) Gene Structural gene Codes for \(\beta\)-galactoside transacetylase; transfers acetyl groups.
Negative inducible control of the lac operon: repressor inactivation by inducer allows transcription of structural genes z, y, and a.
NEET High-Yield Nuances of the Lac Operon:
  • Basal Level Requirement: A very low basal level of lac operon expression must ALWAYS be present in E. coli; otherwise, permease would be completely absent and exogenous lactose could never enter the cell!
  • Physiological Inducer: Allolactose (an isomer generated by \(\beta\)-galactosidase) is the genuine physiological inducer.
  • Substrate Specificity: Glucose or galactose cannot act as inducers. When glucose is present, the operon remains inactive even if lactose is abundant (catabolite repression / positive regulation).

8. Human Genome Project & DNA Fingerprinting

8. मानव जीनोम परियोजना एवं डीएनए अंगुलिछापी

8.1 Human Genome Project (HGP, 1990–2003)

8.1 मानव जीनोम परियोजना

HGP was a \(13\)-year international megaproject coordinated by the U.S. Department of Energy and the National Institutes of Health (\(\mathrm{NIH}\)), joined by Wellcome Trust (U.K.) and partners in Japan, France, Germany, and China.

Two Methodological Approaches:
  1. Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs): Targeted sequencing focused exclusively on identifying all genes that are transcribed into functional \(\mathrm{RNA}\).
  2. Sequence Annotation: The blind, unbiased sequencing of the entire genome (both coding exons and non-coding regions), followed by assigning functional boundaries to specific chromosomal regions using computational algorithms.

Total genomic DNA was sheared randomly, cloned into host organisms (bacteria and yeast) using specialized vectors — BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosomes) and YAC (Yeast Artificial Chromosomes) — and sequenced on automated machines using Frederick Sanger's dideoxy chain-termination method.

8.2 Salient Features of the Human Genome

8.2 मानव जीनोम के प्रमुख लक्षण (एनसीईआरटी तथ्य)

  • The human genome contains \(3164.7\text{ million}\) base pairs (\(\approx 3.16\times 10^9\text{ bp}\)).
  • Total number of genes is estimated at \(\approx 30,000\) (vastly lower than earlier estimates of \(80,000\text{–}140,000\)).
  • The average gene size is \(3,000\text{ bases}\); the largest known human gene is Dystrophin spanning \(2.4\text{ million bases}\).
  • Almost all (\(99.9\%\)) nucleotide bases are exactly identical among all human individuals; individuality stems from merely \(0.1\%\) variation!
  • Functions are unknown for over \(50\%\) of discovered genes.
  • Less than \(2\%\) of the genome codes for functional proteins.
  • Repetitive sequences make up a very large portion; they have no direct coding function but illuminate chromosome structure, dynamics, and evolution.
  • Chromosome 1 has the most genes (\(2,968\)); the Y chromosome has the fewest (\(231\)).
  • Scientists identified about \(1.4\text{ million locations}\) where single-base differences occur: SNPs (Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms).

8.3 DNA Fingerprinting

8.3 डीएनए अंगुलिछापी (डीएनए फिंगरप्रिंटिंग)

Pioneered by Alec Jeffreys. It relies on identifying variations in non-coding repetitive DNA, which is separated from bulk genomic DNA as distinct satellite peaks during density gradient centrifugation:

Key Fundamentals of DNA Profiling:
  • Repetitive DNA vs. Satellite DNA: Bulk genomic DNA forms the major density peak; smaller satellite peaks constitute satellite DNA, categorized into micro-satellites and mini-satellites based on base composition (\(\mathrm{A:T}\) or \(\mathrm{G:C}\) richness), segment length, and repetition number.
  • Variable Number of Tandem Repeats (VNTR): Belongs to mini-satellites. A short DNA sequence tandemly repeated many times; the copy number varies widely among chromosomes and individuals, yielding fragment sizes from \(0.1\) to \(20\text{ kb}\).
  • Inheritable DNA Polymorphism: Mutations accumulating in non-coding sequences without affecting reproductive fitness are inherited stably from parents to offspring, providing the scientific basis for forensic identification and paternity disputes.
Technical Workflow of Southern Blot Hybridization:
  1. Isolation and extraction of cellular DNA.
  2. Digestion of DNA into fragments by restriction endonucleases.
  3. Separation of DNA fragments based on molecular size via agarose gel electrophoresis.
  4. Denaturation and transfer (blotting) of separated DNA fragments onto synthetic membranes (nitrocellulose or nylon).
  5. Hybridization using radiolabeled VNTR probes.
  6. Detection of hybridized band patterns via autoradiography on X-ray film.

9. Standard Worked Quantitative & Analytical Examples

9. मानक हल किए गए संख्यात्मक एवं विश्लेषणात्मक उदाहरण

Worked Example 1 (Chargaff's Rule Calculation):
Problem: A double-stranded DNA sample is chemically analyzed and found to contain \(28\%\) Cytosine. Calculate the percentage of Adenine, Guanine, and Thymine in this DNA.
Detailed Solution:
  1. According to Chargaff's rules for double-stranded DNA: \[ [\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{C}] \quad \text{and} \quad [\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}] \]
  2. Given \([\mathrm{C}] = 28\%\), it directly follows that \([\mathrm{G}] = 28\%\).
  3. Total pyrimidine and purine content equals \(100\%\): \[ [\mathrm{G}] + [\mathrm{C}] = 28\% + 28\% = 56\% \]
  4. The remaining percentage represents Adenine plus Thymine: \[ [\mathrm{A}] + [\mathrm{T}] = 100\% - 56\% = 44\% \]
  5. Since \([\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}]\): \[ [\mathrm{A}] = \frac{44\%}{2} = 22\% \quad \text{and} \quad [\mathrm{T}] = 22\% \]
  6. Final Answer: Cytosine \(= 28\%\), Guanine \(= 28\%\), Adenine \(= 22\%\), Thymine \(= 22\%\).
Worked Example 2 (DNA Length and Nucleosome Number):
Problem: A mammalian diploid nucleus contains \(6.6 \times 10^9\text{ bp}\). Calculate: (a) the total physical length of the double helix in metres, and (b) the theoretical number of nucleosome core particles present in this cell.
Detailed Solution:
  1. Part (a): Total Physical Length: The distance between consecutive base pairs in standard B-DNA is \(0.34\text{ nm} = 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m}\). \[ \text{Length} = \text{Total base pairs} \times \text{Distance between bp} \] \[ \text{Length} = 6.6 \times 10^9\text{ bp} \times 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\text{ m/bp} = 2.244\text{ metres} \approx 2.2\text{ m} \]
  2. Part (b): Theoretical Number of Nucleosomes: One standard nucleosome contains \(\approx 200\text{ bp}\) of DNA wrapped around the histone octamer core. \[ \text{Number of nucleosomes} = \frac{\text{Total bp in diploid genome}}{200\text{ bp/nucleosome}} \] \[ \text{Number of nucleosomes} = \frac{6.6 \times 10^9}{200} = 3.3 \times 10^7 \text{ nucleosomes (33 million beads)} \]
Worked Example 3 (Meselson-Stahl Generational Isotope Distribution):
Problem: E. coli cells fully adapted to \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) medium are transferred to \(^{14}\mathrm{N}\) medium and allowed to divide for \(80\text{ minutes}\). What will be the proportion of heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)), hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)), and light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA molecules?
Detailed Solution:
  1. E. coli generation time is \(20\text{ minutes}\). In \(80\text{ minutes}\), the number of generations \(n = \frac{80}{20} = 4\).
  2. Total DNA molecules produced from \(1\) initial parental duplex after \(4\) generations \(= 2^4 = 16\) duplexes.
  3. Because replication is semiconservative, the \(2\) original heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}\)) strands remain conserved as templates forever, yielding exactly \(2\) hybrid duplexes (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
  4. The remaining \(16 - 2 = 14\) duplexes consist exclusively of newly synthesized light nitrogen: \(14\) light duplexes (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
  5. Heavy molecules (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)): \(0\).
  6. Ratios and Proportions: \[ \text{Hybrid} : \text{Light} = 2 : 14 = 1 : 7 \] Proportion of Hybrid \(= \frac{2}{16} = 12.5\%\); Proportion of Light \(= \frac{14}{16} = 87.5\%\).

English: In a double-stranded DNA segment having \(1200\) total base pairs, the number of Adenine bases is found to be \(360\). What will be the total number of hydrogen bonds stabilizing this DNA segment?

B) \(3240\)
C) \(3120\)
D) \(3600\)

हिंदी: एक द्विकुंडलीत डीएनए खंड में कुल \(1200\) क्षार युग्म (base pairs) हैं, जिनमें एडेनिन क्षारों की संख्या \(360\) है। इस डीएनए खंड को स्थिर रखने वाले कुल हाइड्रोजन बंधों की संख्या क्या होगी?

B) \(3240\)
C) \(3120\)
D) \(3600\)
Correct Answer: B) \(3240\)
English Solution:
1. Total base pairs \(= 1200\), which means there are \(1200\) paired bases on each strand (total \(2400\) nucleotides).
2. Number of Adenine (\(\mathrm{A}\)) bases \(= 360\). By Chargaff's rule, Thymine (\(\mathrm{T}\)) \(= 360\).

\[ \mathrm{H\text{-}bonds}_{(G \equiv C)} = 840 \times 3 = 2520 \] 6. Total hydrogen bonds \(= 720 + 2520 = 3240\).

कुल क्षार युग्म \(= 1200\) हैं। एडेनिन (\(\mathrm{A}\)) \(= 360\) है, अतः थायमिन (\(\mathrm{T}\)) \(= 360\) होगा।

शेष \(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\) युग्मों की संख्या \(= 1200 - 360 = 840\) होगी।
प्रत्येक \(\mathrm{G}\) और \(\mathrm{C}\) के बीच \(3\) हाइड्रोजन बंध होते हैं: \(840 \times 3 = 2520\)।

12. Concise Final Recap

12. संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

Deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) stores the complete blueprint of biological life in an antiparallel, double-stranded, right-handed helical conformation stabilized by complementary hydrogen bonds (\(\mathrm{A=T}\) and \(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\)) and base stacking. It replicates semiconservatively via an asymmetric replication fork utilizing leading and lagging strand mechanics. Transcription transcribes information from a designated template strand into RNA, which undergoes capping, splicing, and tailing in eukaryotes. The triplet, degenerate, and universal genetic code is translated by ribosomes and tRNA adapters into proteins. In prokaryotes, operons such as the lac operon coordinate metabolic responses to substrate availability via repressor-operator dynamics. Modern genomic advances, including the Human Genome Project and VNTR-based DNA Fingerprinting, harness nucleotide sequence polymorphisms for evolutionary, clinical, and forensic breakthroughs.
