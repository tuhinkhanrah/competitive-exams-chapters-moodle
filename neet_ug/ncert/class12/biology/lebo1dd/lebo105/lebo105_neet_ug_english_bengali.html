1. Chemical Architecture & Packaging of DNA

English

1.1 Polynucleotide Chain & Double Helix

Nucleic acids—deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA)—are linear polymers of nucleotides. Each nucleotide consists of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar (2′-deoxyribose in DNA; ribose with a reactive 2′-OH group in RNA), and a phosphate group.

  • Nitrogenous Bases: Purines (double-ring: Adenine [A], Guanine [G]) and Pyrimidines (single-ring: Cytosine [C], Thymine [T] in DNA, Uracil [U] in RNA). Thymine is chemically 5-methyl uracil.
  • Linkages: The base attaches to the 1′ carbon of the pentose sugar via an N-glycosidic bond to form a nucleoside (e.g., adenosine, deoxythymidine). Adding a phosphate to the 5′-OH via a phosphoester bond creates a nucleotide. Adjacent nucleotides join through 3′–5′ phosphodiester bonds, leaving a free 5′-phosphate at one end and a free 3′-OH at the other.

In 1869, Friedrich Meischer isolated an acidic nuclear substance he called nuclein. In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick—using X-ray diffraction data from Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins, alongside Erwin Chargaff's base-ratio rules—proposed the double-helix model of B-DNA:

  • Two antiparallel polynucleotide strands (one 5′→3′, the other 3′→5′) coil in a right-handed helix.
  • Chargaff's Equivalence: In double-stranded DNA, \(\mathrm{[A] = [T]}\) and \(\mathrm{[G] = [C]}\), so \(\frac{\mathrm{[A]+[G]}}{\mathrm{[T]+[C]}} = 1\).
  • Hydrogen Bonding: Adenine pairs with Thymine via 2 hydrogen bonds (\(A = T\)), and Guanine pairs with Cytosine via 3 hydrogen bonds (\(G \equiv C\)). Because a purine always pairs with a pyrimidine, the helix diameter stays uniform.
  • Helix Geometry: Pitch = \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\), containing approximately \(10\,\mathrm{bp}\) per turn; rise per base pair = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(0.34 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}\)). Plane-to-plane base stacking and H-bonds stabilize the helix.

Francis Crick's Central Dogma describes genetic information flow as \(\text{DNA} \rightarrow \text{RNA} \rightarrow \text{Protein}\) (with reverse transcription, \(\text{RNA} \rightarrow \text{DNA}\), occurring in retroviruses).

1.2 Packaging of DNA Helix

A diploid human cell contains roughly \(2.2\,\mathrm{m}\) of DNA inside a nucleus of diameter \(\sim 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\). In prokaryotes like E. coli, negatively charged DNA is organized into large loops held by positively charged proteins in a region called the nucleoid.

In eukaryotes, negatively charged DNA wraps around a positively charged histone octamer (rich in basic amino acids lysine and arginine) to form a nucleosome. A typical nucleosome holds 200 bp of DNA and is sealed by linker histone H1. Under an electron microscope, nucleosomes appear as beads-on-string. Further coiling into chromatin fibers and metaphase chromosomes requires Non-Histone Chromosomal (NHC) proteins.

  • Euchromatin: Loosely packed, stains light, transcriptionally active.
  • Heterochromatin: Densely packed, stains dark, transcriptionally inactive.

2. Search for the Genetic Material & The RNA World

English

Three landmark experiments established DNA as the hereditary molecule:

  1. Griffith's Transforming Principle (1928): Using Streptococcus pneumoniae in mice, Frederick Griffith showed that smooth (S) strain bacteria have a polysaccharide coat and are virulent (mice die), whereas rough (R) strain lack the coat and are non-virulent (mice live). Heat-killed S strain did not kill mice, but a mixture of heat-killed S + live R killed the mice, and live S bacteria were recovered. A "transforming principle" passed from dead S to live R cells.
  2. Biochemical Characterization (Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty, 1933–1944): They purified fractions from heat-killed S cells and tested enzymes. Proteases and RNases did not stop transformation, whereas DNase abolished transformation—proving DNA was the transforming substance.
  3. Hershey–Chase Experiment (1952): Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase gave unequivocal proof using T2 bacteriophages infecting E. coli. Phages grown on radioactive sulfur (³⁵S) had labeled protein coats (DNA lacks sulfur), while phages grown on radioactive phosphorus (³²P) had labeled DNA (proteins lack phosphorus). After Infection, Blending (agitating to shear viral coats), and Centrifugation (separating heavier bacterial pellets from lighter supernatant), ³²P radioactivity appeared in the bacterial pellet while ³⁵S stayed in the supernatant—confirming DNA enters the host cell.

2.1 Why DNA is Preferred Over RNA for Storage

A genetic material must (i) replicate, (ii) be chemically and structurally stable, (iii) allow slow mutations for evolution, and (iv) express Mendelian traits. Although RNA was the first genetic material (RNA World hypothesis—essential processes like metabolism, translation, and splicing evolved around RNA, which can also act as a ribozyme catalyst), DNA evolved from RNA for superior stability:

  • RNA has a reactive 2′-OH group on every ribose sugar, making it labile and easily degradable.
  • DNA has thymine instead of uracil, providing greater chemical stability and repair efficiency.
  • Double-stranded DNA with complementary strands resists damage and undergoes slower mutation than single-stranded RNA genomes (such as TMV or Qβ bacteriophage, which mutate and evolve faster).

3. Semiconservative DNA Replication

English

3.1 Experimental Proof: Meselson & Stahl (1958)

Watson and Crick proposed that each parental strand serves as a template for a new complementary strand, producing daughter duplexes with one old and one new strand (semiconservative replication). Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl proved this in E. coli:

  • E. coli were grown for many generations in heavy nitrogen medium (\(^{15}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\); note that \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) is a heavy non-radioactive isotope) so all DNA became heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)).
  • Cells were transferred to normal light medium (\(^{14}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\)), and DNA densities were separated by cesium chloride (\(\mathrm{CsCl}\)) density gradient centrifugation.
  • After 1 generation (20 min): 100% of DNA had intermediate/hybrid density (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
  • After 2 generations (40 min): 50% hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) and 50% light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA.

In 1958, Taylor and colleagues used radioactive thymidine on Vicia faba (faba beans) root tips to prove semiconservative replication in eukaryotic chromosomes as well.

3.2 Replication Machinery & The Replication Fork

DNA replication initiates at a specific sequence called the origin of replication (ori) (in eukaryotes, replication occurs during the S-phase of the cell cycle; failure of cell division after replication causes polyploidy). Because separating the entire duplex at once requires immense energy, unwinding occurs locally at a Y-shaped replication fork.

  • Main Enzyme: DNA-dependent DNA polymerase (~2000 bp/s in E. coli, finishing \(4.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{bp}\) in ~18 minutes). It polymerizes deoxyribonucleotides strictly in the 5′→3′ direction and cannot initiate synthesis de novo.
  • Dual Role of dNTPs: Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dATP, dGTP, dCTP, dTTP) act both as substrates and as the energy source (via their two terminal high-energy phosphates).
  • Leading vs. Lagging Strand: On the 3′→5′ template, synthesis is continuous (leading strand). On the 5′→3′ template, synthesis is discontinuous (lagging strand fragments), which are later sealed by DNA ligase.

4. Transcription & Eukaryotic RNA Processing

English

Transcription copies genetic information from a single strand of DNA into RNA (where Adenine pairs with Uracil instead of Thymine). Both DNA strands are not transcribed simultaneously because (i) they would produce two different proteins from the same segment, and (ii) the two complementary RNAs would anneal into double-stranded RNA, blocking translation.

4.1 The Transcription Unit

A transcription unit has three regions: a Promoter, a Structural gene, and a Terminator.

  • Template Strand (3′→5′): Read by DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, which synthesizes RNA in the 5′→3′ direction.
  • Coding Strand (5′→3′): Displaced during transcription; its sequence matches the synthesized RNA (except it has T where RNA has U). All positional references use the coding strand: the promoter is at the 5′-end (upstream), and the terminator is at the 3′-end (downstream).

A cistron is a DNA segment coding for a polypeptide. Prokaryotic structural genes are usually polycistronic, whereas eukaryotic structural genes are monocistronic and split—containing coding exons interrupted by non-coding introns.

4.2 Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes

In bacteria, a single DNA-dependent RNA polymerase transcribes mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA. The core enzyme performs elongation and associates transiently with the sigma (\(\sigma\)) factor for initiation and the rho (\(\rho\)) factor for termination. Because there is no nuclear membrane and bacterial mRNA needs no processing, transcription and translation are coupled.

In eukaryotes, three nuclear RNA polymerases divide the labor, and the primary transcript (hnRNA) undergoes three processing steps before leaving the nucleus:

RNA Polymerase I rRNAs (28S, 18S, 5.8S) 1. Capping: Methyl guanosine triphosphate (\(^\mathrm{m}\mathrm{Gppp}\)) added at 5′-end.
2. Splicing: Introns removed, exons joined in defined order.
3. Tailing (Polyadenylation): 200–300 adenylate residues added at 3′-end (template-independent).

5. Genetic Code, Mutations & Translation

English

5.1 Salient Features of the Genetic Code

George Gamow deduced that a triplet code of 4 bases gives \(4^3 = 64\) codons to specify 20 amino acids. H. G. Khorana (chemical synthesis of defined RNA homopolymers/copolymers), Marshall Nirenberg (cell-free protein synthesis), and Severo Ochoa's enzyme (polynucleotide phosphorylase for template-independent RNA synthesis) deciphered the code:

  • Triplet & Contiguous: 61 codons code for amino acids; 3 are stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA). Read without punctuation.
  • Degenerate: Most amino acids are encoded by more than one codon (e.g., Phe by UUU, UUC).
  • Nearly Universal: Identical from bacteria to humans, with minor exceptions in mitochondrial codons and some protozoans.
  • Dual Role of AUG: Codes for Methionine (Met) and acts as the initiator codon.

5.2 Point & Frameshift Mutations

A single-base substitution (point mutation) in the \(\beta\)-globin gene changes glutamate to valine, causing sickle-cell anemia. Inserting or deleting 1 or 2 bases shifts the entire triplet reading frame downstream (frameshift mutation), whereas inserting or deleting 3 (or multiples of 3) bases adds or removes whole amino acids without altering the downstream reading frame.

5.3 tRNA & Translation

tRNA acts as the adapter molecule (2D clover-leaf shape; actual 3D compact inverted-L shape). It has an anticodon loop complementary to the mRNA codon and a 3′ amino acid acceptor end. Stop codons have no tRNAs.

Translation (peptide bond formation on ribosomes) proceeds in four stages:

  1. Charging (Aminoacylation) of tRNA: Amino acids are activated with ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA.
  2. Initiation: The small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA at the start codon (AUG), recognized by initiator tRNA. mRNA contains Untranslated Regions (UTRs) at both 5′- and 3′-ends for efficient translation.
  3. Elongation: Charged tRNAs enter the large subunit sequentially; 23S rRNA in bacteria acts as a ribozyme catalyzing peptide bond formation.
  4. Termination: A release factor binds the stop codon, releasing the polypeptide.

6. Regulation of Gene Expression & The lac Operon

English

In eukaryotes, gene expression is regulated at four levels: (i) transcriptional level, (ii) processing/splicing level, (iii) mRNA transport from nucleus to cytoplasm, and (iv) translational level. In prokaryotes, transcriptional initiation is the predominant control point.

François Jacob and Jacques Monod elucidated the lac operon in E. coli, a polycistronic unit regulated by a common promoter and operator:

  • i gene (inhibitor): Constitutively expresses the repressor protein (always synthesized at a steady rate).
  • Structural genes: z codes for \(\beta\)-galactosidase (hydrolyzes lactose into galactose + glucose); y codes for permease (increases cell permeability to \(\beta\)-galactosides); a codes for transacetylase.
  • In absence of inducer: The active repressor binds to the operator (o), blocking RNA polymerase (negative regulation).
  • In presence of inducer (lactose / allolactose): A very low basal level of permease allows lactose to enter the cell. Lactose binds and inactivates the repressor, freeing the operator so RNA polymerase transcribes lac mRNA. Note that glucose and galactose cannot act as inducers. Once all lactose is consumed, the repressor re-binds the operator and shuts the operon off.

7. Human Genome Project (HGP) & DNA Fingerprinting

English

7.1 Human Genome Project (1990–2003)

Launched in 1990 as a 13-year mega project (Chromosome 1 was completed in May 2006), HGP drove the rise of Bioinformatics. Two methodologies were used: Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs) (identifying all genes expressed as RNA) and Sequence Annotation (sequencing the entire genome—coding and non-coding—and assigning functions). DNA fragments were cloned in bacterial/yeast hosts using BAC and YAC vectors and sequenced via automated Sanger sequencing.

  • Human genome size: 3164.7 million bp; estimated genes: ~30,000; average gene length: 3,000 bases (largest gene: dystrophin at 2.4 million bases).
  • 99.9% of nucleotide bases are identical across all humans; <2% of the genome codes for proteins; over 50% of discovered genes have unknown functions.
  • Chromosome 1 has the most genes (2,968); Y chromosome has the fewest (231).
  • ~1.4 million SNPs (Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms) identified.

7.2 DNA Fingerprinting

Developed by Alec Jeffreys, DNA fingerprinting exploits differences in non-coding repetitive DNA (separated as satellite DNA peaks during density gradient centrifugation). A locus shows DNA polymorphism when an inheritable mutation occurs in a population at a frequency >0.01. Jeffreys used VNTR (Variable Number of Tandem Repeats), a class of mini-satellite DNA (0.1 to 20 kb), as a radiolabeled probe in Southern blot hybridization:

Steps: (1) DNA isolation → (2) Digestion by restriction endonucleases → (3) Gel electrophoresis → (4) Southern blotting onto nitrocellulose/nylon membrane → (5) Hybridization with labeled VNTR probe → (6) Autoradiography. Sensitivity is amplified using PCR (allowing analysis from a single cell). Banding patterns are unique to every individual except monozygotic (identical) twins, enabling forensic identification and paternity testing.

Worked Example 1: Chargaff's Rule & Helix Length

Scenario (Hypothetical): Suppose a newly isolated double-stranded viral DNA molecule contains \(1{,}200\,\mathrm{bp}\) and has \(22\%\) guanine residues. Calculate (a) the percentage of adenine residues, and (b) the total length of this DNA molecule in nanometers.

Solution:

  • (a) By Chargaff's rule, \(\mathrm{[G] = [C]} = 22\%\). Thus, \(\mathrm{[G] + [C]} = 44\%\). The remaining \(100\% - 44\% = 56\%\) is shared equally by A and T, so \(\mathrm{[A]} = \frac{56\%}{2} = 28\%\).
  • (b) Distance between consecutive base pairs \(= 0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\). Total length \(= 1{,}200 \times 0.34\,\mathrm{nm} = 408\,\mathrm{nm}\).

Worked Example 2: Meselson–Stahl Generation Proportions

Scenario (Hypothetical): Assume E. coli grown exclusively in \(^{15}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) medium is transferred to \(^{14}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) medium and allowed to divide for \(80\,\mathrm{minutes}\) (generation time \(= 20\,\mathrm{minutes}\)). What will be the ratio of heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)), hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)), and light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA molecules?

Solution:

Number of generations \(n = \frac{80}{20} = 4\). Starting from 1 heavy DNA duplex, total duplexes after 4 generations \(= 2^4 = 16\). Because replication is semiconservative, the 2 original \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) strands remain conserved in 2 hybrid molecules, while the remaining \(16 - 2 = 14\) molecules are completely light. Ratio (Heavy : Hybrid : Light) \(= 0 : 2 : 14 = 0 : 1 : 7\) (\(12.5\%\) hybrid, \(87.5\%\) light).

ব্যাখ্যা: বিবৃতি I সঠিক। বিবৃতি II ভুল, কারণ ³⁵S ভাইরাসের প্রোটিন আবরণকে চিহ্নিত করে যা উপরের তরলে (supernatant) থাকে; ব্যাকটেরিয়ার তলানিতে ³²P পাওয়া যায়।

Practice Question 3 (Single-Correct MCQ)

In a hypothetical double-stranded DNA fragment of \(100\,\mathrm{bp}\), \(30\%\) of the bases are adenine. How many hydrogen bonds hold the two strands together in this fragment?

  1. 240
  2. 260
  3. 230
  4. 300
Answer: A

Explanation: In \(100\,\mathrm{bp}\), \(\mathrm{A = T} = 30\,\mathrm{bp}\) and \(\mathrm{G = C} = 20\,\mathrm{bp}\) (since \(50 - 30 = 20\)). Total H-bonds \(= (30 \times 2) + (20 \times 3) = 60 + 60 = 120\)? Wait—let us check carefully! Total bases \(= 200\); \(30\%\) of \(200 = 60\) A residues, meaning \(60\) A–T base pairs! Thus, A–T pairs \(= 60\) and G–C pairs \(= 100 - 60 = 40\). Total H-bonds \(= (60 \times 2) + (40 \times 3) = 120 + 120 = 240\).

10. Common Pitfalls, Revision Checklist & Final Recap

English
High-Frequency NEET UG Traps to Avoid:
  • ¹⁵N is NOT radioactive: Meselson–Stahl separated ¹⁵N and ¹⁴N DNA by density in CsCl, not radioactivity. Hershey–Chase used radioactive ³⁵S and ³²P.
  • Promoter & Terminator positions: Always defined with respect to the coding strand (Promoter = 5′ upstream; Terminator = 3′ downstream).
  • i gene in lac operon: Stands for inhibitor, not inducer.
  • Polyadenylation: Happens in a template-independent manner at the 3′-end of hnRNA.
Quick Final Recap

DNA (double-stranded, 2′-deoxyribose, thymine) is the stable storage molecule for genetic information, replicating semiconservatively in the 5′→3′ direction. Transcription produces RNA from one DNA strand, which in eukaryotes requires capping, splicing, and polyadenylation. The contiguous, degenerate triplet genetic code is decoded by tRNA adapters on ribosomes (with 23S rRNA acting as a ribozyme) to synthesize proteins. Gene expression in bacteria is exemplified by negative regulation in the lac operon, while HGP and VNTR-based DNA fingerprinting anchor modern genomics and forensics.

ডিএনএ (দ্বি-তন্ত্রী, ২′-ডিঅক্সিরাইবোজ, থাইমিন) হলো জিনগত তথ্যের স্থায়ী ভাণ্ডার, যা ৫′→৩′ অভিমুখে অর্ধ-সংরক্ষণশীল পদ্ধতিতে প্রতিলিপিকৃত হয়। ট্রান্সক্রিপশনের মাধ্যমে উৎপন্ন RNA ইউক্যারিওটে ক্যাপিং, স্প্লাইসিং ও টেইলিং-এর মাধ্যমে পরিণত mRNA হয়। tRNA অ্যাডাপ্টার ও রাইবোজোম (23S rRNA রাইবোজাইমসহ) বিরামহীন জেনেটিক কোড পড়ে প্রোটিন তৈরি করে। ব্যাকটেরিয়ায় lac অপেরন ঋণাত্মক নিয়ন্ত্রণের আদর্শ উদাহরণ, আর HGP ও VNTR-ভিত্তিক ডিএনএ ফিঙ্গারপ্রিন্টিং আধুনিক জিনোমিক্স ও ফরেনসিক বিজ্ঞানের ভিত্তি।
