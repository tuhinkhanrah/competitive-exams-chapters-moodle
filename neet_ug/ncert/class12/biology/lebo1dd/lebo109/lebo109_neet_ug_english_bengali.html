2. Core Principles of Biotechnology / জৈবপ্রযুক্তির মূল নীতিসমূহ

English

2.1 Meaning and EFB Definition

Biotechnology harnesses living organisms, cells, or their enzymes to create products and processes beneficial to humanity. Traditional practices like curd, bread, and wine fermentation rely on microbes, whereas modern biotechnology employs genetically modified organisms (GMOs), in vitro fertilization, synthetic genes, DNA vaccines, and gene therapy.

European Federation of Biotechnology (EFB) Definition

“The integration of natural science and organisms, cells, parts thereof, and molecular analogues for products and services.”

2.2 Two Core Pillars of Modern Biotechnology

  • Genetic Engineering: Techniques that alter the chemistry of genetic material (DNA and RNA), introduce it into host organisms, and thereby modify the host’s phenotype.
  • Bioprocess Engineering: Maintaining a strictly sterile (contamination-free) environment in chemical engineering vessels so only the desired microbe or eukaryotic cell grows in large quantities to manufacture antibiotics, vaccines, and enzymes.

2.3 Why Recombinant DNA Overcomes Traditional Hybridisation

Sexual reproduction generates genetic variation, whereas asexual reproduction preserves identical genetic information. Traditional plant and animal hybridisation often transfers undesirable genes alongside desirable traits. Genetic engineering solves this by using recombinant DNA (rDNA), gene cloning, and gene transfer to isolate and introduce only specific desirable genes.

An isolated piece of alien DNA transferred into a host usually fails to multiply unless it integrates into a host chromosome or a vector carrying an origin of replication (ori)—the specific DNA sequence where replication initiates. Linking alien DNA to an ori enables it to replicate and produce multiple identical copies (cloning).

2.4 The First Artificial Recombinant DNA (1972)

In 1972, Stanley Cohen and Herbert Boyer constructed the first artificial rDNA molecule. They isolated an antibiotic-resistance gene from a native plasmid of Salmonella typhimurium using restriction enzymes (“molecular scissors”), joined it to a plasmid vector using DNA ligase, and introduced the recombinant plasmid into Escherichia coli. Using the host’s DNA polymerase, the gene replicated into multiple copies.

Three Basic Steps in Genetically Modifying an Organism:

  1. Identification of DNA containing desirable genes.
  2. Introduction of the identified DNA into the host.
  3. Maintenance of the introduced DNA in the host and its transfer to progeny.

3. Tools of Recombinant DNA Technology / রিকম্বিন্যান্ট ডিএনএ প্রযুক্তির সরঞ্জাম

English

3.1 Restriction Enzymes (Molecular Scissors)

In 1963, two enzymes restricting bacteriophage growth in E. coli were isolated: one added methyl groups to DNA (methylase), while the other cut DNA (restriction endonuclease). Five years later, Hind II became the first characterised restriction endonuclease whose cutting depended on a specific 6-base-pair recognition sequence. Today, over 900 restriction enzymes are known from more than 230 bacterial strains.

Naming Convention (Example: EcoRI)

  • First letter (E): Genus name (Escherichia).
  • Next two letters (co): Species name (coli).
  • Fourth letter (R): Strain name (RY 13).
  • Roman numeral (I): Order of isolation from that bacterial strain (1st isolated).

Exonucleases vs. Endonucleases & Palindromic Sequences

Nucleases are of two types: exonucleases remove nucleotides from DNA ends, whereas endonucleases cut at specific internal positions. Restriction endonucleases inspect DNA length and bind to a specific palindromic nucleotide sequence—a sequence of base pairs that reads the same on both strands when the reading orientation is kept identical (e.g., \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) on both strands):

\[ \begin{aligned} 5' &\longrightarrow \mathrm{G\,A\,A\,T\,T\,C} \longrightarrow 3' \\ 3' &\longleftarrow \mathrm{C\,T\,T\,A\,A\,G} \longleftarrow 5' \end{aligned} \]

EcoRI cuts both strands slightly away from the centre of the palindrome, between G and A on opposite strands, creating single-stranded overhanging stretches called sticky ends (5'-AATT-3'). Sticky ends form hydrogen bonds with complementary cut counterparts, facilitating joining by DNA ligase. Cutting both vector and foreign DNA with the same restriction enzyme ensures identical sticky ends for recombinant DNA assembly.

3.2 Separation and Isolation of DNA Fragments (Gel Electrophoresis)

Restriction digestion produces DNA fragments of varying lengths. Because DNA is negatively charged (due to phosphate groups), fragments migrate toward the anode (positive electrode) under an electric field through a porous matrix—commonly agarose, a natural polymer extracted from seaweeds.

  • Sieving Effect: Smaller DNA fragments experience less resistance and travel farther toward the anode than larger fragments.
  • Visualisation: Pure DNA is invisible in normal light. The gel is stained with ethidium bromide (EtBr) and exposed to UV radiation, revealing bright orange-coloured bands.
  • Elution: The desired DNA band is cut out from the agarose gel and extracted/purified from the gel piece; this step is called elution.

3.3 Cloning Vectors

Plasmids and bacteriophages replicate inside bacterial cells independently of chromosomal DNA. Bacteriophages achieve very high copy numbers per cell, whereas plasmids range from 1–2 copies to 15–100+ copies per cell. An engineered cloning vector must possess:

  1. Origin of Replication (ori): Sequence where replication begins and which controls the copy number of the linked DNA.
  2. Selectable Marker: Helps identify and eliminate non-transformants and selectively permit growth of transformants (e.g., genes for resistance to ampicillin, chloramphenicol, tetracycline, or kanamycin in E. coli).
  3. Cloning Sites (Unique Recognition Sites): Very few—ideally a single—recognition site for each commonly used restriction enzyme. Multiple sites for the same enzyme would fragment the vector and complicate cloning.

Selection in pBR322 vs. Insertional Inactivation (Blue–White Screening)

In the classic E. coli cloning vector pBR322, inserting foreign DNA at the BamH I site inside the tetracycline-resistance gene (tetR) inactivates tetracycline resistance. Transformants still grow on ampicillin (due to intact ampR), while non-transformants die. Replica plating onto tetracycline medium distinguishes recombinants (grow on ampicillin only) from non-recombinants (grow on both ampicillin and tetracycline).

Because dual-antibiotic plating is cumbersome, an alternative chromogenic method uses insertional inactivation of the β-galactosidase gene. In the presence of a chromogenic substrate:

  • Non-recombinants (no insert): Active β-galactosidase produces blue-coloured colonies.
  • Recombinants (insert present): The β-galactosidase gene is inactivated, yielding colourless (white) colonies.

Vectors for Plants and Animals (Disarmed Pathogens)

  • Plants: Agrobacterium tumefaciens (a pathogen of dicot plants) naturally delivers a DNA segment called T-DNA via its Ti (tumour-inducing) plasmid to cause crown gall tumours. Scientists have disarmed the Ti plasmid so it is non-pathogenic yet efficiently delivers genes of interest into plants.
  • Animals: Retroviruses naturally transform normal animal cells into cancerous cells; disarmed retroviruses are now used to deliver desirable genes into animal cells.

4.1 Isolation of Pure Genetic Material (DNA)

To cut DNA with restriction enzymes, it must be free from RNA, proteins, polysaccharides, and lipids:

  • Cell Wall Lysis: Lysozyme (bacteria), Cellulase (plant cells), Chitinase (fungus).
  • Removal of Macromolecules: Ribonuclease (RNase) removes RNA; Protease digests proteins (including histones).
  • Precipitation & Spooling: Adding chilled ethanol precipitates purified DNA as fine threads, which are collected by spooling.

4.2 Amplification of Gene of Interest using PCR

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) synthesises multiple copies of a target DNA sequence in vitro using two sets of primers (short chemically synthesised oligonucleotides complementary to target regions), deoxynucleotides (dNTPs), and a thermostable DNA polymerase—Taq polymerase, isolated from the thermophilic bacterium Thermus aquaticus, which remains active at high denaturation temperatures.

\[ N = N_0 \times 2^n \]

After \(n = 30\) cycles starting from \(N_0 = 1\) dsDNA copy, \(N = 2^{30} \approx 1.07 \times 10^9\) (approx. 1 billion) copies are generated.

When a protein-encoding gene is expressed in a heterologous host, the product is called a recombinant protein. Cells can be multiplied in a continuous culture system—where used medium is drained from one side and fresh medium is added from the other—keeping cells in their physiologically most active log/exponential phase for higher biomass and yield.

For industrial production (100–1000 litres), bioreactors provide optimal growth conditions (temperature, pH, substrate, salts, vitamins, oxygen). Stirred-tank bioreactors are cylindrical with a curved base to facilitate mixing:

  • Simple Stirred-Tank Bioreactor: Features an agitator system (flat-bladed impellers), oxygen delivery system (sterile air), foam breaker, temperature & pH control systems, steam sterilisation port, and sampling ports.
  • Sparged Stirred-Tank Bioreactor: Sterile air bubbles are sparged through the culture medium, dramatically increasing the surface area for oxygen transfer.

Downstream Processing: After biosynthesis, the product undergoes separation and purification, formulation with suitable preservatives, thorough clinical trials (for drugs), and strict quality control testing before marketing.

Worked Example 1: PCR Amplification Calculation (Hypothetical Scenario)

Problem: Suppose a forensic laboratory starts a PCR reaction with \(N_0 = 5\) identical double-stranded DNA template molecules (assumed hypothetical value) and runs the thermal cycler for \(n = 12\) complete cycles. Assuming 100% amplification efficiency, how many copies of the target DNA will be present at the end of the 12th cycle?

Solution:

Each PCR cycle doubles the number of double-stranded DNA molecules. Using the exponential amplification relation:

\[ N = N_0 \times 2^n \]

Substituting \(N_0 = 5\) and \(n = 12\):

\[ N = 5 \times 2^{12} = 5 \times 4096 = 20{,}480\text{ copies} \]

Answer: \(20{,}480\) copies of target DNA.

Worked Example 2: Selection of Recombinants Using pBR322

Problem: A researcher ligates a hypothetical human insulin-coding fragment into the Pst I restriction site of the plasmid pBR322 and transforms competent E. coli cells. How will the researcher distinguish (a) non-transformants, (b) non-recombinant transformants, and (c) recombinant transformants using ampicillin and tetracycline agar plates?

Solution:

In pBR322, the Pst I site lies within the ampicillin-resistance gene (ampR), while the tetracycline-resistance gene (tetR) remains intact.

  • Non-transformants: Carry no plasmid, so they fail to grow on both tetracycline and ampicillin plates.
  • Non-recombinant transformants: Carry intact pBR322; both tetR and ampR are functional, so they grow on both tetracycline and ampicillin plates.
  • Recombinant transformants: Insertion at Pst I inactivates ampR, while tetR stays functional. Thus, they grow on tetracycline plates but die on ampicillin plates.

7. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist / সাধারণ ভুল ও পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

English
Common NEET UG Pitfalls to Avoid
  • Anode vs. Cathode in Electrophoresis: DNA is negatively charged, so it always moves toward the anode (+), never the cathode (-).
  • Blue vs. White Colonies: White (colourless) colonies are recombinants (insertional inactivation of β-galactosidase), whereas blue colonies are non-recombinants.
  • Same Restriction Enzyme: Vector and foreign DNA must be cut with the same restriction enzyme to generate compatible sticky ends.
  • Chilled Ethanol vs. EtBr: Chilled ethanol precipitates DNA during isolation (spooling); ethidium bromide (EtBr) stains DNA in agarose gel for UV visualisation.

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Cohen & Boyer (1972): S. typhimurium plasmid + E. coli host.
  • Hind II: first characterised restriction endonuclease (6 bp recognition site).
  • pBR322 markers: ampR (Pst I, Pvu I) and tetR (BamH I, Sal I).
  • Bioreactor volume: 100–1000 litres; sparged type increases oxygen transfer area via bubbles.

8. Concise Final Recap / সংক্ষিপ্ত চূড়ান্ত সারমর্ম

English

Modern biotechnology rests on genetic engineering (altering DNA/RNA and introducing it into hosts) and bioprocess engineering (sterile large-scale cultivation). Constructing recombinant DNA requires isolating pure DNA (lysozyme/cellulase/chitinase + RNase + protease + chilled ethanol), cutting both source DNA and a cloning vector (e.g., pBR322 with ori and selectable markers) with the same restriction endonuclease, separating fragments via agarose gel electrophoresis, amplifying target genes via PCR (Taq polymerase), joining fragments with DNA ligase, introducing rDNA into a competent host (Ca²⁺ heat shock, micro-injection, gene gun, or disarmed pathogens), expressing the recombinant protein in bioreactors, and completing downstream processing before clinical use.

বাংলা (Bengali)

আধুনিক জৈবপ্রযুক্তি জিনগত প্রকৌশল ও বায়োপ্রসেস প্রকৌশলের ওপর প্রতিষ্ঠিত। rDNA প্রযুক্তির মূল প্রবাহ হলো: বিশুদ্ধ ডিএনএ পৃথকীকরণ (লাইসোজাইম/সেলুলেজ/কাইটিনেজ + RNase + প্রোটিয়েজ + শীতল ইথানল) → উৎস ডিএনএ এবং ক্লোনিং ভেক্টরকে (যেমন pBR322) একই রেস্ট্রিকশন এন্ডোনিউক্লিয়েজ দিয়ে কর্তন → অ্যাগারোজ জেল ইলেক্ট্রোফোরেসিস ও ইল্যুশন → পিসিআর (PCR) দ্বারা বিবর্ধন → ডিএনএ লাইগেজ দ্বারা সংযোগ → যোগ্য পোষক কোষে প্রবেশ → বায়োরিয়্যাক্টরে বৃহৎ-পরিসরে প্রোটিন উৎপাদন → এবং বাজারজাতকরণের পূর্বে ডাউনস্ট্রিম প্রসেসিং।
