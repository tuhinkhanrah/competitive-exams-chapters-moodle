1. Learning Objectives & Exam Blueprint • শিখন উদ্দেশ্য ও অধ্যায় রূপরেখা

The structure of the atom forms the bedrock of modern chemistry and physical principles tested in the National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (NEET UG). By studying this comprehensive tutorial, the aspirant will master:

  • Discovery and Characteristics of Sub-atomic Particles: Cathode rays, Thomson's \(e/m_e\) determination, Millikan's oil drop experiment, discovery of protons (canal rays) and neutrons.
  • Evolution of Atomic Models: Thomson's plum pudding model, Rutherford's \(\alpha\)-particle scattering experiment, nuclear model, and its classical electrodynamic limitations.
  • Dual Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation & Planck's Quantum Theory: Wave parameters (\(c = \nu\lambda\), \(\bar{\nu}\)), black body radiation, photoelectric effect, work function (\(W_0\)), and threshold frequency (\(\nu_0\)).
  • Hydrogen Line Spectrum & Bohr's Atomic Model: Rydberg equation, spectral series (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen, Brackett, Pfund), quantized angular momentum (\(mvr = nh/2\pi\)), orbital radii, energies, and limitations.

  • Quantum Mechanical Model of the Atom: Schrödinger equation (\(\hat{H}\Psi = E\Psi\)), physical significance of \(\Psi\) and \(|\Psi|^2\), quantum numbers (\(n, l, m_l, m_s\)), boundary surface diagrams, radial and angular nodes.
  • Electronic Rules & Orbital Configurations: Aufbau principle, Pauli's exclusion principle, Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity, exchange energy, and anomalous stability of half-filled and fully filled subshells (\(\mathrm{Cr}\) and \(\mathrm{Cu}\)).

2. Discovery of Sub-atomic Particles & Early Atomic Models • উপ-পারমাণবিক কণার আবিষ্কার ও প্রাথমিক পারমাণবিক মডেল

2.1 Discovery of Sub-atomic Particles

Until the late 19th century, Dalton's atomic theory regarded the atom as the ultimate indivisible building block of matter. However, experimental electrical discharge through gases revealed that atoms are composed of sub-atomic constituent particles: electrons, protons, and neutrons.

Discovery of the Electron & Cathode Ray Experiments

Michael Faraday (1830s to 1850s) studied electrical discharge in partially evacuated glass tubes (cathode ray discharge tubes). At very low pressure (\(\sim 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{atm}\)) and very high voltage (\(> 10,000\,\mathrm{V}\)), a stream of particles travels from the negative electrode (cathode) to the positive electrode (anode). These were termed cathode rays.

  • Cathode rays travel from the cathode toward the anode in straight lines in the absence of external fields.
  • They cause phosphorescence or fluorescence when striking specific materials, such as zinc sulphide (\(\mathrm{ZnS}\)).
  • In the presence of electric and magnetic fields, they deflect in the direction characteristic of negatively charged particles.
  • Crucial Invariant: The characteristics (charge-to-mass ratio) of cathode rays are completely independent of the material of the electrodes and the nature of the gas inside the tube. Hence, electrons are universal constituents of all matter.
Figure 1: Schematic diagram of a cathode ray discharge tube with a perforated anode and zinc sulphide phosphorescent screen.

Determination of Charge-to-Mass Ratio (\(e/m_e\)) & Millikan's Oil Drop Experiment

In 1897, J.J. Thomson determined the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron by applying mutually perpendicular electric (\(E\)) and magnetic (\(B\)) fields to an electron beam. The deflection depends on:

  1. Magnitude of negative charge on the particle (greater charge \(\implies\) greater deflection).
  2. Mass of the particle (lighter mass \(\implies\) greater deflection).
  3. Strength of electric or magnetic field applied.
\[ \frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{C \ kg^{-1}} \]

Between 1906 and 1914, R.A. Millikan performed the famous oil drop experiment. By ionizing air with X-rays, oil droplets captured integral units of electrical charge. By balancing gravitational, electrostatic, and viscous forces, he showed charge is quantized: \(q = n e\), where \(n = 1, 2, 3 \dots\). He determined:

\[ e = -1.602176 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{C} \] \[ m_e = \frac{e}{e/m_e} = \frac{1.602176 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{C}}{1.758820 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{C \ kg^{-1}}} = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31} \ \mathrm{kg} \]

Discovery of Protons & Neutrons

E. Goldstein (1886) discovered canal rays (positive rays) in a discharge tube with a perforated cathode:

  • Unlike cathode rays, the mass and \(e/m\) ratio of positive rays depend on the nature of gas in the tube because canal rays are simply ionized gaseous cations.
  • The lightest and smallest positive ion was obtained from hydrogen gas and was identified as the proton (formalized in 1919).
  • In 1932, James Chadwick discovered the neutron by bombarding a thin sheet of beryllium with \(\alpha\)-particles: \[ \mathrm{^{9}_{4}Be + ^{4}_{2}\alpha \longrightarrow ^{12}_{6}C + ^{1}_{0}n} \] The emitted neutral particles had a mass slightly greater than that of a proton (\(1.67493 \times 10^{-27} \ \mathrm{kg}\)).

2.2 Early Atomic Models: Thomson vs. Rutherford

Thomson's Model (1898): Envisioned the atom as a spherical cloud of uniform positive charge (radius \(\approx 10^{-10} \ \mathrm{m}\)) with electrons embedded within it like seeds in a watermelon or raisins in a pudding. It assumed mass was uniformly distributed. While it explained overall electrical neutrality, it could not account for large-angle \(\alpha\)-scattering.

Rutherford's \(\alpha\)-Particle Scattering Experiment (1909–1911): Geiger and Marsden directed high-energy \(\alpha\)-particles (\(\mathrm{He^{2+}}\)) from a radioactive source at a very thin gold foil (\(\sim 100 \ \mathrm{nm}\) thick) surrounded by a circular fluorescent \(\mathrm{ZnS}\) screen.

Figure 2: Experimental setup of Rutherford's \(\alpha\)-particle scattering and submicroscopic deflection trajectories near the dense atomic nucleus.

Key Observations and Deductions:

  • Observation: Most \(\alpha\)-particles passed straight through without deflection. \(\implies\) Conclusion: Most of the space in an atom is empty.
  • Observation: A small fraction suffered small-angle deflections. \(\implies\) Conclusion: Positive charge is concentrated in a tiny volume exerting immense electrostatic repulsion.
  • Observation: Very few (\(\sim 1\) in 20,000) deflected by nearly \(180^\circ\). \(\implies\) Conclusion: The entire positive charge and almost the whole mass of the atom are concentrated in an extraordinarily small volume called the nucleus.
Scale Comparison: Radius of atom \(\approx 10^{-10} \ \mathrm{m}\) (\(1 \ \text{Å}\)); Radius of nucleus \(\approx 10^{-15} \ \mathrm{m}\) (\(1 \ \mathrm{fm}\)). Ratio of volumes \(= \frac{V_{\text{atom}}}{V_{\text{nucleus}}} \approx \frac{(10^{-10})^3}{(10^{-15})^3} = 10^{15}\). If a cricket ball represents the nucleus, the radius of the atom would be about \(5 \ \mathrm{km}\).

Drawbacks of Rutherford's Nuclear Model

  1. Instability of the Atom: According to classical electromagnetic theory (Maxwell), an accelerating charged particle must continuously emit electromagnetic radiation. An electron orbiting the nucleus undergoes centripetal acceleration (\(a = v^2/r\)). It should lose energy continuously, causing its orbit to shrink, spiraling into the nucleus in \(\sim 10^{-8} \ \mathrm{s}\). Thus, Rutherford's model cannot explain the stability of atoms.
  2. Failure to Explain Atomic Spectra: Continuous spiraling should emit a continuous spectrum of all wavelengths, but real atoms emit sharp, discrete line spectra. It also says nothing about electron distribution and energies.

2.3 Nuclear Composition: Isotopes, Isobars, and Related Species

  • Atomic Number (\(Z\)): Number of protons in the nucleus = Number of electrons in a neutral atom.
  • Mass Number (\(A\)): Total number of nucleons = Protons (\(Z\)) + Neutrons (\(n\)). Hence, \(n = A - Z\). Standard notation: \(\mathrm{^{A}_{Z}X}\).
  • Isotopes: Atoms of the same element with identical \(Z\) but different \(A\) (differ in number of neutrons). E.g., Protium (\(\mathrm{^{1}_{1}H}\)), Deuterium (\(\mathrm{^{2}_{1}H}\) or \(\mathrm{D}\)), Tritium (\(\mathrm{^{3}_{1}H}\) or \(\mathrm{T}\)); \(\mathrm{^{35}_{17}Cl}\) and \(\mathrm{^{37}_{17}Cl}\). Chemical properties of isotopes are identical because they are governed by valence electrons.
  • Isobars: Atoms of different elements having the same mass number (\(A\)) but different atomic numbers (\(Z\)). E.g., \(\mathrm{^{14}_{6}C}\) and \(\mathrm{^{14}_{7}N}\); \(\mathrm{^{40}_{18}Ar}\), \(\mathrm{^{40}_{19}K}\), and \(\mathrm{^{40}_{20}Ca}\).
  • Isotones: Atoms possessing the same number of neutrons (\(A - Z\)). E.g., \(\mathrm{^{14}_{6}C}\) (\(n = 8\)), \(\mathrm{^{15}_{7}N}\) (\(n = 8\)), and \(\mathrm{^{16}_{8}O}\) (\(n = 8\)).
  • Isoelectronic Species: Atoms or ions containing the same total number of electrons. E.g., \(\mathrm{N^{3-}}\), \(\mathrm{O^{2-}}\), \(\mathrm{F^-}\), \(\mathrm{Ne}\), \(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{Al^{3+}}\) (each has \(10\) electrons).

ইলেকট্রনের আবিষ্কার ও ক্যাথোড রশ্মি পরীক্ষা

মাইকেল ফ্যারাডে এবং পরবর্তীকালে উইলিয়াম ক্রুকস ও জে.জে. থমসন আংশিক বায়ুশূন্য কাচ নলে (ক্যাথোড রশ্মি ক্ষরণ নল) নিম্নচাপে (\(\sim 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{atm}\)) এবং উচ্চ বিভবপ্রভেদে (\(> 10,000\,\mathrm{V}\)) পরীক্ষা চালান। ক্যাথোড (ঋণাত্মক তড়িৎদ্বার) থেকে অ্যানোডের (ধনাত্মক তড়িৎদ্বার) দিকে অদৃশ্য কণার স্রোত প্রবাহিত হয়, যাকে ক্যাথোড রশ্মি বলে।

  • বাহ্যিক ক্ষেত্রহীন অবস্থায় ক্যাথোড রশ্মি সরলরেখায় চলে এবং এদের গতিপথে অস্বচ্ছ বস্তু রাখলে তীক্ষ্ণ ছায়া তৈরি হয়।
  • জিঙ্ক সালফাইড (\(\mathrm{ZnS}\)) প্রলেপে আঘাত করলে এই রশ্মি সবুজ প্রতিপ্রভা (fluorescence) সৃষ্টি করে।
  • তড়িৎ ও চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্রের প্রভাবে এই রশ্মি ঋণাত্মক আধানযুক্ত কণার মতো বিচ্যুত হয়।
  • মৌলিক সিদ্ধান্ত: ক্যাথোড রশ্মির আধান ও ভরের অনুপাত (\(e/m_e\)) তড়িৎদ্বারের ধাতু বা নলের ভেতরের গ্যাসের প্রকৃতির ওপর নির্ভর করে না। সুতরাং ইলেকট্রন সমস্ত পদার্থের সার্বজনীন উপাদান।

আধান ও ভরের অনুপাত (\(e/m_e\)) ও মিলিকানের তেলবিন্দু পরীক্ষা

\[ \frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{C \ kg^{-1}} \]

১৯০৯ সালে আর.এ. মিলিকান তাঁর তেলবিন্দু পরীক্ষার মাধ্যমে তেলের ফোঁটার ওপর আহিত আধান পরিমাপ করেন এবং প্রমাণ করেন যে আধান কোয়ান্টায়িত (\(q = ne\)):

\[ e = -1.602176 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{C} \] \[ m_e = \frac{e}{e/m_e} = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31} \ \mathrm{kg} \]

প্রোটন ও নিউট্রন আবিষ্কার

১৮৮৬ সালে গোল্ডস্টাইন ছিদ্রযুক্ত ক্যাথোড ব্যবহার করে ধনাত্মক আধানযুক্ত ক্যানাল রশ্মি আবিষ্কার করেন। ক্যাথোড রশ্মির বিপরীতভাবে, ক্যানাল রশ্মির কণার ভর নলের গ্যাসটির প্রকৃতির ওপর নির্ভরশীল। হাইড্রোজেন গ্যাস থেকে প্রাপ্ত ক্ষুদ্রতম ও হালকা ধনাত্মক আয়নকে প্রোটন বলা হয়। ১৯৩২ সালে জেমস চ্যাডউইক বেরিলিয়াম পাতে আলফা (\(\alpha\)) কণা আঘাত করে তড়িৎ-নিরপেক্ষ নিউট্রন আবিষ্কার করেন (\(\mathrm{^{9}_{4}Be + ^{4}_{2}\alpha \to ^{12}_{6}C + ^{1}_{0}n}\))।

3. Quantum Nature of Radiation & Hydrogen Atomic Spectra • বিকিরণের কোয়ান্টাম প্রকৃতি ও হাইড্রোজেন বর্ণালী

3.1 Wave Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation

James Clerk Maxwell (1870) demonstrated that accelerating charged particles generate alternating, mutually perpendicular electric (\(\vec{E}\)) and magnetic (\(\vec{B}\)) fields that propagate through space as electromagnetic waves. Unlike acoustic or seismic waves, electromagnetic radiation requires no material medium and propagates through vacuum at a constant speed:

\[ c = 2.9979 \times 10^8 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}} \approx 3.00 \times 10^8 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}} \] \[ c = \nu \lambda \implies \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} \] \[ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \quad (\text{Wavenumber in } \mathrm{m^{-1}} \text{ or } \mathrm{cm^{-1}}) \]
Figure 3: Mutually perpendicular oscillating electric (\(\vec{E}\)) and magnetic (\(\vec{B}\)) field components of an electromagnetic wave.

The Electromagnetic Spectrum (in order of increasing frequency \(\nu\) and decreasing wavelength \(\lambda\)):

Radio waves (\(\lambda \sim 10^3\,\mathrm{m}\)) < Microwaves (\(\sim 10^{-2}\,\mathrm{m}\)) < Infrared (\(\sim 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{m}\)) < Visible light (\(400\,\mathrm{nm} \text{ [Violet]} - 750\,\mathrm{nm} \text{ [Red]}\)) < Ultraviolet (\(\sim 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}\)) < X-rays (\(\sim 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)) < \(\gamma\)-rays (\(< 10^{-12}\,\mathrm{m}\)).

3.2 Particle Nature of Radiation: Planck's Quantum Theory & Photoelectric Effect

Classical wave theory failed to explain: (1) Black body radiation distribution, (2) The photoelectric effect, (3) Heat capacity variation of solids with temperature, and (4) Atomic line spectra.

Planck's Quantum Theory (1900)

Max Planck proposed that hot bodies do not absorb or emit radiant energy continuously, but rather in discontinuous, discrete energy packets termed quanta (called photons in light). The energy \(E\) of each quantum is proportional to its frequency:

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \] \[ \text{where } h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \mathrm{J \ s} \quad (\text{Planck's Constant}) \] \[ E_{\text{total}} = n h \nu = \frac{n hc}{\lambda} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3 \dots) \]

The Photoelectric Effect (H. Hertz 1887, A. Einstein 1905)

When electromagnetic radiation of sufficiently high frequency strikes a clean metal surface (such as \(\mathrm{K, Rb, Cs}\)) inside an evacuated tube, electrons are instantaneously ejected.

Figure 4: The photoelectric effect apparatus illustrating photon bombardment, work function barrier (\(W_0\)), and kinetic energy transfer.

Core Laws of the Photoelectric Effect:

  1. Absence of Time Lag: Emission occurs instantaneously upon incidence (\(< 10^{-9} \ \mathrm{s}\)).
  2. Threshold Frequency (\(\nu_0\)): Characteristic minimum frequency below which no photoelectrons are emitted, regardless of light intensity. For instance, potassium (\(\nu_0 = 5.0 \times 10^{14} \ \mathrm{Hz}\)) ejects electrons under dim yellow light but remains unresponsive to intense red light.
  3. Kinetic Energy vs. Intensity: \(\mathrm{KE}_{\max}\) depends solely on the frequency of the radiation, not its intensity.
  4. Number of Photoelectrons vs. Intensity: The number of emitted electrons is directly proportional to incident light intensity.
Einstein's Photoelectric Equation: \[ h\nu = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v_{\max}^2 \] \[ \mathrm{KE}_{\max} = h(\nu - \nu_0) = hc \left( \frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda_0} \right) \] \[ \text{Work Function: } W_0 = h\nu_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0} \quad (\lambda_0 = \text{Threshold / Maximum Wavelength}) \] \[ \text{Stopping Potential } (V_0): \quad eV_0 = \mathrm{KE}_{\max} = h(\nu - \nu_0) \]

3.3 Atomic Spectra & The Hydrogen Line Spectrum

When white light passes through a prism, it disperses into a continuous spectrum where colors merge seamlessly into one another. In contrast, volatile elements or heated gases emit radiation at specific, discrete wavelengths with dark intervening regions. This is an atomic emission line spectrum, which serves as a unique fingerprint for element identification (e.g., discovery of \(\mathrm{Rb, Cs, Tl, In, Ga}\), and \(\mathrm{He}\) in the sun).

4. Bohr's Atomic Model, Matter Waves & Heisenberg Uncertainty • বোরের পরমাণু মডেল, পদার্থ-তরঙ্গ ও অনিশ্চয়তা নীতি

4.1 Bohr's Model for Hydrogen and Hydrogen-like Species

In 1913, Niels Bohr introduced a semiclassical model based on Planck's quantization concept to resolve the instability and spectral shortcomings of Rutherford's nuclear model.

Bohr's Fundamental Postulates

  1. Stationary Orbits: The electron revolves around the nucleus in specific non-radiating circular orbits of fixed radius and energy. The energy of an electron in a stationary orbit remains constant with time.
  2. Quantization of Angular Momentum: An electron can only occupy orbits in which its orbital angular momentum (\(L = m_e v r\)) is an integral multiple of \(h/2\pi\): \[ m_e v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3 \dots) \] where \(n\) is the Principal Quantum Number.
  3. Bohr's Frequency Condition: Energy is absorbed or emitted only when an electron jumps from one stationary state to another. The frequency \(\nu\) of the absorbed/emitted photon is: \[ \nu = \frac{\Delta E}{h} = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{h} \] where \(E_1\) and \(E_2\) are the energies of the lower and higher energy states, respectively.

Energy of \(n^{\text{th}}\) Orbit: \[ E_n = -R_{\mathrm{H}} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) \ \mathrm{J \ atom^{-1}} = -13.6 \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) \ \mathrm{eV \ atom^{-1}} \] \[ E_n = -\frac{1312 \ Z^2}{n^2} \ \mathrm{kJ \ mol^{-1}} \] 3. Kinetic & Potential Energy Relationships: \[ \mathrm{KE}_n = -E_n = +13.6 \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) \ \mathrm{eV} \] \[ \mathrm{PE}_n = 2 E_n = -27.2 \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) \ \mathrm{eV} \implies \mathrm{PE} = -2\,\mathrm{KE} \] 4. Physical Meaning of Negative Electronic Energy: The energy of a free electron at rest at an infinite distance from the nucleus is defined as zero (\(E_\infty = 0\)). As the electron approaches the nucleus, attractive electrostatic forces lower its potential energy below zero. The negative sign denotes that the electron is electrostatically bound to the nucleus, requiring positive external work to ionize it.

Limitations of Bohr's Model

  • It is applicable only to single-electron species (\(\mathrm{H, He^+, Li^{2+}, Be^{3+}}\)) and fails for multi-electron atoms (even neutral helium, with two electrons).
  • It cannot account for the spectral fine structure (closely spaced doublets/triplets) resolved by high-power spectrometers.
  • It cannot explain the splitting of spectral lines in external magnetic fields (Zeeman effect) or external electric fields (Stark effect).
  • It fails to explain the directional geometry of chemical bonds and molecular shape.
  • Fundamentally, it assumes fixed orbits with simultaneously known positions and velocities, which directly violates the de Broglie matter-wave hypothesis and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

4.2 Dual Behaviour of Matter (de Broglie Relation)

In 1924, Louis de Broglie reasoned by symmetry: since radiation exhibits both wave and particle attributes, material entities (such as electrons, protons, and atoms) must also exhibit dual wave-particle character. The momentum \(p\) and wavelength \(\lambda\) of a matter particle are related by:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} \] \[ \text{In terms of Kinetic Energy (KE): } p = \sqrt{2m \cdot \mathrm{KE}} \implies \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m \cdot \mathrm{KE}}} \] \[ \text{For a Charged Particle accelerated through Potential Difference } V: \quad \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}} \] \[ \text{For an Electron: } \lambda = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}} \ \text{Å} = \sqrt{\frac{150}{V}} \ \text{Å} = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}} \ \mathrm{nm} \] \[ \text{For a Gas Molecule at Temperature } T: \quad \mathrm{KE} = \frac{3}{2} k_B T \implies \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{3m k_B T}} \]

Macroscopic vs. Microscopic Regimes:

  • For a macroscopic body (e.g., a ball of mass \(0.1 \ \mathrm{kg}\) moving at \(10 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}}\)): \[ \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{(0.1)(10)} = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \mathrm{m} \] This wavelength is \(10^{19}\) times smaller than a nucleus and completely undetectable.
  • For a microscopic electron moving at \(2.19 \times 10^6 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}}\): \[ \lambda = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{(9.11 \times 10^{-31})(2.19 \times 10^6)} = 3.32 \times 10^{-10} \ \mathrm{m} = 3.32 \ \text{Å} \] This lies in the X-ray range, comparable to interatomic lattice spacing in crystals. Experimental electron diffraction (Davisson and Germer, 1927) confirmed this wave nature, enabling the transmission electron microscope with \(\sim 15\) million times magnification.
Derivation of Bohr's Quantization from de Broglie Standing Waves: For a stable circular electronic orbit, the orbital circumference must be an integral multiple of the de Broglie wavelength to avoid destructive interference: \[ 2\pi r = n \lambda = n \left(\frac{h}{mv}\right) \implies mvr = n \frac{h}{2\pi} \] Thus, Bohr's angular momentum quantization condition arises as a natural consequence of constructive matter-wave interference!

4.3 Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle

Werner Heisenberg (1927) established that it is fundamentally impossible to measure simultaneously both the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of a microscopic particle with arbitrary precision.

\[ \Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \] \[ \Delta x \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} \] \[ \text{Energy-Time Conjugate Form: } \Delta E \cdot \Delta t \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \]

Physical Mechanism (The \(\gamma\)-Ray Microscope Thought Experiment):
To locate an electron with small positional uncertainty \(\Delta x\), one must illuminate it with radiation of very short wavelength (\(\lambda \le \Delta x\)). However, short-wavelength photons possess immense momentum (\(p = h/\lambda\)). Upon collision, the photon transfers substantial and unpredictable momentum to the electron (Compton scattering). Thus, the act of precise position localization inherently introduces a severe uncertainty in momentum (\(\Delta p\)).

Demise of the Concept of Fixed Trajectories:
In classical mechanics, the path (trajectory) of a particle is uniquely determined by knowing both its exact position and exact velocity at any instant. Because quantum mechanics precludes simultaneous exact knowledge of both variables for electrons, the classical concept of fixed planetary orbits (as in Bohr's model) is completely invalid. Fixed orbits must be replaced by probability distributions in 3D space.

5. Quantum Mechanical Model, Orbitals & Electronic Configurations • কোয়ান্টাম বলবিদ্যা মডেল, কক্ষক ও ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস

5.1 The Quantum Mechanical Model of the Atom

Classical mechanics, governed by Newton's laws, fails for microscopic particles because it neglects wave-particle duality and the uncertainty principle. In 1926, Erwin Schrödinger established quantum mechanics based on the wave equation of matter.

The Time-Independent Schrödinger Wave Equation: \[ \hat{H}\Psi = E\Psi \] \[ \text{In Cartesian coordinates: } \frac{\partial^2\Psi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2\Psi}{\partial y^2} + \frac{\partial^2\Psi}{\partial z^2} + \frac{8\pi^2 m}{h^2}(E - V)\Psi = 0 \] where \(\hat{H}\) is the Hamiltonian Operator (representing total energy = kinetic + potential energy), \(E\) is the total quantized energy eigenvalue, and \(\Psi\) is the wave function.

Physical Significance of \(\Psi\) and \(|\Psi|^2\):

  • \(\Psi\) (the orbital wave function) has no direct physical meaning; it is a mathematical amplitude function of spatial coordinates \((r, \theta, \phi)\) or \((x, y, z)\).
  • According to Max Born's probability interpretation, the square of the absolute wave function, \(|\Psi|^2\), represents the probability density (probability per unit volume) of finding the electron at a given point in space. It is always real, positive, and non-negative.
  • The probability of locating an electron in an infinitesimal volume element \(dV = dx\,dy\,dz\) is \(dP = |\Psi|^2 dV\).
  • Atomic Orbital: A one-electron wave function \(\Psi\), or physically, the three-dimensional region of space around the nucleus where the probability of finding the electron is maximum (typically defined by a 90% boundary contour).
Bohr Orbit Quantum Mechanical Orbital
A well-defined circular planar trajectory in which an electron is postulated to move. A 3D spatial region around the nucleus where the electron probability density (\(|\Psi|^2\)) is high (~90%).
Planar (2-dimensional) circular path. Three-dimensional volume with specific directional shapes (\(s, p, d, f\)).
Contradicts de Broglie duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle. Fully consistent with wave-particle duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

5.2 The Four Quantum Numbers

An electron in an atom is completely specified by a set of four quantum numbers:

  1. Principal Quantum Number (\(n\)):
    • Permissible values: \(n = 1, 2, 3, 4 \dots\) (corresponds to shells \(K, L, M, N \dots\)).
    • Determines the main energy shell and predominantly governs the size and energy of the orbital.
    • Total subshells in \(n^{\text{th}}\) shell \(= n\).
    • Total orbitals in \(n^{\text{th}}\) shell \(= n^2\).
    • Maximum electron capacity in \(n^{\text{th}}\) shell \(= 2n^2\).
  2. Azimuthal / Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number (\(l\)):
    • Permissible values for a given \(n\): \(l = 0, 1, 2 \dots (n - 1)\) (total \(n\) values).
    • Defines the subshell and the 3D spatial geometry (shape) of the orbital:
      • \(l = 0 \implies \mathbf{s}\) (spherical, sharp)
      • \(l = 1 \implies \mathbf{p}\) (dumbbell, principal)
      • \(l = 2 \implies \mathbf{d}\) (double dumbbell, diffuse)
      • \(l = 3 \implies \mathbf{f}\) (complex 8-lobed, fundamental)
    • Orbital Angular Momentum (\(L\)): \[ L = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi} = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\hbar \] Crucial NEET Point: For any \(s\)-orbital (\(l = 0\)), the orbital angular momentum is zero (\(L = 0\)).
  3. Magnetic Orbital Quantum Number (\(m_l\)):
    • Permissible values for a given \(l\): \(m_l = -l \dots 0 \dots +l\) (total \(2l + 1\) values).
    • Describes the spatial orientation of the orbital in space relative to coordinate axes.
    • For \(l = 0 \implies m_l = 0\) (one \(s\) orbital).
    • For \(l = 1 \implies m_l = -1, 0, +1\) (three \(p\) orbitals: \(p_x, p_y, p_z\)).
    • For \(l = 2 \implies m_l = -2, -1, 0, +1, +2\) (five \(d\) orbitals: \(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{zx}, d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}\)). Conventionally, \(d_{z^2}\) has \(m_l = 0\).
  4. Electron Spin Quantum Number (\(m_s\)):
    • Proposed by Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) to account for spectral line doublets.
    • An electron possesses an intrinsic spin angular momentum: \[ S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\frac{h}{2\pi} \quad \left(s = \frac{1}{2}\right) \]
    • Permissible orientations: \(m_s = +\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\uparrow\), spin-up) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\downarrow\), spin-down).
    • Spin-Only Magnetic Moment (\(\mu_s\)): \[ \mu_s = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \ \mathrm{BM} \quad (\text{Bohr Magnetons}) \] where \(n\) is the number of unpaired electrons (\(1 \ \mathrm{BM} = 9.274 \times 10^{-24} \ \mathrm{J \ T^{-1}}\)).

5.4 Energies of Orbitals, Shielding & The \((n + l)\) Aufbau Rule

Hydrogen vs. Multi-Electron Atoms:

  • In hydrogen and single-electron ions (\(\mathrm{He^+, Li^{2+}}\)), the only electrostatic interaction is nucleus-electron attraction. Hence, orbital energy depends solely on \(n\): \[ 1s < 2s = 2p < 3s = 3p = 3d < 4s = 4p = 4d = 4f \] All subshells within a given principal shell are strictly degenerate (equal in energy).
  • In multi-electron atoms, electron-electron repulsions shield outer electrons from the full nuclear charge (\(Ze\)). \[ Z_{\text{eff}} = Z - \sigma \quad (\sigma = \text{Screening / Shielding Constant}) \] Due to spherical symmetry, \(s\)-electrons penetrate closest to the nucleus and shield more effectively than \(p\)-electrons, which in turn penetrate better than \(d\) and \(f\) electrons. Penetration and shielding power order: \[ s > p > d > f \] Consequently, within a shell in multi-electron atoms, orbital energies split as: \(s < p < d < f\).

The \((n + l)\) Rule (Bohr-Bury Rule)

  1. Between two orbitals, the orbital with the lower \((n + l)\) value has lower energy and is filled first.
  2. If two orbitals have the same \((n + l)\) value, the orbital with the lower \(n\) value has lower energy.

5.5 Rules for Filling Orbitals in Atoms

  1. Aufbau Principle: Electrons sequentially occupy orbitals of lowest available energy in the ground state before filling higher levels.
  2. Pauli Exclusion Principle (1926): "No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers." Consequently, an individual orbital can hold a maximum of two electrons, and these electrons must have opposite (antiparallel) spins (\(m_s = +1/2\) and \(-1/2\)).
  3. Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: "Pairing of electrons in degenerate orbitals (\(p, d, f\)) cannot occur until each orbital in that subshell is singly occupied with parallel spins."

5.6 Exceptional Stability of Half-Filled and Fully Filled Subshells

Subshells that are exactly half-filled (such as \(p^3, d^5, f^7\)) or completely filled (\(p^6, d^{10}, f^{14}\)) possess extraordinary stability attributable to two physical factors:

  1. Symmetrical Distribution of Electron Density: A completely filled or half-filled subshell has an isotropic (or highly symmetrical) spherical electron cloud. Highly symmetrical distributions minimize interelectronic repulsion and maximize nuclear attraction.
  2. Exchange Energy: When two or more electrons with parallel spins occupy degenerate orbitals of a subshell, they exchange their positions. Each exchange releases a quantum of stabilization energy known as exchange energy. Maximum exchanges occur when subshells are half-filled or completely filled.

Mathematical Calculation of Possible Exchanges: \[ K = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2} \] where \(n\) is the number of electrons with parallel spin.
  • For Chromium (\(\mathrm{Cr}, Z = 24\)): Expected \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^4 4s^2 \implies\) For \(3d^4\), \(K = \frac{4 \times 3}{2} = 6\) exchanges. Actual configuration is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1 \implies\) For \(3d^5\), \(K = \frac{5 \times 4}{2} = 10\) exchanges. The gain of \(4\) extra exchanges releases massive exchange energy that far exceeds the slight promotion energy of shifting an electron from \(4s\) to \(3d\).
  • For Copper (\(\mathrm{Cu}, Z = 29\)): Expected \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^9 4s^2\). Actual is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10} 4s^1\), attaining a fully filled, spherically symmetric \(d^{10}\) subshell with \(2 \times \frac{5 \times 4}{2} = 20\) total exchanges.

৫.২ চারটি কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যা

  1. মুখ্য কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যা (\(n\)): অনুমোদিত মান \(n = 1, 2, 3 \dots\) (শক্তিস্তর \(K, L, M, N \dots\))। এটি কক্ষকের আকার ও শক্তি নির্ধারণ করে। মোট কক্ষক সংখ্যা \(= n^2\) এবং মোট ইলেকট্রন ধারণক্ষমতা \(= 2n^2\)।
  2. গৌণ / দিগংশীয় কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যা (\(l\)): অনুমোদিত মান \(l = 0, 1, 2 \dots (n - 1)\)। এটি উপকক্ষ ও কক্ষকের ত্রিমাত্রিক আকৃতি নির্ধারণ করে (\(l=0 \to s\) গোলকাকার; \(l=1 \to p\) ডাম্বেল; \(l=2 \to d\) ডাবল ডাম্বেল; \(l=3 \to f\) জটিল)। কক্ষীয় কৌণিক ভরবেগ \(L = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi}\)। যেকোনো \(s\)-কক্ষকের জন্য \(L = 0\)।
  3. চৌম্বকীয় কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যা (\(m_l\)): মান \(-l \dots 0 \dots +l\) (মোট \(2l + 1\) টি মান)। এটি বিভিন্ন অক্ষ বরাবর কক্ষকের স্থানিক বিন্যাস নির্দেশ করে।
  4. ঘূর্ণন কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যা (\(m_s\)): মান \(+\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\uparrow\)) অথবা \(-\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\downarrow\))। এটি ইলেকট্রনের নিজস্ব অক্ষের ঘূর্ণন নির্দেশ করে। ঘূর্ণন চৌম্বক ভ্রামক \(\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n+2)} \ \mathrm{BM}\)।

6.2 Step-by-Step Worked Conceptual & Numerical Examples

Worked Example 1: Photoelectric Work Function & Stopping Potential

Problem: When radiation of wavelength \(300 \ \mathrm{nm}\) shines on a sodium metal surface, photoelectrons are ejected with a maximum kinetic energy of \(1.68 \times 10^5 \ \mathrm{J \ mol^{-1}}\). Calculate (a) the work function of sodium in \(\mathrm{eV}\), and (b) the stopping potential required to arrest the photoelectrons.

Solution:

Step 1: Calculate energy of incident photon for one electron.
\[ E_{\text{photon}} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{(6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \mathrm{J \ s})(3.00 \times 10^8 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}})}{300 \times 10^{-9} \ \mathrm{m}} = 6.626 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J} \] In electron-volts: \[ E_{\text{photon}} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J \ eV^{-1}}} = 4.136 \ \mathrm{eV} \]

Step 2: Convert maximum kinetic energy to per-electron and per-eV basis.
\[ \mathrm{KE}_{\max} (\text{per electron}) = \frac{1.68 \times 10^5 \ \mathrm{J \ mol^{-1}}}{6.022 \times 10^{23} \ \mathrm{mol^{-1}}} = 2.79 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J} \] \[ \mathrm{KE}_{\max} (\mathrm{eV}) = \frac{2.79 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19} \ \mathrm{J \ eV^{-1}}} = 1.741 \ \mathrm{eV} \]

Step 3: Calculate Work Function (\(W_0\)).
From Einstein's photoelectric equation: \[ W_0 = E_{\text{photon}} - \mathrm{KE}_{\max} = 4.136 \ \mathrm{eV} - 1.741 \ \mathrm{eV} = 2.395 \ \mathrm{eV} \approx 2.40 \ \mathrm{eV} \]

Step 4: Determine Stopping Potential (\(V_0\)).
Since \(e V_0 = \mathrm{KE}_{\max}\): \[ V_0 = \frac{\mathrm{KE}_{\max}}{e} = 1.741 \ \mathrm{V} \]

Worked Example 2: Equivalent Spectral Transition in Hydrogen for He⁺

Problem: What transition in the hydrogen atom spectrum would emit radiation of the exact same wavelength as the Balmer transition \(n = 4 \to n = 2\) in the \(\mathrm{He^+}\) spectrum?

Solution:

For a single-electron species, the Rydberg formula is: \[ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_{\mathrm{H}} Z^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) \] For \(\mathrm{He^+}\) (\(Z = 2\)), transitioning from \(n_2 = 4\) to \(n_1 = 2\): \[ \bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{He^+}} = R_{\mathrm{H}} (2)^2 \left( \frac{1}{2^2} - \frac{1}{4^2} \right) = R_{\mathrm{H}} \cdot 4 \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{16} \right) = R_{\mathrm{H}} \cdot 4 \left( \frac{3}{16} \right) = R_{\mathrm{H}} \left( \frac{3}{4} \right) \] For Hydrogen (\(Z = 1\)), we require: \[ \bar{\nu}_{\mathrm{H}} = R_{\mathrm{H}} (1)^2 \left( \frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2} \right) = R_{\mathrm{H}} \left( \frac{3}{4} \right) \] Notice that: \[ \frac{1}{1^2} - \frac{1}{2^2} = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4} \] Therefore: \[ n_1 = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad n_2 = 2 \] Conclusion: The Lyman transition \(n = 2 \to n = 1\) in the hydrogen spectrum has the identical wavelength as the \(n = 4 \to n = 2\) transition in \(\mathrm{He^+}\).

Worked Example 3: Heisenberg Uncertainty with Percentage Precision

Problem: An electron is moving with a velocity of \(600 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}}\) measured with an accuracy of \(0.005\%\). Calculate the minimum uncertainty in determining its position. (\(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \mathrm{J \ s}, m_e = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \ \mathrm{kg}\)).

Solution:

Step 1: Calculate uncertainty in velocity (\(\Delta v\)).
\[ \Delta v = 600 \times \frac{0.005}{100} = 600 \times 5 \times 10^{-5} = 0.03 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}} \]

Step 2: Apply Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.
\[ \Delta x \cdot m \Delta v \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \implies \Delta x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m \Delta v} \] \[ \Delta x \ge \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \ \mathrm{J \ s}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times (9.11 \times 10^{-31} \ \mathrm{kg}) \times (0.03 \ \mathrm{m \ s^{-1}})} \] \[ \Delta x \ge \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{3.434 \times 10^{-31}} = 1.93 \times 10^{-3} \ \mathrm{m} = 1.93 \ \mathrm{mm} \] Deduction: The positional uncertainty (\(1.93 \ \mathrm{mm}\)) is macroscopically large—nearly \(10^7\) times the diameter of the atom itself! This highlights why classical orbits are meaningless for electrons.

The correct order of increasing energy of the atomic orbitals \(4d, 5p, 5f,\) and \(6p\) is:

(A) \(4d < 5p < 6p < 5f\)
(B) \(5p < 4d < 6p < 5f\)
(C) \(4d < 5p < 5f < 6p\)
(D) \(5p < 4d < 5f < 6p\)
Correct Answer: (A) \(4d < 5p < 6p < 5f\)
Step-by-Step Explanation:
1. Calculate the \((n + l)\) value for each orbital:
- \(4d\): \(n = 4, l = 2 \implies n + l = 4 + 2 = 6\)
- \(5p\): \(n = 5, l = 1 \implies n + l = 5 + 1 = 6\)
- \(6p\): \(n = 6, l = 1 \implies n + l = 6 + 1 = 7\)
- \(5f\): \(n = 5, l = 3 \implies n + l = 5 + 3 = 8\)
2. Comparing \(4d\) and \(5p\): Both have \((n + l) = 6\). By the Bohr-Bury rule, the orbital with the lower \(n\) value has lower energy. Hence, \(4d < 5p\).
3. For \(6p\), \((n + l) = 7\), which is higher than \(6\) but lower than \(8\).
4. For \(5f\), \((n + l) = 8\), which is the highest energy among the four.
5. Combining these yields the unambiguous order: \[ 4d < 5p < 6p < 5f \]

8. Common Pitfalls & High-Yield Revision Checklist • সাধারণ বিভ্রান্তি ও রিভিশন চেকলিস্ট

8.1 Frequent Exam Traps & Conceptual Pitfalls in NEET UG

  • Orbital Angular Momentum vs. Bohr Angular Momentum: In Bohr's theory, the ground state electron has angular momentum \(L = \frac{1 \cdot h}{2\pi}\). In modern quantum mechanics, the orbital angular momentum is given by \(L = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi}\). For any \(s\)-orbital (\(1s, 2s, 3s\dots\)), \(l = 0\), which means its orbital angular momentum is identically zero!
  • Degeneracy in Single-Electron vs. Multi-Electron Systems: In the hydrogen atom (or \(\mathrm{He^+, Li^{2+}}\)), subshell energies depend solely on \(n\): \(3s = 3p = 3d\). The \((n + l)\) rule applies only to multi-electron atoms where interelectronic repulsion and shielding split subshell energies.
  • Electron Filling Order vs. Ionization Loss Order: According to the Aufbau principle, \(4s\) fills before \(3d\) because \((n+l)_{4s} = 4 < (n+l)_{3d} = 5\). However, upon ionization of transition elements, electrons are always removed first from the outermost shell (highest \(n\))! \[ \mathrm{Fe}: [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6 4s^2 \xrightarrow{-2e^-} \mathrm{Fe^{2+}}: [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6 \quad (\text{NOT } [\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^4 4s^2) \]
  • Threshold Wavelength vs. Threshold Frequency: Since \(\nu = c/\lambda\), threshold frequency (\(\nu_0\)) is the minimum frequency required for photoemission, but threshold wavelength (\(\lambda_0 = c/\nu_0\)) is the maximum (longest) wavelength that can cause photoemission. Radiation with \(\lambda > \lambda_0\) will never eject electrons.
  • Intensity vs. Kinetic Energy in Photoelectric Effect: Increasing light intensity increases the number of photons per second, hence increasing the number of ejected electrons and the photocurrent. It has zero effect on the maximum kinetic energy (\(\mathrm{KE}_{\max}\)) or stopping potential (\(V_0\)), which depend solely on photon frequency \(\nu\).
  • Radial vs. Angular vs. Total Nodes: Always verify the distinction: \[ N_{\text{radial}} = n - l - 1, \qquad N_{\text{angular}} = l, \qquad N_{\text{total}} = n - 1 \] A \(3p\) orbital has \(3 - 1 - 1 = 1\) radial node and \(l = 1\) angular node (total 2 nodes).

9. Concise Final Recap • চূড়ান্ত সংক্ষেপ

The progression from Dalton's solid billiard-ball atom through Thomson's plum pudding and Rutherford's planetary model culminated in Bohr's quantized angular momentum orbits. The experimental reality of wave-particle duality (de Broglie) and the Heisenberg uncertainty principle demonstrated that electrons do not follow deterministic circular tracks, necessitating Schrödinger's wave mechanics. Today, electrons in atoms are described by atomic orbitals characterized by four quantum numbers (\(n, l, m_l, m_s\)), with ground-state electron configurations dictated by the Aufbau principle, Pauli's exclusion principle, and Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity.
