2. Subatomic Particles & Early Atomic Models | অতিপারমাণবিক কণা ও প্রাথমিক পরমাণু মডেল

English

2.1 Fundamental Particles

Gas-discharge experiments at low pressure and high voltage revealed that atoms are divisible and contain three primary subatomic particles:

  • Electron (\(e^-\)): Discovered via cathode rays moving from cathode to anode. Its charge-to-mass ratio (\(e/m_e = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}\)) was measured by J.J. Thomson using perpendicular electric and magnetic fields, and is independent of the gas or electrode material. R.A. Millikan's oil-drop experiment showed electric charge is quantized (\(q = ne\)) and determined \(e = -1.602176 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\), yielding \(m_e = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\).
  • Proton (\(p^+\)): Discovered from positively charged canal (anode) rays, whose \(e/m\) depends on the nature of the gas inside the tube. Hydrogen yielded the lightest positive ion, the proton.
  • Neutron (\(n^0\)): Discovered by James Chadwick (1932) by bombarding a thin beryllium sheet with \(\alpha\)-particles, releasing neutral particles slightly heavier than protons.

2.2 Atomic Number, Mass Number & Nuclide Relations

An atom is represented as \(^A_Z\mathrm{X}\), where atomic number \(Z\) equals the number of protons (and electrons in a neutral atom), and mass number \(A = Z + N\) equals the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons).

  • Isotopes: Atoms with the same \(Z\) but different \(A\) (different neutron count), e.g., \(^1_1\mathrm{H}\) (protium), \(^2_1\mathrm{H}\) (deuterium), \(^3_1\mathrm{H}\) (tritium); \(^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\) and \(^{37}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\). Isotopes share identical chemical properties because electron count is unchanged.
  • Isobars: Atoms with the same \(A\) but different \(Z\), e.g., \(^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}\) and \(^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}\).
  • Isoelectronic species: Atoms or ions containing the same total number of electrons, e.g., \(\mathrm{N}^{3-}, \mathrm{O}^{2-}, \mathrm{F}^-, \mathrm{Ne}, \mathrm{Na}^+, \mathrm{Mg}^{2+}\) (all have 10 electrons).

2.3 Thomson & Rutherford Models

Thomson's Model (1898): Visualized the atom as a uniform sphere of positive charge (radius \(\sim 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)) with electrons embedded inside like seeds in a watermelon. It explained electrical neutrality but failed to explain \(\alpha\)-scattering results.

Rutherford's \(\alpha\)-Scattering Experiment (1909): High-energy \(\alpha\)-particles (\(\mathrm{He}^{2+}\) nuclei) were directed at a thin gold foil (\(\sim 100\,\mathrm{nm}\)):

  • Most \(\alpha\)-particles passed straight through undeflected \(\implies\) most of the atom is empty space.
  • A small fraction deflected through small angles, and very few (\(\sim 1\) in \(20,000\)) bounced back by \(\sim 180^\circ\) \(\implies\) all positive charge and almost all mass are concentrated in a tiny central nucleus (radius \(\sim 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\) vs atomic radius \(\sim 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)).

Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model: According to Maxwell's classical electrodynamics, an accelerating charged electron revolving in a circular orbit must continuously emit electromagnetic radiation, lose energy, and spiral into the nucleus within \(\sim 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}\) (or \(\sim 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{s}\)). Thus, Rutherford's model could not explain atomic stability or discrete line spectra.

২.২ পারমাণবিক সংখ্যা, ভর সংখ্যা ও নিউক্লাইডের সম্পর্ক

যেকোনো পরমাণুকে \(^A_Z\mathrm{X}\) রূপে প্রকাশ করা হয়, যেখানে পারমাণবিক সংখ্যা \(Z\) হলো প্রোটন সংখ্যা (নিস্তড়িৎ পরমাণুতে ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যার সমান) এবং ভর সংখ্যা \(A = Z + N\) হলো মোট নিউক্লিয়ন (প্রোটন + নিউট্রন) সংখ্যা।

  • আইসোটোপ (সমস্থানিক): একই \(Z\) কিন্তু ভিন্ন \(A\) (ভিন্ন নিউট্রন সংখ্যা) বিশিষ্ট পরমাণু, যেমন \(^1_1\mathrm{H}\) (প্রোটিয়াম), \(^2_1\mathrm{H}\) (ডয়টেরিয়াম), \(^3_1\mathrm{H}\) (ট্রিটিয়াম); \(^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\) ও \(^{37}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\)। ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা সমান হওয়ায় এদের রাসায়নিক ধর্ম অভিন্ন।
  • আইসোবার (সমভর): একই \(A\) কিন্তু ভিন্ন \(Z\) বিশিষ্ট পরমাণু, যেমন \(^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}\) এবং \(^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}\)।

3. Electromagnetic Radiation, Planck's Quantum Theory & Photoelectric Effect | তড়িৎচুম্বকীয় বিকিরণ, প্ল্যাঙ্কের কোয়ান্টাম তত্ত্ব ও আলোকতড়িৎ ক্রিয়া

English

3.1 Wave Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation

Accelerated charges generate oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of wave propagation. All electromagnetic waves travel in vacuum at \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) without requiring a medium.

\[ c = \nu \lambda \qquad \text{and} \qquad \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \]

Here \(\nu\) is frequency (\(\mathrm{Hz}\) or \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)), \(\lambda\) is wavelength (\(\mathrm{m}\), \(\mathrm{nm} = 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}\), \(1\,\mathrm{Å} = 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)), and \(\bar{\nu}\) is wavenumber (commonly \(\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) or \(\mathrm{m^{-1}}\)).

Visible light spans roughly \(400\,\mathrm{nm}\) (violet, \(7.5 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}\)) to \(750\,\mathrm{nm}\) (red, \(4.0 \times 10^{14}\,\mathrm{Hz}\)).

3.2 Blackbody Radiation & Planck's Quantum Theory

Classical wave theory failed to explain blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect, and atomic line spectra. Max Planck (1900) proposed that atoms emit or absorb electromagnetic energy in discrete packets called quanta (or photons for light):

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = hc\bar{\nu}, \qquad h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s} \]

For quick NEET calculations in \(\mathrm{eV}\): \(hc \approx 1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm} = 12400\,\mathrm{eV\cdot Å}\).

3.3 Photoelectric Effect (Einstein, 1905)

When light of frequency \(\nu\) strikes a clean metal surface in vacuum, electrons are ejected instantaneously only if \(\nu \ge \nu_0\) (threshold frequency):

  • No time lag: Ejection occurs immediately upon photon collision.
  • Intensity effect: Higher light intensity means more photons per second, increasing the number of photoelectrons (photocurrent), not their maximum kinetic energy.
  • Frequency effect: Maximum kinetic energy increases linearly with \(\nu\) above \(\nu_0\).
\[ h\nu = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v_{\max}^2 = h\nu_0 + eV_s \]

where \(W_0 = h\nu_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0}\) is the work function and \(V_s\) is the stopping potential.

  • তীব্রতার প্রভাব: আলোর তীব্রতা বাড়ালে প্রতি সেকেন্ডে ফোটন সংখ্যা বাড়ে, ফলে নির্গত ইলেকট্রনের সংখ্যা (আলোকতড়িৎ প্রবাহ) বাড়ে, কিন্তু সর্বোচ্চ গতিশক্তি বাড়ে না।

4.2 Bohr's Postulates & Quantitative Results

  • Electrons revolve only in specific circular stationary states (\(n = 1, 2, 3, \dots\)) without radiating energy.
  • Angular momentum is quantized: \(m_e v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi}\).
  • Radiation is absorbed or emitted only during a transition between states: \(\Delta E = E_{\text{high}} - E_{\text{low}} = h\nu\).

For hydrogen and hydrogen-like one-electron species (\(\mathrm{He}^+, \mathrm{Li}^{2+}, \mathrm{Be}^{3+}\)) with atomic number \(Z\):

\[ r_n = \frac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{pm} = \frac{0.529\,n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å} \] \[ E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right)\,\mathrm{J\,atom^{-1}} = -13.6 \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right)\,\mathrm{eV\,atom^{-1}} \] \[ v_n \propto \frac{Z}{n} \quad \left(v_1 \text{ for H} \approx 2.18 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\right) \]

Meaning of Negative Energy: A free electron infinitely far from the nucleus (\(n = \infty\)) is assigned \(E = 0\). Bound electrons have lower (negative) energy; \(n = 1\) is the most negative and most stable ground state.

Limitations of Bohr's Model: Fails for multi-electron atoms (even \(\mathrm{He}\)), cannot explain fine-structure doublets, magnetic/electric field splitting (Zeeman/Stark effects), chemical bonding, or why it contradicts de Broglie duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

5. Matter Waves & Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle | পদার্থের তরঙ্গ ধর্ম ও হাইজেনবার্গের অনিশ্চয়তা নীতি

English

5.1 de Broglie Relation (1924)

Just as light has dual wave-particle character, material particles in motion exhibit wave properties (confirmed by electron diffraction). The de Broglie wavelength is:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m\,\mathrm{KE}}} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}} \]

For macroscopic objects, \(m\) is large so \(\lambda\) is negligibly small; for electrons and subatomic particles, \(\lambda\) is measurable. Bohr's angular momentum rule \(2\pi r = n\lambda\) naturally follows from standing matter waves around the nucleus.

5.2 Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (1927)

It is impossible to simultaneously determine the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of a microscopic particle:

\[ \Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \qquad \text{or} \qquad \Delta x \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} \]

Significance: A well-defined trajectory requires knowing both position and velocity simultaneously. Because this is impossible for an electron, classical Bohr orbits have no physical reality and must be replaced by probability.

6.1 Schrödinger Equation & Probability Density

The time-independent Schrödinger equation is \(\hat{H}\psi = E\psi\), where \(\hat{H}\) is the Hamiltonian operator, \(E\) is the quantized energy, and \(\psi\) is the wave function (atomic orbital). While \(\psi\) itself has no physical meaning, \(|\psi|^2\) gives the probability density of finding the electron at a point.

6.2 The Four Quantum Numbers

Quantum Number Symbol Permitted Values Physical Meaning
Principal \(n\) \(1, 2, 3, \dots\) Shell (K, L, M, N...); orbital size & main energy; \(n^2\) orbitals per shell, max \(2n^2\) electrons
Azimuthal \(l\) \(0, 1, \dots, (n-1)\) Subshell (\(s, p, d, f\)); orbital shape; orbital angular momentum \(= \sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi}\)
Magnetic \(m_l\) \(-l, \dots, 0, \dots, +l\) Spatial orientation; \((2l+1)\) orbitals per subshell
Spin \(m_s\) \(+\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\uparrow\)) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\) (\(\downarrow\)) Intrinsic spin orientation; max 2 electrons per orbital with opposite spins

6.3 Orbital Shapes & Nodes

  • \(s\)-orbitals (\(l=0\)): Spherically symmetric; size increases as \(4s > 3s > 2s > 1s\).

  • \(d\)-orbitals (\(l=2\)): Five degenerate orbitals (\(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}, d_{x^2-y^2}\) cloverleaf-shaped; \(d_{z^2}\) dumbbell with a central ring/torus).

7. Orbital Energies & Electronic Configuration Rules | অরবিটালের শক্তি ও ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাসের নিয়ম

English

7.1 Hydrogen vs Multi-Electron Orbital Energies

  • Hydrogen & 1-electron ions: Energy depends only on \(n\): \(1s < 2s = 2p < 3s = 3p = 3d < \dots\) (orbitals with the same \(n\) are degenerate).
  • Multi-electron atoms: Electron-electron repulsion and inner-shell shielding reduce the nuclear pull to an effective nuclear charge (\(Z_{\text{eff}}\)). Penetration power follows \(s > p > d > f\), so orbital energy depends on both \(n\) and \(l\), governed by the \((n + l)\) rule: lower \((n+l)\) has lower energy; if \((n+l)\) is equal, lower \(n\) has lower energy (e.g., \(4s\) with \(4+0=4\) fills before \(3d\) with \(3+2=5\)).

7.2 Three Governing Principles

  • Aufbau Principle: Electrons fill orbitals in increasing order of energy: \(1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p, 7s\dots\)
  • Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in the same atom can have the exact same set of four quantum numbers; an orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins.
  • Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Pairing in degenerate orbitals (\(p, d, f\)) begins only after every orbital in that subshell is singly occupied with parallel spins.

7.3 Exceptions: Half-Filled & Completely Filled Stability

Elements like Chromium (\(Z=24\): \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5\,4s^1\)) and Copper (\(Z=29\): \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10}\,4s^1\)) shift an electron from \(4s\) to \(3d\) because half-filled (\(d^5\)) and completely filled (\(d^{10}\)) subshells gain extra stability from symmetrical electron distribution (reduced shielding) and maximum exchange energy (number of parallel-spin pair exchanges \(= \frac{k(k-1)}{2}\), which is 10 for \(d^5\)).

Worked Example 1: Subatomic Composition of an Ion

Assumption: Consider a hypothetical divalent cation \(M^{2+}\) containing \(25\) electrons and \(30\) neutrons. Determine its atomic number, mass number, and complete nuclide symbol.

Solution:

  • Since \(M^{2+}\) has lost \(2\) electrons, protons in the neutral atom \(Z = 25 + 2 = 27\) (Cobalt, \(\mathrm{Co}\)).
  • Mass number \(A = Z + N = 27 + 30 = 57\).
  • Complete symbol: \(^{57}_{27}\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\).
উদাহরণ ১: আয়নের অতিপারমাণবিক গঠন

অনুমান: ধরা যাক একটি কাল্পনিক দ্বিযোজী ক্যাটায়ন \(M^{2+}\)-এ \(25\)টি ইলেকট্রন ও \(30\)টি নিউট্রন আছে। এর পারমাণবিক সংখ্যা, ভর সংখ্যা ও সম্পূর্ণ প্রতীক নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

  • যেহেতু \(M^{2+}\) দুটি ইলেকট্রন ত্যাগ করেছে, তাই প্রোটন সংখ্যা \(Z = 25 + 2 = 27\) (কোবাল্ট, \(\mathrm{Co}\))।
  • ভর সংখ্যা \(A = Z + N = 27 + 30 = 57\)।
  • সম্পূর্ণ প্রতীক: \(^{57}_{27}\mathrm{Co}^{2+}\)।
Worked Example 2: Photoelectric Kinetic Energy

Assumption: A hypothetical metal surface has a work function \(W_0 = 2.50\,\mathrm{eV}\). It is irradiated with monochromatic ultraviolet light of wavelength \(\lambda = 310\,\mathrm{nm}\). Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected photoelectrons (use \(hc = 1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}\)).

Solution:

Energy of each incident photon: \(E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}}{310\,\mathrm{nm}} = 4.00\,\mathrm{eV}\).

By Einstein's photoelectric equation: \(\mathrm{KE}_{\max} = E - W_0 = 4.00\,\mathrm{eV} - 2.50\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.50\,\mathrm{eV}\).

উদাহরণ ২: আলোকতড়িৎ গতিশক্তি নির্ণয়

অনুমান: একটি কাল্পনিক ধাতব পৃষ্ঠের কার্যাপেক্ষক \(W_0 = 2.50\,\mathrm{eV}\)। এর ওপর \(\lambda = 310\,\mathrm{nm}\) তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্যের আলো আপতিত হলে নির্গত ইলেকট্রনের সর্বোচ্চ গতিশক্তি কত হবে? (\(hc = 1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}\) ধরো)।

সমাধান:

আপতিত ফোটনের শক্তি: \(E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}}{310\,\mathrm{nm}} = 4.00\,\mathrm{eV}\)।

আইনস্টাইনের সমীকরণ অনুযায়ী: \(\mathrm{KE}_{\max} = E - W_0 = 4.00\,\mathrm{eV} - 2.50\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.50\,\mathrm{eV}\)।

Worked Example 3: Bohr Radius & Energy for Hydrogen-Like Ions

Calculate (a) the radius of the \(3\text{rd}\) Bohr orbit of \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z = 3\)), and (b) the energy of an electron in the \(2\text{nd}\) orbit of \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z = 2\)).

Solution:

(a) \(r_n = \frac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{pm} \implies r_3(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}) = \frac{52.9 \times 3^2}{3} = 52.9 \times 3 = 158.7\,\mathrm{pm}\).

(b) \(E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right)\,\mathrm{J} \implies E_2(\mathrm{He}^+) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{2^2}{2^2}\right) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{J}\) (or \(-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}\)).

উদাহরণ ৩: হাইড্রোজেন-সদৃশ আয়নের বোর ব্যাসার্ধ ও শক্তি

(ক) \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z = 3\))-এর ৩য় বোর কক্ষপথের ব্যাসার্ধ এবং (খ) \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z = 2\))-এর ২য় কক্ষপথে ইলেকট্রনের শক্তি নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

(ক) \(r_n = \frac{52.9\,n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{pm} \implies r_3(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}) = \frac{52.9 \times 3^2}{3} = 158.7\,\mathrm{pm}\)।

(খ) \(E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right)\,\mathrm{J} \implies E_2(\mathrm{He}^+) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{2^2}{2^2}\right) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{J}\) (অথবা \(-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}\))।

Worked Example 4: Nodes & Capacity of a \(4d\) Subshell

For an electron in a \(4d\) orbital, find the number of radial nodes, angular nodes, total nodes, and the maximum number of electrons the \(4d\) subshell can accommodate.

Solution:

For \(4d\): \(n = 4\) and \(l = 2\).

  • Radial nodes \(= n - l - 1 = 4 - 2 - 1 = 1\).
  • Angular nodes \(= l = 2\).
  • Total nodes \(= n - 1 = 3\).
  • Number of \(4d\) orbitals \(= 2l + 1 = 5\), so maximum electrons \(= 5 \times 2 = 10\).
উদাহরণ ৪: \(4d\) উপকক্ষের নোড ও ইলেকট্রন ধারণক্ষমতা

একটি \(4d\) অরবিটালের রেডিয়াল নোড, কৌণিক নোড, মোট নোড এবং \(4d\) উপকক্ষের সর্বাধিক ইলেকট্রন ধারণক্ষমতা নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

\(4d\)-এর জন্য \(n = 4\) এবং \(l = 2\)।

  • রেডিয়াল নোড \(= n - l - 1 = 4 - 2 - 1 = 1\)।
  • কৌণিক নোড \(= l = 2\)।
  • মোট নোড \(= n - 1 = 3\)।
  • \(4d\) উপকক্ষে অরবিটাল সংখ্যা \(= 2l + 1 = 5\), তাই সর্বাধিক ইলেকট্রন \(= 5 \times 2 = 10\)।

Practice Question 1 (Single-Correct MCQ)

If the radius of an atom is approximately \(10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\) and that of its nucleus is \(10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\), what is the approximate ratio of the volume of the atom to the volume of the nucleus?

  • (A) \(10^{5}\)
  • (B) \(10^{10}\)
  • (C) \(10^{15}\)
  • (D) \(10^{-15}\)
Answer: (C) \(10^{15}\)
Solution: Assuming spherical geometry, \(V \propto r^3\). Therefore, \(\frac{V_{\text{atom}}}{V_{\text{nucleus}}} = \left(\frac{r_{\text{atom}}}{r_{\text{nucleus}}}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{10^{-10}}{10^{-15}}\right)^3 = (10^5)^3 = 10^{15}\).

একটি পরমাণুর ব্যাসার্ধ প্রায় \(10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\) এবং এর নিউক্লিয়াসের ব্যাসার্ধ প্রায় \(10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\) হলে, পরমাণুর আয়তন ও নিউক্লিয়াসের আয়তনের অনুপাত কত?

  • (A) \(10^{5}\)

  • (C) \(10^{15}\)
  • (D) \(10^{-15}\)
উত্তর: (C) \(10^{15}\)

Practice Question 2 (Single-Correct MCQ)

A hypothetical \(60\,\mathrm{W}\) monochromatic light source emits photons of wavelength \(662.6\,\mathrm{nm}\). How many photons are emitted per second? (\(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\), \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\))

  • (A) \(1.0 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
  • (B) \(2.0 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
  • (C) \(3.0 \times 10^{19}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
  • (D) \(6.0 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
Answer: (B) \(2.0 \times 10^{20}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)
Solution: Energy per photon \(E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8}{662.6 \times 10^{-9}} = 3.0 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}\). Common NEET UG Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Forgetting \(Z^2\) or \(Z\) for H-like ions: Always multiply \(E_n\) by \(Z^2\) and divide \(r_n\) by \(Z\) for \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z=2\)) or \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z=3\)).

Final Recap: Atoms consist of a dense positive nucleus surrounded by quantized electron waves described by orbitals (\(n, l, m_l, m_s\)). Electromagnetic energy is quantized (\(E = h\nu\)), matter has wavelength \(\lambda = h/mv\), position and momentum obey \(\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge h/4\pi\), and ground-state configurations follow \((n+l)\), Pauli, and Hund's rules with half/full-subshell stability exceptions.

  • রেডিয়াল ও মোট নোডের বিভ্রান্তি: রেডিয়াল নোড \(= n - l - 1\), কিন্তু মোট নোড \(= n - 1\)।
  • সন্ধিগত মৌল থেকে ইলেকট্রন অপসারণ: \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\) বা \(\mathrm{Cu}^+\) আয়ন গঠনের সময় \(3d\)-এর আগে সর্ববহিস্থ \(4s\) থেকে ইলেকট্রন সরাতে হয়।

সারসংক্ষেপ: পরমাণুর কেন্দ্রে ঘন ধনাত্মক নিউক্লিয়াস এবং চারপাশে কোয়ান্টায়িত ইলেকট্রন তরঙ্গ (অরবিটাল: \(n, l, m_l, m_s\)) অবস্থিত। শক্তি কোয়ান্টায়িত (\(E = h\nu\)), পদার্থের তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্য \(\lambda = h/mv\), অবস্থান-ভরবেগ অনিশ্চয়তা \(\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge h/4\pi\) এবং ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস \((n+l)\), পাউলি ও হুন্ডের নিয়ম (অর্ধপূর্ণ ও পূর্ণ উপকক্ষের ব্যতিক্রমসহ) মেনে চলে।
