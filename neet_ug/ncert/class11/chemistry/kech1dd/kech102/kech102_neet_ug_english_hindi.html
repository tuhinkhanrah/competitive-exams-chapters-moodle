2. Discovery of Subatomic Particles & Classical Models | उप-परमाण्विक कणों की खोज एवं पारंपरिक मॉडल

English

Cathode Rays and the Electron

In mid-1850s, Michael Faraday and subsequent physicists investigated electrical discharge in partially evacuated glass tubes (cathode ray tubes) at very low pressures (\(10^{-2}\) to \(10^{-4}\,\mathrm{atm}\)) and high potentials (\(>10,000\,\mathrm{V}\)). A stream of negatively charged particles—cathode rays—traveled from the negative electrode (cathode) toward the positive electrode (anode).

  • They travel in straight lines in the absence of external electric and magnetic fields.
  • In electric and magnetic fields, they deflect in the direction expected for negatively charged particles.
  • Their charge-to-mass ratio (\(e/m_e\)) is completely independent of the nature of the gas inside the tube and the electrode material, proving electrons are universal constituents of all matter.

J.J. Thomson's Charge-to-Mass Ratio (1897):

\[ \frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11} \ \mathrm{C\ kg^{-1}} \]

Millikan's Oil Drop Experiment (1906–1914): Charge on electron is quantized as \(q = ne\), where \(e = -1.602176 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}\).

Mass of Electron:

\[ m_e = \frac{e}{e/m_e} = \frac{1.602176 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{C}}{1.758820 \times 10^{11}\ \mathrm{C\ kg^{-1}}} = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\ \mathrm{kg} \]

Canal Rays (Protons) and Neutrons

E. Goldstein discovered positive rays (canal rays) in perforated cathode tubes. Unlike cathode rays, their \(e/m\) ratio depends on the nature of the gas present. The lightest positive particle obtained from hydrogen was termed a proton (\(m_p \approx 1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\)). In 1932, James Chadwick discovered the neutral neutron (\(m_n \approx 1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\)) by bombarding a beryllium sheet with α-particles: \(^9_4\mathrm{Be} + ^4_2\mathrm{He} \rightarrow ^{12}_6\mathrm{C} + ^1_0\mathrm{n}\).

Atomic Models and Their Limitations

1. Thomson Model (1898): Visualized atom as a spherical positive charge distribution (radius \(\sim 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m}\)) with electrons embedded uniformly like raisins in a pudding (or seeds in a watermelon). It accounted for neutrality but failed to explain the α-particle scattering results.

2. Rutherford Nuclear Model (1911): Stream of α-particles (\(\mathrm{He}^{2+}\)) bombarded a thin gold foil (\(\sim 100\ \mathrm{nm}\) thick) surrounded by a circular fluorescent \(\mathrm{ZnS}\) screen.

  • Observations: (i) >99% α-particles passed through undeflected; (ii) A small fraction deflected by small angles; (iii) ~1 in 20,000 deflected by >90° (some rebounded by nearly 180°).
  • Conclusions: Positive charge and almost entire mass is concentrated in an exceedingly tiny central region called the nucleus (radius \(r_n \sim 10^{-15}\ \mathrm{m}\)). The atomic radius is \(r_a \sim 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m}\). Volume ratio is \(V_{\text{nucleus}} / V_{\text{atom}} \sim 10^{-15}\).
  • Drawbacks: Classical electrodynamics (Maxwell's theory) dictates that an accelerating charged particle emits electromagnetic radiation. A planet-like orbiting electron would continually lose energy, spiraling into the nucleus in \(\sim 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{s}\). Thus, it fails to explain atomic stability and the discrete line spectrum of atoms.

Atomic and Mass Numbers:

\[ Z = \text{Atomic Number} = \text{Number of protons in nucleus} \] \[ A = \text{Mass Number} = \text{Number of nucleons} = Z + n \]

Isotopes: Same \(Z\), different \(A\) (e.g., \(^1_1\mathrm{H}, ^2_1\mathrm{D}, ^3_1\mathrm{T}\); \(^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}, ^{37}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\)). They exhibit identical chemical behavior because chemical properties depend on outer electrons.

Isobars: Same \(A\), different \(Z\) (e.g., \(^{14}_6\mathrm{C}\) and \(^{14}_7\mathrm{N}\)).

3. Dual Nature of Light & Planck's Quantum Theory | प्रकाश की द्वैत प्रकृति एवं प्लांक का क्वांटम सिद्धांत

English

Wave Nature of Electromagnetic Radiation

James Clerk Maxwell (1870) demonstrated that accelerating charged particles emit coupled, mutually perpendicular oscillating electric (\(E\)) and magnetic (\(B\)) fields that propagate transverse to the wave direction at the speed of light in vacuum, \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}\). EM waves require no material medium.

Fundamental Wave Equations:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \quad \implies \quad \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} \]

Wavenumber (\(\bar{\nu}\)): Number of complete waves per unit length:

\[ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \quad (\text{SI unit: }\mathrm{m^{-1}},\ \text{common: }\mathrm{cm^{-1}}) \]

Electromagnetic Spectrum Order (Increasing Frequency / Decreasing Wavelength)

Radio waves (\(\sim 10^6\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < Microwaves (\(\sim 10^{10}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < Infrared (IR, \(\sim 10^{13}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < Visible (\(400\text{--}750\ \mathrm{nm}\), \(\sim 10^{15}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < Ultraviolet (UV, \(\sim 10^{16}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < X-rays (\(\sim 10^{18}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)) < γ-rays (\(\sim 10^{22}\ \mathrm{Hz}\)).

Planck's Quantum Theory (1900)

Classical wave theory failed to explain (i) Black-body radiation intensity-wavelength distribution, (ii) Photoelectric effect, (iii) Temperature dependence of heat capacity of solids, and (iv) Atomic line spectra. Max Planck postulated that radiant energy is emitted or absorbed not continuously, but discontinuously in discrete packets called quanta (or photons in case of light).

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = hc\bar{\nu} \]

where \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\ s}\) (Planck's constant). Total energy emitted: \(E_{\text{total}} = nh\nu\), where \(n = 1, 2, 3 \ldots\)

विद्युत-चुंबकीय विकिरण की तरंग प्रकृति

मैक्सवेल (1870) के अनुसार त्वरित आवेशित कण परस्पर लंबवत दोलायमान विद्युत (\(E\)) तथा चुंबकीय (\(B\)) क्षेत्र उत्पन्न करते हैं। ये तरंगें निर्वात में प्रकाश की चाल (\(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}\)) से गति करती हैं तथा इन्हें संचरण के लिए किसी माध्यम की आवश्यकता नहीं होती।

मूलभूत तरंग संबंध:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \quad \implies \quad \nu = \frac{c}{\lambda} \]

तरंग संख्या (\(\bar{\nu}\)): प्रति इकाई लंबाई में तरंगों की संख्या:

\[ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \quad (\text{SI मात्रक: }\mathrm{m^{-1}},\ \text{प्रचलित: }\mathrm{cm^{-1}}) \]

विद्युत-चुंबकीय स्पेक्ट्रम (बढ़ती आवृत्ति का क्रम)

रेडियो तरंगें < सूक्ष्म तरंगें (माइक्रोवेव) < अवरक्त (IR) < दृश्य प्रकाश (\(400\text{--}750\ \mathrm{nm}\)) < पराबैंगनी (UV) < X-किरणें < गामा (γ) किरणें।

प्लांक का क्वांटम सिद्धांत (1900)

चिरसम्मत तरंग सिद्धांत कृष्णिका विकिरण एवं प्रकाश-विद्युत प्रभाव को समझाने में विफल रहा। मैक्स प्लांक ने प्रतिपादित किया कि ऊर्जा का अवशोषण या उत्सर्जन सतत न होकर ऊर्जा के छोटे-छोटे पैकेटों में होता है, जिन्हें क्वांटम (प्रकाश के संदर्भ में फोटॉन) कहते हैं।

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = hc\bar{\nu} \]

जहाँ \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\ \mathrm{J\ s}\) (प्लांक स्थिरांक)। कुल ऊर्जा: \(E = nh\nu\), जहाँ \(n = 1, 2, 3 \ldots\)

4. Photoelectric Effect & Einstein's Explanation | प्रकाश-विद्युत प्रभाव एवं आइंस्टीन की व्याख्या

English

H. Hertz (1887) observed that clean metal surfaces (especially alkali metals like \(\mathrm{K, Rb, Cs}\)) eject electrons when exposed to a beam of light in vacuum.

  • Instantaneous Ejection: Electrons eject immediately with zero detectable time lag (< \(10^{-9}\ \mathrm{s}\)).
  • Threshold Frequency (\(\nu_0\)): Photoemission occurs only if \(\nu \ge \nu_0\). Below \(\nu_0\), no electrons are emitted regardless of light intensity.
  • Kinetic Energy Dependence: The maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends linearly on the frequency (\(\nu\)) of incident radiation and is independent of intensity.
  • Intensity Dependence: The rate of ejection (photoelectric current) is directly proportional to the incident light intensity (number of incident photons per second).

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation (1905):

\[ E_{\text{photon}} = W_0 + K.E._{\text{max}} \] \[ h\nu = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 \] \[ K.E._{\text{max}} = h(\nu - \nu_0) = hc\left(\frac{1}{\lambda} - \frac{1}{\lambda_0}\right) = eV_0 \]

where \(W_0 = h\nu_0\) is the metal's work function, \(\lambda_0\) is the threshold wavelength, and \(V_0\) is the stopping potential.

5. Bohr's Model for Hydrogen-like Species & Atomic Spectra | बोर का परमाणु मॉडल एवं हाइड्रोजन का रेखा स्पेक्ट्रम

English

Postulates of Bohr's Theory (1913)

  1. An electron moves around the central positive nucleus in certain fixed, non-radiating concentric circular paths called stationary orbits.
  2. Quantization of Orbital Angular Momentum: An electron can only revolve in orbits where its orbital angular momentum is an integral multiple of \(h/2\pi\): \[ m_e v r = \frac{nh}{2\pi}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3 \ldots \]
  3. Bohr's Frequency Condition: Radiation is absorbed or emitted only when an electron jumps between two allowed stationary states: \[ \Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \]

Master Formulae for Hydrogenic Species (\(\mathrm{H, He^+, Li^{2+}, Be^{3+}}\)):

1. Radius of \(n\)-th orbit:

\[ r_n = \frac{a_0 n^2}{Z} = 52.9\ \frac{n^2}{Z}\ \mathrm{pm} = 0.529\ \frac{n^2}{Z}\ \text{Å} \]

2. Velocity of electron in \(n\)-th orbit:

\[ v_n = v_0 \frac{Z}{n} = 2.18 \times 10^6 \frac{Z}{n}\ \mathrm{m\ s^{-1}} \]

3. Energy of \(n\)-th orbit:

\[ E_n = -R_H \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right) = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \left(\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\right)\ \mathrm{J\ atom^{-1}} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\ \mathrm{eV\ atom^{-1}} \]

4. Rydberg Equation for Transitions (\(n_2 \to n_1\)):

\[ \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = R_H Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right) \]

where \(R_H = 109,677\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}} = 1.09677 \times 10^7\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}\).

Significance of Negative Electronic Energy

The energy is zero at \(n = \infty\) (electron completely separated from the nucleus at rest). As the electron approaches the attractive nucleus, work is done by the electrostatic force, releasing energy. Thus \(E_n\) becomes increasingly negative and reaches its most stable minimum at \(n = 1\) (ground state).

6. de Broglie Dual Nature & Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle | दे ब्रॉग्ली द्वैत प्रकृति एवं हाइजेनबर्ग अनिश्चितता सिद्धांत

English

1. de Broglie Hypothesis (1924)

Louis de Broglie proposed that just as radiation exhibits particle-like properties, matter particles (such as electrons, protons, and atoms) exhibit wave properties in motion.

de Broglie Wavelength:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv} \]

In terms of Kinetic Energy (\(K.E.\)):

\[ p = \sqrt{2m(K.E.)} \quad \implies \quad \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m(K.E.)}} \]

For an electron accelerated through potential difference \(V\) volts:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m_e eV}} = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}}\ \mathrm{nm} = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}}\ \text{Å} \]

Bohr's Quantization Derived from de Broglie: For a stable standing wave in an electron orbit, the circumference must contain an integral number of de Broglie wavelengths:

\[ 2\pi r = n\lambda = n\left(\frac{h}{mv}\right) \quad \implies \quad mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \]

2. Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (1927)

It is fundamentally impossible to measure simultaneously both the exact position and exact momentum (or velocity) of a microscopic particle like an electron with arbitrary accuracy.

\[ \Delta x \times \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \] \[ \Delta x \times \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} \]

where \(\Delta x\) is uncertainty in position, and \(\Delta p_x = m\Delta v_x\) is uncertainty in momentum.

Energy-Time Conjugate Form: \(\Delta E \times \Delta t \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}\)

Implication: Well-defined trajectories or circular orbits (as proposed by Bohr) do not exist for electrons. The classical concept of deterministic orbit is replaced by the probabilistic concept of atomic orbital.

7. Quantum Mechanical Model, Quantum Numbers & Orbitals | क्वांटम यांत्रिकी मॉडल, क्वांटम संख्याएं एवं कक्षक

English

Schrödinger Wave Equation (1926)

For a time-independent system like an atom: \(\hat{H}\Psi = E\Psi\), where \(\hat{H}\) is the Hamiltonian operator, \(E\) is total energy, and \(\Psi\) is the wave function.

  • Physical Significance of \(\Psi\): \(\Psi\) represents the amplitude of the electron wave. By itself, it has no direct physical meaning.
  • Physical Significance of \(|\Psi|^2\): According to Max Born, \(|\Psi|^2\) represents the probability density (probability of finding the electron per unit volume) at that point. It is always real and non-negative.
  • Atomic Orbital: The three-dimensional region of space around the nucleus where the probability of finding the electron is maximum (typically chosen as \(\sim 90\%\)).

The Four Quantum Numbers

  1. Principal Quantum Number (\(n\)): Designates shell (\(K, L, M, N \ldots\)), size, and main energy level. Values: \(n = 1, 2, 3 \ldots\)
    • Total number of orbitals in a shell = \(n^2\)
    • Maximum electron capacity of a shell = \(2n^2\)
  2. Azimuthal / Orbital Angular Momentum Quantum Number (\(l\)): Defines the 3D shape of the orbital and subshell. Permitted values: \(l = 0, 1, 2 \ldots (n - 1)\).
    • \(l = 0 \to s\) (spherical); \(l = 1 \to p\) (dumb-bell); \(l = 2 \to d\) (cloverleaf / double dumb-bell); \(l = 3 \to f\) (complex).
    • Orbital angular momentum: \(\mu_l = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\,\frac{h}{2\pi} = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\,\hbar\)
  3. Magnetic Orbital Quantum Number (\(m_l\)): Dictates spatial orientation of orbitals in a subshell. Permitted values: \(m_l = -l \ldots 0 \ldots +l\) (total \(2l + 1\) values).
  4. Electron Spin Quantum Number (\(m_s\)): Discovered by Uhlenbeck and Goudsmit (1925) to explain spectral doublets. Dictates intrinsic spin orientation: \(m_s = +\frac{1}{2}\ (\uparrow)\) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\ (\downarrow)\). Spin angular momentum: \(S = \sqrt{s(s+1)}\,\frac{h}{2\pi}\).

Nodes and Nodal Planes Formulae:

\[ \text{Radial Nodes (Spherical Nodes)} = n - l - 1 \] \[ \text{Angular Nodes (Nodal Planes)} = l \] \[ \text{Total Nodes} = \text{Radial} + \text{Angular} = (n - l - 1) + l = n - 1 \]

  1. मुख्य क्वांटम संख्या (\(n\)): कोश (\(K, L, M \ldots\)), कक्षक के आकार एवं मुख्य ऊर्जा को दर्शाती है। \(n = 1, 2, 3 \ldots\)

    • \(l = 0 \to s\) (गोलाकार); \(l = 1 \to p\) (डंबलाकार); \(l = 2 \to d\) (द्वि-डंबलाकार); \(l = 3 \to f\) (जटिल)।
    • कक्षीय कोणीय संवेग: \(\mu_l = \sqrt{l(l+1)}\,\frac{h}{2\pi}\)
  3. चुंबकीय क्वांटम संख्या (\(m_l\)): त्रिविम में अभिविन्यास (orientation) को दर्शाती है। मान: \(-l\) से \(+l\) तक (कुल \(2l + 1\) मान)।

8. Orbital Filling Rules & Electronic Configurations | कक्षक भराव नियम एवं इलेक्ट्रॉनिक विन्यास

English

Rules Governing Electronic Distribution

  1. Aufbau Principle ("Building Up"): In the ground state of atoms, orbitals are filled in order of their increasing energies: \[ 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s \dots \]
    • \((n + l)\) Rule (Bohr-Bury Rule): Lower \((n + l)\) orbital has lower energy. E.g., for \(4s\) (\(4+0=4\)) vs \(3d\) (\(3+2=5\)), \(4s\) is filled before \(3d\).
    • If two orbitals have identical \((n + l)\) values, the orbital with lower \(n\) possesses lower energy (e.g., \(2p\) with \(2+1=3\) has lower energy than \(3s\) with \(3+0=3\)).
  2. Pauli Exclusion Principle (1926): "No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers."
    • An orbital can accommodate a maximum of two electrons, and they must have opposite (antiparallel) spins (\(\uparrow\downarrow\)).
  3. Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Electron pairing in degenerate orbitals belonging to the same subshell (\(p, d, f\)) does not occur until each orbital is singly occupied with parallel spins.

Stability of Half-Filled and Fully-Filled Subshells

Configurations having half-filled (\(p^3, d^5, f^7\)) or completely filled (\(p^6, d^{10}, f^{14}\)) degenerate sets possess extra stability due to:

  • Symmetrical Distribution: Highly symmetric charge distribution minimizes interelectronic repulsions.
  • Maximum Exchange Energy: Electrons with parallel spins can exchange positions within degenerate orbitals. The energy released during such exchanges is called exchange energy. For \(m\) parallel electrons in degenerate orbitals, the total number of exchanges is: \[ \text{Exchanges} = \frac{m(m - 1)}{2} \] For \(d^5\) (Chromium, \([Ar]\,3d^5 4s^1\)): \(\text{Exchanges} = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10\). In contrast, \(d^4 4s^2\) would have only \(3 + 2 + 1 = 6\) exchanges. Hence \(3d^5 4s^1\) is energetically favored.

Worked Example 1: Photoelectric Effect & Work Function Type: Quantum Mechanics
English

Problem: When radiation of wavelength \(300\,\mathrm{nm}\) strikes sodium metal, photoelectrons are ejected with a maximum kinetic energy of \(1.68 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}}\). Calculate the work function of sodium in electron-volts (\(\mathrm{eV}\)) and the maximum threshold wavelength (\(\lambda_0\)) capable of ejecting an electron.

(Given: \(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\ s}\), \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}\), \(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\), \(1\,\mathrm{eV} = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}\))

हिन्दी

प्रश्न: जब सोडियम धातु पर \(300\,\mathrm{nm}\) तरंगदैर्ध्य का विकिरण आपतित होता है, तो निष्कासित इलेक्ट्रॉनों की अधिकतम गतिज ऊर्जा \(1.68 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}}\) पाई जाती है। सोडियम के कार्य-फलन का मान इलेक्ट्रॉन-वोल्ट (\(\mathrm{eV}\)) में ज्ञात कीजिए तथा देहली तरंगदैर्ध्य (\(\lambda_0\)) का मान परिकलित कीजिए।

Worked Solution / संपूर्ण हल:

1. Energy of one photon of incident light:

\[ E_{\text{photon}} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\ s} \times 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\ s^{-1}}}{300 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m}} = 6.626 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} \]

2. Incident energy per mole of photons:

\[ E_{\text{mole}} = (6.626 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}) \times (6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}) = 3.99 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}} \]

3. Work function per mole:

\[ W_{0,\text{mole}} = E_{\text{mole}} - K.E. = 3.99 \times 10^5 - 1.68 \times 10^5 = 2.31 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}} \]

4. Work function per atom (in \(\mathrm{J}\) and \(\mathrm{eV}\)):

\[ W_0 = \frac{2.31 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{J\ mol^{-1}}}{6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}} = 3.836 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} \] \[ W_0 (\mathrm{eV}) = \frac{3.836 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J}}{1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J\ eV^{-1}}} = 2.39\,\mathrm{eV} \]

5. Threshold wavelength (\(\lambda_0\)):

\[ \lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{W_0} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^8}{3.836 \times 10^{-19}} \approx 5.18 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m} = 518\,\mathrm{nm} \]
Worked Example 2: de Broglie Wavelength & Heisenberg Uncertainty Type: Dual Nature
English

Problem: An electron is accelerated through a potential difference of \(100\,\mathrm{V}\). (a) Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. (b) If its position is measured with an uncertainty of \(0.01\,\mathrm{nm}\), calculate the minimum uncertainty in its velocity.

हिन्दी

प्रश्न: एक इलेक्ट्रॉन को \(100\,\mathrm{V}\) के विभवांतर से त्वरित किया जाता है। (a) इसकी दे ब्रॉग्ली तरंगदैर्ध्य की गणना कीजिए। (b) यदि इसकी स्थिति में अनिश्चितता \(0.01\,\mathrm{nm}\) है, तो इसके वेग में न्यूनतम अनिश्चितता ज्ञात कीजिए।

Worked Solution / संपूर्ण हल:

(a) de Broglie Wavelength:

\[ \lambda = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{V}}\,\mathrm{nm} = \frac{1.227}{\sqrt{100}}\,\mathrm{nm} = \frac{1.227}{10} = 0.1227\,\mathrm{nm} = 1.227\ \text{Å} \]

(b) Uncertainty in velocity:

\[ \Delta x \times m\Delta v \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \implies \Delta v = \frac{h}{4\pi m \Delta x} \] \[ \Delta x = 0.01\,\mathrm{nm} = 10^{-11}\,\mathrm{m}, \quad m = 9.11 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg} \] \[ \Delta v = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times (9.11 \times 10^{-31}) \times 10^{-11}} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{1.1448 \times 10^{-40}} \approx 5.79 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\ s^{-1}} \]

English

The total number of orbitals associated with the principal quantum number \(n = 4\) and the maximum number of electrons having \(m_s = -\frac{1}{2}\) in this shell are, respectively:

(A) 16 and 16
(B) 16 and 32
(C) 4 and 16
(D) 8 and 16

Correct Answer: (A)

Explanation: For shell \(n = 4\):

Total number of orbitals = \(n^2 = 4^2 = 16\) (one \(4s\), three \(4p\), five \(4d\), seven \(4f\)).

Total electrons = \(2n^2 = 2(16) = 32\).

Since each orbital accommodates two electrons with opposite spins (\(+\frac{1}{2}\) and \(-\frac{1}{2}\)), exactly half of the total electrons will have \(m_s = -\frac{1}{2}\):

\[ \text{Electrons with } m_s = -\frac{1}{2} = \frac{32}{2} = 16 \]
हिन्दी

मुख्य क्वांटम संख्या \(n = 4\) से संबंधित कुल कक्षकों की संख्या तथा इस कोश में \(m_s = -\frac{1}{2}\) चक्रण वाले अधिकतम इलेक्ट्रॉनों की संख्या क्रमशः होगी:

(A) 16 तथा 16
(B) 16 तथा 32
(C) 4 तथा 16
(D) 8 तथा 16

सही उत्तर: (A)

हल: \(n = 4\) कोश के लिए:

कुल कक्षकों की संख्या \(= n^2 = 4^2 = 16\)।

कुल अधिकतम इलेक्ट्रॉन \(= 2n^2 = 32\)।

प्रत्येक कक्षक में विपरीत चक्रण के 2 इलेक्ट्रॉन होते हैं। अतः ठीक आधे इलेक्ट्रॉनों (16) का चक्रण \(m_s = -\frac{1}{2}\) होगा।

11. Common Pitfalls & NEET Revision Checklist | सामान्य गलतियां एवं त्वरित पुनरावलोकन

English
Pitfall 1: Confusing \((n + l)\) Values in Hydrogen vs Multi-Electron Atoms

In a single-electron species (like \(\mathrm{H, He^+, Li^{2+}}\)), subshell energies depend solely on \(n\). Hence, \(2s = 2p\) and \(3s = 3p = 3d\). In multi-electron atoms, shielding and electron-electron repulsion split degenerate subshells, making energy depend on \((n + l)\).

Pitfall 2: Orbit vs Orbital

An orbit is a circular planar trajectory (disproven by uncertainty principle). An orbital is a 3D probabilistic wave function \(\Psi\) characterized by three quantum numbers (\(n, l, m_l\)).

Pitfall 3: Order of Ionization in Transition Metals

When filling neutral \(3d\)-series atoms, \(4s\) fills before \(3d\). However, upon cation formation (e.g., \(\mathrm{Fe} \to \mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\)), electrons are always removed from the outermost \(4s\) orbital first, not from \(3d\)!

Pitfall 4: Balmer Series Region

The Balmer series lies in the visible region only for atomic hydrogen. For \(\mathrm{He}^+\) and \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\), the Balmer series shifts into the ultraviolet region due to the \(Z^2\) energy multiplier.

हिन्दी
गलती 1: हाइड्रोजन तथा बहु-इलेक्ट्रॉन परमाणुओं में ऊर्जा क्रम की भ्रांति

एक-इलेक्ट्रॉन वाली स्पीशीज (\(\mathrm{H, He^+}\)) में कक्षकों की ऊर्जा केवल \(n\) पर निर्भर करती है (\(2s = 2p\) तथा \(3s = 3p = 3d\))। बहु-इलेक्ट्रॉन परमाणुओं में परिरक्षण प्रभाव के कारण ऊर्जा \((n + l)\) पर निर्भर करती है।

गलती 2: कक्षा (Orbit) बनाम कक्षक (Orbital)

कक्षा बोर द्वारा प्रस्तावित एक वृत्ताकार द्विविम पथ है जो अनिश्चितता सिद्धांत का उल्लंघन करता है। कक्षक एक त्रिविम तरंग फलन \(\Psi\) है जो प्रायिकता घनत्व \(|\Psi|^2\) को व्यक्त करता है।

गलती 3: संक्रमण तत्वों से इलेक्ट्रॉन निष्कासन का क्रम

यद्यपि विन्यास लिखते समय \(4s\) पहले भरा जाता है, परंतु आयन बनते समय इलेक्ट्रॉन सदैव बाह्यतम \(4s\) कक्षक से पहले निकलते हैं, \(3d\) से नहीं (जैसे \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+} = 3d^6\))।

गलती 4: बामर श्रेणी का स्पेक्ट्रमी क्षेत्र

हाइड्रोजन के लिए बामर श्रेणी दृश्य क्षेत्र में होती है। परंतु \(\mathrm{He}^+\) अथवा \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) हेतु \(Z^2\) गुणक के कारण बामर श्रेणी पराबैंगनी (UV) क्षेत्र में खिसक जाती है।

12. Final High-Yield Formula Sheet | त्वरित सूत्र संग्रह

English
  • Energy of Photon: \(E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = hc\bar{\nu}\)
  • Photoelectric Equation: \(h\nu = W_0 + K.E._{\text{max}} = h\nu_0 + eV_0\)
  • Bohr Radius: \(r_n = 0.529\,\frac{n^2}{Z}\ \text{Å} = 52.9\,\frac{n^2}{Z}\ \mathrm{pm}\)
  • Bohr Energy: \(E_n = -13.6\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\ \mathrm{eV} = -2.18 \times 10^{-18}\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\ \mathrm{J}\)
  • Rydberg Equation: \(\bar{\nu} = 109,677 \times Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right)\ \mathrm{cm^{-1}}\)
  • Number of Spectral Lines (from \(n\) to ground state): \(N = \frac{n(n - 1)}{2}\)
  • de Broglie Wavelength: \(\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2m(K.E.)}} = \frac{12.27}{\sqrt{V}}\ \text{Å (for electron)}\)
  • Heisenberg Uncertainty: \(\Delta x \times \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}\)
  • Orbital Angular Momentum: \(\mu_l = \sqrt{l(l + 1)}\,\frac{h}{2\pi}\)
  • Radial Nodes: \(n - l - 1\); Angular Nodes: \(l\); Total Nodes: \(n - 1\)
  • Spin Only Magnetic Moment: \(\mu_s = \sqrt{n(n + 2)}\ \mathrm{B.M.}\) (where \(n\) is number of unpaired electrons)
