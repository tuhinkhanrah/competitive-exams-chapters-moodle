Some Basic Concepts of Chemistry

Learning Objectives & Exam Overview

English

Chemistry explores the composition, structure, properties, and transformations of matter at the atomic and molecular scale. In NEET UG, this foundational unit is high-yield because mole calculations, limiting reagent analysis, empirical formulas, and solution concentration conversions appear both directly and embedded inside physical, organic, and inorganic problems.

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Classify matter into states (solid, liquid, gas) and chemical categories (elements, compounds, homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures).
  • Apply SI base units, metric prefixes, temperature conversions, dimensional analysis, and significant-figure rules accurately.
  • Explain the five fundamental laws of chemical combination and Dalton's atomic theory along with its limitations.
  • Compute average atomic mass from isotopic abundances, molecular mass, formula mass, and molar mass.
  • Interconvert mass, moles, number of particles, and gas volumes using the mole concept.
  • Determine percentage composition, empirical formula, molecular formula, limiting reagent, and reaction yield.
  • Calculate and interconvert mass per cent, mole fraction, molarity, and molality, and recognize which quantities depend on temperature.

1. Role of Chemistry & Classification of Matter / रसायन की भूमिका और द्रव्य का वर्गीकरण

English

Chemistry underpins modern medicine, agriculture, materials science, and environmental protection. Life-saving pharmaceuticals such as cisplatin and taxol (used in cancer therapy) and AZT (azidothymidine, used in HIV/AIDS management) rely on chemical synthesis. Chemistry also enables safer refrigerants to replace ozone-depleting chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), superconducting ceramics, conducting polymers, and optical fibres. Historically, Indian traditions such as Rasayan Shastra included metallurgy, dyes, cosmetics, and medicinal preparations (such as metal bhasmas), and around 600 BCE Acharya Kanda proposed indivisible ultimate particles called Paramānu in the Vaiseshika Sutras.

States of Matter

Matter is anything that possesses mass and occupies space. Under ordinary conditions, it exists in three interconvertible physical states:

  • Solid: Particles are tightly packed in an ordered arrangement with minimal freedom of movement; solids have both definite shape and definite volume.
  • Liquid: Particles remain close together but can slide past one another; liquids have definite volume but take the shape of their container.
  • Gas: Particles are far apart and move rapidly in all directions; gases have neither definite shape nor definite volume and expand to fill the entire container.

Pure Substances vs. Mixtures

At the macroscopic level, matter is divided into pure substances (fixed chemical composition) and mixtures (variable composition of two or more pure components).

  • Elements: Contain only one kind of atom, occurring either as single atoms (e.g., \(\mathrm{Na}\), \(\mathrm{Cu}\)) or homonuclear molecules (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2}\), \(\mathrm{O_2}\)).
  • Compounds: Formed when atoms of two or more different elements chemically combine in a fixed mass ratio (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\)). Their properties differ completely from their constituent elements, and their components can only be separated by chemical methods.
  • Homogeneous Mixtures: Uniform composition throughout (e.g., aqueous sugar solution, clean air).
  • Heterogeneous Mixtures: Non-uniform composition where distinct phases or particles may be visible (e.g., salt mixed with sand, pulses with small stones). Mixtures can be separated by physical methods such as filtration, distillation, or crystallisation.

  • ठोस: कण अत्यंत पास और सुव्यवस्थित क्रम में बँधे होते हैं; इनका आकार और आयतन दोनों निश्चित होते हैं।

  • यौगिक: दो या अधिक भिन्न तत्वों के परमाणुओं के निश्चित द्रव्यमान अनुपात में रासायनिक संयोजन से बनते हैं (जैसे \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\))। इनके गुण अवयवी तत्वों से सर्वथा भिन्न होते हैं और इन्हें केवल रासायनिक विधियों से पृथक किया जा सकता है।
  • समांगी मिश्रण: पूरे मिश्रण में संघटन एकसमान रहता है (जैसे जल में चीनी का विलयन, स्वच्छ वायु)।
  • विषमांगी मिश्रण: पूरे मिश्रण में संघटन एकसमान नहीं होता (जैसे नमक और रेत का मिश्रण)। मिश्रणों को निस्यंदन, आसवन या क्रिस्टलीकरण जैसी भौतिक विधियों से अलग किया जा सकता है।

Key derived relationships and laboratory units:

  • Mass vs. Weight: Mass is the constant amount of matter in a body, whereas weight is the gravitational force acting on it and varies with local gravity.

Scientific Notation, Precision, Accuracy & Significant Figures

  • Scientific Notation: Numbers are written as \(N \times 10^n\), where \(1.000\dots \le N < 10\) and \(n\) is an integer. For addition/subtraction, first equalize the exponent \(n\); for multiplication/division, add or subtract exponents.
  • Precision vs. Accuracy: Precision is the closeness of repeated measurements to each other; accuracy is the closeness of a measurement (or mean) to the true value.
  • Significant Figures Rules: (1) All non-zero digits are significant. (2) Leading zeros before the first non-zero digit are not significant (e.g., \(0.0042\) has 2). (3) Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (e.g., \(3.007\) has 4). (4) Trailing zeros to the right of a decimal point are significant (e.g., \(0.400\) has 3); trailing zeros without a decimal point are ambiguous unless written in scientific notation. (5) Exact counted numbers (e.g., \(12\) eggs) have infinite significant figures.
  • Arithmetic Rules: In addition/subtraction, the result cannot have more decimal places than the term with the fewest decimal places. In multiplication/division, the result cannot have more significant figures than the factor with the fewest significant figures.
  • Rounding Off when the Dropped Digit is 5: If the preceding digit is odd, increase it by 1 (e.g., \(7.35 \to 7.4\)); if it is even, leave it unchanged (e.g., \(7.25 \to 7.2\)).
  • Dimensional Analysis (Unit Factor Method): Multiply by conversion ratios equal to 1 (such as \(\frac{100\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{m}}\)) so unwanted units cancel algebraically.

  • गणना नियम: जोड़/घटाव में परिणाम में दशमलव के बाद उतने ही अंक रखे जाते हैं जितने न्यूनतम दशमलव स्थान वाली संख्या में हों। गुणा/भाग में परिणाम में उतने ही सार्थक अंक रखे जाते हैं जितने न्यूनतम सार्थक अंकों वाली संख्या में हों।
  • अंक 5 हटाते समय पूर्णांकन: यदि पिछला अंक विषम है तो उसे 1 बढ़ाते हैं (\(7.35 \to 7.4\)); यदि सम है तो अपरिवर्तित रखते हैं (\(7.25 \to 7.2\))।
  • विमीय विश्लेषण: वांछित मात्रक प्राप्त करने के लिए \(1\) के बराबर मात्रक गुणकों (जैसे \(\frac{100\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{m}}\)) से गुणा किया जाता है।

3. Laws of Chemical Combination & Dalton's Theory / रासायनिक संयोजन के नियम एवं डाल्टन का सिद्धांत

English
Law (Proponent, Year) Core Statement Illustrative Example
Conservation of Mass
(Antoine Lavoisier, 1789)		 Matter is neither created nor destroyed in physical or chemical changes; total mass of reactants equals total mass of products. In a closed vessel, \(12\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{C}\) reacts completely with \(32\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) to yield \(44\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\).
Definite Proportions
(Joseph Proust)		 A pure chemical compound always contains the same elements in the same fixed proportion by mass, irrespective of its source. Pure \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) from rain, river, or synthesis always contains \(\mathrm{H}\) and \(\mathrm{O}\) in a \(1:8\) mass ratio.
Multiple Proportions
(John Dalton, 1803)		 When two elements form more than one compound, the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other bear a simple whole-number ratio. In \(\mathrm{CO}\) and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), masses of \(\mathrm{O}\) combining with \(12\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{C}\) are \(16\,\mathrm{g}\) and \(32\,\mathrm{g}\) (ratio \(1:2\)).
Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes (1808) At constant temperature and pressure, gases react or are produced in simple whole-number ratios by volume. \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{N_2(g)}\) + \(300\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{H_2(g)}\) \(\rightarrow\) \(200\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{NH_3(g)}\) (ratio \(1:3:2\)).
Avogadro's Law (1811) Equal volumes of all gases under identical temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. At the same \(T, P\), \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) of \(\mathrm{H_2}\) and \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) contain the same number of molecules.

Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808)

Dalton proposed that: (1) matter consists of indivisible atoms; (2) all atoms of a given element have identical properties and mass, while atoms of different elements differ in mass; (3) compounds form when atoms of different elements combine in fixed ratios; and (4) chemical reactions only rearrange atoms without creating or destroying them.

Limitation: Dalton's theory explained mass-based laws of combination, but it could not explain Gay-Lussac's law of gaseous volumes or why atoms combine (which required Avogadro's distinction between atoms and polyatomic molecules).

4. Atomic Mass, Molecular Mass & The Mole Concept / परमाणु द्रव्यमान, आणविक द्रव्यमान व मोल संकल्पना

English
  • Unified Atomic Mass Unit (\(\mathrm{u}\) or \(\mathrm{amu}\)): Defined as exactly \(\frac{1}{12}\) of the mass of one \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) atom. \[ 1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{g} = \frac{1}{N_A}\,\mathrm{g} \]
  • Average Atomic Mass: For an element with naturally occurring isotopes of masses \(M_i\) and fractional abundances \(f_i\) (or percentage abundances \(p_i\)): \[ \text{Average Atomic Mass} = \sum f_i M_i = \frac{\sum p_i M_i}{100} \]
  • Molecular Mass vs. Formula Mass: Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a discrete molecule (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH_4} = 12.011 + 4 \times 1.008 = 16.043\,\mathrm{u}\)). Formula mass is used for ionic solids like \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) that form 3D crystal lattices rather than discrete molecules (\(\mathrm{NaCl} = 23.0 + 35.5 = 58.5\,\mathrm{u}\)).
  • The Mole (\(\mathrm{mol}\)) & Avogadro Constant (\(N_A\)): One mole contains exactly \(6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\) elementary entities (commonly taken as \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\) in NEET calculations).
  • Molar Mass: The mass of \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a substance in grams (\(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)), which is numerically equal to its atomic, molecular, or formula mass in \(\mathrm{u}\).
Core Mole Relations: \[ n = \frac{\text{Given Mass }(w)}{\text{Molar Mass }(M)} = \frac{\text{Number of Entities }(N)}{N_A} = \frac{\text{Volume of Gas at STP }(V)}{V_m} \] Note on STP molar volume: Under older IUPAC convention (\(0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}, 1\,\mathrm{atm}\)), \(V_m = 22.4\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\); under modern IUPAC STP (\(0\,^\circ\mathrm{C}, 1\,\mathrm{bar}\)), \(V_m = 22.7\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\). Follow whichever STP pressure convention a question specifies.

हिन्दी
  • एकीकृत परमाणु द्रव्यमान मात्रक (\(\mathrm{u}\) या \(\mathrm{amu}\)): यह एक \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) परमाणु के द्रव्यमान का ठीक \(\frac{1}{12}\)वाँ भाग होता है। \[ 1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{g} = \frac{1}{N_A}\,\mathrm{g} \]

  • आणविक द्रव्यमान बनाम सूत्र द्रव्यमान: आणविक द्रव्यमान किसी अणु में उपस्थित सभी परमाणुओं के परमाणु द्रव्यमानों का योग है। \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) जैसे आयनिक ठोसों में पृथक अणु नहीं होते, अतः उनके लिए सूत्र द्रव्यमान (\(\mathrm{NaCl} = 23.0 + 35.5 = 58.5\,\mathrm{u}\)) प्रयुक्त होता है।
  • मोल (\(\mathrm{mol}\)) तथा आवोगाद्रो स्थिरांक (\(N_A\)): \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) में ठीक \(6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\) मूल कण होते हैं (NEET गणनाओं में सामान्यतः \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\) प्रयुक्त होता है)।

मोल के मुख्य संबंध: \[ n = \frac{\text{दिया गया द्रव्यमान }(w)}{\text{मोलर द्रव्यमान }(M)} = \frac{\text{कणों की संख्या }(N)}{N_A} = \frac{\text{STP पर गैस का आयतन }(V)}{V_m} \] STP मोलर आयतन: \(1\,\mathrm{atm}\) पर \(V_m = 22.4\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\) तथा आधुनिक \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) मानक पर \(V_m = 22.7\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\)।

5. Percentage Composition, Empirical & Molecular Formulas / प्रतिशत संघटन, मूलानुपाती एवं आणविक सूत्र

English
\[ \text{Mass \% of an element} = \frac{\text{Total mass of that element in 1 mol of compound}}{\text{Molar mass of the compound}} \times 100 \]
  • Empirical Formula (EF): Shows the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
  • Molecular Formula (MF): Shows the exact number of atoms of each element in one molecule: \(\text{MF} = (\text{EF})_n\), where \[ n = \frac{\text{Molar Mass}}{\text{Empirical Formula Mass}} \]
  • 4-Step Procedure: (1) Treat mass percentages as grams in \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) of compound. (2) Divide each mass by its atomic mass to obtain moles. (3) Divide all mole values by the smallest mole value to get the simplest ratio. (4) Multiply by a small integer (if needed) to convert fractions (e.g., \(1.5\) or \(1.33\)) into whole numbers.

6. Stoichiometry, Limiting Reagent & Solution Concentrations / रससमीकरणमिति, सीमांत अभिकर्मक व विलयन सांद्रता

English

Stoichiometry uses balanced chemical equations to relate quantitative amounts of reactants and products. Never alter chemical subscripts while balancing; adjust only stoichiometric coefficients.

Limiting Reagent (LR): When reactants are not mixed in exact stoichiometric proportions, the reactant that is completely consumed first limits the amount of product formed. Quick NEET shortcut: divide the given moles of each reactant by its stoichiometric coefficient; the reactant with the smallest \(\frac{n}{\text{coefficient}}\) ratio is the limiting reagent.

Worked Example 1: Average Atomic Mass

Problem: Assume a hypothetical element \(X\) has two naturally occurring isotopes: \(^{63}X\) with isotopic mass \(62.93\,\mathrm{u}\) (abundance \(70.0\%\)) and \(^{65}X\) with isotopic mass \(64.93\,\mathrm{u}\) (abundance \(30.0\%\)). Calculate the average atomic mass of \(X\).

Solution:

\[ \text{Average atomic mass} = \frac{(70.0 \times 62.93) + (30.0 \times 64.93)}{100} = 44.051 + 19.479 = 63.53\,\mathrm{u} \]

Worked Example 2: Empirical and Molecular Formula

Problem: Assume a pure organic compound contains \(40.0\%\) carbon, \(6.67\%\) hydrogen, and \(53.33\%\) oxygen by mass. Its molar mass is \(180\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\). Determine its empirical and molecular formulas. (Atomic masses: \(\mathrm{C}=12\,\mathrm{u}\), \(\mathrm{H}=1\,\mathrm{u}\), \(\mathrm{O}=16\,\mathrm{u}\))

Solution: In \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) of compound:

  • Moles of \(\mathrm{C} = \frac{40.0}{12} = 3.33\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Moles of \(\mathrm{H} = \frac{6.67}{1} = 6.67\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Moles of \(\mathrm{O} = \frac{53.33}{16} = 3.33\,\mathrm{mol}\)

Dividing by \(3.33\) gives the simplest ratio \(\mathrm{C:H:O} = 1:2:1\), so the empirical formula is \(\mathrm{CH_2O}\) (empirical mass \(= 12 + 2 + 16 = 30\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)).

\[ n = \frac{180}{30} = 6 \implies \text{Molecular formula} = (\mathrm{CH_2O})_6 = \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} \]

Worked Example 3: Limiting Reagent & Yield

Problem: In a hypothetical synthesis, \(10.8\,\mathrm{g}\) of aluminium reacts with \(21.3\,\mathrm{g}\) of chlorine gas according to \(\mathrm{2Al(s) + 3Cl_2(g) \rightarrow 2AlCl_3(s)}\). Identify the limiting reagent and calculate the mass of \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\) formed. (\(\mathrm{Al}=27.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), \(\mathrm{Cl}=35.5\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\))

Solution:

  • \(n(\mathrm{Al}) = \frac{10.8}{27.0} = 0.40\,\mathrm{mol}\); coefficient ratio \(= \frac{0.40}{2} = 0.20\).
  • \(n(\mathrm{Cl_2}) = \frac{21.3}{71.0} = 0.30\,\mathrm{mol}\); coefficient ratio \(= \frac{0.30}{3} = 0.10\).

Since \(0.10 < 0.20\), \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\) is the limiting reagent. From stoichiometry, \(3\,\mathrm{mol\,Cl_2}\) produces \(2\,\mathrm{mol\,AlCl_3}\):

\[ n(\mathrm{AlCl_3}) = \frac{2}{3} \times 0.30 = 0.20\,\mathrm{mol} \]

Molar mass of \(\mathrm{AlCl_3} = 27.0 + 3(35.5) = 133.5\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), so mass formed \(= 0.20 \times 133.5 = 26.7\,\mathrm{g}\).

हल: तनुकरण समीकरण \(M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2\) से, \(0.50\,\mathrm{M} \times V_1 = 0.10\,\mathrm{M} \times 250\,\mathrm{mL} \implies V_1 = \frac{25}{0.50} = 50\,\mathrm{mL}\)।
English

Practice Question 8 (Statement-Based MCQ): Read the following two statements carefully:
Statement I: A pure compound always contains the same elements combined in a fixed proportion by mass, regardless of how or where it is prepared.
Statement II: Solid sodium chloride (\(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) consists of discrete neutral molecules, so \(58.5\,\mathrm{u}\) is strictly its molecular mass.

  • (A) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
  • (B) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
  • (C) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
  • (D) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct.
Answer: (C) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
Worked Solution: Statement I is a direct statement of the Law of Definite Proportions. Statement II is incorrect because solid \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) forms an extended 3D ionic lattice of \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) and \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) ions (each \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) surrounded by six \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) ions and vice versa) rather than discrete molecules; hence \(58.5\,\mathrm{u}\) is its formula mass.

अभ्यास प्रश्न 8 (कथन-आधारित MCQ): निम्नलिखित दो कथनों को ध्यानपूर्वक पढ़ें:

कथन II: ठोस सोडियम क्लोराइड (\(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) पृथक उदासीन अणुओं से बना होता है, इसलिए \(58.5\,\mathrm{u}\) यथार्थ रूप से इसका आणविक द्रव्यमान है।

  • (B) कथन I और कथन II दोनों गलत हैं।
  • (C) कथन I सही है, परंतु कथन II गलत है।

9. Common Pitfalls & Exam Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ व पुनरावृत्ति सूची

English
Avoid These Common NEET Mistakes:
  • Confusing molarity (\(M\)) with molality (\(m\)): molarity uses total solution volume (temperature-dependent), whereas molality uses solvent mass (temperature-independent).
  • Using the mass of the excess reactant instead of the limiting reagent when calculating product yield.
  • Forgetting atomicity when counting atoms: \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) contains \(N_A\) molecules but \(2N_A\) oxygen atoms.
  • Confusing trailing zeros after a decimal point (\(0.200\) has 3 sig figs) with leading zeros (\(0.002\) has 1 sig fig).
हिन्दी
NEET परीक्षा में इन सामान्य त्रुटियों से बचें:
  • मोलरता (\(M\)) और मोललता (\(m\)) में भ्रम: मोलरता में पूरे विलयन का आयतन (ताप-निर्भर) तथा मोललता में केवल विलायक का द्रव्यमान (ताप-अनिर्भर) लिया जाता है।
  • उत्पाद की मात्रा निकालते समय सीमांत अभिकर्मक के बजाय आधिक्य (excess) अभिकारक के मोलों का उपयोग कर देना।
  • परमाणु गिनते समय परमाणुकता भूल जाना: \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) में \(N_A\) अणु परंतु \(2N_A\) ऑक्सीजन परमाणु होते हैं।
  • प्रारंभिक शून्यों (\(0.002\) में 1 सार्थक अंक) और दशमलव के बाद के अंतिम शून्यों (\(0.200\) में 3 सार्थक अंक) को एक समझ लेना।
