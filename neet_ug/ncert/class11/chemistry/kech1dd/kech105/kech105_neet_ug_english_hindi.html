NEET UG Syllabus Scope & Learning Objectives

Thermodynamics is one of the most critical high-yield chapters in physical chemistry for NEET UG, contributing typically 2 to 3 direct questions (8–12 marks) each year. It forms the energetic foundation for chemical equilibrium, electrochemistry, and reaction kinetics.

Key Mastery Goals for NEET Candidates:
  • Distinguish rigorously between open, closed, and isolated systems based on matter and energy transfer across boundaries.
  • Differentiate state functions (\(U, H, S, G, T, P, V\)) from path functions (\(q, w\)), and extensive from intensive properties.
  • Apply the First Law of Thermodynamics (\(\Delta U = q + w\)) under IUPAC sign conventions to calculate expansion work, heat, and internal energy changes.
  • Correlate enthalpy change with internal energy change: \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\).
  • Analyze calorimetry principles for bomb calorimeters (\(q_V = \Delta U\)) and constant-pressure coffee-cup calorimeters (\(q_p = \Delta H\)).
  • Master Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation, standard enthalpies of formation, combustion, atomization, bond enthalpies, and the Born-Haber cycle.
  • Formulate spontaneity criteria via the Second Law of Thermodynamics, entropy changes (\(\Delta S = q_{\mathrm{rev}}/T\)), and Gibbs free energy (\(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\)).
  • Interrelate standard Gibbs energy change with the equilibrium constant: \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -RT \ln K = -2.303 RT \log K\).

1. Thermodynamic Terms: System, Surroundings, and Boundaries

Chemical thermodynamics focuses on macroscopic energy transformations accompanying physical transformations and chemical reactions. The subject operates on three foundational definitions:

  • System (निकाय): The specific part of the universe chosen for thermodynamic investigation, where energy and matter changes are observed.
  • Surroundings (परिवेश): The remaining portion of the universe outside the system. For all practical purposes, the surroundings represent the immediate region in the neighbourhood of the system capable of exchanging energy or matter with it.
  • Boundary (परिसीमा): The real or imaginary, rigid or non-rigid, conducting or insulating envelope separating the system from its surroundings.
\[ \text{Universe} = \text{System} + \text{Surroundings} \]

Classification of Thermodynamic Systems

Based on the transfer of matter and energy across the boundary, systems are classified into three distinct categories:

  1. Open System (खुला निकाय): Both energy and matter can cross the boundary between the system and surroundings. Example: Chemical reactants boiling in an open beaker.
  2. Closed System (बंद निकाय): Energy (heat or work) can be transferred across the boundary, but matter cannot enter or leave. Example: A sealed metallic container containing a reacting gas mixture.
  3. Isolated System (विलगित निकाय): Neither matter nor energy can be exchanged between the system and surroundings. The boundary is completely sealed, rigid, and thermally insulating (adiabatic). Example: A reaction mixture in an ideal thermos flask.

2. State of a System, State Functions, and System Properties

The macroscopic condition of a system is defined quantitatively by measurable macroscopic variables, such as pressure (\(p\)), volume (\(V\)), temperature (\(T\)), and amount of substance (\(n\)).

State Functions vs Path Functions

  • State Functions (अवस्था फलन): Properties whose values depend solely upon the current thermodynamic state of the system, completely independent of the path or method used to reach that state. For any cyclic process (\(\oint\)), the change in a state function is zero: \(\oint dX = 0\).
    Examples: Internal energy (\(U\)), Enthalpy (\(H\)), Entropy (\(S\)), Gibbs energy (\(G\)), Temperature (\(T\)), Pressure (\(p\)), Volume (\(V\)).
  • Path Functions (पथ फलन): Quantities whose values depend directly on the route or mechanism by which the change of state is carried out.
    Examples: Heat (\(q\)) and Work (\(w\)). Neither heat nor work is a property of a state; they exist only during a process as energy in transit.

Extensive vs Intensive Properties

Thermodynamic properties are classified by whether they scale with the size of the system:

  • Extensive Properties (विस्तीर्ण या मात्रात्मक गुण): Properties whose numerical values depend directly on the total quantity, mass, or size of matter present in the system.
    Examples: Mass (\(m\)), Volume (\(V\)), Internal energy (\(U\)), Enthalpy (\(H\)), Entropy (\(S\)), Gibbs free energy (\(G\)), Heat capacity (\(C\)).
  • Intensive Properties (गहन या विशिष्ट गुण): Properties whose numerical values are independent of the quantity or size of matter present.
    Examples: Temperature (\(T\)), Pressure (\(p\)), Density (\(d\)), Refractive index, Molar volume (\(V_m = V/n\)), Molar heat capacity (\(C_m = C/n\)), Specific heat capacity (\(c = C/m\)), Electromotive force (\(E_{\text{cell}}\)), Boiling point, Freezing point.
Vital NEET Rule: The ratio of any two extensive properties is always an intensive property!
For example: \(\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass (Extensive)}}{\text{Volume (Extensive)}} = \text{Intensive}\); \(\text{Molar Enthalpy} = \frac{H}{n} = \text{Intensive}\).

2. निकाय की अवस्था, अवस्था फलन एवं गुणधर्म

किसी ऊष्मागतिक निकाय की अवस्था को उसके मापने योग्य स्थूल चरों जैसे दाब (\(p\)), आयतन (\(V\)), ताप (\(T\)) तथा पदार्थ की मात्रा (\(n\)) द्वारा व्यक्त किया जाता है।

अवस्था फलन बनाम पथ फलन

  • अवस्था फलन (State Functions): वे ऊष्मागतिक चर जिनका मान केवल निकाय की वर्तमान अवस्था (प्रारंभिक एवं अंतिम अवस्था) पर निर्भर करता है, इस बात पर नहीं कि वह अवस्था किस प्रकार प्राप्त की गई है। चक्रीय प्रक्रम के लिए इनका कुल परिवर्तन शून्य होता है: \(\oint dX = 0\)।
    उदाहरण: आंतरिक ऊर्जा (\(U\)), एन्थैल्पी (\(H\)), एन्ट्रॉपी (\(S\)), गिब्स ऊर्जा (\(G\)), ताप (\(T\)), दाब (\(p\)), आयतन (\(V\))।
  • पथ फलन (Path Functions): वे राशियाँ जिनका मान उस पथ या विधि पर निर्भर करता है जिसके द्वारा प्रक्रम संपन्न होता है।
    उदाहरण: ऊष्मा (\(q\)) एवं कार्य (\(w\))। ऊष्मा और कार्य किसी निकाय के आंतरिक गुण नहीं हैं, बल्कि ये प्रक्रम के दौरान ऊर्जा स्थानांतरण के रूप हैं।

विस्तीर्ण (मात्रात्मक) बनाम गहन (विशिष्ट) गुण

पदार्थ के परिमाण पर निर्भरता के आधार पर गुणों को दो वर्गों में विभाजित किया जाता है:

  • विस्तीर्ण गुण (Extensive Properties): वे गुण जिनका मान निकाय में उपस्थित पदार्थ के द्रव्यमान या परिमाण (आकार) पर निर्भर करता है।
    उदाहरण: द्रव्यमान (\(m\)), आयतन (\(V\)), आंतरिक ऊर्जा (\(U\)), एन्थैल्पी (\(H\)), एन्ट्रॉपी (\(S\)), गिब्स ऊर्जा (\(G\)), ऊष्मा धारिता (\(C\))।
  • गहन गुण (Intensive Properties): वे गुण जिनका मान निकाय में उपस्थित पदार्थ के परिमाण या आकार पर निर्भर नहीं करता।
    उदाहरण: ताप (\(T\)), दाब (\(p\)), घनत्व (\(d\)), अपवर्तनांक, मोलर आयतन (\(V_m\)), मोलर ऊष्मा धारिता (\(C_m\)), विशिष्ट ऊष्मा (\(c\)), मानक इलेक्ट्रोड विभव (\(E^\circ\))।

3. The First Law of Thermodynamics, Internal Energy, and PV Work

The First Law of Thermodynamics states that energy can neither be created nor destroyed; the total energy of an isolated system remains constant.

Internal Energy (\(U\))

Internal energy is the grand total of all microscopic kinetic and potential energy forms associated with molecules, atoms, electrons, and nuclei in a system. While its absolute value cannot be determined, its change (\(\Delta U = U_2 - U_1\)) is an experimentally measurable state function.

IUPAC Sign Conventions

Thermodynamic Quantity Sign (+ve) Sign (−ve)
Heat (\(q\)) Heat absorbed by system from surroundings (\(q > 0\)) Heat released by system to surroundings (\(q < 0\))
Work (\(w\)) Work done on the system (Compression, \(w > 0\)) Work done by the system (Expansion, \(w < 0\))
Internal Energy Change (\(\Delta U\)) Internal energy increases (\(\Delta U > 0\)) Internal energy decreases (\(\Delta U < 0\))
Mathematical Statement of the First Law: \[ \Delta U = q + w \] Special Cases:
  • Adiabatic Process (\(q = 0\)): \(\Delta U = w_{\text{ad}}\). (Work done on system increases its temperature; work done by system cools it).
  • Isochoric Process (\(\Delta V = 0\)): Since expansion work \(w = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V = 0\), \(\Delta U = q_V\).
  • Cyclic Process (\(\Delta U = 0\)): \(q = -w\).

Pressure-Volume (\(PV\)) Work

Consider a gas enclosed in a cylinder fitted with a frictionless, movable piston of area \(A\). If external opposing pressure is \(p_{\text{ex}}\), the mechanical work done upon volume change \(dV\) is:

\[ w = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f} p_{\text{ex}} dV \]

1. Irreversible Expansion against Constant External Pressure

When expansion or compression occurs against a constant external pressure \(p_{\text{ex}}\) in a single step:

\[ w = -p_{\text{ex}} (V_f - V_i) = -p_{\text{ex}} \Delta V \]

2. Reversible Isothermal Expansion of an Ideal Gas

In a reversible process, the external pressure matches the internal gas pressure infinitesimally at every stage: \(p_{\text{ex}} = p_{\text{in}} \pm dp\). Substituting \(p = \frac{nRT}{V}\):

\[ w_{\text{rev}} = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{nRT}{V} dV = -nRT \ln \frac{V_f}{V_i} = -2.303 nRT \log_{10} \left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \] Using Boyle's law at constant temperature (\(p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2\)): \[ w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 nRT \log_{10} \left(\frac{p_i}{p_f}\right) \]

3. Free Expansion (\(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\))

When a gas expands into a vacuum, there is no opposing force: \(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\). Therefore, no mechanical work is done (\(w = 0\)). For an ideal gas undergoing isothermal free expansion, Joule proved that \(q = 0\) and consequently \(\Delta U = 0\).

3. ऊष्मागतिकी का प्रथम नियम, आंतरिक ऊर्जा एवं दाब-आयतन कार्य

ऊष्मागतिकी के प्रथम नियम के अनुसार, ऊर्जा न तो उत्पन्न की जा सकती है और न ही नष्ट; किसी विलगित निकाय की कुल ऊर्जा सदैव स्थिर रहती है।

आंतरिक ऊर्जा (\(U\))

आंतरिक ऊर्जा निकाय में उपस्थित सभी आणविक, परमाण्विक, इलेक्ट्रॉनिक तथा नाभिकीय गतिज व स्थितिज ऊर्जाओं का कुल योग है। इसका निरपेक्ष मान ज्ञात नहीं किया जा सकता, केवल परिवर्तन (\(\Delta U\)) मापा जा सकता है।

IUPAC चिन्ह परिपाटी

ऊष्मागतिक राशि धनात्मक (+ve) चिन्ह ऋणात्मक (−ve) चिन्ह
ऊष्मा (\(q\)) निकाय द्वारा परिवेश से अवशोषित ऊष्मा (\(q > 0\)) निकाय द्वारा परिवेश को दी गई ऊष्मा (\(q < 0\))
कार्य (\(w\)) निकाय पर किया गया कार्य (संपीड़न, \(w > 0\)) निकाय द्वारा किया गया कार्य (प्रसार, \(w < 0\))
आंतरिक ऊर्जा (\(\Delta U\)) आंतरिक ऊर्जा में वृद्धि (\(\Delta U > 0\)) आंतरिक ऊर्जा में कमी (\(\Delta U < 0\))
प्रथम नियम का गणितीय रूप: \[ \Delta U = q + w \] विशेष स्थितियाँ:
  • रुद्धोष्म प्रक्रम (Adiabatic Process, \(q = 0\)): \(\Delta U = w_{\text{ad}}\)। (निकाय पर कार्य करने से ताप बढ़ता है)।
  • समआयतनिक प्रक्रम (Isochoric Process, \(\Delta V = 0\)): \(w = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V = 0 \implies \Delta U = q_V\)।
  • चक्रीय प्रक्रम (Cyclic Process, \(\Delta U = 0\)): \(q = -w\)।

दाब-आयतन कार्य (\(PV\) कार्य)

यदि कोई गैस स्थिर बाह्य दाब \(p_{\text{ex}}\) के विरुद्ध प्रसारित होती है, तो किया गया कार्य:

\[ w = -p_{\text{ex}} (V_f - V_i) = -p_{\text{ex}} \Delta V \]

आदर्श गैस का उत्क्रमणीय समतापीय प्रसार

उत्क्रमणीय समतापीय प्रक्रम में गैस द्वारा किया गया अधिकतम कार्य:

\[ w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 nRT \log_{10} \left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303 nRT \log_{10} \left(\frac{p_i}{p_f}\right) \]

मुक्त प्रसार (Free Expansion in Vacuum)

निर्वात में गैस के प्रसार हेतु बाह्य दाब शून्य होता है (\(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\))। अतः मुक्त प्रसार में किया गया कार्य शून्य होता है (\(w = 0\))। समतापीय मुक्त प्रसार के लिए \(q = 0\) तथा \(\Delta U = 0\)।

4. Enthalpy (\(H\)), \(\Delta H\) vs \(\Delta U\), and Heat Capacities

Most chemical laboratory and industrial reactions occur in open vessels under constant atmospheric pressure rather than constant volume. Under constant pressure, a system may expand or contract, performing mechanical work against the atmosphere. To measure heat changes under these practical conditions, we define Enthalpy (\(H\)):

\[ H = U + pV \] At constant pressure: \[ \Delta H = \Delta U + p\Delta V = q_p \]

Thus, heat absorbed at constant pressure (\(q_p\)) equals the change in enthalpy (\(\Delta H\)), while heat absorbed at constant volume (\(q_V\)) equals the change in internal energy (\(\Delta U\)).

Relationship between \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta U\) for Gas Reactions

For chemical reactions involving ideal gases at constant temperature and pressure:

\[ p\Delta V = p(V_{\text{products}} - V_{\text{reactants}}) = (n_p - n_r)RT = \Delta n_g RT \] \[ \Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT \] where \(\Delta n_g = \sum n_g(\text{gaseous products}) - \sum n_g(\text{gaseous reactants})\).
Significance of \(\Delta n_g\):
  • If \(\Delta n_g = 0\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2(g) + I_2(g) \rightarrow 2HI(g)}\)), then \(\mathbf{\Delta H = \Delta U}\).
  • If \(\Delta n_g > 0\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{PCl_5(g) \rightarrow PCl_3(g) + Cl_2(g)}\)), then \(\mathbf{\Delta H > \Delta U}\).
  • If \(\Delta n_g < 0\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)}\)), then \(\mathbf{\Delta H < \Delta U}\).
  • For condensed phase systems (solids and liquids only), volume change is negligible (\(\Delta V \approx 0\)), so \(\Delta H \approx \Delta U\).

Heat Capacity and the Relation \(C_p - C_v = R\)

The heat capacity (\(C\)) is the heat required to raise the temperature of a sample by \(1\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) (or \(1\,\mathrm{K}\)): \(q = C \Delta T\).

  • Specific Heat Capacity (\(c\)): Heat capacity per unit mass (\(\mathrm{J\,g^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)): \(q = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T\).
  • Molar Heat Capacity (\(C_m\)): Heat capacity per mole (\(\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)): \(q = n \cdot C_m \cdot \Delta T\).

At constant volume: \(q_V = \Delta U = n C_v \Delta T \implies C_v = \left(\frac{\partial U}{\partial T}\right)_V\).

At constant pressure: \(q_p = \Delta H = n C_p \Delta T \implies C_p = \left(\frac{\partial H}{\partial T}\right)_p\).

Mayer's Relation for 1 mole of an Ideal Gas:

Since \(H = U + pV = U + RT\) for 1 mole of an ideal gas:

\[ \Delta H = \Delta U + R\Delta T \] \[ C_p \Delta T = C_v \Delta T + R\Delta T \implies \mathbf{C_p - C_v = R} \] Note: \(C_p > C_v\) because at constant pressure, part of the heat supplied is consumed in doing expansion work against the surroundings in addition to raising the internal energy (temperature).

5. Experimental Measurement of \(\Delta U\) and \(\Delta H\): Calorimetry

Calorimetry is the experimental procedure of determining heat transferred in physical or chemical transformations by monitoring the temperature rise (\(\Delta T\)) of a calibrated system.

1. \(\Delta U\) Measurement: The Bomb Calorimeter (Constant Volume)

  • Apparatus: A strong, rigid, sealed stainless steel vessel (the "bomb") immersed in a known mass of water inside an insulated outer container.
  • Procedure: A combustible sample is weighed in a platinum/sample cup, pressurized with pure oxygen (\(\approx 30\,\mathrm{atm}\)), and electrically ignited via firing leads.
  • Condition: Because the bomb is rigid and sealed, the volume is strictly constant (\(\Delta V = 0\)). Thus, mechanical work \(w = -p\Delta V = 0\).
  • Energy Equation: \[ q_{\text{reaction}} = -C_{\text{cal}} \Delta T \] where \(C_{\text{cal}}\) is the total calibrated heat capacity of the calorimeter assembly. \[ \Delta U = q_V = \frac{q_{\text{reaction}}}{n} \]

2. \(\Delta H\) Measurement: Constant Pressure Calorimeter

  • Apparatus: A nested foamed polystyrene (coffee-cup) calorimeter open to atmospheric pressure.
  • Procedure: Typically used for reactions in aqueous solution (neutralization, precipitation, solution formation).
  • Condition: Pressure is constant (\(p = 1\,\mathrm{atm}\)). Therefore, the measured heat exchange directly equals the enthalpy of reaction: \[ q_p = \Delta_r H = -m_{\text{soln}} \cdot c_s \cdot \Delta T \]

6. Thermochemistry, Standard States, and Hess's Law

The enthalpy change accompanying a chemical reaction is represented as the Reaction Enthalpy (\(\Delta_r H\)):

\[ \Delta_r H = \sum a_i H_{\text{products}} - \sum b_i H_{\text{reactants}} \] where \(a_i\) and \(b_i\) are the stoichiometric coefficients of products and reactants in the balanced chemical equation.

Standard State (मानक अवस्था)

The standard state of a substance at a specified temperature (usually \(298.15\,\mathrm{K}\)) is its pure, most stable aggregate form under a pressure of exactly 1 bar (earlier \(1\,\mathrm{atm}\)). Standard quantities are denoted by the superscript \(\ominus\) or \(^\circ\).

Enthalpy Changes during Phase Transformations

Transformation Process Equation Symbol & Definition
Enthalpy of Fusion \(\mathrm{H_2O(s) \rightarrow H_2O(l)}\) \(\Delta_{\mathrm{fus}}H^\ominus = +6.01\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) at \(273\,\mathrm{K}\). Enthalpy change when 1 mol solid melts at 1 bar.
Enthalpy of Vaporization \(\mathrm{H_2O(l) \rightarrow H_2O(g)}\) \(\Delta_{\mathrm{vap}}H^\ominus = +40.79\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) at \(373\,\mathrm{K}\) (or \(+44.01\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\)). Enthalpy change when 1 mol liquid boils/vaporizes at 1 bar.
Enthalpy of Sublimation \(\mathrm{CO_2(s) \rightarrow CO_2(g)}\) \(\Delta_{\mathrm{sub}}H^\ominus = \Delta_{\mathrm{fus}}H^\ominus + \Delta_{\mathrm{vap}}H^\ominus\). Enthalpy change when 1 mol solid sublimes directly into gas.

Standard Enthalpy of Formation (\(\Delta_f H^\ominus\))

The standard molar enthalpy of formation is the enthalpy change accompanying the formation of one mole of a compound from its constituent elements in their most stable reference states of aggregation at \(298.15\,\mathrm{K}\) and \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\).

Fundamental Convention: By thermodynamic convention, the standard enthalpy of formation of every pure element in its reference state is identically zero: \[ \Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{O_2(g)}] = 0, \quad \Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{H_2(g)}] = 0, \quad \Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{C(graphite)}] = 0, \quad \Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{Br_2(l)}] = 0, \quad \Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{S(rhombic)}] = 0 \] Note for NEET: Allotropes not in reference state have non-zero values (e.g., \(\Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{C(diamond)}] = +1.89\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\); \(\Delta_f H^\ominus [\mathrm{Br_2(g)}] = +30.91\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)).
Calculation of Reaction Enthalpy from Enthalpies of Formation: \[ \Delta_r H^\ominus = \sum a_i \Delta_f H^\ominus (\text{products}) - \sum b_i \Delta_f H^\ominus (\text{reactants}) \]

Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation

Because enthalpy is a state function, if a chemical reaction can take place in several steps, its overall standard reaction enthalpy is the sum of the standard enthalpies of the intermediate reactions into which the overall reaction may be divided at the same temperature.

\[ \Delta_r H = \Delta H_1 + \Delta H_2 + \Delta H_3 + \dots \]

7. Enthalpies of Different Reactions, Bond Enthalpies, and Born-Haber Cycle

1. Standard Enthalpy of Combustion (\(\Delta_c H^\ominus\))

The standard enthalpy of combustion is the enthalpy change per mole of a substance when it undergoes complete oxidation in excess dioxygen, with all reactants and products in standard states. Combustion is always exothermic (\(\Delta_c H^\ominus < 0\)).

\[ \mathrm{CH_4(g) + 2O_2(g) \rightarrow CO_2(g) + 2H_2O(l)}, \quad \Delta_c H^\ominus = -890.3\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \] \[ \mathrm{C_6H_6(l) + \frac{15}{2}O_2(g) \rightarrow 6CO_2(g) + 3H_2O(l)}, \quad \Delta_c H^\ominus = -3267.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]

2. Enthalpy of Atomization (\(\Delta_a H^\ominus\))

The enthalpy change accompanying the complete cleavage of all bonds in one mole of a substance to obtain isolated atoms in the gaseous phase.

  • For diatomic gases: \(\mathrm{H_2(g) \rightarrow 2H(g)}\), \(\Delta_a H^\ominus = +435.0\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) (identical to bond dissociation enthalpy).
  • For polyatomic molecules: \(\mathrm{CH_4(g) \rightarrow C(g) + 4H(g)}\), \(\Delta_a H^\ominus = +1665\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).
  • For metallic solids: \(\mathrm{Na(s) \rightarrow Na(g)}\), \(\Delta_a H^\ominus = +108.4\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) (identical to enthalpy of sublimation).

3. Bond Dissociation Enthalpy vs Mean Bond Enthalpy

  • Bond Dissociation Enthalpy: The enthalpy required to break one mole of a specific bond in a gaseous covalent compound. In polyatomic molecules like \(\mathrm{CH_4}\), successive \(\mathrm{C-H}\) bond ruptures require different energies (\(427, 439, 452, 347\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)).
  • Mean Bond Enthalpy: The arithmetic average of bond dissociation energies across all identical bonds in the molecule: \[ \Delta_{\mathrm{C-H}}H^\ominus = \frac{1}{4} \Delta_a H^\ominus [\mathrm{CH_4}] = \frac{1665}{4} = 416.25\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]
Reaction Enthalpy from Bond Enthalpies (All Gaseous Species): \[ \mathbf{\Delta_r H^\ominus = \sum \text{Bond Enthalpies (Reactants)} - \sum \text{Bond Enthalpies (Products)}} \]

4. Lattice Enthalpy and Born-Haber Cycle

The lattice enthalpy (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus\)) of an ionic solid is the enthalpy change required to dissociate one mole of an ionic crystalline solid into isolated gaseous ions infinitely far apart:

\[ \mathrm{NaCl(s) \rightarrow Na^+(g) + Cl^-(g)}, \quad \Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus = +788\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]

Lattice enthalpies cannot be measured directly by calorimetry. Instead, we use the Born-Haber Cycle (an enthalpy loop applying Hess's Law):

\[ \Delta_f H^\ominus = \Delta_{\text{sub}}H^\ominus(\mathrm{Na}) + \Delta_i H^\ominus(\mathrm{Na}) + \frac{1}{2}\Delta_{\text{bond}}H^\ominus(\mathrm{Cl_2}) + \Delta_{\text{eg}}H^\ominus(\mathrm{Cl}) - \Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus \]

5. Enthalpy of Solution (\(\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus\)) and Hydration

When an ionic solid dissolves in water, the crystal lattice must be dismantled (endothermic, \(\Delta_{\text{lattice}}H > 0\)) and the resulting gaseous ions undergo hydration by water dipoles (exothermic, \(\Delta_{\text{hyd}}H < 0\)):

\[ \mathbf{\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus = \Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus + \Delta_{\text{hyd}}H^\ominus} \] For \(\mathrm{NaCl}\): \(\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus = (+788) + (-784) = +4\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) (slightly endothermic, solubility increases with temperature).

8. Spontaneity, Entropy, and the Second Law of Thermodynamics

A spontaneous process is an irreversible process that has the natural potential to proceed on its own without continuous assistance from an external agency. Spontaneity dictates the direction of feasibility, not the kinetic rate.

Why Enthalpy Decrease (\(\Delta H < 0\)) Alone Cannot Explain Spontaneity

Although many spontaneous reactions are exothermic (minimization of enthalpy), many endothermic processes proceed spontaneously:

  • Evaporation of water at room temperature (\(\Delta H > 0\)).
  • Dissolution of \(\mathrm{NH_4Cl(s)}\) in water (\(\Delta H > 0\)).
  • Spontaneous inter-diffusion of two ideal gases isolated from the surroundings (\(\Delta H = 0, \Delta U = 0\)).

Therefore, energy minimization is not the only driving force. Nature possesses a second fundamental driving force: maximization of disorder or randomness.

Entropy (\(S\))

Entropy is a thermodynamic state function that measures the degree of disorder, randomness, or chaotic distribution of energy in a system.

Thermodynamic Definition of Entropy Change: \[ \Delta S = \frac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T} \] Units: \(\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\).

Physical Significance: Heat added at a lower temperature creates vastly greater relative chaos than the same quantity of heat added at a high temperature. Thus, \(\Delta S\) is inversely proportional to \(T\).

The Second Law of Thermodynamics

The entropy of the universe (system + surroundings) always increases in every spontaneous (irreversible) process.

\[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} > 0 \quad (\text{Spontaneous Process}) \] \[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} = 0 \quad (\text{System at Equilibrium}) \] \[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} < 0 \quad (\text{Non-spontaneous Process}) \]

Qualitative Prediction of Entropy Change (\(\Delta S\))

  • Phase Transitions: \(S_{\text{solid}} < S_{\text{liquid}} \ll S_{\text{gas}}\). Melting and boiling increase entropy (\(\Delta S > 0\)).
  • Dissociation: \(\mathrm{H_2(g) \rightarrow 2H(g)}\) increases particle count \(\implies \Delta S > 0\).
  • Decomposition: \(2\mathrm{NaHCO_3(s) \rightarrow Na_2CO_3(s) + CO_2(g) + H_2O(g)}\) produces gas moles \(\implies \Delta S > 0\).
  • Cooling/Freezing: Crystallization decreases randomness \(\implies \Delta S < 0\).

9. Gibbs Energy (\(G\)), Spontaneity Criteria, and the Third Law

Chemical reactions occur under conditions of constant temperature and constant pressure in open or closed vessels. To evaluate spontaneity using system properties alone (without computing surroundings entropy), Josiah Willard Gibbs introduced the Gibbs Energy (\(G\)):

\[ G = H - TS \] At constant temperature and pressure: \[ \mathbf{\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S} \] This is the celebrated Gibbs-Helmholtz Equation.

Derivation of the Spontaneity Criterion

Heat released by the system to the surroundings at constant pressure is \(q_{\text{surr}} = -\Delta H_{\text{sys}}\). Therefore, \(\Delta S_{\text{surr}} = -\frac{\Delta H_{\text{sys}}}{T}\).

\[ \Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} - \frac{\Delta H_{\text{sys}}}{T} \] Multiplying through by \(-T\): \[ -T\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta H_{\text{sys}} - T\Delta S_{\text{sys}} = \Delta G_{\text{sys}} \] Since \(\Delta S_{\text{total}} > 0\) for a spontaneous change:
Criteria for Spontaneity at Constant \(T\) and \(p\):
  • \(\mathbf{\Delta G < 0}\): Process is spontaneous in the forward direction.
  • \(\mathbf{\Delta G = 0}\): System is in dynamic equilibrium.
  • \(\mathbf{\Delta G > 0}\): Process is non-spontaneous in the forward direction (reverse process is spontaneous).

Effect of Temperature on Reaction Spontaneity

\(\Delta_r H^\ominus\) \(\Delta_r S^\ominus\) \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = \Delta H - T\Delta S\) Spontaneity & Temperature Dependence Example Process
− (Exo) + (Disorder \(\uparrow\)) Always − Spontaneous at all temperatures \(2\mathrm{O_3(g) \rightarrow 3O_2(g)}\)
− (Exo) − (Order \(\uparrow\)) − at low \(T\); + at high \(T\) Spontaneous only at LOW temperature (Enthalpy-driven, \(|\Delta H| > |T\Delta S|\)) \(\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightarrow 2NH_3(g)}\); Freezing of water
+ (Endo) + (Disorder \(\uparrow\)) + at low \(T\); − at high \(T\) Spontaneous only at HIGH temperature (Entropy-driven, \(T\Delta S > \Delta H\)) \(\mathrm{CaCO_3(s) \rightarrow CaO(s) + CO_2(g)}\); Vaporization of water
+ (Endo) − (Order \(\uparrow\)) Always + Non-spontaneous at all temperatures \(3\mathrm{O_2(g) \rightarrow 2O_3(g)}\)
Equilibrium Temperature: The transition temperature where spontaneity flips (\(\Delta G = 0\)) is calculated as: \[ T_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\Delta H}{\Delta S} \]

The Third Law of Thermodynamics

The entropy of any perfectly crystalline, pure substance approaches zero as the absolute temperature approaches zero Kelvin (\(0\,\mathrm{K}\)).

\[ \lim_{T \to 0} S = 0 \quad (\text{for pure crystalline solids}) \]

Importance: Unlike internal energy or enthalpy, the Third Law permits the determination of absolute molar entropies (\(S^\ominus\)) of substances from purely thermal experimental data: \[ S_T = \int_0^T \frac{C_p}{T} dT \]

10. Gibbs Energy Change and Chemical Equilibrium Constant

For any reversible reaction \(a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \rightleftharpoons c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}\), the real-time reaction Gibbs energy (\(\Delta_r G\)) under non-standard conditions is related to the reaction quotient (\(Q\)) by the thermodynamic isotherm:

\[ \Delta_r G = \Delta_r G^\ominus + RT \ln Q \] At chemical equilibrium, the net driving force vanishes: \(\mathbf{\Delta_r G = 0}\) and \(Q = K\) (the thermodynamic equilibrium constant). \[ 0 = \Delta_r G^\ominus + RT \ln K \] \[ \mathbf{\Delta_r G^\ominus = -RT \ln K = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K} \]

Physical Insights for NEET Problem-Solving

  • If \(\Delta_r G^\ominus < 0\), then \(-RT \ln K < 0 \implies \ln K > 0 \implies \mathbf{K > 1}\). The products predominate heavily at equilibrium; the forward reaction is highly favourable under standard conditions.
  • If \(\Delta_r G^\ominus > 0\), then \(\mathbf{K < 1}\). Reactants predominate; very little product is formed under standard conditions.
  • If \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = 0\), then \(\mathbf{K = 1}\).
Crucial Conceptual Distinction:
\(\mathbf{\Delta_r G}\) is the driving force under the prevailing conditions. At equilibrium, \(\mathbf{\Delta_r G = 0}\) always.
\(\mathbf{\Delta_r G^\ominus}\) is the change in Gibbs energy when all reactants and products are in their 1 bar standard states. It is a constant at a given temperature and is NOT necessarily zero at equilibrium!

\[ \Delta_r G = \Delta_r G^\ominus + RT \ln Q \] साम्यावस्था पर \(\mathbf{\Delta_r G = 0}\) तथा \(Q = K\): \[ \mathbf{\Delta_r G^\ominus = -RT \ln K = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K} \]

NEET हेतु मुख्य निष्कर्ष

  • यदि \(\Delta_r G^\ominus > 0\), तो \(\mathbf{K < 1}\) (अभिकारक अधिक मात्रा में रहते हैं)।
  • अति-महत्वपूर्ण अंतर: साम्यावस्था पर \(\mathbf{\Delta_r G = 0}\) होता है, परंतु \(\mathbf{\Delta_r G^\ominus}\) का शून्य होना आवश्यक नहीं है!

Example 1 (English)
Worked Example 1: Expansion Work under Varied Conditions

Two litres of an ideal gas at an initial pressure of \(10\,\mathrm{atm}\) expands isothermally at \(25\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) until its total volume reaches \(10\,\mathrm{L}\). Calculate the heat absorbed (\(q\)) and work done (\(w\)) if the expansion occurs:

  1. Into a vacuum (free expansion).
  2. Against a constant external pressure of \(1\,\mathrm{atm}\).
  3. Reversibly for \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of gas.
Step-by-Step Solution:

For an isothermal process of an ideal gas, temperature is constant, so internal energy change \(\Delta U = 0\). From the First Law, \(\Delta U = q + w = 0 \implies q = -w\).

Part (a): Free expansion into vacuum (\(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\)):

\[ w = -p_{\text{ex}} \Delta V = -(0)(10 - 2)\,\mathrm{L\,atm} = 0 \] \[ q = -w = 0 \]

Conclusion: No work is done and no heat is absorbed.

Part (b): Irreversible expansion against \(p_{\text{ex}} = 1\,\mathrm{atm}\):

\[ w = -p_{\text{ex}} (V_f - V_i) = -(1\,\mathrm{atm})(10\,\mathrm{L} - 2\,\mathrm{L}) = -8\,\mathrm{L\,atm} \] \[ q = -w = +8\,\mathrm{L\,atm} \] Converting to Joules (\(1\,\mathrm{L\,atm} = 101.325\,\mathrm{J}\)): \[ w = -8 \times 101.325 = -810.6\,\mathrm{J}, \quad q = +810.6\,\mathrm{J} \]

Part (c): Reversible isothermal expansion:

\[ w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 nRT \log_{10} \left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) \] \[ w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 \times (1\,\mathrm{mol}) \times (8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}) \times (298.15\,\mathrm{K}) \times \log_{10} \left(\frac{10}{2}\right) \] Since \(\log_{10} 5 = 0.6990\): \[ w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 \times 8.314 \times 298.15 \times 0.6990 = -3988\,\mathrm{J} = -3.988\,\mathrm{kJ} \] \[ q_{\text{rev}} = -w_{\text{rev}} = +3.988\,\mathrm{kJ} \]

Example 2 (English)
Worked Example 2: Correlating \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta U\) in Vaporization

Assuming water vapour to behave as an ideal gas, the molar enthalpy of vaporization of water at \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) and \(100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) is \(41.00\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\). Calculate the internal energy change (\(\Delta U\)) when \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of liquid water is vaporized at \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) and \(100\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\).

Step-by-Step Solution:

The phase transition equation is:

\[ \mathrm{H_2O(l) \rightarrow H_2O(g)} \]

Change in moles of gas:

\[ \Delta n_g = n_g(\text{products}) - n_g(\text{reactants}) = 1 - 0 = 1\,\mathrm{mol} \]

Absolute temperature: \(T = 100 + 273.15 = 373.15\,\mathrm{K}\).

Applying the relation \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\):

\[ \Delta U = \Delta H - \Delta n_g RT \] \[ \Delta n_g RT = (1\,\mathrm{mol}) \times (8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}) \times (373.15\,\mathrm{K}) = 3102.4\,\mathrm{J} \approx 3.10\,\mathrm{kJ} \] \[ \Delta U = 41.00\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} - 3.10\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} = 37.90\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]

Insight: Out of the \(41.00\,\mathrm{kJ}\) of heat energy supplied, \(37.90\,\mathrm{kJ}\) goes into overcoming intermolecular hydrogen bonds (increasing internal energy), while \(3.10\,\mathrm{kJ}\) is expended as expansion work (\(p\Delta V\)) pushing back the atmosphere.

13. Common NEET Pitfalls & High-Yield Revision Checklist

Frequent Conceptual Traps in NEET Thermodynamics:
  1. Confusing Sign Conventions: Remember IUPAC convention: Work done by the gas (expansion) is negative (\(w < 0\)); Work done on the gas (compression) is positive (\(w > 0\)). Heat absorbed is \(q > 0\); heat released is \(q < 0\).
  2. Gaseous Moles (\(\Delta n_g\)) Errors: In \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\), count only gaseous species. Liquids (\(l\)) and solids (\(s\)) have zero contribution to \(\Delta n_g\). For water produced at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) is liquid!
  3. Unit Inconsistency in \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\): \(\Delta H\) is almost always given in \(\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\), while \(\Delta S\) is given in \(\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\). Always convert \(\Delta S\) to \(\mathrm{kJ\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\) (divide by \(1000\)) or \(\Delta H\) to Joules before subtracting!
  4. Confusing \(\Delta_r G\) with \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\): At equilibrium, \(\Delta_r G = 0\), but \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -RT \ln K\), which is non-zero unless \(K = 1\).
  5. Extensive vs Intensive Confusion: Molar or specific quantities (\(C_m, V_m, c\)) are intensive even though the total quantities (\(C, V\)) are extensive.
  6. Bond Enthalpy Formula Order: For bond enthalpies, it is \(\sum \text{B.E.}(\text{reactants}) - \sum \text{B.E.}(\text{products})\). Do NOT invert this like \(\Delta_f H^\ominus\)!
High-Yield Formula Quick Sheet:
  • First Law: \(\Delta U = q + w\)
  • Expansion Work (constant \(p_{\text{ex}}\)): \(w = -p_{\text{ex}} \Delta V\)
  • Reversible Isothermal Work: \(w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303 nRT \log_{10}(V_f / V_i) = -2.303 nRT \log_{10}(p_i / p_f)\)
  • Enthalpy: \(H = U + pV \implies \Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\)
  • Mayer's Equation: \(C_p - C_v = R\) (for \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) ideal gas)
  • Calorimetry: \(q_V = \Delta U = -C_{\text{cal}}\Delta T\); \(q_p = \Delta H = -m c \Delta T\)
  • Hess's Law: \(\Delta_r H^\ominus = \sum a_i \Delta_f H^\ominus(\text{products}) - \sum b_i \Delta_f H^\ominus(\text{reactants})\)
  • Reaction Enthalpy from Bonds: \(\Delta_r H^\ominus = \sum \text{B.E.}(\text{reactants}) - \sum \text{B.E.}(\text{products})\)
  • Lattice & Hydration: \(\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus = \Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus + \Delta_{\text{hyd}}H^\ominus\)
  • Entropy Definition: \(\Delta S = \frac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T}\)
  • Spontaneity Condition: \(\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0\)
  • Gibbs Equation: \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\) (\(\Delta G < 0\) for spontaneous)
  • Equilibrium Constant: \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K\)

14. Concise Final Recap

Thermodynamics governs chemical feasibility through energy conservation and universal entropy expansion. In open systems both energy and matter move; closed systems transfer only energy; isolated systems transfer neither. Internal energy (\(U\)) reflects total molecular energy, and its change governs the First Law (\(\Delta U = q + w\)). Under constant pressure, heat exchanged becomes Enthalpy (\(\Delta H = q_p = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\)). Bomb calorimetry monitors \(\Delta U\) at constant volume, while coffee-cup calorimetry measures \(\Delta H\) at constant pressure. Standard formation enthalpies (\(\Delta_f H^\ominus\)) of pure reference elements are zero, enabling Hess's law to determine any reaction enthalpy. Spontaneity is dictated by total entropy (\(\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0\)), consolidated at constant \(T\) and \(p\) into the Gibbs free energy (\(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\)). At absolute zero, crystalline entropy vanishes (Third Law). Finally, standard free energy quantitatively couples to the chemical equilibrium constant via \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K\).

14. अंतिम त्वरित सारांश

ऊष्मागतिकी ऊर्जा संरक्षण तथा ब्रह्मांडीय एन्ट्रॉपी विस्तार के नियमों द्वारा रासायनिक प्रक्रमों की संभाव्यता निर्धारित करती है। खुले निकाय में द्रव्य व ऊर्जा दोनों, बंद निकाय में केवल ऊर्जा तथा विलगित निकाय में किसी का विनिमय नहीं होता। आंतरिक ऊर्जा परिवर्तन प्रथम नियम (\(\Delta U = q + w\)) का आधार है। स्थिर दाब पर उत्सर्जित या अवशोषित ऊष्मा एन्थैल्पी (\(\Delta H = q_p = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\)) कहलाती है। बॉम्ब कैलोरीमीटर स्थिर आयतन पर \(\Delta U\) तथा कॉफी कप कैलोरीमीटर स्थिर दाब पर \(\Delta H\) मापता है। संदर्भ अवस्था में तत्वों की विरचन एन्थैल्पी शून्य होती है, जिससे हेस के नियम द्वारा अभिक्रिया एन्थैल्पी ज्ञात की जाती है। स्वतःप्रवर्तिता की अंतिम कसौटी गिब्स मुक्त ऊर्जा (\(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\)) है। परम शून्य ताप पर शुद्ध क्रिस्टल की एन्ट्रॉपी शून्य होती है (तृतीय नियम)। मानक गिब्स ऊर्जा साम्य स्थिरांक से \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K\) द्वारा संबंधित होती है।
