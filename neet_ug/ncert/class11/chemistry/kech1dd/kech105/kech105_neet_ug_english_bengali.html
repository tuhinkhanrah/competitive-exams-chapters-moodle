1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

Chemical thermodynamics investigates how energy transforms during physical and chemical processes, and whether a given transformation can occur on its own. Unlike chemical kinetics, which studies reaction speed, thermodynamics deals strictly with macroscopic equilibrium states—the initial and final conditions of a system.

By mastering this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish between open, closed, and isolated systems, as well as intensive and extensive properties.
  • Apply the First Law of Thermodynamics (\(\Delta U = q + w\)) using IUPAC sign conventions for heat and pressure–volume work.
  • Relate enthalpy change (\(\Delta H\)) and internal energy change (\(\Delta U\)) for gaseous reactions and calorimetry experiments.
  • Calculate reaction enthalpies using Hess's Law, standard enthalpies of formation, bond enthalpies, and Born–Haber cycles.
  • Predict reaction spontaneity and equilibrium constants using entropy (\(\Delta S\)), the Second and Third Laws, and Gibbs free energy (\(\Delta G\)).

2. Thermodynamic Foundations: Systems, Variables & Properties

To analyze energy flow, we divide the universe into two parts separated by a real or imaginary boundary:

  • System: The specific part of the universe chosen for observation (for example, reactants inside a flask).
  • Surroundings: Everything outside the boundary that can interact with the system. Together, \(\text{Universe} = \text{System} + \text{Surroundings}\).
System Type Matter Exchange Energy Exchange Example
Open System Allowed Allowed Hot tea in an uncovered cup; reactants in an open beaker
Closed System Not allowed Allowed Gas in a sealed conducting steel cylinder
Isolated System Not allowed Not allowed Hot water inside a perfectly insulated thermos flask
Tutorial Diagram 1: Classification of thermodynamic systems based on matter and energy exchange across the boundary.

State Functions vs. Path Functions

  • State Functions: Properties whose values depend solely on the current state of the system, not on how that state was reached. Examples: Pressure (\(p\)), Volume (\(V\)), Temperature (\(T\)), Internal Energy (\(U\)), Enthalpy (\(H\)), Entropy (\(S\)), Gibbs Energy (\(G\)). For any cyclic process, the net change in a state function is zero (\(\oint dX = 0\)).
  • Path Functions: Quantities that depend on the route taken between initial and final states. Examples: Heat (\(q\)) and Work (\(w\)).

Extensive vs. Intensive Properties

  • Extensive Properties: Depend on the amount or size of matter in the system (additive). Examples: Mass (\(m\)), Volume (\(V\)), Internal Energy (\(U\)), Enthalpy (\(H\)), Entropy (\(S\)), Gibbs Energy (\(G\)), Heat Capacity (\(C\)).
  • Intensive Properties: Independent of the amount of matter (non-additive). Examples: Temperature (\(T\)), Pressure (\(p\)), Density (\(\rho\)), Molar Volume (\(V_m\)), Specific Heat Capacity (\(c\)), Molar Enthalpy (\(\Delta H_m\)), Concentration, Refractive Index, pH.
NEET Key Rule: The ratio of two extensive properties is always an intensive property! For instance, \(\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass (Ext)}}{\text{Volume (Ext)}}\) and \(\text{Molar Heat Capacity } C_m = \frac{C \text{ (Ext)}}{n \text{ (Ext)}}\) are intensive.

3. Internal Energy, Work, Heat & The First Law

The Internal Energy (\(U\)) is the sum of all microscopic kinetic and potential energies of the particles inside a system. Absolute \(U\) cannot be measured, only its change \(\Delta U = U_2 - U_1\).

First Law of Thermodynamics (Conservation of Energy): \[\Delta U = q + w\] For an isolated system, \(q = 0\) and \(w = 0\), so \(\Delta U = 0\) (energy of an isolated system remains constant).

IUPAC Sign Conventions (Chemistry Standard)

Quantity Positive (+) Sign Negative (−) Sign
Heat (\(q\)) Heat absorbed by the system Heat released by the system
Work (\(w\)) Work done on the system (Compression) Work done by the system (Expansion)

Pressure–Volume (\(p\)–\(V\)) Work in Various Processes

When a gas expands or is compressed against an external pressure \(p_{\text{ex}}\), the mechanical work is:

  • Irreversible Process (Constant \(p_{\text{ex}}\)): \[w_{\text{irrev}} = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V = -p_{\text{ex}}(V_f - V_i)\] If expanding (\(V_f > V_i\)), \(w < 0\). If compressed (\(V_f < V_i\)), \(w > 0\).
  • Free Expansion (Vacuum, \(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\)): \[w = 0\] For an ideal gas undergoing isothermal free expansion, \(\Delta U = 0\), \(w = 0\), and therefore \(q = 0\).
  • Reversible Isothermal Process (Ideal Gas, \(T = \text{constant}\)): Since the process proceeds infinitely slowly through equilibrium states, \(p_{\text{ex}} = p_{\text{in}} \pm dp\), and \(\Delta U = 0\): \[w_{\text{rev}} = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{nRT}{V}\,dV = -nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303\,nRT \log\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303\,nRT \log\left(\frac{p_i}{p_f}\right)\] And \(q_{\text{rev}} = -w_{\text{rev}}\).
  • Isochoric Process (\(\Delta V = 0\)): \(w = 0 \implies \Delta U = q_V\).
  • Adiabatic Process (\(q = 0\)): \(\Delta U = w_{\text{ad}}\).

বিভিন্ন প্রক্রিয়ায় চাপ–আয়তন (\(p\)–\(V\)) কার্য

বাহ্যিক চাপ \(p_{\text{ex}}\)-এর বিরুদ্ধে গ্যাসের প্রসারণ বা সংকোচন ঘটলে যান্ত্রিক কার্য হয়:

  • অপরাবর্ত প্রক্রিয়া (ধ্রুবক \(p_{\text{ex}}\)): \[w_{\text{irrev}} = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V = -p_{\text{ex}}(V_f - V_i)\] প্রসারণে (\(V_f > V_i\)), \(w < 0\)। সংকোচনে (\(V_f < V_i\)), \(w > 0\)।
  • মুক্ত প্রসারণ (শূন্যস্থানে, \(p_{\text{ex}} = 0\)): \[w = 0\] আদর্শ গ্যাসের সমোষ্ণ মুক্ত প্রসারণে \(\Delta U = 0\), \(w = 0\), এবং তাই \(q = 0\)।
  • পরাবর্ত সমোষ্ণ প্রক্রিয়া (আদর্শ গ্যাস, \(T = \text{ধ্রুবক}\)): অসীম ধীরে সাম্যাবস্থার মধ্য দিয়ে প্রক্রিয়াটি ঘটায় \(p_{\text{ex}} = p_{\text{in}} \pm dp\) এবং \(\Delta U = 0\): \[w_{\text{rev}} = -\int_{V_i}^{V_f} \frac{nRT}{V}\,dV = -nRT \ln\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303\,nRT \log\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303\,nRT \log\left(\frac{p_i}{p_f}\right)\] এবং \(q_{\text{rev}} = -w_{\text{rev}}\)।
  • সম-আয়তন প্রক্রিয়া (\(\Delta V = 0\)): \(w = 0 \implies \Delta U = q_V\)।
  • রুদ্ধতাপ প্রক্রিয়া (\(q = 0\)): \(\Delta U = w_{\text{ad}}\)।

4. Enthalpy (\(H\)), Heat Capacity & Calorimetry

Most laboratory reactions occur in open vessels at constant atmospheric pressure rather than constant volume. To track heat flow under constant pressure, we define Enthalpy (\(H\)):

\[H = U + pV \qquad \text{and at constant pressure, } \Delta H = \Delta U + p\Delta V = q_p\] For chemical reactions involving ideal gases at constant \(T\) and \(p\): \[\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\] where \(\Delta n_g = (\text{moles of gaseous products}) - (\text{moles of gaseous reactants})\).
  • If \(\Delta H < 0\): Exothermic reaction (heat is evolved to surroundings).
  • If \(\Delta H > 0\): Endothermic reaction (heat is absorbed from surroundings).
  • For reactions involving only solids and liquids, \(\Delta V \approx 0\) and \(\Delta n_g = 0\), so \(\Delta H \approx \Delta U\).

Heat Capacity (\(C\)) and Mayer's Relation

Heat absorbed is proportional to temperature rise: \(q = C\Delta T = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T = n \cdot C_m \cdot \Delta T\), where \(c\) is specific heat capacity and \(C_m\) is molar heat capacity.

  • At constant volume: \(q_V = C_V\Delta T = \Delta U\)
  • At constant pressure: \(q_p = C_p\Delta T = \Delta H\)
  • For \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of an ideal gas: \(C_{p,m} - C_{V,m} = R\) (and for \(n\,\mathrm{mol}\), \(C_p - C_V = nR\)).

Experimental Measurement (Calorimetry)

  • Bomb Calorimeter (Constant Volume): A sealed steel vessel immersed in a water bath measures \(q_V = \Delta U = -C_{\text{cal}}\Delta T\) for combustion reactions.
  • Constant-Pressure Calorimeter: An insulated cup open to the atmosphere measures \(q_p = \Delta H\) directly for solution reactions.

৪. এনথ্যালপি (\(H\)), তাপগ্রাহিতা ও ক্যালোরিমিতি

পরীক্ষাগারের অধিকাংশ বিক্রিয়া স্থির আয়তনের পরিবর্তে স্থির বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপে খোলা পাত্রে ঘটে। স্থির চাপে তাপের পরিবর্তন পরিমাপের জন্য এনথ্যালপি (\(H\)) সংজ্ঞায়িত করা হয়:

\[H = U + pV \qquad \text{এবং স্থির চাপে, } \Delta H = \Delta U + p\Delta V = q_p\] স্থির উষ্ণতা \(T\) ও চাপে \(p\) গ্যাসীয় বিক্রিয়ার ক্ষেত্রে: \[\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\] যেখানে \(\Delta n_g = (\text{গ্যাসীয় বিক্রিয়াজাত পদার্থের মোল সংখ্যা}) - (\text{গ্যাসীয় বিক্রিয়কের মোল সংখ্যা})\)।

  • যদি \(\Delta H > 0\) হয়: তাপগ্রাহী (Endothermic) বিক্রিয়া (পরিবেশ থেকে তাপ শোষিত হয়)।
  • কেবল কঠিন ও তরল পদার্থের বিক্রিয়ার ক্ষেত্রে \(\Delta V \approx 0\) ও \(\Delta n_g = 0\), তাই \(\Delta H \approx \Delta U\)।

শোষিত তাপ উষ্ণতা বৃদ্ধির সমানুপাতিক: \(q = C\Delta T = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T = n \cdot C_m \cdot \Delta T\), যেখানে \(c\) হলো আপেক্ষিক তাপ এবং \(C_m\) হলো মোলার তাপগ্রাহিতা।

  • স্থির আয়তনে: \(q_V = C_V\Delta T = \Delta U\)
  • স্থির চাপে: \(q_p = C_p\Delta T = \Delta H\)

5. Thermochemistry, Hess's Law & Enthalpy Types

Standard State (\(\ominus\)): Pure form of a substance at \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) pressure and a specified temperature (commonly \(298\,\mathrm{K}\)). By convention, the standard molar enthalpy of formation (\(\Delta_f H^\ominus\)) of an element in its most stable reference state (e.g., \(\mathrm{C}_{\text{graphite}}\), \(\mathrm{O}_2(g)\), \(\mathrm{H}_2(g)\), \(\mathrm{Br}_2(l)\), \(\mathrm{S}_{\text{rhombic}}\)) is zero.

Hess's Law of Constant Heat Summation: Because \(H\) is a state function, the total enthalpy change of a reaction is identical whether it occurs in one step or multiple intermediate steps: \[\Delta_r H^\ominus = \Delta_r H_1^\ominus + \Delta_r H_2^\ominus + \Delta_r H_3^\ominus + \cdots\] Using Standard Enthalpies of Formation: \[\Delta_r H^\ominus = \sum a_i \Delta_f H^\ominus(\text{products}) - \sum b_i \Delta_f H^\ominus(\text{reactants})\] Using Bond Enthalpies (Gas-phase reactions): \[\Delta_r H^\ominus \approx \sum \Delta_{\text{bond}} H^\ominus(\text{reactants}) - \sum \Delta_{\text{bond}} H^\ominus(\text{products})\]

Key Standard Enthalpy Definitions

  • Enthalpy of Combustion (\(\Delta_c H^\ominus\)): Enthalpy change when \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a substance undergoes complete combustion in excess \(\mathrm{O}_2\) under standard conditions (always negative).
  • Enthalpy of Phase Change: Fusion (\(\Delta_{\text{fus}}H^\ominus\)), Vaporization (\(\Delta_{\text{vap}}H^\ominus\)), and Sublimation (\(\Delta_{\text{sub}}H^\ominus = \Delta_{\text{fus}}H^\ominus + \Delta_{\text{vap}}H^\ominus\)). All are endothermic (\(+\)).
  • Enthalpy of Atomization (\(\Delta_a H^\ominus\)): Enthalpy required to break \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a substance completely into gaseous atoms.
  • Lattice Enthalpy (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus\)) & Born–Haber Cycle: Enthalpy required to dissociate \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of an ionic solid into gaseous ions. Determined indirectly using Hess's Law.
  • Enthalpy of Solution (\(\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus\)): \(\Delta_{\text{sol}}H^\ominus = \Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus + \Delta_{\text{hyd}}H^\ominus\).

  • জালক এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}}H^\ominus\)) ও বর্ন-হ্যাবার চক্র: \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) আয়নীয় কঠিন পদার্থকে গ্যাসীয় আয়নে বিয়োজিত করতে প্রয়োজনীয় এনথ্যালপি। হেসের সূত্রের সাহায্যে পরোক্ষভাবে নির্ণীত হয়।

6. Spontaneity, Entropy (\(S\)) & Gibbs Free Energy (\(G\))

A spontaneous process has the natural potential to proceed in a given direction without continuous external assistance (thermodynamics does not predict its rate). Neither enthalpy decrease alone nor system entropy increase alone guarantees spontaneity.

Entropy (\(S\)) and the Second & Third Laws

Entropy (\(S\)) is a state function measuring molecular randomness or disorder (\(S_{\text{gas}} > S_{\text{liquid}} > S_{\text{solid}}\)).

\[\Delta S_{\text{sys}} = \frac{q_{\text{rev}}}{T} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Delta S_{\text{surr}} = -\frac{\Delta H_{\text{sys}}}{T} \text{ (at constant } p, T\text{)}\] Second Law Criterion for Spontaneity: \[\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0 \quad (\text{and } \Delta S_{\text{total}} = 0 \text{ at equilibrium})\]

Third Law of Thermodynamics: The entropy of any pure, perfectly crystalline substance approaches zero as the temperature approaches absolute zero (\(0\,\mathrm{K}\)).

Gibbs Free Energy (\(G\)) and Equilibrium

At constant temperature and pressure, we combine enthalpy and entropy into Gibbs Energy (\(G = H - TS\)):

\[\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\]
  • \(\Delta G < 0\): Process is spontaneous (exergonic).
  • \(\Delta G = 0\): System is at equilibrium.
  • \(\Delta G > 0\): Process is non-spontaneous (endergonic).
Relation with Equilibrium Constant (\(K\)): \[\Delta_r G^\ominus = -RT \ln K = -2.303\,RT \log K\]
\(\Delta H\) \(\Delta S\) \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S\) Spontaneity Behaviour
\(-\) \(+\) Always \(-\) Spontaneous at all temperatures
\(+\) \(-\) Always \(+\) Non-spontaneous at all temperatures
\(-\) \(-\) \(-\) at low \(T\); \(+\) at high \(T\) Spontaneous at low \(T\) (\(T < \Delta H/\Delta S\))
\(+\) \(+\) \(+\) at low \(T\); \(-\) at high \(T\) Spontaneous at high \(T\) (\(T > \Delta H/\Delta S\))

Worked Example 1: Reversible Isothermal Expansion

Assume \(2.0\,\mathrm{mol}\) of an ideal gas expands isothermally and reversibly at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) from an initial volume of \(5.0\,\mathrm{L}\) to a final volume of \(20.0\,\mathrm{L}\). Calculate \(w\), \(q\), and \(\Delta U\). (Take \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\), \(\log 4 = 0.602\)).

Solution:
For an isothermal process of an ideal gas, temperature is constant, so \(\Delta U = 0\).
Work done in reversible isothermal expansion: \[w = -2.303\,nRT \log\left(\frac{V_f}{V_i}\right) = -2.303 \times 2.0 \times 8.314 \times 300 \times \log\left(\frac{20.0}{5.0}\right)\] \[w = -11488.3 \times 0.602 \approx -6916\,\mathrm{J} = -6.92\,\mathrm{kJ}\] From the First Law, \(\Delta U = q + w = 0 \implies q = -w = +6.92\,\mathrm{kJ}\).

Worked Example 2: Relating \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta U\)

Assume the combustion of \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of liquid octane (\(\mathrm{C}_8\mathrm{H}_{18}\)) in a bomb calorimeter at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) releases \(5460\,\mathrm{kJ}\) of heat at constant volume. Calculate \(\Delta H\) for the reaction at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\). (Take \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\)).

Solution:
Balanced combustion equation: \[\mathrm{C}_8\mathrm{H}_{18}(l) + \frac{25}{2}\mathrm{O}_2(g) \rightarrow 8\mathrm{CO}_2(g) + 9\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(l)\] Here, \(\Delta U = -5460\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).
Change in gaseous moles: \(\Delta n_g = 8 - 12.5 = -4.5\,\mathrm{mol}\).
\[\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT = -5460 + (-4.5)(8.314 \times 10^{-3})(300)\] \[\Delta H = -5460 - 11.22 = -5471.22\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\]

Worked Example 3: Temperature Threshold for Spontaneity

For a hypothetical decomposition reaction, assume \(\Delta H^\ominus = +150\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) and \(\Delta S^\ominus = +250\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\). Assuming both remain constant with temperature, above what temperature will the reaction become spontaneous?

Solution:
At equilibrium (\(\Delta G^\ominus = 0\)): \[T_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\Delta H^\ominus}{\Delta S^\ominus} = \frac{150 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}{250\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}} = 600\,\mathrm{K}\] Since both \(\Delta H^\ominus > 0\) and \(\Delta S^\ominus > 0\), \(\Delta G^\ominus = \Delta H^\ominus - T\Delta S^\ominus < 0\) when \(T > 600\,\mathrm{K}\). Thus, the reaction becomes spontaneous above \(600\,\mathrm{K}\).

9. Common NEET Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

Common Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Physics vs. Chemistry Sign of Work: In Chemistry (IUPAC), \(w = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V\) and \(\Delta U = q + w\) (expansion work is negative). Do not confuse this with Physics (\(\Delta U = q - w\)).
  • Unit Mismatch in \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\): \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta U\) are usually in \(\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\), whereas \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\) is in Joules. Always multiply \(RT\) by \(10^{-3}\) before adding to \(\mathrm{kJ}\).
  • Counting Solids/Liquids in \(\Delta n_g\): Only include gaseous species when computing \(\Delta n_g\).
  • Bond Enthalpy Formula Direction: Unlike \(\Delta_f H^\ominus\) (Products − Reactants), bond enthalpy uses Reactants − Products.

Quick Revision Checklist

  • \(\Delta U = q + w\); at constant \(V\), \(\Delta U = q_V\); at constant \(p\), \(\Delta H = q_p\).
  • \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\); \(C_{p,m} - C_{V,m} = R\).
  • \(w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303\,nRT \log(V_f/V_i)\); free expansion \(w = 0\).
  • \(\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0\) for spontaneity.
  • \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\) for spontaneity; \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -2.303\,RT \log K\).

৯. সাধারণ ভুল ও পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

যে ভুলগুলি এড়িয়ে চলতে হবে:
  • পদার্থবিদ্যা বনাম রসায়নে কার্যের চিহ্ন: রসায়নে (IUPAC) \(w = -p_{\text{ex}}\Delta V\) এবং \(\Delta U = q + w\) (প্রসারণ কার্য ঋণাত্মক)। পদার্থবিদ্যার (\(\Delta U = q - w\)) সাথে গুলিয়ে ফেলবেন না।
  • \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\)-তে এককের অমিল: \(\Delta H\) ও \(\Delta U\) সাধারণত \(\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) এককে থাকে, কিন্তু \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\) জুল এককে। যোগ করার আগে সর্বদা \(RT\)-কে \(10^{-3}\) দিয়ে গুণ করুন।
  • \(\Delta n_g\) গণনায় কঠিন/তরল ধরা: \(\Delta n_g\) নির্ণয়ে শুধুমাত্র গ্যাসীয় অণুগুলিই গণ্য হয়।
  • বন্ধন এনথ্যালপির সূত্রের দিক: \(\Delta_f H^\ominus\) (বিক্রিয়াজাত − বিক্রিয়ক)-এর বিপরীতে, বন্ধন এনথ্যালপিতে বিক্রিয়ক − বিক্রিয়াজাত ব্যবহৃত হয়।

দ্রুত পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

  • \(\Delta U = q + w\); স্থির \(V\)-তে \(\Delta U = q_V\); স্থির \(p\)-তে \(\Delta H = q_p\)।
  • \(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\); \(C_{p,m} - C_{V,m} = R\)।
  • \(w_{\text{rev}} = -2.303\,nRT \log(V_f/V_i)\); মুক্ত প্রসারণে \(w = 0\)।
  • স্বতঃস্ফূর্ততার জন্য \(\Delta S_{\text{total}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} > 0\)।
  • স্বতঃস্ফূর্ততার জন্য \(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\); \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -2.303\,RT \log K\)।

10. Concise Final Recap

Thermodynamics governs energy conservation (\(\Delta U = q + w\)), heat changes at constant pressure (\(\Delta H = \Delta U + \Delta n_g RT\)), and the natural direction of processes (\(\Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0\)). Combining Hess's Law with standard enthalpies and linking \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\) to the equilibrium constant \(K\) equips you to solve virtually any NEET UG thermodynamics problem with speed and precision.
