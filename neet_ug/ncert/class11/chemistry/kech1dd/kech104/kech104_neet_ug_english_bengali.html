Chemical bonding is central to NEET UG Chemistry, accounting for 3 to 4 direct questions every year (12 to 16 marks) spanning physical, inorganic, and organic chemistry. After studying this comprehensive tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Understand the Kössel-Lewis approach to chemical bonding and write Lewis dot symbols for atoms and ions.
  • State the octet rule, calculate formal charges accurately, and identify incomplete, odd-electron, and expanded octets.
  • Explain electrovalent (ionic) bonding, factors favoring ionic bond formation, and calculate/interpret lattice enthalpy.
  • Apply Fajans' rules to determine the percentage covalent character in ionic bonds.
  • Evaluate bond parameters: bond length, bond angle, bond enthalpy, and bond order, and understand resonance stabilization.
  • Deduce dipole moments and rationalize polarity differences (e.g., \(NH_3\) vs \(NF_3\), \(CO_2\) vs \(H_2O\)).
  • Predict molecular shapes and distortions using VSEPR theory.
  • Explain covalent bond formation via Valence Bond Theory (VBT) and orbital overlap (\(\sigma\) vs \(\pi\) bonds).
  • Master hybridisation (\(sp, sp^2, sp^3, sp^3d, sp^3d^2\)) and rationalize geometries of molecules like \(PCl_5\) and \(SF_6\).
  • Apply Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) for homonuclear diatomic molecules (\(H_2\) to \(Ne_2\)), predicting bond order, stability, and magnetic nature.
  • Distinguish between intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding and their effects on physical properties.

2. Kössel-Lewis Approach, Lewis Symbols, Formal Charge & Octet Exceptions
কোসেল-লুইস পদ্ধতি, লুইস প্রতীক, ফর্মাল চার্জ ও অষ্টক সূত্রের সীমাবদ্ধতা

English

2.1 Kössel-Lewis Approach & Lewis Symbols

In 1916, Kössel and Lewis independently provided a logical explanation of valence based on the inertness of noble gases. Lewis pictured the atom as a positively charged 'Kernel' (nucleus + inner electrons) surrounded by a cubical outer shell accommodating up to 8 electrons.

Lewis Symbols: Only the outer shell electrons (valence electrons) participate in chemical bonding. The notation consists of the element symbol surrounded by dots representing valence electrons. The group valence is generally equal to the number of dots or \(8 - \text{number of dots}\).

Lewis Dot Symbols of Second Period Elements showing valence shell electrons

2.2 The Octet Rule & Covalent Bond Formation

According to the electronic theory of chemical bonding (Kössel-Lewis, 1916), atoms combine either by transfer of valence electrons or by sharing pairs of electrons to attain a stable valence-shell octet (\(ns^2 np^6\)) resembling the nearest noble gas (or a duplet for helium).

Langmuir (1919) introduced the term covalent bond. When atoms share one, two, or three pairs of electrons, they form single, double, or triple covalent bonds respectively (e.g., \(Cl-Cl\), \(O=C=O\), \(H_2C=CH_2\), \(N\equiv N\), and \(HC\equiv CH\)).

2.3 Formal Charge Calculation

Formal charge is the hypothetical charge assigned to an individual atom in a Lewis structure assuming equal sharing of bonding pairs:

\[ \text{Formal Charge (F.C.)} = V - L - \frac{1}{2} S \]

where \(V\) = total valence electrons in isolated free atom,
\(L\) = total non-bonding (lone pair) electrons, and
\(S\) = total shared (bonding) electrons.

Significance in NEET: Lowest energy Lewis structures have the smallest formal charges, with negative formal charges localized on the most electronegative atoms.

2.4 Limitations of the Octet Rule

  1. Incomplete Octet of Central Atom: Elements with fewer than 4 valence electrons form stable molecules where the central atom has < 8 electrons. Examples: \(LiCl\) (2e⁻), \(BeH_2\) (4e⁻), \(BeCl_2\) (4e⁻), \(BCl_3\) (6e⁻), \(BF_3\) (6e⁻), \(AlCl_3\) (6e⁻).
  2. Odd-Electron Molecules: Molecules having an odd number of valence electrons cannot satisfy the octet rule for every atom. Examples: Nitric oxide (\(NO\), 11 valence electrons) and nitrogen dioxide (\(NO_2\), 17 valence electrons).
  3. Expanded Octet (Hypervalent Molecules): Elements of Period 3 and beyond possess vacant \(d\)-orbitals in addition to \(s\) and \(p\) subshells, enabling them to accommodate > 8 electrons. Examples: \(PF_5\) (10e⁻), \(SF_6\) (12e⁻), \(H_2SO_4\) (12e⁻ around S), \(IF_7\) (14e⁻).
  4. Noble Gas Compounds: Noble gases like \(Xe\) and \(Kr\) form stable fluorides and oxides (\(XeF_2, XeF_4, XeOF_2, KrF_2\)).
  5. Inability to Predict Shapes & Relative Stability: The octet theory does not explain why molecules possess definite 3D shapes or account for the energetics of bond formation.

কোনো লুইস কাঠামোতে উপস্থিত কোনো পরমাণুর ওপর আরোপিত কাল্পনিক আধানকে ফর্মাল চার্জ বলে:

\[ \mathrm{L}_{1} = V - L - \frac{1}{2} S \]L1: ফর্মাল চার্জ (F.C.)

যেখানে \(V\) = মুক্ত পরমাণুর যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা,
\(L\) = নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা (non-bonding electrons), এবং
\(S\) = বন্ধন সৃষ্টিকারী ভাগ করা ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা (shared electrons)।

3. Ionic Bonding, Lattice Enthalpy & Fajans' Rules
আয়নীয় বন্ধন, ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি ও ফাযানের নিয়মাবলী

English

3.1 Factors Favoring Ionic Bond Formation

An electrovalent or ionic bond is formed by the complete transfer of one or more valence electrons from an electropositive atom to an electronegative atom, resulting in cations and anions held together by electrostatic attractions. Ionic bond formation is favored by:

  • Low Ionization Enthalpy (\(\Delta_i H\)) of the metal atom: Ease of cation formation (\(M(g) \rightarrow M^+(g) + e^-\)).
  • High Negative Electron Gain Enthalpy (\(\Delta_{eg} H\)) of the non-metal: Ease of anion formation (\(X(g) + e^- \rightarrow X^-(g)\)).
  • High Lattice Enthalpy (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}} H\)): Enthalpy released during crystal lattice stabilization.
NEET Concept Note: Net Energetics of Ionic Solids
In \(NaCl\), \(\Delta_i H(\text{Na}) = +495.8\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) and \(\Delta_{eg} H(\text{Cl}) = -348.7\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\). The net sum is positive (\(+147.1\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)). However, solid \(NaCl\) forms spontaneously because the lattice enthalpy of \(-788\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) heavily overcompensates for the energy absorbed!

3.2 Lattice Enthalpy Definition

Lattice Enthalpy is defined as the energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic compound into its gaseous constituent ions to infinite separation:

\[ \mathrm{NaCl(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Na^+(g)} + \mathrm{Cl^-(g)}; \quad \Delta_{\text{lattice}} H^\circ = +788\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]

3.3 Fajans' Rules (Covalent Character in Ionic Bonds)

No bond is 100% ionic. When a cation approaches an anion, the positively charged cation attracts the electron cloud of the anion and repels its nucleus, causing polarization (distortion) of the anion electron cloud. Higher polarization yields higher covalent character. Fajans formulated the following rules:

  • Size of Cation: Smaller cation \(\rightarrow\) higher charge density \(\rightarrow\) greater polarizing power \(\rightarrow\) greater covalent character (e.g., \(LiCl > NaCl > KCl\)).
  • Size of Anion: Larger anion \(\rightarrow\) outer electrons held less tightly \(\rightarrow\) higher polarizability \(\rightarrow\) greater covalent character (e.g., \(LiI > LiBr > LiCl > LiF\)).
  • Charge on Ions: Higher charge on cation or anion \(\rightarrow\) stronger polarizing effect \(\rightarrow\) greater covalent character (e.g., \(SnCl_4 > SnCl_2\), \(AlCl_3 > MgCl_2 > NaCl\)).
  • Electronic Configuration of Cation: Cations with pseudo-noble gas configuration \((n-1)d^{10}ns^0\) have greater polarizing power than cations of the same size and charge with noble gas configuration \(ns^2 np^6\) due to poorer shielding by \(d\)-electrons (e.g., \(CuCl\) is much more covalent than \(NaCl\); \(r(\mathrm{Cu^+}) \approx r(\mathrm{Na^+})\)).

৩.১ আয়নীয় বন্ধন গঠনের অনুকূল শর্ত

তড়িৎযোজী বা আয়নীয় বন্ধন গঠিত হয় একটি তড়িৎ-ধনাত্মক ধাতু থেকে তড়িৎ-ঋণাত্মক অধাতুতে ইলেকট্রন স্থানান্তরের মাধ্যমে। উৎপন্ন ক্যাটায়ন ও অ্যানায়নগুলি কুলম্বীয় স্থির-তড়িৎ আকর্ষণ বল দ্বারা যুক্ত থাকে। অনুকূল শর্তগুলি হলো:

  • ধাতুর নিম্ন আয়নায়ন এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_i H\)): সহজে ইলেকট্রন বর্জন করে ক্যাটায়ন গঠন।
  • অধাতুর উচ্চ ঋণাত্মক ইলেকট্রন গ্রহণ এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_{eg} H\)): সহজে ইলেকট্রন গ্রহণ করে অ্যানায়ন গঠন।
  • উচ্চ ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}} H\)): কেলাস জালক গঠনের সময় বিপুল শক্তি নির্গমন।

\(NaCl\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে আয়নায়ন শক্তি ও ইলেকট্রন গ্রহণ এনথ্যালপির সমষ্টি ধনাত্মক (\(+147.1\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) হওয়া সত্ত্বেও, ল্যাটিস শক্তি (\(-788\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) নির্গত হওয়ায় যৌগটি অত্যন্ত স্থিতিশীল হয়।

৩.২ ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি (Lattice Enthalpy)

এক মোল কঠিন আয়নীয় যৌগের কেলাসকে তার উপাদান গ্যাসীয় আয়নে সম্পূর্ণ পৃথক করতে যে পরিমাণ শক্তির প্রয়োজন হয়, তাকে ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি বলে।

৩.৩ ফাযানের নিয়মাবলী (আয়নীয় বন্ধনে সমযোজী চরিত্র)

ক্যাটায়ন দ্বারা অ্যানায়নের ইলেকট্রন মেঘ বিকৃত (পোলারাইজড) হওয়ার ঘটনাকে ধ্রুবায়ন বলে। ধ্রুবায়ন যত বৃদ্ধি পায়, সমযোজী চরিত্র তত বৃদ্ধি পায়:

  • ক্যাটায়নের আকার: ক্যাটায়নের আকার ছোট হলে তার ধ্রুবায়ন ক্ষমতা বৃদ্ধি পায় \(\rightarrow\) সমযোজী চরিত্র বাড়ে (যেমন \(LiCl > NaCl\))।
  • অ্যানায়নের আকার: অ্যানায়নের আকার বড় হলে তা সহজে ধ্রুবায়িত হয় \(\rightarrow\) সমযোজী চরিত্র বাড়ে (যেমন \(LiI > LiBr > LiCl > LiF\))।
  • আধানের পরিমাণ: ক্যাটায়ন বা অ্যানায়নের আধান বৃদ্ধি পেলে সমযোজী চরিত্র বাড়ে (যেমন \(AlCl_3 > MgCl_2 > NaCl\))।
  • ক্যাটায়নের ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস: ছদ্ম-নিষ্ক্রিয় গ্যাসীয় বিন্যাসযুক্ত ক্যাটায়ন \((n-1)d^{10}ns^0\) সাধারণ নিষ্ক্রিয় গ্যাসীয় বিন্যাসযুক্ত \(ns^2 np^6\) ক্যাটায়নের চেয়ে বেশি শক্তিশালী ধ্রুবায়ন ঘটায়, কারণ \(d\)-ইলেকট্রনের আবরণী ক্ষমতা দুর্বল (যেমন \(CuCl\) যৌগটি \(NaCl\) অপেক্ষা অধিক সমযোজী)।

4. Bond Parameters, Resonance & Dipole Moments
বন্ধন পরামিতি, রেজোন্যান্স ও দ্বিমেরু ভ্রামক

4.1 Bond Parameters Overview

  • Bond Length: The equilibrium distance between the nuclei of two bonded atoms in a molecule. In a covalent bond, \(R = r_A + r_B\). Covalent radius is half the internuclear distance between two identical bonded atoms. The van der Waals radius represents the overall size of an atom in a nonbonded situation and is always larger than the covalent radius (for Chlorine, \(r_c = 99\,\mathrm{pm}\), while \(r_{\text{vdW}} = 180\,\mathrm{pm}\)).
  • Bond Angle: The angle between orbitals containing bonding electron pairs around the central atom (e.g., \(104.5^\circ\) in \(H_2O\)).
  • Bond Enthalpy (\(\Delta_a H^\circ\)): Amount of energy required to break one mole of bonds in gaseous state. For polyatomic molecules, mean or average bond enthalpy is used. For water: \[ \text{Average O-H Bond Enthalpy} = \frac{502 + 427}{2} = 464.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \]
  • Bond Order: In Lewis theory, given by the number of bonds between two atoms (\(H_2 = 1\), \(O_2 = 2\), \(N_2 = 3\), \(CO = 3\)). Isoelectronic species have identical bond orders (\(F_2\) and \(O_2^{2-}\) have bond order 1; \(N_2, CO, NO^+\) have bond order 3).
    Golden Trend: As Bond Order increases \(\rightarrow\) Bond Enthalpy increases \(\rightarrow\) Bond Length decreases.

4.2 Resonance in Molecules

When a single Lewis structure cannot accurately depict the observed physical properties (such as equivalent bond lengths), the molecule is represented as a resonance hybrid of two or more canonical forms:

  • Ozone (\(O_3\)): Canonical forms show one single bond (\(148\,\mathrm{pm}\)) and one double bond (\(121\,\mathrm{pm}\)). Experimentally, both \(O-O\) bond lengths in \(O_3\) are completely identical (\(128\,\mathrm{pm}\)), intermediate between single and double bonds.
  • Carbonate Ion (\(CO_3^{2-}\)): All three \(C-O\) bonds are equivalent with equal bond length and partial double bond character (bond order = \(4/3 \approx 1.33\)).
  • Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\)): Experimental \(C-O\) length is \(115\,\mathrm{pm}\), lying between \(C=O\) (\(121\,\mathrm{pm}\)) and \(C\equiv O\) (\(110\,\mathrm{pm}\)).

Key Principles: Canonical forms do not have real physical existence; there is no dynamic equilibrium between them. The resonance hybrid has lower energy and greater stability than any contributing canonical structure.

Resonance in Ozone (O₃): Equal intermediate bond lengths of 128 pm in the resonance hybrid

4.3 Polarity of Bonds & Dipole Moment

In heteronuclear diatomic molecules, electronegativity differences lead to shift of electron density, creating a polar bond. The extent of polarity is quantified by the dipole moment (\(\mu\)):

\[ \mu = \text{charge } (Q) \times \text{distance of separation } (r) \]

Unit: Debye (\(\mathrm{D}\)), where \(1\,\mathrm{D} = 3.33564 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\).

Dipole moment is a vector quantity. In polyatomic molecules, the net dipole moment is the vector sum of individual bond dipoles:

  • Linear \(BeF_2\) and \(CO_2\): Two equal bond dipoles act in exactly opposite directions (\(180^\circ\)), yielding \(\mu_{\text{net}} = 0\).
  • Bent \(H_2O\): Bond angle is \(104.5^\circ\); bond dipoles and lone pair orbital dipoles reinforce, giving \(\mu = 1.85\,\mathrm{D}\) (\(6.17 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\)).
  • Trigonal Planar \(BF_3\): Three equal \(B-F\) dipoles at \(120^\circ\) cancel out completely (\(\mu = 0\)).
  • High-Yield NEET Comparison: \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) vs \(\mathrm{NF_3}\):
    Both molecules possess trigonal pyramidal geometry with one lone pair. Fluorine is more electronegative than nitrogen, yet \(\mu(\mathrm{NH_3}) = 1.47\,\mathrm{D}\) (\(4.90 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\)) is significantly greater than \(\mu(\mathrm{NF_3}) = 0.23\,\mathrm{D}\) (\(0.80 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{C\,m}\)).
    Reason: In \(NH_3\), the individual \(N-H\) bond moments are directed towards nitrogen and reinforce the lone-pair orbital dipole. In \(NF_3\), the highly electronegative fluorine atoms pull electron density away from nitrogen, opposing and canceling the lone-pair dipole moment!

5. Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) Theory
যোজ্যতা কক্ষ ইলেকট্রন জোড় বিকর্ষণ (VSEPR) তত্ত্ব

English

5.1 Core Postulates of VSEPR Theory

Proposed by Sidgwick and Powell (1940) and refined by Nyholm and Gillespie (1957), VSEPR theory predicts the 3D shapes of molecules based on repulsions among electron pairs:

  1. The shape of a molecule depends on the total number of valence shell electron pairs (bonding and non-bonding) around the central atom.
  2. Electron pairs repel each other because their electron clouds are negatively charged; they orient themselves in space to minimize repulsions and maximize distance.
  3. Order of Repulsive Interactions:
    Lone Pair (lp) – Lone Pair (lp) > Lone Pair (lp) – Bond Pair (bp) > Bond Pair (bp) – Bond Pair (bp)
    Rationale: A lone pair is localized on the central atom and attracted by only one nucleus, so it occupies more spatial volume than a shared bonding pair.
  4. Multiple bonds are treated as a single electron pair ("super pair") for geometry prediction.

  • \(\mathrm{ClF_3}\) (T-আকৃতি): ৩টি বন্ধন জোড় ও ২টি নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় রয়েছে। উভয় নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় নিরক্ষীয় অবস্থানে থাকে।

6. Valence Bond Theory (VBT) & Orbital Overlap Concept
যোজ্যতা বন্ধন তত্ত্ব (VBT) ও পারমাণবিক কক্ষকের অভিলেপন

English

6.1 Energetics of \(H_2\) Molecule Formation

Developed by Heitler and London (1927) and Pauling, VBT explains bond formation through energy changes and quantum mechanical overlap of atomic orbitals. When two hydrogen atoms \(A\) and \(B\) with nuclei \(N_A, N_B\) and electrons \(e_A, e_B\) approach:

  • Attractive Forces: \(N_A - e_A\), \(N_B - e_B\) (old) and \(N_A - e_B\), \(N_B - e_A\) (new).
  • Repulsive Forces: \(e_A - e_B\) and \(N_A - N_B\).

The magnitude of attractive forces exceeds repulsive forces, lowering the potential energy until an equilibrium internuclear distance of \(74\,\mathrm{pm}\) is reached, corresponding to an energy minimum with bond enthalpy release of \(435.8\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).

Potential Energy curve for H₂ formation as a function of internuclear distance

6.2 Types of Orbital Overlap: Sigma (\(\sigma\)) vs Pi (\(\pi\)) Bonds

  • Sigma (\(\sigma\)) Bond: Formed by end-to-end (head-on) axial overlap of orbitals along the internuclear axis. Overlap can occur via:
    • \(s-s\) overlap • \(s-p_z\) overlap • \(p_z-p_z\) overlap (taking \(z\) as internuclear axis).
    Feature: High extent of overlap; cylindrical symmetry around the bond axis; rotation around a single \(\sigma\) bond is free.
  • Pi (\(\pi\)) Bond: Formed by lateral (sidewise) overlap of atomic orbitals perpendicular to the internuclear axis.
    Feature: Overlap consists of two saucer-like charge clouds above and below the internuclear plane; lower extent of overlap makes it inherently weaker than a \(\sigma\) bond.

Overlap Signs (Phases): Positive overlap occurs when wavefunctions have the same sign (phase) and proper orientation, leading to bond formation. Negative or zero overlap leads to no bond.

৬.১ \(H_2\) অণু গঠন ও স্থিতিশক্তি চিত্র

হাইটলার ও লন্ডন (১৯২৭) এবং পাউলিং কর্তৃক যোজ্যতা বন্ধন তত্ত্ব (VBT) প্রতিষ্ঠিত হয়। যখন দুটি হাইড্রোজেন পরমাণু পরস্পরের নিকটবর্তী হয়, তখন দুটি নিউক্লিয়াস ও ইলেকট্রনের মধ্যে নতুন আকর্ষণ ও বিকর্ষণ বল কার্যকর হয়। আকর্ষণ বল প্রাধান্য পাওয়ায় স্থিতিশক্তি হ্রাস পায় এবং \(74\,\mathrm{pm}\) সাম্যাবস্থা দূরত্বে স্থিতিশক্তি সর্বনিম্ন মানে পৌঁছায় ও \(435.8\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) বন্ধন শক্তি মুক্ত হয়।

৬.২ সিগমা (\(\sigma\)) ও পাই (\(\pi\)) বন্ধন

  • সিগমা (\(\sigma\)) বন্ধন: দুটি পারমাণবিক কক্ষকের আন্তঃনিউক্লীয় অক্ষ বরাবর মুখোমুখি (head-on / axial) অভিলেপনের ফলে গঠিত হয় (\(s-s\), \(s-p\), বা \(p-p\))। অভিলেপনের মাত্রা বেশি হওয়ায় সিগমা বন্ধন শক্তিশালী।
  • পাই (\(\pi\)) বন্ধন: আন্তঃনিউক্লীয় অক্ষের সাথে লম্বভাবে অবস্থিত সমান্তরাল কক্ষকগুলির পার্শ্বীয় (sidewise / lateral) অভিলেপনে গঠিত হয়। অভিলেপন কম হওয়ায় পাই বন্ধন অপেক্ষাকৃত দুর্বল।
  • একাধিক বন্ধন বিশিষ্ট অণুতে প্রথমটি সিগমা বন্ধন এবং পরবর্তীগুলি পাই বন্ধন হয়।

7. Hybridisation Concept & Detailed Molecular Geometry
সংকরায়ণ (Hybridisation) ধারণা ও বিস্তারিত আণবিক জ্যামিতি

English

7.1 Concept & Salient Features of Hybridisation

Pauling introduced hybridisation: the phenomenon of intermixing atomic orbitals of slightly different energies in an isolated atom to redistribute their energies and produce an equal number of new, equivalent hybrid orbitals of identical shape and energy.

  • Number of hybrid orbitals formed = Number of atomic orbitals intermixing.
  • Hybrid orbitals form stronger, more directional \(\sigma\) bonds than pure unhybridized orbitals.
  • Promotion of electrons is not an essential prerequisite; even completely filled orbitals can participate.

7.3 Crucial NEET Study Cases: \(PCl_5\) and \(SF_6\)

Formation of \(PCl_5\) (\(sp^3d\) Hybridisation): Phosphorus ground state is \(3s^2 3p^3\). In the excited state, one \(3s\) electron promotes to \(3d\), yielding five half-filled orbitals (\(3s^1 3p^3 3d^1\)) that hybridize to form five \(sp^3d\) orbitals directed to the vertices of a trigonal bipyramid.

  • Equatorial Bonds: Three \(P-Cl\) bonds lie in a plane at \(120^\circ\).
  • Axial Bonds: Two \(P-Cl\) bonds lie along the vertical axis at \(90^\circ\) to the equatorial plane.
  • Why are axial bonds longer and weaker? Axial bond pairs suffer greater repulsive interaction from three equatorial bond pairs at \(90^\circ\), whereas equatorial pairs experience only two \(90^\circ\) repulsions. Hence, axial bonds are longer (\(240\,\mathrm{pm}\)) than equatorial bonds (\(202\,\mathrm{pm}\)), making \(PCl_5\) highly reactive: \[ \mathrm{PCl_5(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{PCl_3(g)} + \mathrm{Cl_2(g)} \]

Formation of \(SF_6\) (\(sp^3d^2\) Hybridisation): Sulfur ground state \(3s^2 3p^4\) excites to \(3s^1 3p^3 3d^2\). Six equivalent \(sp^3d^2\) orbitals overlap with fluorine \(2p\) orbitals forming a regular octahedron with all \(S-F\) bond lengths equal and angles \(90^\circ\).

8. Molecular Orbital Theory (MOT) & Electronic Configurations
আণবিক কক্ষক তত্ত্ব (MOT) ও ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস

English

8.1 Salient Features & LCAO Method

Formulated by F. Hund and R.S. Mulliken (1932), Molecular Orbital Theory treats electrons in a molecule as occupying molecular orbitals that encompass the entire molecule (polycentric), unlike monocentric atomic orbitals.

  • LCAO (Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals): Wave functions add or subtract: \[ \psi_{\text{MO}} = \psi_A \pm \psi_B \] • Bonding MO (\(\sigma = \psi_A + \psi_B\)): Constructive interference; electron density accumulates between nuclei; lower energy, stabilizes molecule.
    • Antibonding MO (\(\sigma^* = \psi_A - \psi_B\)): Destructive interference; creates a nodal plane between nuclei (zero electron density); higher energy, destabilizes molecule.

8.2 MO Energy Level Sequences

1. For Diatomics with Total Electrons ≤ 14 (\(B_2, C_2, N_2\)) [Due to \(2s-2p\) mixing]:

\[ \sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z \]

2. For Diatomics with Total Electrons > 14 (\(O_2, F_2, Ne_2\)) [No \(2s-2p\) mixing]:

\[ \sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z \]

8.3 Bond Order & Magnetic Properties

\[ \text{Bond Order (B.O.)} = \frac{1}{2} (N_b - N_a) \]
  • If \(N_b > N_a \rightarrow\) Molecule is stable (\(\text{B.O.} > 0\)).
  • If all electrons are paired \(\rightarrow\) Diamagnetic.
  • If one or more unpaired electrons exist \(\rightarrow\) Paramagnetic.

হান্ড এবং মুলিকেন (১৯৩২) এই তত্ত্বটি প্রস্তাব করেন। পরমাণুর ইলেকট্রন যেমন পরমাণুকেন্দ্রিক (monocentric) পারমাণবিক কক্ষকে থাকে, অণুর ইলেকট্রনগুলি তেমনি বহুকেন্দ্রিক (polycentric) আণবিক কক্ষকে অবস্থান করে।

    • বন্ধন সৃষ্টিকারী আণবিক কক্ষক (BMO, \(\sigma\)): তরঙ্গ অপেক্ষকের গঠনমূলক ব্যতিচারে উৎপন্ন হয়; শক্তি কম এবং স্থায়িত্ব বেশি।

9. Hydrogen Bonding: Types & Anomalous Properties
হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধন: প্রকারভেদ ও ভৌত ধর্মের ওপর প্রভাব

English

9.1 Cause and Conditions of Hydrogen Bond Formation

When hydrogen is covalently bonded to a strongly electronegative element (\(F, O, N\)), the shared electron pair is displaced towards the electronegative atom. As a result, the hydrogen atom acquires a fractional positive charge (\(\delta^+\)) while the electronegative atom acquires a fractional negative charge (\(\delta^-\)). The attractive electrostatic force between this partially positive hydrogen and an electronegative atom of another (or same) molecule is termed a hydrogen bond.

\[ \cdots \mathrm{H^{\delta+} - F^{\delta-}} \cdots \mathrm{H^{\delta+} - F^{\delta-}} \cdots \mathrm{H^{\delta+} - F^{\delta-}} \cdots \]

Hydrogen bonds are weaker than covalent bonds (typical energy \(10 - 40\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) vs \(400\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) for covalent bonds), but significantly stronger than typical van der Waals forces.

9.2 Types of Hydrogen Bonds

  1. Intermolecular Hydrogen Bond: Formed between two separate molecules of the same or different compounds. Examples: \(HF\), \(H_2O\), ethanol (\(C_2H_5OH\)), ammonia (\(NH_3\)).
    Effect: Causes molecular association, resulting in anomalously high boiling points, higher viscosity, and high water solubility.
  2. Intramolecular Hydrogen Bond: Formed between a hydrogen atom and an electronegative atom (\(O, N, F\)) within the same molecule (forming a 5- or 6-membered chelate ring).
    Example: o-Nitrophenol, salicylaldehyde.
    Comparison: o-Nitrophenol forms intramolecular H-bonds, preventing intermolecular association; hence, it is steam-volatile and has a lower boiling point than p-nitrophenol (which forms extensive intermolecular H-bonds).

Example 1: Calculating Formal Charges in the Ozone (\(O_3\)) Molecule

Problem: Assign formal charges to all three oxygen atoms in the Lewis structure of ozone \(O_3\), where central oxygen is marked 1, end oxygen with double bond is marked 2, and end oxygen with single bond is marked 3.

Step-by-step Solution:

  • Total valence electrons in free oxygen atom, \(V = 6\).
  • For Central Atom 1: Possesses 1 lone pair (\(L = 2\)) and forms 3 bonds (one double, one single \(\rightarrow S = 6\)): \[ \text{F.C.}_1 = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = 6 - 2 - 3 = +1 \]
  • For Double-bonded Oxygen 2: Possesses 2 lone pairs (\(L = 4\)) and forms 1 double bond (\(S = 4\)): \[ \text{F.C.}_2 = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 6 - 4 - 2 = 0 \]
  • For Single-bonded Oxygen 3: Possesses 3 lone pairs (\(L = 6\)) and forms 1 single bond (\(S = 2\)): \[ \text{F.C.}_3 = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = 6 - 6 - 1 = -1 \]

Conclusion: The formal charges are \(+1\) on central oxygen, \(0\) on double-bonded oxygen, and \(-1\) on single-bonded oxygen. Net charge = \(+1 + 0 + (-1) = 0\).

Example 2: Determining Bond Order & Magnetic Character of \(C_2\) and \(O_2\)

Problem: Using Molecular Orbital Theory, explain why \(C_2\) has a double bond consisting solely of \(\pi\) bonds, and determine the magnetic behavior of \(O_2\).

Solution:

  • For \(C_2\) (\(12\,e^-\)): The MO configuration is \(\mathrm{KK} (\sigma 2s)^2 (\sigma^* 2s)^2 (\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\). \[ \text{Bond Order} = \frac{N_b - N_a}{2} = \frac{8 - 4}{2} = 2 \] The last four electrons reside entirely in the degenerate bonding \(\pi 2p_x\) and \(\pi 2p_y\) molecular orbitals. Therefore, both bonds in the \(C_2\) double bond are \(\pi\) bonds. Since all electrons are paired, \(C_2\) is diamagnetic.
  • For \(O_2\) (\(16\,e^-\)): Following Hund's rule, the last two electrons enter the degenerate antibonding orbitals singly: \((\pi^* 2p_x^1 = \pi^* 2p_y^1)\). Because of these two unpaired electrons, \(O_2\) is paramagnetic, with a bond order of \(\frac{10 - 6}{2} = 2\).

Although both \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) and \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) have a trigonal pyramidal shape with one lone pair on the nitrogen atom, the dipole moment of \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (1.47 D) is significantly higher than that of \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) (0.23 D). The correct rationale is:

  • (C) \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) has a planar structure while \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) is pyramidal.

Detailed Explanation:
In \(\mathrm{NH_3}\), nitrogen is more electronegative than hydrogen; hence, the three \(\mathrm{N-H}\) bond dipoles point towards nitrogen and reinforce the upward lone-pair orbital dipole. This results in a very low net dipole moment in \(\mathrm{NF_3}\).

অরথো-নাইট্রোফেনলে পাশাপাশি থাকা \(\mathrm{-OH}\) এবং \(\mathrm{-NO_2}\) মূলকের মধ্যে অন্তঃআণবিক হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধন তৈরি হয়, যার ফলে পার্শ্ববর্তী অণুর সাথে কোনো সংযোগ তৈরি হয় না। তাই এটি সহজে বাষ্পীভূত হয় (steam volatile)। কিন্তু প্যারা-নাইট্রোফেনলে আন্তঃআণবিক হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধনের মাধ্যমে বহুসংখ্যক অণু পরস্পরের সাথে যুক্ত থাকে, যা স্ফুটনাঙ্ক বৃদ্ধি করে। সুতরাং উভয় বিবৃতিই সঠিক।

Practice Question 9 Single Correct MCQ • Octet Rule Exceptions

Which one of the following species is an odd-electron molecule that violates the octet rule?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{BCl_3}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{NO_2}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{SF_6}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\)
Correct Answer: (B)

Detailed Explanation:
Let us calculate total valence electrons:
• \(\mathrm{BCl_3}\): \(3 + 3(7) = 24\) (even; incomplete octet with 6 electrons around B).
• \(\mathrm{NO_2}\): \(5 + 2(6) = 17\) (odd number of valence electrons; contains an unpaired electron on nitrogen).
• \(\mathrm{SF_6}\): \(6 + 6(7) = 48\) (even; expanded octet with 12 electrons around S).
• \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\): \(2 + 2(7) = 16\) (even; incomplete octet with 4 electrons around Be).
Hence, \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) is the odd-electron molecule.

অনুশীলন প্রশ্ন ৯ একক সঠিক উত্তর • অষ্টক সূত্রের ব্যতিক্রম

নিম্নলিখিত প্রজাতিগুলির মধ্যে কোনটি একটি বিজোড়-ইলেকট্রন বিশিষ্ট অণু (Odd-electron molecule) যা অষ্টক নিয়ম মেনে চলে না?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{BCl_3}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{NO_2}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{SF_6}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\)
সঠিক উত্তর: (B)

বিস্তারিত সমাধান:
নাইট্রোজেন ডাইঅক্সাইড (\(\mathrm{NO_2}\))-এ মোট যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা = \(5 + 2(6) = 17\)। যেহেতু মোট ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা বিজোড়, তাই সব পরমাণুর পক্ষে অষ্টক পূরণ করা অসম্ভব এবং নাইট্রোজেনের ওপর একটি অযুগ্ম ইলেকট্রন অবস্থান করে। তাই \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) একটি বিজোড়-ইলেকট্রন অণু।

Practice Question 10 Single Correct MCQ • Bond Length & Bond Order

The correct increasing order of bond lengths among the given species is:

  • (A) \(\mathrm{N_2 < O_2 < F_2}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{F_2 < O_2 < N_2}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{O_2 < N_2 < F_2}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{N_2 < F_2 < O_2}\)
Correct Answer: (A)

Detailed Explanation:
Bond order is inversely proportional to bond length:
• \(\mathrm{N_2}\) (\(\mathrm{N\equiv N}\)): Bond Order = 3 → Bond length = \(109\,\mathrm{pm}\).
• \(\mathrm{O_2}\) (\(\mathrm{O=O}\)): Bond Order = 2 → Bond length = \(121\,\mathrm{pm}\).
• \(\mathrm{F_2}\) (\(\mathrm{F-F}\)): Bond Order = 1 → Bond length = \(144\,\mathrm{pm}\).
Therefore, the increasing order of bond length is \(\mathrm{N_2 < O_2 < F_2}\).

English
Top 5 Traps to Avoid in NEET UG
  1. Confusing Molecular Geometry with Electron Pair Geometry: \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) has a tetrahedral electron pair geometry, but its molecular shape is trigonal pyramidal. Never write tetrahedral as the shape of ammonia!
  2. Dipole Moment Vector Orientation: In Chemistry, the crossed arrow points from positive to negative (\(\mathrm{\overset{+}{H}\rightarrow\overset{-}{F}}\)), opposite to the classical physics convention.
  3. Assuming All Double Bonds are \(\sigma + \pi\): In \(\mathrm{C_2}\), both bonds in the double bond are \(\pi\) bonds.
  4. Forgetting Equatorial Preference for Lone Pairs in TBP: In \(sp^3d\) (\(\mathrm{SF_4, ClF_3, XeF_2}\)), lone pairs go to equatorial positions to minimize \(90^\circ\) repulsions.
  5. Mixing MOT Energy Sequence: Do NOT use the \(\sigma 2p_z < \pi 2p_{x,y}\) order for \(N_2\) or \(C_2\). \(2s-2p\) mixing shifts \(\sigma 2p_z\) higher in energy than \(\pi 2p_{x,y}\) for \(\le 14\,e^-\) species.

  • ✓ Lattice enthalpy overcompensates endothermic ionization in \(NaCl\) (\(\Delta_{\text{lattice}} H = -788\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)).

13. Final Chapter Summary Recap
অধ্যায়ের চূড়ান্ত সারসংক্ষেপ

English

Chemical bonding represents nature's universal mechanism to lower system energy and achieve thermodynamic stability. The journey from Kössel and Lewis's electronic theory and octet rule to modern quantum mechanical models highlights progressive refinement: Lewis dot structures track valence electrons and formal charges; VSEPR successfully accounts for 3D shapes by minimizing electron-pair repulsions; Valence Bond Theory introduces orbital overlap and hybridisation to explain directional bonds and bond strengths; and Molecular Orbital Theory seamlessly explains paramagnetism in \(O_2\), fractional bond orders, and the non-existence of molecules like \(He_2\) and \(Be_2\). Finally, hydrogen bonding explains anomalous physical behaviors of vital biological and environmental liquids like water.
