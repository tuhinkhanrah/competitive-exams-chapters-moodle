English

Redox (reduction-oxidation) reactions form the backbone of chemical transformations involving energy release, metallurgical extraction, batteries, biochemical pathways, and environmental phenomena like the ozone layer dynamics and hydrogen economy. For NEET UG, master the following core competencies:

  • Differentiate between classical, electronic, and oxidation-state-based definitions of oxidation, reduction, oxidants, and reductants.
  • Assign oxidation numbers accurately following standard IUPAC priority rules, resolving the paradox of fractional oxidation states through chemical structure.
  • Classify redox reactions into combination, decomposition, displacement (metal/non-metal), and disproportionation.
  • Master systematic balancing using both the Oxidation Number Method and the Ion-Electron (Half-Reaction) Method in acidic and basic media.
  • Analyze redox titrations, including permanganometry, dichromate indicators, and iodometric/iodimetric processes with starch indicator.
  • Interpret electrochemical processes in terms of standard reduction potentials (\(E^\circ\)), redox couples, the Daniell cell, and predict reaction feasibility.

2. Evolution of Redox Concepts: Classical to Electronic

২. জারণ-বিজারণ ধারণার বিকাশ: সনাতন থেকে ইলেকট্রনীয় তত্ত্ব

English

2.1 The Classical Concept

Historically, redox processes were identified by the movement of oxygen and hydrogen, which was later generalized to electronegative and electropositive elements:

  • Oxidation: The addition of oxygen or an electronegative element to a substance, or the removal of hydrogen or an electropositive element from a substance.
    Examples: \[ 2\,\mathrm{Mg(s)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{MgO(s)} \quad (\text{Addition of oxygen}) \] \[ \mathrm{Mg(s)} + \mathrm{F_2(g)} \rightarrow \mathrm{MgF_2(s)} \quad (\text{Addition of electronegative fluorine}) \] \[ 2\,\mathrm{H_2S(g)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{S(s)} + 2\,\mathrm{H_2O(l)} \quad (\text{Removal of hydrogen from }\mathrm{H_2S}) \] \[ 2\,\mathrm{K_4[Fe(CN)_6](aq)} + \mathrm{H_2O_2(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6](aq)} + 2\,\mathrm{KOH(aq)} \] In this reaction, the removal of the electropositive element potassium (\(\mathrm{K}\)) from potassium ferrocyanide oxidizes it to potassium ferricyanide.
  • Reduction: The removal of oxygen or an electronegative element from a substance, or the addition of hydrogen or an electropositive element to a substance.
    Examples: \[ 2\,\mathrm{HgO(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\mathrm{Hg(l)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \quad (\text{Removal of oxygen}) \] \[ 2\,\mathrm{FeCl_3(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{FeCl_2(aq)} + 2\,\mathrm{HCl(aq)} \quad (\text{Removal of electronegative chlorine}) \] \[ \mathrm{CH_2=CH_2(g)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)} \rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3-CH_3(g)} \quad (\text{Addition of hydrogen}) \] \[ 2\,\mathrm{HgCl_2(aq)} + \mathrm{SnCl_2(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Hg_2Cl_2(s)} + \mathrm{SnCl_4(aq)} \quad (\text{Addition of electropositive Hg to }\mathrm{HgCl_2}) \]

2.2 The Electronic Transfer Concept

Because oxidation and reduction occur simultaneously, the modern foundation treats redox reactions as electron transfer processes:

  • Oxidation: Loss of electron(s) by a species (\(\text{OIL: Oxidation Is Loss}\)).
  • Reduction: Gain of electron(s) by a species (\(\text{RIG: Reduction Is Gain}\)).
  • Oxidizing Agent (Oxidant): An electron acceptor; it oxidizes another species and gets reduced itself.
  • Reducing Agent (Reductant): An electron donor; it reduces another species and gets oxidized itself.

For example, the formation of sodium chloride from its constituent elements:

\[ \text{Oxidation half-reaction: } 2\,\mathrm{Na(s)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{Na^+} + 2\,\mathrm{e}^- \] \[ \text{Reduction half-reaction: } \mathrm{Cl_2(g)} + 2\,\mathrm{e}^- \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{Cl}^- \] \[ \text{Overall redox reaction: } 2\,\mathrm{Na(s)} + \mathrm{Cl_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{NaCl(s)} \]

3. Oxidation Number: Definition, IUPAC Rules & Stock Notation

৩. জারণ সংখ্যা: সংজ্ঞা, IUPAC নিয়মাবলী ও স্টক সংকেত

English

Definition: The oxidation number of an element in a compound is defined as the residual electrical charge that its atom would carry if all shared electron pairs in every covalent bond were assigned completely to the more electronegative atom.

Mandatory Rules for Assigning Oxidation Numbers

  1. Free Elements: Any atom in its uncombined elemental state has an oxidation number of 0 (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2, O_2, Cl_2, O_3, P_4, S_8, Na, Mg, Al}\)).
  2. Monoatomic Ions: The oxidation number equals the net charge on the ion (\(\mathrm{Na^+} = +1\), \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}} = +2\), \(\mathrm{Al^{3+}} = +3\), \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}} = +3\), \(\mathrm{Cl^-} = -1\), \(\mathrm{O^{2-}} = -2\)). Group 1 metals (alkali metals) are always +1 and Group 2 (alkaline earths) are always +2 in their compounds. Fluorine is always -1.
  3. Oxygen:
    • In normal oxides: -2 (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2O, CO_2, MgO}\)).
    • In peroxides: -1 (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2O_2, Na_2O_2, BaO_2}\)) where an \(\mathrm{-O-O-}\) linkage exists.
    • In superoxides: \(-1/2\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{KO_2, RbO_2, CsO_2}\)).
    • In fluorides of oxygen: +2 in \(\mathrm{OF_2}\) and +1 in \(\mathrm{O_2F_2}\), because fluorine is more electronegative than oxygen.
  4. Hydrogen: Assigned +1 in most compounds, except in binary metal hydrides (ionic hydrides such as \(\mathrm{LiH, NaH, CaH_2}\)) where it is -1.
  5. Halogens: Fluorine is always -1. Chlorine, bromine, and iodine are -1 when bonded to less electronegative elements (as halides). However, when bonded to oxygen or fluorine (in oxoacids, oxoanions, and interhalogens), they exhibit positive oxidation states (up to +7).
  6. Algebraic Sum: The sum of oxidation numbers of all atoms in a neutral molecule is 0. In a polyatomic ion, the sum equals the net charge on the ion.

Stock Notation

Devised by Alfred Stock, this notation indicates the oxidation state of a metal by writing a Roman numeral in parentheses immediately following the metal's name or symbol: e.g., \(\mathrm{Au(III)Cl_3}\), \(\mathrm{Sn(II)Cl_2}\), \(\mathrm{Sn(IV)Cl_4}\), \(\mathrm{Fe(II)O}\), \(\mathrm{Fe_2(III)O_3}\), \(\mathrm{Mn(IV)O_2}\), \(\mathrm{Cu(I)_2O}\).

Modern Definitions via Oxidation Number:
  • Oxidation: An algebraic increase in the oxidation number of an element.
  • Reduction: An algebraic decrease in the oxidation number of an element.
  • Oxidant (Oxidizing Agent): A reagent that increases the oxidation number of another element (and its own oxidation number decreases).
  • Reductant (Reducing Agent): A reagent that decreases the oxidation number of another element (and its own oxidation number increases).

জারণ সংখ্যা নির্ধারণের মৌলিক নিয়মাবলী

  1. মুক্ত বা অপ্রশমিত মৌল: মুক্ত অবস্থায় প্রতিটি মৌলের পরমাণুর জারণ সংখ্যা সর্বদা ০ (শূন্য) (যেমন- \(\mathrm{H_2, O_2, O_3, P_4, S_8, Na, Mg}\))।
  2. এক-পরমাণুক আয়ন ও নির্দিষ্ট ধাতু: আয়নের জারণ সংখ্যা তার আধানের সমান। ক্ষার ধাতুগুলির (Group 1) যৌগে জারণ সংখ্যা সর্বদা +1 এবং ক্ষারীয় মৃত্তিকা ধাতুগুলির (Group 2) সর্বদা +2। অ্যালুমিনিয়াম সর্বদা +3। ফ্লোরিনের জারণ সংখ্যা সর্বদা -1।
  3. অক্সিজেনের ক্ষেত্রে:
    • সাধারণ অক্সাইডে: -2 (যেমন- \(\mathrm{H_2O, CaO}\))।
    • পারঅক্সাইডে: -1 (যেমন- \(\mathrm{H_2O_2, Na_2O_2}\))।
    • সুপারঅক্সাইডে: \(-1/2\) (যেমন- \(\mathrm{KO_2}\))।
    • ফ্লোরিনের সাথে যৌগে: \(\mathrm{OF_2}\)-তে +2 এবং \(\mathrm{O_2F_2}\)-তে +1।
  4. হাইড্রোজেনের ক্ষেত্রে: অধাতুর সাথে যুক্ত থাকলে +1, কিন্তু সক্রিয় ধাতুর সাথে গঠিত ধাতব হাইড্রাইডে (\(\mathrm{NaH, CaH_2}\)) এর জারণ সংখ্যা -1।
  5. হ্যালোজেন: ফ্লোরিন সর্বদা -1। ক্লোরিন, ব্রোমিন ও আয়োডিন সাধারণত -1 হলেও অক্সিজেনের সাথে গঠিত অক্সোঅ্যাসিড বা অক্সোঅ্যানায়নে ধনাত্মক জারণ অবস্থা (+1 থেকে +7) প্রদর্শন করে।
  6. বীজগাণিতিক যোগফল: প্রশম অণুতে সব পরমাণুর জারণ সংখ্যার যোগফল শূন্য (০) এবং জটিল আয়নে তা আয়নের মোট আধানের সমান।

স্টক সংকেত (Stock Notation)

জার্মান রসায়নবিদ অ্যালফ্রেড স্টকের নামানুসারে যৌগে ধাতুর জারণ অবস্থাকে রোমান সংখ্যায় বন্ধনীর মধ্যে চিহ্নের পাশে লেখা হয়; যেমন- \(\mathrm{Fe(II)O}\), \(\mathrm{Fe_2(III)O_3}\), \(\mathrm{Cu(I)I}\), \(\mathrm{Mn(IV)O_2}\), \(\mathrm{HAu(III)Cl_4}\)।

4. Paradox of Fractional Oxidation Numbers & Structural Insights

৪. ভগ্নাংশ জারণ সংখ্যার বিভ্রান্তি ও গাঠনিক তাৎপর্য

English

Electrons are discrete fundamental particles and cannot be transferred or shared in fractions. When the classical formula calculation gives a fractional oxidation state, it represents the average oxidation state of atoms of that element existing in non-equivalent structural environments. The actual integer oxidation states are revealed by the molecular Lewis structures.

Compound / Ion Formula Calculation (Average) Actual Structural Oxidation States Chemical Identity & Bonding Context
Carbon suboxide (\(\mathrm{C_3O_2}\)) \(3x + 2(-2) = 0 \implies x = +\frac{4}{3}\) Two terminal carbons: +2 each; Central carbon: 0 Linear structure: \(\mathrm{O=\overset{+2}{C}=\overset{0}{C}=\overset{+2}{C}=O}\)
Tribromooctaoxide (\(\mathrm{Br_3O_8}\)) \(3x + 8(-2) = 0 \implies x = +\frac{16}{3}\) Two terminal bromines: +6 each; Central bromine: +4 Each terminal Br is double-bonded to three oxygens; central Br to two oxygens.

5. Classification of Redox Reactions

৫. জারণ-বিজারণ বিক্রিয়ার শ্রেণিবিন্যাস

English

1. Combination Reactions

Denoted as \(\mathrm{A + B \rightarrow C}\). For this to be a redox reaction, at least one of the reacting species (\(\mathrm{A}\) or \(\mathrm{B}\)) must be in an uncombined elemental state.
Examples: \[ \overset{0}{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{(s)} + \overset{0}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \overset{+4}{\mathrm{C}}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \] \[ 3\,\overset{0}{\mathrm{Mg}}\mathrm{(s)} + \overset{0}{\mathrm{N}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \overset{+2}{\mathrm{Mg}}_3\overset{-3}{\mathrm{N}}_2\mathrm{(s)} \]

2. Decomposition Reactions

The breakdown of a compound into two or more components, where at least one of the products is in the elemental state.
Examples: \[ 2\,\overset{+1}{\mathrm{H}}_2\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{(l)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\overset{0}{\mathrm{H}}_2\mathrm{(g)} + \overset{0}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \] \[ 2\,\overset{+1}{\mathrm{K}}\overset{+5}{\mathrm{Cl}}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}_3\mathrm{(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\overset{+1}{\mathrm{K}}\overset{-1}{\mathrm{Cl}}\mathrm{(s)} + 3\,\overset{0}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \] Crucial NEET Point: Not all decomposition reactions are redox! For example, the thermal decomposition of limestone: \[ \overset{+2}{\mathrm{Ca}}\overset{+4}{\mathrm{C}}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}_3\mathrm{(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \overset{+2}{\mathrm{Ca}}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{(s)} + \overset{+4}{\mathrm{C}}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \] No atom changes its oxidation state; hence, it is a non-redox decomposition.

3. Displacement Reactions (\(\mathrm{X + YZ \rightarrow XZ + Y}\))

  • Metal Displacement: A more electropositive metal displaces a less electropositive metal from its aqueous salt solution or oxide. Extensively used in metallurgy: \[ \mathrm{V_2O_5(s)} + 5\,\mathrm{Ca(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2\,\mathrm{V(s)} + 5\,\mathrm{CaO(s)} \] \[ \mathrm{TiCl_4(l)} + 2\,\mathrm{Mg(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Ti(s)} + 2\,\mathrm{MgCl_2(s)} \] \[ \mathrm{Cr_2O_3(s)} + 2\,\mathrm{Al(s)} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{Al_2O_3(s)} + 2\,\mathrm{Cr(s)} \quad (\text{Thermite process}) \]
  • Non-Metal Displacement:
    • Hydrogen displacement: Highly active alkali and alkaline earth metals (\(\mathrm{Na, Ca}\)) displace \(\mathrm{H_2}\) from cold water. Less active metals (\(\mathrm{Mg, Fe}\)) displace \(\mathrm{H_2}\) from steam. Metals above hydrogen in the activity series (\(\mathrm{Zn, Fe}\)) displace \(\mathrm{H_2}\) from dilute non-oxidizing acids (\(\mathrm{HCl, H_2SO_4}\)). Unreactive metals (\(\mathrm{Ag, Au}\)) do not displace \(\mathrm{H_2}\).
    • Halogen displacement & the Layer Test: Oxidizing power decreases down Group 17: \(\mathrm{F_2 > Cl_2 > Br_2 > I_2}\). Fluorine is so potent it oxidizes water directly (\(2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + 2\,\mathrm{F_2} \rightarrow 4\,\mathrm{HF} + \mathrm{O_2}\)). Chlorine displaces bromide and iodide from aqueous solutions: \[ \mathrm{Cl_2(g)} + 2\,\mathrm{Br^-(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{Cl^-(aq)} + \mathrm{Br_2(l)} \quad (\text{Orange/brown in }\mathrm{CCl_4}) \] \[ \mathrm{Cl_2(g)} + 2\,\mathrm{I^-(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{Cl^-(aq)} + \mathrm{I_2(s)} \quad (\text{Violet in }\mathrm{CCl_4}) \]

4. Disproportionation Reactions

A specialized redox reaction in which an element in an intermediate oxidation state is simultaneously oxidized to a higher state and reduced to a lower state. Condition: The reacting element must be able to exhibit at least three different oxidation states.

  • Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide: \[ 2\,\mathrm{H_2}\overset{-1}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{H_2}\overset{-2}{\mathrm{O}}\mathrm{(l)} + \overset{0}{\mathrm{O}}_2\mathrm{(g)} \] Oxygen changes from \(-1\) to \(-2\) (reduction) and \(0\) (oxidation).
  • Phosphorus in Alkaline Medium: \[ \overset{0}{\mathrm{P}}_4\mathrm{(s)} + 3\,\mathrm{OH^-(aq)} + 3\,\mathrm{H_2O(l)} \rightarrow \overset{-3}{\mathrm{P}}\mathrm{H_3(g)} + 3\,\mathrm{H_2}\overset{+1}{\mathrm{P}}\mathrm{O_2^-(aq)} \]
  • Sulphur in Alkaline Medium: \[ \overset{0}{\mathrm{S}}_8\mathrm{(s)} + 12\,\mathrm{OH^-(aq)} \rightarrow 4\,\overset{-2}{\mathrm{S}^{2-}}\mathrm{(aq)} + 2\,\overset{+2}{\mathrm{S}}_2\mathrm{O_3^{2-}}\mathrm{(aq)} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O(l)} \]
  • Halogens in Alkali: \[ \overset{0}{\mathrm{Cl}}_2\mathrm{(g)} + 2\,\mathrm{OH^-(aq)} \xrightarrow{\text{cold, dil.}} \overset{+1}{\mathrm{Cl}}\mathrm{O^-(aq)} + \overset{-1}{\mathrm{Cl}}\mathrm{^-(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2O(l)} \] High-Yield Rule: Fluorine does NOT disproportionate! Being the most electronegative element, it exhibits only 0 and -1 oxidation states: \[ 2\,\mathrm{F_2(g)} + 2\,\mathrm{OH^-(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{F^-(aq)} + \mathrm{OF_2(g)} + \mathrm{H_2O(l)} \] Also, oxoanions where the central halogen is already in its highest maximum oxidation state (such as \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\) where \(\mathrm{Cl} = +7\)) cannot disproportionate because they cannot be oxidized further.

    • অধাতু প্রতিস্থাপন: সক্রিয় ক্ষার ধাতু শীতল জল থেকে এবং \(\mathrm{Zn, Fe}\) অ্যাসিড থেকে \(\mathrm{H_2}\) অপসারিত করে। হ্যালোজেনের ক্ষেত্রে তীব্রতা ক্রম: \(\mathrm{F_2 > Cl_2 > Br_2 > I_2}\)। ক্লোরিন জলীয় ব্রোমাইড ও আয়োডাইডকে জারিত করে \(\mathrm{Br_2}\) ও \(\mathrm{I_2}\)-তে রূপান্তরিত করে, যা 'লেয়ার টেস্ট' (Layer Test)-এর মূল ভিত্তি।
  4. অসমঞ্জসন বিক্রিয়া (Disproportionation): যে বিশেষ রেডক্স বিক্রিয়ায় একটি মধ্যবর্তী জারণ অবস্থায় থাকা মৌল একই সাথে উচ্চতর জারণ অবস্থায় জারিত এবং নিম্নতর জারণ অবস্থায় বিজারিত হয়। \[ 2\,\mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{O_2} \] \[ \mathrm{Cl_2} + 2\,\mathrm{OH^-} \rightarrow \mathrm{ClO^-} + \mathrm{Cl^-} + \mathrm{H_2O} \] গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ব্যতিক্রম: ফ্লোরিন সর্বাধিক তড়িৎ-ঋণাত্মক হওয়ায় এটি কোনো ধনাত্মক জারণ অবস্থা প্রদর্শন করতে পারে না; তাই ফ্লোরিন কখনো অসমঞ্জসন বিক্রিয়া দেয় না। একইভাবে \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\)-এ ক্লোরিন সর্বোচ্চ (+7) জারণ অবস্থায় থাকায় এটি অসমঞ্জসন বিক্রিয়ায় অংশ নিতে পারে না।

6. Systematic Balancing: Oxidation Number & Ion-Electron Methods

৬. জারণ-বিজারণ সমীকরণ সমতাবিধান: জারণ সংখ্যা ও আয়ন-ইলেকট্রন পদ্ধতি

English

Method A: The Oxidation Number Method

Follow these 5 unambiguous steps:

  • Step 1: Write the skeleton equation with correct formulas for all reactants and products.
  • Step 2: Assign oxidation numbers to all atoms to identify which are oxidized and which are reduced.
  • Step 3: Calculate the total increase in oxidation number for the oxidized species and the total decrease for the reduced species. Equalize the total increase and total decrease by multiplying with suitable stoichiometric integers.
  • Step 4: Balance the net ionic charge:
    • In acidic medium: Add \(\mathrm{H^+}\) ions to the deficient side.
    • In basic medium: Add \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) ions to the deficient side.
  • Step 5: Balance hydrogen and oxygen atoms by adding required \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) molecules to the appropriate side. Conduct a final atom-and-charge check.

Method B: The Ion-Electron (Half-Reaction) Method

This is the preferred method for aqueous ionic reactions:

  1. Split the skeletal ionic reaction into two separate half-reactions: the Oxidation Half-Reaction and the Reduction Half-Reaction.
  2. In each half-reaction, balance all atoms other than \(\mathrm{O}\) and \(\mathrm{H}\).
  3. Balance oxygen atoms by adding \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) molecules to the oxygen-deficient side.
  4. Balance hydrogen atoms:
    • In Acidic Medium: Add \(\mathrm{H^+}\) ions to the hydrogen-deficient side.
    • In Basic Medium: Add \(\mathrm{H^+}\) to balance \(\mathrm{H}\) as in acidic medium, then add an equal number of \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) ions to both sides. Combine \(\mathrm{H^+ + OH^-}\) into \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) on the side where they co-exist, and cancel excess \(\mathrm{H_2O}\).
  5. Balance electrical charge in each half-reaction by adding electrons (\(\mathrm{e^-}\)) to the side with greater positive charge.
  6. Multiply the half-reactions by minimum integers so that the number of electrons lost equals the number of electrons gained.
  7. Add the two half-reactions together, cancel electrons and common water molecules, and verify mass and charge conservation.

৭. জারণ-বিজারণ টাইট্রেশন, তড়িৎদ্বার প্রক্রিয়া ও ড্যানিয়েল কোষ

English

7.1 Redox Titrations & Indicators

  • Permanganate Titration (Self-Indicator): \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\) is intensely deep purple. In acidic media, it is reduced to virtually colourless \(\mathrm{Mn^{2+}}\). The moment all reductant (\(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) or \(\mathrm{C_2O_4^{2-}}\)) is consumed, the very first excess drop of \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\) gives a permanent light pink colour at concentrations as low as \(10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\). Hence, \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\) acts as a self-indicator.
  • Dichromate Titration (Internal Redox Indicator): \(\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}\) is not a self-indicator because its reduction product \(\mathrm{Cr^{3+}}\) is green. Instead, an internal redox indicator like diphenylamine is used, which is oxidized by the first slight excess of dichromate after the equivalence point to yield an intense blue/violet colour.
  • Iodometry and Iodimetry (Starch Indicator): Used for reagents capable of oxidizing iodide (\(\mathrm{I^-}\)) to free iodine (\(\mathrm{I_2}\)), such as \(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}\): \[ 2\,\mathrm{Cu^{2+}(aq)} + 4\,\mathrm{I^-(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Cu_2I_2(s)} + \mathrm{I_2(aq)} \] The liberated iodine reacts with freshly prepared starch to form an intense dark blue starch-iodine inclusion complex. Titration against standard sodium thiosulphate (\(\mathrm{Na_2S_2O_3}\)) discharges the blue colour sharply at the end point: \[ \mathrm{I_2(aq)} + 2\,\mathrm{S_2O_3^{2-}(aq)} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{I^-(aq)} + \mathrm{S_4O_6^{2-}(aq)} \]

7.2 Electrochemical Processes & The Daniell Cell

When a zinc rod is placed directly in \(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\) solution, electrons transfer directly, releasing heat. If the oxidation and reduction halves are physically separated into two beakers connected by an external metallic wire and an internal salt bridge (a U-tube containing an inert electrolyte like \(\mathrm{KCl}\) or \(\mathrm{NH_4NO_3}\) solidified in agar-agar jelly), chemical energy is directly converted into electrical energy. This device is the Daniell Cell.

  • Anode (Negative Electrode): Site of oxidation: \(\mathrm{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zn^{2+}(aq)} + 2\,\mathrm{e}^-\).
  • Cathode (Positive Electrode): Site of reduction: \(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}(aq)} + 2\,\mathrm{e}^- \rightarrow \mathrm{Cu(s)}\).
  • Electron Flow: From Zn anode to Cu cathode through the external wire.
  • Conventional Current: Opposite to electron flow, from cathode to anode.
  • Salt Bridge Functions: Completes the internal electrical circuit and maintains electrical neutrality in both half-cells by migration of ions without mechanical mixing of solutions.

7.3 Standard Reduction Potentials (\(E^\circ\)) & Feasibility

By convention, standard electrode potentials are expressed as reduction potentials measured against the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE, \(E^\circ = 0.00\,\mathrm{V}\)) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), \(1\,\mathrm{atm}\), and \(1\,\mathrm{M}\) concentration.

  • Higher Positive \(E^\circ\): Greater tendency to gain electrons \(\implies\) Stronger oxidizing agent in its oxidized form (e.g., \(\mathrm{F_2}\) with \(E^\circ = +2.87\,\mathrm{V}\) is the strongest oxidant).
  • More Negative \(E^\circ\): Greater tendency to lose electrons \(\implies\) Stronger reducing agent in its reduced form (e.g., \(\mathrm{Li}\) with \(E^\circ = -3.05\,\mathrm{V}\) is the strongest reductant in aqueous solution).
  • Reaction Feasibility: A redox reaction is spontaneous under standard conditions if the cell EMF is positive: \[ E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} > 0 \]

Worked Example 1: Acidic Medium Balancing (Dichromate + Sulphite)

English

Problem: Balance the net ionic equation for the reaction of potassium dichromate(\(\mathrm{VI}\)) with sodium sulphite in acidic aqueous solution to produce chromium(\(\mathrm{III}\)) and sulphate ions.

Solution:

  1. Skeletal equation: \(\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}(aq) + SO_3^{2-}(aq) \rightarrow Cr^{3+}(aq) + SO_4^{2-}(aq)}\)
  2. Assign oxidation numbers: \(\mathrm{Cr}\) goes from \(+6\) to \(+3\) (\(\Delta = -3\) per \(\mathrm{Cr}\), total decrease \(= 2 \times 3 = 6\)). \(\mathrm{S}\) goes from \(+4\) to \(+6\) (\(\Delta = +2\)).
  3. Equalize electrons: Multiply sulphite by \(3\) and dichromate by \(1\): \[ \mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3\,SO_3^{2-} \rightarrow 2\,Cr^{3+} + 3\,SO_4^{2-}} \]
  4. Balance charge in acidic medium: Left charge \(= -2 + 3(-2) = -8\). Right charge \(= 2(+3) + 3(-2) = 0\). Add \(8\,\mathrm{H^+}\) to left: \[ \mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-} + 3\,SO_3^{2-} + 8\,H^+ \rightarrow 2\,Cr^{3+} + 3\,SO_4^{2-}} \]
  5. Balance H and O: Add \(4\,\mathrm{H_2O}\) to right side. \[ \mathbf{\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}(aq) + 3\,SO_3^{2-}(aq) + 8\,H^+(aq) \rightarrow 2\,Cr^{3+}(aq) + 3\,SO_4^{2-}(aq) + 4\,H_2O(l)}} \]

Worked Example 2: Basic Medium Balancing (Permanganate + Bromide)

English

Problem: Permanganate ion oxidizes bromide ion in basic medium to produce manganese dioxide (\(\mathrm{MnO_2}\)) and bromate ion (\(\mathrm{BrO_3^-}\)). Write the balanced net ionic equation.

Solution:

  1. Assign oxidation states: \(\mathrm{Mn}\) changes from \(+7\) to \(+4\) (decrease of \(3\)). \(\mathrm{Br}\) changes from \(-1\) to \(+5\) (increase of \(6\)).
  2. Equalize change: Multiply \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\) by \(2\) and \(\mathrm{Br^-}\) by \(1\): \[ 2\,\mathrm{MnO_4^- + Br^- \rightarrow 2\,MnO_2 + BrO_3^-} \]
  3. Balance charge in basic medium: Left charge \(= 2(-1) + (-1) = -3\). Right charge \(= -1\). Add \(2\,\mathrm{OH^-}\) to the right side: \[ 2\,\mathrm{MnO_4^- + Br^- \rightarrow 2\,MnO_2 + BrO_3^- + 2\,OH^-} \]
  4. Balance H and O: Add \(1\,\mathrm{H_2O}\) to the left side: \[ \mathbf{2\,\mathrm{MnO_4^-(aq) + Br^-(aq) + H_2O(l) \rightarrow 2\,MnO_2(s) + BrO_3^-(aq) + 2\,OH^-(aq)}} \]
বাংলা

সমস্যা: ক্ষারীয় মাধ্যমে পারম্যাঙ্গানেট আয়ন ব্রোমাইড আয়নকে ব্রোমেট (\(\mathrm{BrO_3^-}\))-এ জারিত করে নিজে \(\mathrm{MnO_2}\)-তে বিজারিত হয়। বিক্রিয়াটির সমতাবিধান করো।

সমাধান: ম্যাঙ্গানিজের হ্রাস \(= 3\), ব্রোমিনের বৃদ্ধি \(= 6\)। সুতরাং পারম্যাঙ্গানেটকে ২ দ্বারা গুণ করা হয়। ক্ষারীয় মাধ্যমে আধান সমান করতে ডানপাশে \(2\,\mathrm{OH^-}\) এবং পরমাণু সমতায় বামপাশে \(1\,\mathrm{H_2O}\) যোগ করে সুষম সমীকরণ পাওয়া যায়:

\[ \mathbf{2\,\mathrm{MnO_4^- + Br^- + H_2O \rightarrow 2\,MnO_2 + BrO_3^- + 2\,OH^-}} \]

Step-by-step Reasoning:

For a reaction to be spontaneous under standard conditions, \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} > 0\).

  • For (A): \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}/Fe^{2+}}) - E^\circ(\mathrm{I_2/I^-}) = 0.77 - 0.54 = +0.23\,\mathrm{V} > 0\) (Spontaneous).
  • For (B): \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ(\mathrm{Ag^+/Ag}) - E^\circ(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}) = 0.80 - 0.34 = +0.46\,\mathrm{V} > 0\) (Spontaneous).
  • For (C): \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}/Fe^{2+}}) - E^\circ(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}) = 0.77 - 0.34 = +0.43\,\mathrm{V} > 0\) (Spontaneous).
  • For (D): Reduction is \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}} \rightarrow \mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) (\(0.77\,\mathrm{V}\)) and oxidation is \(\mathrm{Ag} \rightarrow \mathrm{Ag^+}\) (\(0.80\,\mathrm{V}\)).
    \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = 0.77 - 0.80 = -0.03\,\mathrm{V} < 0\).
    Since \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}}\) is negative, this reaction is non-spontaneous.

Practice Question 4 (NEET Disproportionation Concept)

Among the oxoanions of chlorine: \(\mathrm{ClO^-}\), \(\mathrm{ClO_2^-}\), \(\mathrm{ClO_3^-}\), and \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\), which species cannot undergo a disproportionation reaction, and what is the underlying chemical reason?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{ClO^-}\), because chlorine has the lowest oxidation number +1
  • (B) \(\mathrm{ClO_3^-}\), because it has an odd number of oxygen atoms
  • (C) \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\), because chlorine is already present in its maximum oxidation state (+7)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{ClO_2^-}\), because it spontaneously dimerizes in solution
Correct Answer: (C)
Step-by-step Reasoning:

In a disproportionation reaction, the reacting element must be simultaneously oxidized and reduced. For oxidation to occur, the element must be able to increase its oxidation state. In \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\) (perchlorate ion), chlorine has an oxidation state of \(+7\) (\(x + 4(-2) = -1 \implies x = +7\)), which corresponds to its group valence maximum (Group 17, \(17 - 10 = +7\)). Since chlorine cannot lose any more valence electrons to attain a higher oxidation number, \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\) cannot undergo disproportionation.

Practice Question 5 (NEET Assertion-Reason Type)

Assertion (A): Fluorine reacts with dilute aqueous \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) to form \(\mathrm{OF_2}\) and \(\mathrm{F^-}\), but does not undergo disproportionation.

Reason (R): Fluorine is the most electronegative element and cannot exhibit any positive oxidation state in its chemical compounds.

  • (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
  • (B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is NOT the correct explanation of (A).
  • (C) (A) is true but (R) is false.
  • (D) (A) is false but (R) is true.
Correct Answer: (A)
Step-by-step Reasoning:
  • In the reaction \(2\,\mathrm{F_2} + 2\,\mathrm{OH^-} \rightarrow 2\,\mathrm{F^-} + \mathrm{OF_2} + \mathrm{H_2O}\), elemental fluorine (0) is reduced to \(\mathrm{F^-}\) (-1) and oxidized to \(\mathrm{OF_2}\) where oxygen is +2 and fluorine remains -1. Thus, fluorine is solely reduced (from 0 to -1); oxygen of \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) (-2) is oxidized to \(\mathrm{OF_2}\) (+2). Fluorine does not experience disproportionation.
  • Reason (R) is factually correct and explains why: because fluorine lacks d-orbitals and has the highest Pauling electronegativity (4.0), it cannot exhibit positive oxidation states. Hence, Assertion (A) is correctly explained by Reason (R).

১০. সাধারণ ভুলভ্রান্তি ও রিভিশন চেকলিস্ট

English

Common Traps & How to Avoid Them

  • Peroxo Linkage Blindspot: Applying formula blindly to \(\mathrm{CrO_5}\) or \(\mathrm{H_2SO_5}\) gives +10 or +8. Always remember: an element's oxidation state can never exceed its total number of valence electrons (e.g., maximum for \(\mathrm{S} = +6\), \(\mathrm{Cr} = +6\), \(\mathrm{Mn} = +7\)). If your calculation exceeds the group valence, look for peroxo bonds!
  • Reaction of \(\mathrm{Pb_3O_4}\) with Acids: \(\mathrm{Pb_3O_4}\) is \(2\,\mathrm{PbO} \cdot \mathrm{PbO_2}\). With non-reducing \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\), only basic \(\mathrm{PbO}\) reacts in an acid-base neutralization to give \(\mathrm{Pb(NO_3)_2}\) leaving insoluble \(\mathrm{PbO_2}\) as a precipitate. With \(\mathrm{HCl}\), \(\mathrm{PbO_2}\) acts as an oxidizing agent and oxidizes \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) to \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\).
  • Reductant Excess vs. Oxidant Excess: Excess reducing agent yields the element in its lower oxidation state (e.g., \(\mathrm{Fe + S \rightarrow FeS}\) [+2]); excess oxidizing agent yields higher oxidation states (e.g., \(\mathrm{P_4 + 5\,O_2 \rightarrow P_4O_{10}}\) [+5] instead of \(\mathrm{P_4O_6}\) [+3]).
  • Basic Medium Balancing Trap: Forgetting to convert \(\mathrm{H^+}\) and \(\mathrm{OH^-}\) to \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) and failing to cancel common water molecules from both sides.

NEET High-Yield Checklist

  • Can assign oxidation states to all atoms in mixed oxides (\(\mathrm{Fe_3O_4, Pb_3O_4, Mn_3O_4}\)) and organic molecules (\(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH \rightarrow C = -2}\); \(\mathrm{CH_3COOH \rightarrow C = 0}\)).
  • Familiar with disproportionation conditions (element exists in at least 3 oxidation states) and know why \(\mathrm{F_2}\) and \(\mathrm{ClO_4^-}\) cannot disproportionate.
  • Mastered balancing in acidic (\(\mathrm{H^+/H_2O}\)) and basic (\(\mathrm{OH^-/H_2O}\)) solutions.
  • Know the roles in titrations: \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-}\) is self-indicator, diphenylamine for \(\mathrm{Cr_2O_7^{2-}}\), and starch for iodometry.
  • Can predict cell feasibility using \(E^\circ_{\text{cell}} = E^\circ_{\text{cathode}} - E^\circ_{\text{anode}} > 0\).
