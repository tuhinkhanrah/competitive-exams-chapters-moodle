Historical Development: Vital Force Theory & Its Fall

Around 1780, chemists distinguished between organic compounds derived from living organisms (plants and animals) and inorganic compounds from mineral sources. The Swedish chemist Jöns Jacob Berzelius proposed the Vital Force Theory, asserting that a mysterious "vital force" inherent only in living beings was necessary to produce organic compounds.

This doctrine was decisively overthrown in 1828 when the German chemist Friedrich Wöhler synthesised urea (an organic compound excreted in urine) by heating ammonium cyanate (an inorganic salt):

\[ \mathrm{NH_4CNO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{NH_2-CO-NH_2} \]

Subsequent total syntheses of acetic acid by Hermann Kolbe (1845) and methane by Marcellin Berthelot (1856) confirmed beyond doubt that organic compounds follow standard chemical laws and can be synthesised from purely mineral precursors.

2. Tetravalence of Carbon, Hybridisation, and Shapes of Molecules

Carbon has the atomic number 6 with ground-state electronic configuration \(\mathrm{1s^2\,2s^2\,2p_x^1\,2p_y^1\,2p_z^0}\). Upon excitation, one \(\mathrm{2s}\) electron is promoted to the vacant \(\mathrm{2p_z}\) orbital, giving the excited-state configuration \(\mathrm{1s^2\,2s^1\,2p_x^1\,2p_y^1\,2p_z^1}\), thereby providing four unpaired electrons available for covalent bonding (tetravalence).

Types of Hybridisation in Carbon Compounds

  • \(\mathrm{sp^3}\) Hybridisation: Mixing of one \(\mathrm{2s}\) and three \(\mathrm{2p}\) orbitals produces four equivalent \(\mathrm{sp^3}\) hybrid orbitals directed towards the corners of a regular tetrahedron with bond angles of \(109.5^\circ\) (\(109^\circ 28'\)). Example: Methane (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\)), Ethane (\(\mathrm{C_2H_6}\)). Every single bond formed is a \(\sigma\)-bond.
  • \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) Hybridisation: Mixing of one \(\mathrm{2s}\) and two \(\mathrm{2p}\) orbitals produces three planar trigonal \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) hybrid orbitals inclined at \(120^\circ\) to each other. The unhybridised \(\mathrm{2p}\) orbital lies perpendicular to the plane. Example: Ethene (\(\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2}\)). The double bond consists of one \(\sigma\)-bond and one \(\pi\)-bond.
  • \(\mathrm{sp}\) Hybridisation: Mixing of one \(\mathrm{2s}\) and one \(\mathrm{2p}\) orbital yields two linear \(\mathrm{sp}\) hybrid orbitals oriented at \(180^\circ\). Two mutually perpendicular unhybridised \(\mathrm{p}\) orbitals remain. Example: Ethyne (\(\mathrm{HC\equiv CH}\)). The triple bond consists of one \(\sigma\)-bond and two \(\pi\)-bonds.

High-Yield NEET Takeaway: s-Character and Acidity

Greater \(s\)-character implies that bonding electrons are held closer to the positively charged nucleus. Thus, carbon electronegativity increases in the sequence: \[ \mathrm{C(sp^3) < C(sp^2) < C(sp)} \] Consequently, hydrogen attached to an \(\mathrm{sp}\)-hybridised carbon is markedly acidic: \[ \mathrm{HC\equiv CH + NaNH_2 \rightarrow HC\equiv C^- Na^+ + NH_3} \] Alkynes with terminal \(\mathrm{-C\equiv CH}\) proton are acidic, while alkenes and alkanes are neutral.

Key Features of \(\pi\)-Bonds

  • A \(\pi\)-bond is formed by the sideways (lateral) overlap of parallel unhybridised \(\mathrm{p}\) orbitals perpendicular to the internuclear \(\sigma\)-axis.
  • Electron density in a \(\pi\)-bond lies in two lobes: one above and one below the plane of the participating nuclei.
  • Sideways overlap is less effective than head-on axial overlap; hence, a \(\pi\)-bond is fundamentally weaker than a \(\sigma\)-bond (Bond dissociation energy: \(\sigma \approx 348\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\), \(\pi \approx 264\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)).
  • Presence of a \(\pi\)-bond restricts free rotation around the carbon-carbon axis, providing the structural basis for geometrical isomerism (cis-trans) in alkenes.
  • The exposed electron cloud of \(\pi\)-bonds makes alkenes and alkynes electron-rich centres, acting as nucleophiles vulnerable to attack by electrophilic reagents.

  • \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) সংকরায়ন: একটি \(\mathrm{2s}\) ও দুটি \(\mathrm{2p}\) কক্ষকের মিশ্রণে তিনটি সমতলীয় ত্রিকোণাকার \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) সংকর কক্ষক \(120^\circ\) কোণে বিন্যস্ত থাকে। অসংকরায়িত \(\mathrm{p}\) কক্ষকটি সমতলের সাথে লম্বভাবে থাকে। উদাহরণ: ইথিন (\(\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2}\))। দ্বিবন্ধনে একটি \(\sigma\) এবং একটি \(\pi\) বন্ধন থাকে।
  • \(\mathrm{sp}\) সংকরায়ন: একটি \(\mathrm{2s}\) ও একটি \(\mathrm{2p}\) কক্ষক যুক্ত হয়ে দুটি রৈখিক \(\mathrm{sp}\) সংকর কক্ষক গঠন করে (\(180^\circ\) কোণ)। উদাহরণ: ইথাইন (\(\mathrm{HC\equiv CH}\))। ত্রিবন্ধনে একটি \(\sigma\) এবং দুটি \(\pi\) বন্ধন থাকে।

3. Structural Representations and Classification of Organic Compounds

Organic molecular structures are depicted in four conventional formats:

  1. Complete Structural Formula (Lewis Representation): Shows every single atom and all covalent bonds as explicit dashes (e.g., \(\mathrm{H-C-H}\) links). Lone pairs may or may not be shown.
  2. Condensed Structural Formula: Omits some or all individual covalent dashes and clusters identical groups with subscripts. Example: Ethane is \(\mathrm{CH_3CH_3}\); Hexane is \(\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_4CH_3}\).
  3. Bond-Line Structural Representation: Carbon and hydrogen atoms are not explicitly written. Carbon atoms are represented by line junctions (vertices) and line ends. Heteroatoms (O, N, S, halogens) and hydrogens attached to heteroatoms (e.g., \(\mathrm{-OH, -NH_2}\)) are explicitly shown.
  4. Three-Dimensional (3D) Representation (Wedge-and-Dash):
    • Solid line (\(-\)): Bond lying completely within the plane of the paper.
    • Solid Wedge (\(\blacktriangleright\)): Bond projecting out of the plane, towards the observer.
    • Dashed Wedge (\(\cdots\)): Bond projecting behind the plane, away from the observer.

Systematic Classification of Organic Compounds

Organic compounds are classified into two broad categories based on their carbon skeletons:

  • Acyclic or Open-Chain Compounds (Aliphatic): Straight or branched chain structures. Examples: Ethane, Isobutane, Acetaldehyde, Acetic acid.
  • Cyclic or Closed-Chain (Ring) Compounds:
    • Alicyclic Compounds: Rings containing carbon atoms that resemble aliphatic compounds in properties. Examples: Cyclopropane, Cyclobutane, Cyclohexene. Heterocyclic alicyclic rings include Oxirane (Tetrahydrofuran, Oxacyclopropane).
    • Aromatic Compounds: Special cyclic planar systems exhibiting aromatic stability obeying Hückel's Rule (\(4n+2\) \(\pi\)-electrons):
      • Benzenoid Aromatic: Contain at least one benzene ring. Examples: Benzene (\(\mathrm{C_6H_6}\)), Toluene (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5CH_3}\)), Naphthalene (\(\mathrm{C_{10}H_8}\)), Anthracene.
      • Non-benzenoid Aromatic: Aromatic systems lacking a benzene nucleus. Examples: Tropone, Tropolone, Azulene.
      • Heterocyclic Aromatic: Aromatic rings incorporating heteroatoms (N, O, S) in the ring framework. Examples: Furan (with O), Thiophene (with S), Pyrrole (with NH), Pyridine (with N).

Homologous Series and Functional Groups

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms joined in a specific manner that determines the characteristic chemical reactivity of the organic molecule (e.g., \(\mathrm{-OH, -CHO, -COOH, -NH_2}\)).

A homologous series is a family of organic compounds sharing the same functional group, governed by a common general molecular formula, where consecutive members differ in composition by a \(\mathrm{-CH_2-}\) methylene unit (molar mass difference of \(14\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)). Members display regular gradations in physical properties (boiling point, melting point, density) and nearly identical chemical reactions.

4. IUPAC Nomenclature of Organic Compounds

The International Union of Pure and Applied Chemistry (IUPAC) systematic name is built systematically from three core components:

\[ \text{Prefix(es)} + \text{Root Word} + \text{Primary Suffix} + \text{Secondary Suffix} \]
  • Root Word: Indicates the number of carbon atoms in the principal continuous chain (\(\mathrm{C_1}\): meth-, \(\mathrm{C_2}\): eth-, \(\mathrm{C_3}\): prop-, \(\mathrm{C_4}\): but-, \(\mathrm{C_5}\): pent-, \(\mathrm{C_6}\): hex-, \(\mathrm{C_7}\): hept-, \(\mathrm{C_8}\): oct-).
  • Primary Suffix: Denotes saturation/unsaturation: -ane (all single bonds), -ene (double bond), -yne (triple bond).
  • Secondary Suffix: Denotes the highest-priority principal functional group (e.g., -ol for alcohol, -al for aldehyde, -oic acid for carboxylic acid).
  • Prefix(es): Designate substituents, alkyl branches, halogens, nitro groups, or lower-priority functional groups treated as substituents.

Core Rules for Branched Alkanes

  1. Longest Continuous Carbon Chain: Identify the longest continuous chain of carbon atoms. If two chains have the same length, choose the one with the greater number of branching side chains.
  2. Lowest Locant Rule: Number the chain from the end that gives the lowest locant (numerical position) to the first point of difference among substituents. Compare locant sets digit-by-digit: \((2, 5, 6)\) is preferred over \((3, 4, 6)\) because \(2 < 3\) at the first locant.
  3. Prefix Multipliers: Use di-, tri-, tetra- when identical substituents recur (e.g., 2,2-dimethylpropane). These numerical multiplying prefixes are not alphabetised. However, prefixes like iso- and neo- are considered part of the alkyl group name and ARE alphabetised, whereas sec- and tert- are hyphenated and NOT alphabetised.
  4. Alphabetical Ordering: When two different substituents are at equivalent positions from opposite ends, assign the lower locant to the substituent that precedes alphabetically (e.g., 3-ethyl-5-methylheptane, NOT 5-ethyl-3-methylheptane).

5. Isomerism and Fundamental Reaction Mechanisms

Isomers are compounds possessing the identical molecular formula but differing in structural arrangement or three-dimensional spatial orientation of atoms.

A. Structural Isomerism

  • Chain Isomerism: Compounds with identical molecular formula but different carbon skeleton branching. Example: Pentane (\(\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_3CH_3}\)), Isopentane (\(\mathrm{CH_3CH(CH_3)CH_2CH_3}\)), Neopentane (\(\mathrm{C(CH_3)_4}\)).
  • Position Isomerism: Same carbon skeleton, but differing in the position of a functional group, substituent, or multiple bond. Example: Propan-1-ol and Propan-2-ol; But-1-ene and But-2-ene.
  • Functional Group Isomerism: Same molecular formula but having different functional groups, conferring entirely different chemical properties. Example: Ethanol (\(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2OH}\)) and Methoxymethane (\(\mathrm{CH_3-O-CH_3}\)), Propanal and Propanone.
  • Metamerism: Arises from unequal distribution of alkyl carbon chains on either side of a polyvalent functional group (\(\mathrm{-O-, -S-, -NH-, -CO-}\)). Example: Ethoxyethane (\(\mathrm{C_2H_5-O-C_2H_5}\)) and 1-Methoxypropane (\(\mathrm{CH_3-O-C_3H_7}\)).

B. Stereoisomerism

Isomers sharing identical molecular formula and connectivity, but differing in the spatial arrangement of atoms. Divided into Geometrical (cis/trans) isomerism (restricted rotation around \(\mathrm{C=C}\)) and Optical isomerism (non-superimposable mirror images exhibiting chirality).

Reactive Intermediates: Structure and Relative Stability

  • Carbocation (\(\mathrm{R_3C^+}\)): Carbon has 6 valence electrons (sextet), \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) hybridised, trigonal planar geometry with an empty unhybridised \(\mathrm{p}\) orbital. Stability governed by \(+I\) inductive donation and hyperconjugation: \[ \text{Tertiary } (3^\circ) > \text{Secondary } (2^\circ) > \text{Primary } (1^\circ) > \text{Methyl } (\mathrm{\overset{+}{C}H_3}) \] Allylic and benzylic carbocations are additionally stabilised by resonance delocalisation.
  • Free Radical (\(\mathrm{R_3C^\bullet}\)): Carbon has 7 valence electrons, planar \(\mathrm{sp^2}\) geometry with an odd electron in the unhybridised \(\mathrm{p}\) orbital. Stability order: \[ 3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{\dot{C}H_3} \]
  • Carbanion (\(\mathrm{R_3C^-}\)): Carbon has 8 valence electrons (octet with a lone pair), \(\mathrm{sp^3}\) hybridised, pyramidal geometry. Electron-donating groups destabilise it, so stability order is inverted: \[ \mathrm{\overset{-}{C}H_3} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ \]

Electrophiles and Nucleophiles

  • Electrophiles (\(\mathrm{E^+}\), "electron-loving"): Electron-deficient species that accept an electron pair. They can be positively charged (\(\mathrm{H^+, H_3O^+, NO_2^+, Cl^+, Br^+, R_3C^+}\)) or neutral Lewis acids having vacant valence orbitals (\(\mathrm{AlCl_3, BF_3, FeCl_3, SO_3}\)).
  • Nucleophiles (\(\mathrm{Nu^-}\), "nucleus-loving"): Electron-rich species possessing at least one unshared electron pair that donate electrons to an electrophile. They can be negatively charged (\(\mathrm{OH^-, RO^-, CN^-, Cl^-, Br^-, I^-, R^-}\)) or neutral molecules with lone pairs (\(\mathrm{H_2O, NH_3, RNH_2, ROH, ROR}\)).

  • কার্বোক্যাটায়নের সুস্থিতি ক্রম: \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{\overset{+}{C}H_3}\) (+I প্রভাব এবং হাইপারকনজুগেশনের কারণে)।
  • কার্বঅ্যানায়নের সুস্থিতি ক্রম: \(\mathrm{\overset{-}{C}H_3} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ\) (+I প্রভাব ঋণাত্মক আধান ঘনত্ব বৃদ্ধি করে অস্থিতিশীল করে)।
  • ইলেকট্রোফাইল ও নিউক্লিওফাইল: ইলেকট্রন ঘাটতিসম্পন্ন প্রজাতি (যেমন \(\mathrm{AlCl_3, NO_2^+}\)) হলো ইলেকট্রোফাইল; ইলেকট্রন সমৃদ্ধ নিঃসঙ্গ জোড়যুক্ত প্রজাতি (যেমন \(\mathrm{OH^-, NH_3}\)) হলো নিউক্লিওফাইল।

6. Electronic Displacement Effects in Covalent Bonds

The electronic distribution in a ground-state or reacting organic molecule is influenced by four distinct electronic displacement effects. While inductive, resonance, and hyperconjugation effects are permanent, the electromeric effect is purely temporary.

1. Inductive Effect (\(I\)-Effect)

The permanent polarisation of a \(\sigma\)-bond caused by the electronegativity difference between bonded atoms, transmitted along a carbon chain. Its intensity rapidly diminishes with distance and becomes virtually negligible after the third carbon atom (\(\mathrm{C_3}\)).

  • \(-I\) (Electron-Withdrawing): Groups with electronegativity higher than hydrogen pull electrons away: \[ \mathrm{-NO_2 > -CN > -COOH > -F > -Cl > -Br > -I > -OH > -OCH_3 > -C_6H_5 > -H} \]
  • \(+I\) (Electron-Releasing): Groups that push electron density toward carbon: \[ \mathrm{-(CH_3)_3C > -(CH_3)_2CH > -CH_2CH_3 > -CH_3 > -H} \]

Application to Acid Strength: \(-I\) groups stabilise the carboxylate anion by dispersing negative charge, increasing acidity (\(\mathrm{CF_3COOH > CCl_3COOH > CHCl_2COOH > CH_2ClCOOH > CH_3COOH}\)). Conversely, \(+I\) groups intensify the negative charge, decreasing acidity (\(\mathrm{HCOOH > CH_3COOH > CH_3CH_2COOH}\)).

2. Resonance Effect / Mesomeric Effect (\(R\) or \(M\)-Effect)

The permanent polarity produced in a conjugated system through interaction between two \(\pi\)-bonds or between a \(\pi\)-bond and a lone pair on an adjacent atom, transmitted across conjugated double bonds.

  • \(+R\) (Electron-Donating Resonance): Atoms with unshared lone pairs donate electrons into the conjugated \(\pi\)-system (ortho/para directing activating groups in benzene): \[ \mathrm{-O^- > -NH_2 > -NHR > -OH > -OR > -NHCOCH_3 > -halogen} \]
  • \(-R\) (Electron-Withdrawing Resonance): Groups that draw \(\pi\)-electrons away from the ring/conjugated chain into themselves (meta directing deactivating groups): \[ \mathrm{-NO_2 > -CN > -CHO > >C=O > -COOH > -COOR} \]

3. Electromeric Effect (\(E\)-Effect)

A temporary electron displacement occurring only in compounds with multiple bonds (\(\mathrm{C=C}\) or \(\mathrm{C=O}\)) at the immediate demand of an attacking reagent. The \(\pi\)-electron pair is completely transferred to one of the bonded atoms:

  • \(+E\) Effect: \(\pi\)-electrons are transferred to the atom to which the attacking electrophile becomes bonded (e.g., addition of \(\mathrm{H^+}\) to ethene).
  • \(-E\) Effect: \(\pi\)-electrons are transferred to the atom other than the one where the attacking nucleophile attaches (e.g., attack of cyanide \(\mathrm{CN^-}\) on carbonyl carbon \(\mathrm{C=O}\)).

4. Hyperconjugation (Baker-Nathan Effect / No-Bond Resonance)

A permanent electronic interaction involving delocalisation of \(\sigma\)-electrons of a \(\mathrm{C-H}\) bond of an alkyl group into an adjacent empty, half-filled, or \(\pi\)-orbital. It requires the presence of at least one hydrogen atom on the carbon directly attached to the unsaturated or carbocation centre (known as \(\alpha\)-hydrogen).

The number of hyperconjugative canonical structures equals the number of \(\alpha\)-hydrogens. Greater number of \(\alpha\)-hydrogens imparts higher stability to carbocations, radicals, and substituted alkenes:

\[ \text{Stability: } \mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+} \; (9\,\alpha\text{-H}) > \mathrm{(CH_3)_2\overset{+}{C}H} \; (6\,\alpha\text{-H}) > \mathrm{CH_3\overset{+}{C}H_2} \; (3\,\alpha\text{-H}) > \mathrm{\overset{+}{C}H_3} \; (0\,\alpha\text{-H}) \]

  • আবেশীয় প্রভাব (Inductive Effect, \(I\)): তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতার পার্থক্যের জন্য \(\sigma\)-বন্ধন বরাবর সৃষ্ট স্থায়ী পোলারায়ন। এটি দূরত্বের সাথে দ্রুত হ্রাস পায় এবং ৩য় কার্বনের পর প্রায় শূন্য হয়। \(-I\) গ্রুপ (যেমন \(\mathrm{-NO_2, -F, -Cl}\)) কার্বক্সিলিক অ্যাসিডের অম্লতা বৃদ্ধি করে; \(+I\) গ্রুপ অ্যালকাইল শৃঙ্খল অম্লতা হ্রাস করে।

  • ইলেকট্রোমেরিক প্রভাব (\(E\)): আক্রমণকারী বিকারকের উপস্থিতিতে দ্বিবন্ধন বা ত্রিবন্ধনের \(\pi\)-ইলেকট্রন জোড়ের সম্পূর্ণ স্থানান্তরজনিত ক্ষণস্থায়ী প্রভাব। বিকারক অপসারিত হলে অণু পুনরায় স্বাভাবিক অবস্থায় ফিরে আসে।
  • হাইপারকনজুগেশন (নো-বন্ড রেজোন্যান্স): সংলগ্ন কার্বোক্যাটায়নের শূন্য \(\mathrm{p}\)-কক্ষক বা দ্বিবন্ধনের \(\pi\)-কক্ষকের সাথে \(\alpha\)-কার্বনের \(\mathrm{C-H}\) বন্ধনের \(\sigma\)-ইলেকট্রনের স্থানান্তর। যত বেশি \(\alpha\)-হাইড্রোজেন থাকবে, অণুর সুস্থিতি তত বেশি হবে।

7. Methods of Purification of Organic Compounds

A pure organic substance is characterized by sharp melting and boiling points. Common laboratory separation and purification techniques depend on the physical state and specific differences in physical properties:

Method Underlying Principle Typical Applications / Examples
Sublimation Sublimable solid transitions directly from solid to vapour without melting, separating from non-sublimable impurities upon cooling. Camphor, Naphthalene, Benzoic acid, Anthracene, \(\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\).
Crystallisation Differences in solubilities of the compound and impurities in a suitably chosen solvent over a temperature range (soluble at boiling, insoluble cold). Impure benzoic acid purified from hot water; sugar and salt mixtures.
Simple Distillation Separation of volatile liquids from non-volatile impurities, or liquids boiling without decomposition having boiling point differences \(> 25\,\mathrm{K}\) (\(> 25^\circ\mathrm{C}\)). Chloroform (b.p. \(334\,\mathrm{K}\)) and Aniline (b.p. \(457\,\mathrm{K}\)); Ether (b.p. \(308\,\mathrm{K}\)) and Toluene (b.p. \(384\,\mathrm{K}\)).
Fractional Distillation Separation of miscible liquids with close boiling points (\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} < 25\,\mathrm{K}\)) using a fractionating column that provides multiple successive condensation-vaporisation cycles. Separation of crude petroleum fractions; acetone (b.p. \(329\,\mathrm{K}\)) and methyl alcohol (b.p. \(338\,\mathrm{K}\)).
Vacuum Distillation (Reduced Pressure) Distillation of heat-sensitive liquids that decompose at or below their normal boiling points by reducing external pressure (boils when vapour pressure equals reduced pressure). Glycerol (normally decomposes at \(290^\circ\mathrm{C}\); boils safely at \(180^\circ\mathrm{C}\) under \(12\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)); recovery of spent-lye in soap industry.
Steam Distillation Separation of substances that are steam-volatile and completely immiscible with water. The mixture boils when total pressure equals atmospheric: \(P_{\mathrm{total}} = P_{\mathrm{substance}} + P_{\mathrm{water}} = 1\,\mathrm{atm}\). Aniline-water mixture (aniline separated at \(98.5^\circ\mathrm{C}\), well below its pure b.p. \(184^\circ\mathrm{C}\)); nitrobenzene, essential oils.
Differential Extraction Liquid-liquid partition of an organic solute between an aqueous layer and an immiscible organic solvent (e.g., ether, \(\mathrm{CHCl_3}\)) in which the solute has higher solubility. Repeated extractions with smaller solvent volumes are far more efficient than a single extraction with the total volume. Isolation of organic compounds from aqueous reaction mixtures.

Chromatographic Techniques

Chromatography is based on the differential distribution of components between two phases: a stationary phase (solid or immobilised liquid) and a mobile phase (moving liquid or gas).

  • Adsorption Chromatography: Based on the differential adsorption of components on a solid adsorbent (e.g., activated alumina or silica gel).
    • Column Chromatography: The mixture is introduced at the top of a packed adsorbent column and eluted with suitable solvents (eluents). Weakly adsorbed components travel fastest and elute first.
    • Thin-Layer Chromatography (TLC): Adsorbent coated as a thin layer (\(\approx 0.2\,\mathrm{mm}\)) on a glass or aluminium plate. The relative migration is quantified by the Retardation factor (\(R_f\)): \[ R_f = \frac{\text{Distance moved by the substance from baseline}}{\text{Distance moved by the solvent front from baseline}} \] Since the solvent moves furthest, \(R_f\) is always a dimensionless quantity between \(0\) and \(1\).
  • Partition Chromatography: Separation based on continuous differential partitioning of components between stationary liquid and mobile liquid phases. Example: Paper chromatography (water trapped in the cellulose network acts as stationary phase).

৭. জৈব যৌগের বিশুদ্ধিকরণ পদ্ধতিসমূহ

  • ঊর্ধ্বপাতন (Sublimation): উদ্বায়ী কঠিন পদার্থকে অনূদ্বায়ী অপদ্রব্য থেকে সরাসরি বাষ্পে পরিণত করে পৃথকীকরণ (কর্পূর, বেনজোয়িক অ্যাসিড, ন্যাপথালিন)।
  • কেলাসন (Crystallisation): উপযুক্ত দ্রাবকে বিভিন্ন তাপমাত্রায় দ্রাব্যতার পার্থক্যের উপর ভিত্তি করে বিশোধন।
  • সাধারণ পাতন (Simple Distillation): স্ফুটনাঙ্কের পার্থক্য ২৫ ডিগ্রির বেশি হলে এবং বিয়োজিত না হয়ে বাষ্পীভূত হলে প্রযোজ্য (ক্লোরোফর্ম ও অ্যানিলিন)।
  • আংশিক পাতন (Fractional Distillation): স্ফুটনাঙ্কের পার্থক্য ২৫ ডিগ্রির কম হলে ফ্র্যাকশনেটিং কলাম ব্যবহার করে পৃথকীকরণ (পেট্রোলিয়াম শোধন)।
  • হ্রাসপ্রাপ্ত চাপে পাতন / ভ্যাকুয়াম পাতন: যেসব তরল স্বাভাবিক স্ফুটনাঙ্কে বিয়োজিত হয়, তাদের কম চাপে নিম্ন তাপমাত্রায় পাতিত করা (গ্লিসারল বিশোধন)।
  • বাষ্প পাতন (Steam Distillation): জলে অদ্রবণীয় কিন্তু বাষ্পে উদ্বায়ী যৌগের পৃথকীকরণ (অ্যানিলিন-জল মিশ্রণ)।
  • ক্রোমাটোগ্রাফি: স্থির দশা ও গতিশীল দশার মধ্যে উপাদানসমূহের আপেক্ষিক অধিশোষণ বা বণ্টনের পার্থক্যের ভিত্তিতে পৃথকীকরণ। \(R_f\) মান হলো বেসলাইন থেকে উপাদানের অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব ও দ্রাবক ফ্রন্টের দূরত্বের অনুপাত।

8. Qualitative Analysis of Organic Compounds

Qualitative analysis establishes the elemental composition of an unknown organic compound by converting covalently bound elements into water-soluble ionic forms.

1. Detection of Carbon and Hydrogen (Copper Oxide Test)

The organic sample is mixed with dry, pure cupric oxide (\(\mathrm{CuO}\)) in a ratio of approximately \(1:3\) and heated in a hard-glass ignition tube:

\[ \mathrm{C + 2CuO \xrightarrow{\Delta} CO_2\uparrow + 2Cu} \] \[ \mathrm{2H + CuO \xrightarrow{\Delta} H_2O\uparrow + Cu} \]
  • Hydrogen: Condensed water droplets turn white anhydrous copper sulphate (\(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\)) into crystalline blue hydrated copper sulphate: \[ \mathrm{CuSO_4\,(white) + 5H_2O \rightarrow CuSO_4\cdot 5H_2O\,(blue)} \]
  • Carbon: Evolved carbon dioxide gas turns clear limewater (\(\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2}\)) turbid due to insoluble calcium carbonate formation: \[ \mathrm{CO_2 + Ca(OH)_2 \rightarrow CaCO_3\downarrow\,(turbidity) + H_2O} \] (Excess \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) causes the milky turbidity to disappear due to soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate formation: \(\mathrm{CaCO_3 + CO_2 + H_2O \rightarrow Ca(HCO_3)_2}\)).

2. Detection of Other Elements: Lassaigne's Test (Sodium Fusion Test)

Covalently bonded nitrogen, sulphur, and halogens in organic molecules are non-ionised and do not respond to inorganic ionic precipitants. Heating the compound strongly with bright metallic sodium converts them into water-soluble inorganic sodium salts:

\[ \mathrm{Na + C + N \xrightarrow{\Delta} NaCN} \] \[ \mathrm{2Na + S \xrightarrow{\Delta} Na_2S} \] \[ \mathrm{Na + X \xrightarrow{\Delta} NaX \quad (X = Cl, Br, I)} \]

If both nitrogen and sulphur are simultaneously present, sodium thiocyanate is formed:

\[ \mathrm{Na + C + N + S \xrightarrow{\Delta} NaSCN} \]

The red-hot ignition tube is plunged into distilled water, crushed, boiled, and filtered. The resulting clear alkaline filtrate is the Sodium Fusion Extract (Lassaigne's Filtrate).

If nitrogen or sulphur is present, the sodium extract contains \(\mathrm{NaCN}\) or \(\mathrm{Na_2S}\). Adding \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) directly would precipitate white \(\mathrm{AgCN}\) or black \(\mathrm{Ag_2S}\), interfering with halogen identification. 3. Detection of Phosphorus

The organic substance is fused with an oxidising agent (sodium peroxide, \(\mathrm{Na_2O_2}\)). Phosphorus is converted into sodium phosphate:

\[ \mathrm{2P + 5Na_2O_2 \rightarrow 2Na_3PO_4 + 2Na_2O} \]

The fused mass is extracted with water, boiled with concentrated nitric acid (\(\mathrm{HNO_3}\)), and treated with ammonium molybdate solution \(\mathrm{(NH_4)_2MoO_4}\). A characteristic canary yellow precipitate of ammonium phosphomolybdate confirms phosphorus:

\[ \mathrm{Na_3PO_4 + 3HNO_3 \rightarrow H_3PO_4 + 3NaNO_3} \] \[ \mathrm{H_3PO_4 + 12(NH_4)_2MoO_4 + 21HNO_3 \rightarrow (NH_4)_3PO_4\cdot 12MoO_3\downarrow + 21NH_4NO_3 + 12H_2O} \]

৮. জৈব যৌগের গুণগত বিশ্লেষণ

  • কার্বন ও হাইড্রোজেন সনাক্তকরণ: যৌগটিকে উত্তপ্ত শুষ্ক \(\mathrm{CuO}\) সহ উত্তপ্ত করলে নির্গত গ্যাস বর্ণহীন চুনজলকে ঘোলা করে (কার্বনের উপস্থিতি: \(\mathrm{CO_2}\)) এবং সাদা অনার্দ্র \(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\)-কে নীল বর্ণে পরিণত করে (হাইড্রোজেনের উপস্থিতি: \(\mathrm{H_2O}\))।
  • ল্যাসাইন পরীক্ষা (সোডিয়াম নিষ্কাশন): সমযোজী N, S এবং হ্যালোজেনকে সোডিয়াম ধাতুর সাথে গলিয়ে জলে দ্রবণীয় আয়নে রূপান্তরিত করা হয় (\(\mathrm{NaCN, Na_2S, NaX}\))।

    • সালফার: সোডিয়াম নাইট্রোপ্রুসাইড যোগ করলে উজ্জ্বল বেগুনি বর্ণ বা লেড অ্যাসিটেটে কালো \(\mathrm{PbS}\) অধঃক্ষেপ পড়ে।
    • নাইট্রোজেন ও সালফার উভয় উপস্থিত থাকলে: রক্তবর্ণ \(\mathrm{[Fe(SCN)_3]}\) জটিল যৌগ তৈরি হয়।
    • হ্যালোজেন: ল্যাসাইন দ্রবণকে প্রথমে গাঢ় \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) সহ ফুটিয়ে নেওয়া বাধ্যতামূলক (যাতে সায়ানাইড বা সালফাইড গ্যাস হিসেবে অপসারিত হয়), তারপর \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) যোগ করলে \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) (সাদা), \(\mathrm{AgBr}\) (হালকা হলুদ) ও \(\mathrm{AgI}\) (গাঢ় হলুদ) অধঃক্ষেপ পাওয়া যায়।
  • ফসফরাস সনাক্তকরণ: সোডিয়াম পারক্সাইড দ্বারা জারিত করে অ্যামোনিয়াম মলিবডেট সহযোগে উত্তপ্ত করলে ক্যানারি হলুদ বর্ণের অ্যামোনিয়াম ফসফোমলিবডেট অধঃক্ষিপ্ত হয়।

9. Quantitative Elemental Analysis: Principles and Formulae

Quantitative analysis determines the exact percentage composition by mass of each constituent element present in a pure organic sample.

1. Carbon and Hydrogen (Liebig Combustion Method)

A known mass (\(m\,\mathrm{g}\)) of the organic compound is completely burned in a combustion tube in a stream of pure, dry oxygen gas over heated copper(II) oxide:

\[ \mathrm{C_xH_y + \left(x + \frac{y}{4}\right)O_2 \rightarrow x\,CO_2 + \frac{y}{2}\,H_2O} \]
  • Produced water is absorbed in a weighed U-tube containing anhydrous calcium chloride (\(\mathrm{CaCl_2}\)). Increase in mass \(= m_{\mathrm{H_2O}}\,\mathrm{g}\).
  • Carbon dioxide is absorbed in a weighed U-tube containing concentrated potassium hydroxide (\(\mathrm{KOH}\)) solution. Increase in mass \(= m_{\mathrm{CO_2}}\,\mathrm{g}\).
\[ \%\,\mathrm{C} = \frac{12}{44} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}{m} \times 100 \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{H} = \frac{2.016}{18.016} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{H_2O}}}{m} \times 100 \approx \frac{2}{18} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{H_2O}}}{m} \times 100 \]

2. Estimation of Nitrogen

A. Dumas Method

The organic sample (\(m\,\mathrm{g}\)) is heated with copper(II) oxide in an atmosphere of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\). Nitrogen is liberated as dry dinitrogen gas (\(\mathrm{N_2}\)); any nitrogen oxides formed are reduced back to \(\mathrm{N_2}\) by passing over a heated copper spiral. The gases are collected over concentrated aqueous potassium hydroxide (\(\mathrm{KOH}\)), which absorbs \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), leaving pure \(\mathrm{N_2}\) gas.

If \(V\,\mathrm{mL}\) of moist nitrogen is collected at temperature \(T\,\mathrm{K}\) and total barometric pressure \(P\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\), the pressure of dry nitrogen is corrected for aqueous tension (\(p\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)):

\[ P_{\mathrm{dry}} = P - p \]

Convert the volume of dry \(\mathrm{N_2}\) to Standard Temperature and Pressure (STP: \(P_0 = 760\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}, T_0 = 273.15\,\mathrm{K}\)):

\[ V_0 = \frac{(P - p) \times V \times 273.15}{760 \times T} \]

Since \(22400\,\mathrm{mL}\) of dry \(\mathrm{N_2}\) at STP has a mass of \(28\,\mathrm{g}\):

\[ \%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{28}{22400} \times \frac{V_0}{m} \times 100 \]

B. Kjeldahl Method

The organic compound (\(m\,\mathrm{g}\)) is digested with concentrated \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) in the presence of a catalyst (\(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\) or \(\mathrm{Hg}\)) and \(\mathrm{K_2SO_4}\) (which elevates the boiling point of \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)). Nitrogen is quantitatively converted into ammonium sulphate:

\[ \text{Organic Nitrogen} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_2SO_4}} \mathrm{(NH_4)_2SO_4} \]

The digested mixture is boiled with excess concentrated \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) solution to release ammonia gas:

\[ \mathrm{(NH_4)_2SO_4 + 2NaOH \rightarrow Na_2SO_4 + 2NH_3\uparrow + 2H_2O} \]

The liberated \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) is absorbed into a known volume (\(V\,\mathrm{mL}\)) of standard sulphuric acid of molarity \(M\) (or normality \(N\)). The unreacted residual acid is back-titrated against standard \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) solution. Let the volume of standard acid neutralised by \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) be \(V_{\mathrm{acid}}\,\mathrm{mL}\) of normality \(N\):

\[ \%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times N \times V_{\mathrm{acid}}}{m} \]

If acid molarity \(M\) is given for dibasic \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\), recall that Normality \(N = 2 \times M\):

\[ \%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times (2 \times M) \times V_{\mathrm{acid}}}{m} = \frac{2.8 \times M \times V_{\mathrm{acid}}}{m} \]

NEET Must-Know: Scope and Failures of Kjeldahl Method

The Kjeldahl method is NOT applicable to compounds containing:

  1. Nitro groups (\(\mathrm{-NO_2}\)) (e.g., Nitrobenzene).
  2. Azo groups (\(\mathrm{-N=N-}\)) (e.g., Azobenzene).
  3. Nitrogen in heterocyclic ring systems (e.g., Pyridine, Quinoline).

Reason: Under standard digestion conditions, the nitrogen present in these structural environments does not convert quantitatively into ammonium sulphate.

3. Estimation of Halogens (Carius Method)

A known mass (\(m\,\mathrm{g}\)) of organic compound is heated with fuming nitric acid (\(\mathrm{HNO_3}\)) in the presence of silver nitrate (\(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\)) in a sealed hard-glass Carius tube. Carbon and hydrogen are oxidised to \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) and \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), while halogen forms an insoluble silver halide precipitate (\(\mathrm{AgX}\)), which is filtered, washed, dried, and weighed (\(m_{\mathrm{AgX}}\,\mathrm{g}\)):

\[ \%\,\mathrm{Cl} = \frac{35.5}{143.5} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{AgCl}}}{m} \times 100 \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{AgBr}}}{m} \times 100 \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{I} = \frac{127}{235} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{AgI}}}{m} \times 100 \]

4. Estimation of Sulphur (Carius Method)

The organic sample (\(m\,\mathrm{g}\)) is heated with fuming nitric acid or sodium peroxide in a Carius tube. Sulphur is oxidised to sulphuric acid, which is precipitated as barium sulphate (\(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\)) by adding excess barium chloride (\(\mathrm{BaCl_2}\)) solution. The precipitate is filtered, ignited, and weighed (\(m_{\mathrm{BaSO_4}}\,\mathrm{g}\)):

\[ \%\,\mathrm{S} = \frac{32}{233} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{BaSO_4}}}{m} \times 100 \]

5. Estimation of Phosphorus

The sample is oxidised with fuming \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) to phosphoric acid. Phosphorus can be weighed either as:

  • Ammonium phosphomolybdate \(\mathrm{(NH_4)_3PO_4\cdot 12MoO_3}\) (molar mass \(= 1877\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)): \[ \%\,\mathrm{P} = \frac{31}{1877} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{precipitate}}}{m} \times 100 \]
  • Or precipitated with magnesia mixture (\(\mathrm{MgCl_2 + NH_4Cl + NH_4OH}\)) as \(\mathrm{MgNH_4PO_4}\), which upon ignition yields magnesium pyrophosphate (\(\mathrm{Mg_2P_2O_7}\), molar mass \(= 222.6\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)): \[ \%\,\mathrm{P} = \frac{62}{222} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{Mg_2P_2O_7}}}{m} \times 100 \]

6. Estimation of Oxygen

Usually calculated by difference: \(\%\,\mathrm{O} = 100 - (\%\,\mathrm{C} + \%\,\mathrm{H} + \%\,\mathrm{N} + \dots)\).

Direct determination: A known mass is decomposed in a stream of pure \(\mathrm{N_2}\) over heated carbon at \(1373\,\mathrm{K}\) (\(1100^\circ\mathrm{C}\)) to form \(\mathrm{CO}\), which is oxidised by iodine pentoxide (\(\mathrm{I_2O_5}\)) to \(\mathrm{CO_2}\):

\[ \mathrm{Compound \xrightarrow{\Delta} O_2 \dots \xrightarrow{C\,(1373\,K)} 2CO} \] \[ \mathrm{5CO + I_2O_5 \rightarrow 5CO_2 + I_2} \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{O} = \frac{32}{88} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{CO_2}}}{m} \times 100 \]

    • কজেলডাল পদ্ধতি: নমুনাকে গাঢ় \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) দিয়ে ফুটিয়ে নাইট্রোজেনকে অ্যামোনিয়াম সালফেটে রূপান্তরিত করা হয়। ক্ষার দ্বারা অ্যামোনিয়া মুক্ত করে প্রমাণ অ্যাসিডে শোষণ করা হয়: \(\%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times N \times V}{m}\)। সতর্কতা: নাইট্রো (\(\mathrm{-NO_2}\)), অ্যাজো (\(\mathrm{-N=N-}\)) এবং বলয়স্থ নাইট্রোজেনযুক্ত যৌগ (পিরিডিন) কজেলডাল পদ্ধতিতে সাড়া দেয় না।
  • ক্যারিয়াস পদ্ধতিতে হ্যালোজেন ও সালফার: \[ \%\,\mathrm{Cl} = \frac{35.5}{143.5} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{AgCl}}}{m} \times 100 \quad ; \quad \%\,\mathrm{Br} = \frac{80}{188} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{AgBr}}}{m} \times 100 \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{S} = \frac{32}{233} \times \frac{m_{\mathrm{BaSO_4}}}{m} \times 100 \]

10. Worked Illustrative Examples and NEET UG Practice Questions

Worked Example 1: Determination of Hybridisation and \(\sigma\)/\(\pi\) Bonds

Problem: For the compound \(\mathrm{CH_2=CH-CH_2-C\equiv CH}\), determine:

  1. The hybridisation state of each carbon atom numbered from right to left.
  2. The total number of \(\sigma\) and \(\pi\) bonds present in the molecule.

In a Kjeldahl estimation of nitrogen, \(0.50\,\mathrm{g}\) of an organic compound was digested and the evolved ammonia was absorbed in \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) sulphuric acid (\(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)). The residual unreacted acid required \(30.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) sodium hydroxide (\(\mathrm{NaOH}\)) solution for complete neutralisation. The percentage of nitrogen in the compound is:

(A) \(19.6\%\)
(B) \(14.0\%\)
(C) \(28.0\%\)
(D) \(35.0\%\)

কজেলডাল পদ্ধতিতে নাইট্রোজেন নির্ণয়ে \(0.50\,\mathrm{g}\) জৈব যৌগ থেকে উৎপন্ন অ্যামোনিয়াকে \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) সালফিউরিক অ্যাসিডে (\(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)) শোষণ করা হলো। অবশিষ্ট অ্যাসিডকে সম্পূর্ণ প্রশমিত করতে \(30.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) সোডিয়াম হাইড্রোক্সাইড (\(\mathrm{NaOH}\)) দ্রবণের প্রয়োজন হয়। যৌগটিতে নাইট্রোজেনের শতকরা পরিমাণ কত?

(A) \(19.6\%\)
(B) \(14.0\%\)
(C) \(28.0\%\)
(D) \(35.0\%\)
Correct Option: (A)

Step-by-step Calculation:

  1. Express quantities in milliequivalents (meq):
    • Normality of \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) dibasic \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\): \[ N = 2 \times \text{Molarity} = 2 \times 0.10 = 0.20\,\mathrm{N} \]

  3. Apply Kjeldahl formula for percentage nitrogen: \[ \%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times \text{meq of acid consumed}}{m} \] \[ \%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times 7.0}{0.50} = \frac{9.8}{0.50} = 19.6\% \]

Thus, the nitrogen percentage is \(19.6\%\).

Practice Question 11 (Dumas Method with Aqueous Tension) NEET Calculation

In Dumas method for estimation of nitrogen, \(0.30\,\mathrm{g}\) of an organic compound gave \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) of moist nitrogen collected at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) and \(715\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) barometric pressure. If the aqueous tension of water at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) is \(15\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\), the volume of dry nitrogen at STP (\(760\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}, 273\,\mathrm{K}\)) is approximately:

(A) \(41.9\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(B) \(46.0\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(C) \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(D) \(38.5\,\mathrm{mL}\)
বাংলা প্রশ্ন

ডুমা পদ্ধতিতে নাইট্রোজেন নির্ণয়ে \(0.30\,\mathrm{g}\) একটি জৈব যৌগ থেকে \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় ও \(715\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপে \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\) সিক্ত নাইট্রোজেন গ্যাস সংগৃহীত হলো। \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় জলের বাষ্পচাপ (জলীয় টান) \(15\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) হলে, STP-তে শুষ্ক নাইট্রোজেনের আয়তন কত হবে?

(A) \(41.9\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(B) \(46.0\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(C) \(50.0\,\mathrm{mL}\)
(D) \(38.5\,\mathrm{mL}\)
Correct Option: (A)

Step-by-step Calculation:

  1. Find the pressure of dry nitrogen: \[ P_{\mathrm{dry}} = P_{\mathrm{observed}} - p_{\mathrm{aqueous\,tension}} = 715 - 15 = 700\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg} \]
  2. Convert to volume at STP (\(P_0 = 760\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}, T_0 = 273\,\mathrm{K}\)): \[ V_0 = \frac{P_{\mathrm{dry}} \times V_1 \times T_0}{P_0 \times T_1} = \frac{700 \times 50.0 \times 273}{760 \times 300} \] \[ V_0 = \frac{9,555,000}{228,000} \approx 41.9\,\mathrm{mL} \]

The STP volume of dry nitrogen is approximately \(41.9\,\mathrm{mL}\).

11. Common Pitfalls and High-Yield Revision Checklist

Frequent Student Errors in NEET Chemistry

  • Alphabetisation of Prefixes: Confusing multiplier prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-) with structural prefixes (iso-, neo-). Multiplying prefixes are NEVER alphabetised when ordering substituents in IUPAC naming; however, iso- and neo- are single words and ARE alphabetised. Hyphenated prefixes like sec- and tert- are NOT alphabetised.
  • Double Bond vs. Triple Bond Priority: When an alkene and an alkyne are equidistant from chain ends, the double bond gets the lower locant (e.g., pent-1-en-4-yne, NOT pent-4-en-1-yne). However, if one functional group is closer to an end than the other, the lowest locant rule takes precedence regardless of whether it is a double or triple bond.
  • Aqueous Tension in Dumas Method: Forgetting to subtract the aqueous tension of water from the barometric pressure before applying the ideal gas equation. The collected volume contains moist gas, so \(P_{\mathrm{dry}} = P_{\mathrm{total}} - p_{\mathrm{aqueous}}\).
  • Basicity of Sulphuric Acid in Kjeldahl Calculations: Sulphuric acid is dibasic (\(\mathrm{H_2SO_4 \rightarrow 2H^+}\)). If molarity is given instead of normality, you MUST multiply molarity by 2 to get normality (\(N = 2 \times M\)) before substituting into \(\%\,\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times N \times V}{m}\).
  • Carbanion vs. Carbocation Stability Inversion: Due to the negative charge on a carbanion, electron-donating alkyl groups (\(+I\)) intensify electron density and destabilise it. The stability order of simple alkyl carbanions is the EXACT REVERSE of carbocations: \(\mathrm{\overset{-}{C}H_3} > 1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ\).
