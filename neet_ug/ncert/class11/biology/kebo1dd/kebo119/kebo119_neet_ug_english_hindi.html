1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview / अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं अध्याय अवलोकन

English

While the neural system provides rapid, point-to-point electrical signaling across specific nerve fibers, it cannot innervate every cell of the body, and its effects are short-lived. Long-term, continuous, and widespread regulation of cellular metabolism, growth, homeostasis, and reproduction requires a chemical messaging network: the endocrine system. Together, the neural and endocrine systems form an integrated neuro-endocrine control axis.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between exocrine and endocrine glands and state the modern scientific definition of hormones.
  • Classify hormones by chemical nature and link each class to its receptor location and signaling mechanism.
  • Map the anatomical positions, secretions, target tissues, and physiological actions of all major human endocrine glands and hormone-secreting non-endocrine organs.
  • Analyze endocrine disorders caused by hyposecretion or hypersecretion (e.g., acromegaly, diabetes insipidus, goitre, Graves' disease, Addison's disease, diabetes mellitus).
  • Apply antagonistic hormone-pair logic (such as insulin–glucagon and PTH–calcitonin) to solve NEET UG conceptual and clinical questions.

  • रासायनिक प्रकृति के आधार पर हॉर्मोन का वर्गीकरण करना और प्रत्येक वर्ग को उसके ग्राही (receptor) स्थान व क्रियाविधि से जोड़ना।
  • मानव की सभी प्रमुख अंतःस्रावी ग्रंथियों तथा हॉर्मोन-स्रावित करने वाले अन्य अंगों की स्थिति, स्राव, लक्ष्य ऊतक और कार्यों का मानचित्रण करना।

  • NEET UG के वैचारिक और नैदानिक प्रश्नों को हल करने के लिए विरोधी (antagonistic) हॉर्मोन युग्मों (जैसे इंसुलिन–ग्लूकागॉन तथा PTH–कैल्सिटोनिन) के सिद्धांत का उपयोग करना।

2. Principles of Chemical Coordination & Hormone Classification / रासायनिक समन्वय के सिद्धांत एवं हॉर्मोन वर्गीकरण

English

Ductless Glands and the Modern Definition of Hormones

Endocrine glands lack ducts and release their secretions directly into the surrounding extracellular fluid and bloodstream; hence they are called ductless glands. (By contrast, exocrine glands release secretions through ducts onto epithelial surfaces.) Earlier, hormones were defined strictly as chemicals produced by organized endocrine glands and transported via blood to distant target organs. The current scientific definition is broader:

Modern Definition: Hormones are non-nutrient chemicals that act as intercellular messengers and are produced in trace amounts.

This definition encompasses secretions from both organized endocrine glands (pituitary, pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, adrenal, pancreas, gonads) and diffused endocrine cells located in organs such as the heart, kidney, liver, and gastrointestinal tract. Invertebrates possess relatively simple endocrine systems with few hormones, whereas vertebrates utilize a large and diverse array of chemical messengers.

3. The Hypothalamo-Pituitary Control Axis / हाइपोथैलेमो-पीयूष नियंत्रण अक्ष

English

Hypothalamus: The Neuro-Endocrine Bridge

Located at the basal part of the diencephalon (forebrain), the hypothalamus contains groups of neurosecretory cells called nuclei that synthesize two functional categories of regulatory hormones:

  • Releasing hormones: Stimulate the secretion of anterior pituitary hormones (e.g., Gonadotrophin-releasing hormone, GnRH, stimulates gonadotrophin synthesis and release).
  • Inhibiting hormones: Suppress the secretion of anterior pituitary hormones (e.g., somatostatin inhibits Growth Hormone release).

Hypothalamic hormones travel down axons to nerve endings. Those regulating the anterior pituitary enter a specialized hypophyseal portal circulatory system, whereas the posterior pituitary is under direct neural regulation of hypothalamic axons.

Tutorial Diagram 1: Portal vs. Direct Neural Regulation of the Pituitary Gland

Pituitary Gland (Sella Tursica)

The pituitary sits in a bony cavity called the sella tursica and connects to the hypothalamus via a stalk. Anatomically it comprises the adenohypophysis (pars distalis + pars intermedia) and the neurohypophysis (pars nervosa).

  • Pars distalis (Anterior Pituitary) — 6 Hormones:
    • Growth Hormone (GH): Stimulates somatic growth. Hyposecretion in childhood causes pituitary dwarfism; hypersecretion in childhood causes gigantism; hypersecretion in middle-aged adults causes acromegaly (severe facial/bone disfigurement, often undiagnosed early, leading to serious complications and premature death if unchecked).
    • Prolactin (PRL): Regulates mammary gland growth and milk formation.
    • Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH): Stimulates synthesis and secretion of thyroid hormones.
    • Adrenocorticotropic Hormone (ACTH): Stimulates synthesis and secretion of glucocorticoids from the adrenal cortex.
    • Luteinizing Hormone (LH) & Follicle Stimulating Hormone (FSH) [Gonadotrophins]: In males, LH stimulates Leydig cells to secrete androgens, while FSH and androgens regulate spermatogenesis. In females, FSH stimulates ovarian follicle growth, and LH induces ovulation of mature Graafian follicles and maintains the corpus luteum.
  • Pars intermedia — 1 Hormone: Secretes Melanocyte Stimulating Hormone (MSH), which acts on melanocytes to regulate skin pigmentation. In humans, pars intermedia is almost merged with pars distalis.
  • Neurohypophysis (Posterior Pituitary) — 2 Stored/Released Hormones: Synthesized in the hypothalamus and transported axonally:
    • Oxytocin: Acts on smooth muscles; stimulates vigorous uterine contractions during childbirth and milk ejection from mammary glands.
    • Vasopressin / Anti-Diuretic Hormone (ADH): Acts on kidney distal tubules to stimulate water and electrolyte resorption, reducing urine water loss (diuresis). Deficiency/impairment causes Diabetes Insipidus (diminished water conservation, dehydration).

पीयूष ग्रंथि (सेला टर्सिका)

पीयूष ग्रंथि सेला टर्सिका (sella tursica) नामक अस्थिल गुहा में स्थित होती है और एक वृंत द्वारा हाइपोथैलेमस से जुड़ी रहती है। शारीरिक रूप से इसके दो भाग हैं: एडिनोहाइपोफाइसिस (पार्स डिस्टैलिस + पार्स इंटरमीडिया) तथा न्यूरोहाइपोफाइसिस (पार्स नर्वोसा)।

  • पार्स डिस्टैलिस (अग्र पीयूष) — 6 हॉर्मोन:
    • वृद्धि हॉर्मोन (GH): कायिक वृद्धि को प्रेरित करता है। बचपन में अल्पस्राव से पीयूष वामनता (dwarfism), अतिस्राव से भीमकायता (gigantism) तथा वयस्कों (विशेषकर अधेड़ उम्र) में अतिस्राव से अग्रतिकायता (acromegaly) होती है जिसमें चेहरे व अस्थियों में गंभीर विरूपता आती है और उपचार न होने पर अकाल मृत्यु हो सकती है।
    • प्रोलैक्टिन (PRL): स्तन ग्रंथियों की वृद्धि तथा उनमें दुग्ध निर्माण का नियमन करता है।
    • थायरॉइड प्रेरक हॉर्मोन (TSH): थायरॉइड ग्रंथि से थायरॉइड हॉर्मोन के संश्लेषण और स्राव को प्रेरित करता है।
    • एड्रिनोकॉर्टिकोट्रॉफिक हॉर्मोन (ACTH): अधिवृक्क वल्कुट (adrenal cortex) से ग्लूकोकॉर्टिकॉइड्स के संश्लेषण व स्राव को प्रेरित करता है।
    • ल्यूटिनाइजिंग हॉर्मोन (LH) व पुटिका प्रेरक हॉर्मोन (FSH) [गोनैडोट्रॉफिन्स]: नर में LH लेडिग कोशिकाओं से एंड्रोजन स्राव प्रेरित करता है तथा FSH व एंड्रोजन शुक्रजनन (spermatogenesis) का नियमन करते हैं। मादा में FSH अंडाशयी पुटिकाओं की वृद्धि कराता है, और LH पूर्ण परिपक्व ग्राफी पुटिका से अंडोत्सर्ग (ovulation) कराकर कॉर्पस ल्यूटियम को बनाए रखता है।
  • पार्स इंटरमीडिया — 1 हॉर्मोन: मेलेनोसाइट प्रेरक हॉर्मोन (MSH) स्रावित करता है जो त्वचा के वर्णक (melanocytes) का नियमन करता है। मानव में यह भाग पार्स डिस्टैलिस से लगभग जुड़ा होता है।
  • न्यूरोहाइपोफाइसिस (पश्च पीयूष) — 2 संग्रहित/स्रावित हॉर्मोन: ये वास्तव में हाइपोथैलेमस में संश्लेषित होकर तंत्रिकाक्षों द्वारा यहाँ आते हैं:
    • ऑक्सीटोसिन: चिकनी पेशियों पर कार्य कर प्रसव के समय गर्भाशय के तीव्र संकुचन तथा स्तन ग्रंथियों से दुग्ध निष्कासन को प्रेरित करता है।
    • वैसोप्रेसिन / प्रतिमूत्रल हॉर्मोन (ADH): वृक्क की दूरस्थ नलिकाओं में जल एवं विद्युत-अपघट्यों के पुनःअवशोषण को प्रेरित कर मूत्र में जल की हानि (मूत्रलता/diuresis) कम करता है। इसकी कमी से डायबिटीज़ इनसिपिडस (उदकमेह) होता है, जिसमें जल संरक्षण घटने से निर्जलीकरण हो जाता है।

4. Metabolic, Calcium & Circadian Regulators / उपापचयी, कैल्शियम एवं दैनिक लय नियामक

English

Pineal Gland

Located on the dorsal side of the forebrain, the pineal gland secretes melatonin, which regulates the 24-hour (diurnal) rhythm of the body (sleep-wake cycle, body temperature). Melatonin also influences metabolism, pigmentation, the menstrual cycle, and immune defense capability.

Thyroid Gland

Composed of two lobes on either side of the trachea joined by a connective-tissue flap called the isthmus, the thyroid consists of follicles (follicular cells enclosing a cavity) and stromal tissues. Follicular cells synthesize tetraiodothyronine / thyroxine (T₄) and triiodothyronine (T₃), for which dietary iodine is essential.

  • Functions of T₃ & T₄: Regulate basal metabolic rate (BMR), support red blood cell (RBC) formation, control carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism, maintain water-electrolyte balance, and support CNS development and menstrual regularity.
  • Hypothyroidism: Iodine deficiency causes thyroid enlargement (goitre). During pregnancy, it causes defective fetal maturation leading to cretinism (stunted growth, mental retardation, low IQ, abnormal skin, deaf-mutism). In adult women, it causes irregular menstrual cycles.
  • Hyperthyroidism: Thyroid cancer or nodules cause excess hormone secretion. Exophthalmic goitre (Graves' disease) is characterized by thyroid enlargement, protrusion of eyeballs, increased BMR, and weight loss.
  • Thyrocalcitonin (TCT): A protein hormone secreted by the thyroid that lowers blood \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) levels.

Parathyroid Gland

Four parathyroid glands sit on the dorsal (back) side of the thyroid (one pair in each lobe). They secrete the peptide hormone parathyroid hormone (PTH), whose release is regulated by circulating \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) levels. PTH is a hypercalcemic hormone that raises blood \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) by: (1) stimulating bone resorption (demineralization), (2) enhancing \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) reabsorption by renal tubules, and (3) increasing \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\) absorption from digested food. PTH and TCT act antagonistically to maintain calcium homeostasis.

  • \(\alpha\)-cells → Glucagon (peptide): A hyperglycemic hormone acting mainly on hepatocytes to stimulate glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, while reducing cellular glucose uptake.
  • \(\beta\)-cells → Insulin (peptide): A hypoglycemic hormone acting on hepatocytes and adipocytes to enhance cellular glucose uptake/utilization and stimulate glycogenesis.

Prolonged hyperglycemia leads to diabetes mellitus, characterized by loss of glucose in urine (glycosuria) and formation of harmful ketone bodies; it is treated with insulin therapy.

5. Stress, Immunity & Reproductive Regulators / तनाव, प्रतिरक्षा एवं जनन नियामक

English

Adrenal Gland (Paired, Superior to Kidneys)

Each adrenal gland has a central adrenal medulla and an outer adrenal cortex:

  • Adrenal Medulla: Secretes adrenaline (epinephrine) and noradrenaline (norepinephrine), collectively called catecholamines or emergency / Fight-or-Flight hormones. They increase alertness, pupillary dilation, piloerection, sweating, heart rate, strength of cardiac contraction, respiratory rate, and breakdown of glycogen (raising blood glucose), lipids, and proteins.
  • Adrenal Cortex (3 Zones, from outer to inner):
    1. Zona glomerulosa (outer): Secretes mineralocorticoids (mainly aldosterone), which act on renal tubules to stimulate \(\mathrm{Na}^+\) and water reabsorption and \(\mathrm{K}^+\) and phosphate excretion, maintaining electrolytes, body fluid volume, osmotic pressure, and blood pressure.
    2. Zona fasciculata (middle): Secretes glucocorticoids (mainly cortisol), which stimulate gluconeogenesis, lipolysis, proteolysis, and RBC production; inhibit amino acid uptake; maintain cardiovascular and kidney functions; and produce anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.
    3. Zona reticularis (inner): Secretes small amounts of androgenic steroids involved in axial, pubic, and facial hair growth during puberty.
  • Disorder: Underproduction of adrenal cortical hormones alters carbohydrate metabolism causing acute weakness and fatigue, known as Addison's disease.

Thymus

A lobular gland located between the lungs behind the sternum on the ventral side of the aorta. It secretes peptide hormones called thymosins, which drive the differentiation of T-lymphocytes (providing cell-mediated immunity) and promote antibody production (providing humoral immunity). The thymus degenerates in elderly individuals, lowering thymosin levels and weakening immune responses.

Gonads: Testis and Ovary

  • Testis (Male, in scrotal sac): Composed of seminiferous tubules and stromal/interstitial tissue. Leydig (interstitial) cells produce androgens (mainly testosterone), which regulate accessory sex organs (epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicles, prostate, urethra), stimulate spermatogenesis, induce secondary sexual traits (muscular growth, facial/axillary hair, low-pitched voice, aggressiveness), influence libido, and promote protein/carbohydrate anabolism.
  • Ovary (Female, in abdomen): Produces one ovum per menstrual cycle and two groups of steroid hormones:
    • Estrogen: Synthesized mainly by growing ovarian follicles; stimulates female secondary sex organs, follicle development, secondary sexual traits (high-pitched voice), mammary gland development, and female sexual behavior.
    • Progesterone: Secreted mainly by the corpus luteum (formed from the ruptured follicle post-ovulation); supports pregnancy and acts on mammary glands to stimulate alveoli formation (milk-storing sacs) and milk secretion.

7. Molecular Mechanisms of Hormone Action / हॉर्मोन क्रिया की आणविक क्रियाविधि

English

Hormones act only on target tissues possessing specific protein hormone receptors. Binding forms a hormone-receptor complex, triggering biochemical changes. Two distinct pathways operate depending on whether the hormone can cross the lipid bilayer:

  • Membrane-Bound Receptor Pathway (Water-soluble hormones: Peptides/Proteins & Epinephrine): These hormones (e.g., FSH, insulin) do not enter the target cell. Binding to membrane receptors generates intracellular second messengers such as cyclic AMP (cAMP), \(\mathrm{IP}_3\), or \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\), which cascade into biochemical and physiological responses (e.g., ovarian growth).
  • Intracellular / Nuclear Receptor Pathway (Lipid-soluble hormones: Steroids & Iodothyronines): These hormones (e.g., estrogen, cortisol, T₃/T₄) cross the plasma membrane and bind to intracellular (mostly nuclear) receptors. The hormone-receptor complex interacts directly with the genome, regulating gene expression (mRNA → proteins), resulting in tissue growth and differentiation.

Worked Example 1: Differential Diagnosis of Excessive Water Loss

Hypothetical Scenario: Two adult patients (Patient A and Patient B) present with excessive urination and dehydration. Lab tests (assumed values) show Patient A has normal fasting blood glucose (\(90\,\mathrm{mg/dL}\)) and no urinary glucose or ketone bodies, whereas Patient B has fasting blood glucose of \(210\,\mathrm{mg/dL}\) with glucose and ketone bodies in urine. Identify the hormonal impairment in each patient.

Solution:

  • Patient A: Normal blood glucose rules out diabetes mellitus. Excess water loss and dehydration point to impaired synthesis or release of vasopressin (ADH) from the hypothalamo-neurohypophyseal system, reducing water resorption in the kidney's distal tubules. This condition is Diabetes Insipidus.
  • Patient B: Prolonged hyperglycemia with glycosuria and ketone bodies indicates Diabetes Mellitus, caused by deficiency or resistance to insulin (secreted by pancreatic \(\beta\)-cells).

Worked Example 2: Tracing Receptor Pathways in Target Cells

Hypothetical Scenario: A researcher adds two radiolabeled hormones—Hormone X (a thyroid iodothyronine, \(\mathrm{T}_3\)) and Hormone Y (a pituitary glycoprotein, \(\mathrm{TSH}\))—to cultured target cells in vitro. Which hormone requires a second messenger such as \(\mathrm{cAMP}\), and which directly alters mRNA transcription via a nuclear receptor?

Solution:

  • Hormone Y (\(\mathrm{TSH}\)): Being a protein/glycoprotein hormone, it cannot cross the lipid bilayer. It binds to membrane-bound receptors on thyroid follicular cells and generates second messengers (such as \(\mathrm{cAMP}\)) to regulate cellular metabolism.
  • Hormone X (\(\mathrm{T}_3\)): Being an iodothyronine, it enters the target cell and binds to intracellular (nuclear) receptors. The hormone-receptor complex interacts directly with the genome to alter mRNA transcription and protein synthesis.

10. Common Pitfalls & NEET UG Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं NEET UG पुनरीक्षण जाँचसूची

English
High-Frequency Exam Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Posterior Pituitary Origin: Oxytocin and ADH are released by the posterior pituitary, NOT synthesized there—their synthesis occurs in hypothalamic nuclei.
  • Thyroid Hormone vs. TCT: \(\mathrm{T}_3\)/\(\mathrm{T}_4\) are iodothyronines (nuclear receptors), whereas TCT (thyrocalcitonin) from the same gland is a protein hormone.
  • Diabetes Insipidus vs. Diabetes Mellitus: Insipidus = ADH deficiency (water loss, normal blood glucose); Mellitus = Insulin deficiency/resistance (hyperglycemia, glycosuria, ketone bodies).
  • Adrenal Cortex Zone Order: Outer → Middle → Inner = Glomerulosa → Fasciculata → Reticularis (GFR).

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Verified all 4 chemical classes of hormones and their receptor locations?
  • Memorized all 6 pars distalis hormones, 1 pars intermedia hormone, and 2 neurohypophysis hormones?
  • Mastered antagonistic pairs: PTH vs. TCT (\(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\)) and Glucagon vs. Insulin (glucose)?
  • Reviewed non-endocrine organ hormones: ANF (heart), Erythropoietin (kidney JG cells), Gastrin/Secretin/CCK/GIP (GI tract)?

परीक्षा में बचने योग्य प्रमुख त्रुटियाँ:
  • पश्च पीयूष का स्रोत: ऑक्सीटोसिन और ADH पश्च पीयूष से केवल मुक्त होते हैं, वहाँ बनते नहीं—इनका संश्लेषण हाइपोथैलेमस में होता है।
  • थायरॉइड हॉर्मोन बनाम TCT: \(\mathrm{T}_3\)/\(\mathrm{T}_4\) आयोडोथायरोनिन (केंद्रकीय ग्राही) हैं, जबकि इसी ग्रंथि का TCT एक प्रोटीन हॉर्मोन है।
  • डायबिटीज़ इनसिपिडस बनाम मेलिटस: इनसिपिडस = ADH की कमी (जल हानि, सामान्य ग्लूकोज); मेलिटस = इंसुलिन की कमी (अतिग्लूकोजरक्तता, मूत्र में ग्लूकोज व कीटोन काय)।

11. Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Hormones are trace, non-nutrient intercellular messengers that coordinate long-term physiological regulation alongside the rapid neural system. The hypothalamus directs the anterior pituitary via portal blood vessels and the posterior pituitary via direct axons. Peripheral glands (pineal, thyroid, parathyroid, thymus, adrenals, pancreas, gonads) and non-endocrine organs (heart, kidney, GI tract) regulate BMR, calcium and glucose homeostasis, stress responses, immunity, and reproduction. Water-soluble hormones act through membrane receptors and second messengers (\(\mathrm{cAMP}\), \(\mathrm{IP}_3\), \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\)), while lipid-soluble steroids and iodothyronines act through nuclear receptors to directly modulate gene expression.

हिंदी

हॉर्मोन सूक्ष्म मात्रा में बनने वाले अपोषक अंतरकोशिकीय संदेशवाहक हैं जो तीव्र तंत्रिका तंत्र के साथ मिलकर दीर्घकालिक कार्यिकीय नियमन करते हैं। हाइपोथैलेमस निवाहिका रक्त द्वारा अग्र पीयूष और प्रत्यक्ष तंत्रिकाक्षों द्वारा पश्च पीयूष का नियंत्रण करता है। परिधीय ग्रंथियाँ (पीनियल, थायरॉइड, पैराथायरॉइड, थाइमस, अधिवृक्क, अग्न्याशय, जनद) तथा अन्य अंग (हृदय, वृक्क, जठरांत्र पथ) BMR, कैल्शियम व ग्लूकोज समस्थापन, तनाव अनुक्रिया, प्रतिरक्षा और जनन का नियमन करते हैं। जल-विलेय हॉर्मोन झिल्ली ग्राही व द्वितीयक संदेशवाहकों (\(\mathrm{cAMP}\), \(\mathrm{IP}_3\), \(\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}\)) द्वारा कार्य करते हैं, जबकि स्टेरॉइड व आयोडोथायरोनिन केंद्रकीय ग्राही द्वारा सीधे जीन अभिव्यक्ति को नियंत्रित करते हैं।
