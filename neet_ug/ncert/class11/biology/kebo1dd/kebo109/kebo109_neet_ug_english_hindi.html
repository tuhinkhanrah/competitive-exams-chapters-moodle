1. Chemical Composition Analysis & Elemental Abundance | रासायनिक संघटन विश्लेषण एवं तत्व प्रचुरता

English

Every living organism—whether plant, animal, or microbe—contains the same basic chemical elements found in non-living matter such as the Earth's crust. However, quantitative elemental analysis reveals a striking distinction: carbon (C) and hydrogen (H) are far more abundant relative to other elements in living tissues than in the Earth's crust, whereas silicon (Si) is abundant in the crust (27.7%) but negligible in the human body.

To identify organic and inorganic constituents of living tissue, biochemists use two complementary experimental workflows:

  1. Acid Fractionation (for Organic Compounds): Living tissue (such as a leaf or liver piece) is ground in trichloroacetic acid (\(\mathrm{Cl_3CCOOH}\)) using a mortar and pestle to form a thick slurry. Straining this slurry through cheesecloth or cotton separates two fractions:
    • Filtrate (Acid-Soluble Pool): Roughly represents the cytoplasmic composition. Contains thousands of small organic compounds (molecular weight ~18 to 800 Da) and inorganic ions such as \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\) and \(\mathrm{PO_4^{3-}}\).
    • Retentate (Acid-Insoluble Fraction): Contains macromolecules from the cytoplasm and organelles—specifically proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides (all \(\ge 10,000\,\mathrm{Da}\)), and lipids (retained as membrane vesicles).
  2. Destructive Ash Analysis (for Inorganic Elements): Fresh tissue is weighed (wet weight) and dried to evaporate all water, leaving the dry weight. Burning the dry tissue completely oxidises all carbon compounds into gaseous \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) and water vapour. The remaining non-volatile ash contains inorganic elements such as calcium (\(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\)), magnesium (\(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\)), sodium (\(\mathrm{Na^+}\)), and potassium (\(\mathrm{K^+}\)).

2. Micromolecules: Amino Acids, Lipids & Nucleotides | सूक्ष्म अणु: अमीनो अम्ल, लिपिड एवं न्यूक्लियोटाइड

English

A. α-Amino Acids & Zwitterionic Equilibrium

Amino acids are substituted methanes in which the central α-carbon carries four distinct substituents: a hydrogen atom (\(-\mathrm{H}\)), an amino group (\(-\mathrm{NH_2}\)), a carboxyl group (\(-\mathrm{COOH}\)), and a variable side chain (\(-\mathrm{R}\)). Although many amino acids exist in nature, only 20 types occur in proteins:

  • Simple R-group examples: Glycine (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{H}\), the only achiral proteinaceous amino acid), Alanine (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{CH_3}\)), and Serine (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{CH_2OH}\)).
  • Acidic, Basic & Neutral: Glutamic acid (acidic, extra carboxyl group), Lysine (basic, extra amino group), and Valine (neutral).
  • Aromatic amino acids: Tyrosine, Phenylalanine, and Tryptophan.
  • Dietary classification: Essential amino acids cannot be synthesized by our body and must come from dietary proteins; non-essential amino acids are synthesized internally.

Because both \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) and \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) are ionizable, an amino acid's net charge changes with solution pH. At an intermediate pH, it exists as a dipolar zwitterion (\(\mathrm{H_3N^+-CH(R)-COO^-}\)) carrying both positive and negative charges with zero net charge.

B. Lipids & The Acid-Insoluble Paradox

Lipids are generally water-insoluble organic compounds. Simple lipids include fatty acids (a carboxyl group attached to an R-group of 1 to 19 carbons; e.g., palmitic acid has 16 carbons including the carboxyl carbon, and arachidonic acid has 20 carbons including the carboxyl carbon). Fatty acids may be saturated (no \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bonds) or unsaturated (one or more \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bonds).

Glycerol is trihydroxy propane (\(\mathrm{CH_2OH-CHOH-CH_2OH}\)). Fatty acids esterify with glycerol to form monoglycerides, diglycerides, or triglycerides. Based on melting point, triglycerides are classified as fats (higher melting point, solid at room temperature) or oils (lower melting point, e.g., gingelly oil, remaining liquid in winter). Phospholipids (e.g., lecithin) contain phosphorus and a phosphorylated nitrogenous compound and form cell membranes. Neural tissues also contain complex lipids such as cholesterol-derived or glycolipid structures.

Why are lipids in the macromolecular (acid-insoluble) fraction? Lipids have molecular weights below 800 Da and are not true polymers. When tissue is ground, cell membranes break into water-insoluble fragments that spontaneously form vesicles. These vesicles cannot pass through the filter and thus separate with the acid-insoluble retentate.

C. Nitrogenous Bases, Nucleosides & Nucleotides

  • Nitrogenous bases (heterocyclic rings): Substituted purines (Adenine, Guanine) and substituted pyrimidines (Cytosine, Uracil, Thymine).
  • Nucleoside = Nitrogenous base + Pentose sugar (ribose or 2'-deoxyribose). Examples: adenosine, guanosine, cytidine, uridine, thymidine.
  • Nucleotide = Nucleoside + Phosphate group (esterified to the sugar). Examples: adenylic acid, guanylic acid, cytidylic acid, uridylic acid, thymidylic acid.

क. α-अमीनो अम्ल एवं ज़्विटरआयनिक साम्यावस्था

अमीनो अम्ल प्रतिस्थापित मीथेन होते हैं जिनमें केंद्रीय α-कार्बन पर चार भिन्न प्रतिस्थापी जुड़े होते हैं: एक हाइड्रोजन परमाणु (\(-\mathrm{H}\)), एक अमीनो समूह (\(-\mathrm{NH_2}\)), एक कार्बोक्सिल समूह (\(-\mathrm{COOH}\)), तथा एक परिवर्ती पार्श्व श्रृंखला (\(-\mathrm{R}\))। यद्यपि प्रकृति में अनेक अमीनो अम्ल होते हैं, प्रोटीन में केवल 20 प्रकार के अमीनो अम्ल पाए जाते हैं:

  • सरल R-समूह उदाहरण: ग्लाइसीन (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{H}\)), एलानीन (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{CH_3}\)), और सेरीन (\(\mathrm{R} = -\mathrm{CH_2OH}\))।
  • अम्लीय, क्षारीय एवं उदासीन: ग्लूटामिक अम्ल (अम्लीय), लाइसीन (क्षारीय), और वैलीन (उदासीन)।
  • ऐरोमैटिक अमीनो अम्ल: टायरोसीन, फेनिलएलानीन, तथा ट्रिप्टोफैन।
  • आहारीय वर्गीकरण: अनिवार्य (essential) अमीनो अम्ल हमारा शरीर नहीं बना सकता और इन्हें आहार से प्राप्त करना होता है; अनावश्यक (non-essential) अमीनो अम्ल शरीर स्वयं संश्लेषित कर लेता है।

चूंकि \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) और \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) दोनों आयनन योग्य हैं, इसलिए विलयन के pH बदलने पर अमीनो अम्ल का आवेश बदलता है। मध्यवर्ती pH पर यह द्विध्रुवीय ज़्विटरआयन (zwitterion) (\(\mathrm{H_3N^+-CH(R)-COO^-}\)) के रूप में रहता है, जिस पर धनात्मक और ऋणात्मक दोनों आवेश होते हैं (कुल आवेश शून्य)।

ख. लिपिड एवं अम्ल-अविलेय अंश का विरोधाभास

लिपिड सामान्यतः जल में अघुलनशील कार्बनिक यौगिक होते हैं। सरल लिपिड में वसीय अम्ल (fatty acids) आते हैं जिनमें कार्बोक्सिल समूह के साथ 1 से 19 कार्बन वाला R-समूह जुड़ा होता है; जैसे पामिटिक अम्ल में कार्बोक्सिल कार्बन सहित 16 कार्बन होते हैं, और एरैकिडोनिक अम्ल में कार्बोक्सिल कार्बन सहित 20 कार्बन होते हैं। वसीय अम्ल संतृप्त (द्विबंध रहित) या असंतृप्त (एक या अधिक \(\mathrm{C=C}\) द्विबंध युक्त) हो सकते हैं।

ग्लिसरॉल एक ट्राइहाइड्रॉक्सी प्रोपेन (\(\mathrm{CH_2OH-CHOH-CH_2OH}\)) है। वसीय अम्ल ग्लिसरॉल के साथ एस्टरीकृत होकर मोनोग्लिसराइड, डाइग्लिसराइड या ट्राइग्लिसराइड बनाते हैं। गलनांक के आधार पर ट्राइग्लिसराइड को वसा (fats) (उच्च गलनांक) या तेल (oils) (निम्न गलनांक, जैसे तिल का तेल जो सर्दियों में भी द्रव रहता है) कहा जाता है। फॉस्फोलिपिड (जैसे लेसिथिन) में फॉस्फोरस और फॉस्फोरीलेटेड कार्बनिक यौगिक होता है और ये कोशिका झिल्ली में पाए जाते हैं। तंत्रिका ऊतकों में अधिक जटिल संरचना वाले लिपिड भी होते हैं।

लिपिड वृहद अणु (अम्ल-अविलेय) अंश में क्यों आते हैं? लिपिड का अणुभार 800 Da से कम होता है और ये सच्चे बहुलक नहीं हैं। जब ऊतक को पीसा जाता है, तो कोशिका झिल्ली टूटकर जल-अविलेय टुकड़ों में बंट जाती है जो पुटिकाएं (vesicles) बना लेते हैं। ये पुटिकाएं छनती नहीं हैं और अम्ल-अविलेय अवशेष के साथ अलग हो जाती हैं।

ग. नाइट्रोजनी क्षार, न्यूक्लियोसाइड एवं न्यूक्लियोटाइड

A. Proteins: Heteropolymers & Four Structural Levels

Proteins are linear polypeptides formed by linking 20 different amino acids via peptide bonds; hence every protein is a heteropolymer (unlike a homopolymer, which repeats a single monomer). Collagen is the most abundant protein in the animal world, while Ribulose bisphosphate Carboxylase-Oxygenase (RuBisCO) is the most abundant protein in the entire biosphere.

Biologists describe protein architecture at four hierarchical levels:

  • Primary Structure: The linear positional sequence of amino acids. By convention, the left end is the first amino acid (N-terminal) and the right end is the last amino acid (C-terminal).
  • Secondary Structure: Local folding of the polypeptide backbone into a right-handed α-helix (only right-handed helices occur in proteins) or a β-pleated sheet.
  • Tertiary Structure: The long chain folds upon itself like a hollow woolen ball, stabilized by hydrogen bonds and disulphide bonds. Tertiary structure is absolutely necessary for the biological activity of proteins (including enzymes).
  • Quaternary Structure: Spatial assembly of more than one polypeptide subunit. For example, adult human haemoglobin (Hb) consists of 4 subunits: two identical α-subunits and two identical β-subunits (\(\alpha_2\beta_2\)).

B. Polysaccharides & Nucleic Acids

Polysaccharides are long carbohydrate chains. In a branched polysaccharide such as glycogen, the right end is the reducing end and the left end is the non-reducing end.

  • Cellulose: Unbranched homopolymer of glucose; found in plant cell walls, paper pulp, and cotton fibre. It lacks complex helices and therefore cannot hold \(\mathrm{I_2}\) molecules.
  • Starch: Energy-storage homopolymer of glucose in plants; forms helical secondary structures that trap \(\mathrm{I_2}\) to produce a characteristic blue colour.
  • Glycogen: Branched energy-storage homopolymer of glucose in animals.
  • Inulin: Homopolymer of fructose.
  • Chitin: Complex polysaccharide forming arthropod exoskeletons; built from chemically modified amino-sugars (such as N-acetyl glucosamine) and is mostly a homopolymer.

Nucleic acids are polynucleotides made of nucleotide monomers. DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) contains 2'-deoxyribose, whereas RNA (ribonucleic acid) contains ribose.

5. Enzymes: Mechanism, Kinetics, Classification & Co-factors | एंजाइम: क्रियाविधि, गतिकी, वर्गीकरण एवं सह-कारक

English

A. Nature of Enzymes & Catalytic Power

Almost all enzymes are proteins with a 3D tertiary structure containing crevices or pockets called active sites where substrates bind. Certain nucleic acids that act as catalysts are called ribozymes. Unlike inorganic catalysts (which work at high temperatures and pressures), most enzymes denature above 40 °C. However, enzymes from thermophilic organisms (living in hot vents and sulphur springs) retain catalytic stability up to 80–90 °C.

A chemical reaction rate is defined as product formed per unit time: \(\text{rate} = \frac{\delta P}{\delta t}\) (often doubling or halving for every 10 °C change). For example, the hydration of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) to carbonic acid (\(\mathrm{H_2CO_3}\)) produces only ~200 molecules per hour uncatalysed, whereas cytoplasmic carbonic anhydrase produces ~600,000 molecules per second—accelerating the rate by roughly 10 million (\(10^7\)) times.

Enzymes accelerate reactions by lowering the activation energy—the energy barrier between the substrate (\(S\)) and the unstable transition state—without changing the overall energy difference between \(S\) and product (\(P\)). The catalytic cycle follows:

\[E + S \rightleftharpoons ES \longrightarrow EP \longrightarrow E + P\]

B. Factors Affecting Activity & Competitive Inhibition

  • Temperature & pH: Enzymes show bell-shaped activity curves with a sharp optimum temperature and optimum pH. Low temperature preserves enzymes in a temporarily inactive state, whereas high temperature permanently denatures them.
  • Substrate Concentration \([S]\): Velocity rises initially with \([S]\) and then plateaus at \(V_{\max}\) because enzyme molecules become saturated with substrate. The substrate concentration at which velocity reaches \(\frac{V_{\max}}{2}\) is \(K_m\).
  • Competitive Inhibition: A chemical inhibitor closely resembling the substrate competes for the active site. Example: malonate competitively inhibits succinic dehydrogenase by mimicking succinate. Competitive inhibitors are also used to control bacterial pathogens.

C. Six Classes of Enzymes & Co-factors

Enzymes are grouped into 6 classes (each with 4–13 subclasses, designated by a 4-digit number):

  1. Oxidoreductases / Dehydrogenases: Catalyse oxidoreduction between two substrates.
  2. Transferases: Transfer a group \(G\) (other than hydrogen) between substrates.
  3. Hydrolases: Catalyse hydrolysis of ester, ether, peptide, glycosidic, \(\mathrm{C-C}\), \(\mathrm{C-halide}\), or \(\mathrm{P-N}\) bonds.
  4. Lyases: Remove groups from substrates by mechanisms other than hydrolysis, leaving double bonds.
  5. Isomerases: Catalyse interconversion of optical, geometric, or positional isomers.
  6. Ligases: Catalyse linking of two compounds (forming \(\mathrm{C-O, C-S, C-N, P-O}\) bonds).

Non-protein constituents called co-factors bind to the protein portion (apoenzyme) to make it catalytically active; removing the co-factor abolishes activity:

  • Prosthetic groups: Tightly bound organic compounds forming part of the active site (e.g., haem in peroxidase and catalase, which break down \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}\) to water and oxygen).
  • Co-enzymes: Transiently bound organic compounds, often containing vitamins (e.g., NAD and NADP contain the vitamin niacin).
  • Metal ions: Form coordination bonds with active-site side chains and the substrate (e.g., zinc (\(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}\)) for carboxypeptidase).

  • प्रतिस्पर्धी संदमन (Competitive Inhibition): जब कोई संदमक संरचना में क्रियाधार से काफी मिलता-जुलता हो, तो वह सक्रिय स्थल के लिए क्रियाधार से प्रतिस्पर्धा करता है। उदाहरण: मैलोनेट, सक्सिनेट की नकल करके सक्सिनिक डिहाइड्रोजिनेज को संदमित करता है। ऐसे संदमकों का उपयोग जीवाणु रोगजनकों के नियंत्रण में किया जाता है।

  4. लायेज: जल-अपघटन के अतिरिक्त अन्य विधि से समूह हटाकर द्विबंध बनाते हैं।

Worked Example 1: Fractionation & Molecular Weight Classification

Assumed Experimental Scenario: A student homogenizes 10 g of fresh plant leaf tissue in trichloroacetic acid (\(\mathrm{Cl_3CCOOH}\)) and filters it. Four compounds are detected across the two fractions: Compound W (glycine, MW 75 Da), Compound X (a membrane phospholipid, MW 750 Da), Compound Y (starch, MW 120,000 Da), and Compound Z (RuBisCO enzyme, MW 540,000 Da). Which compounds appear in the filtrate and which appear in the retentate, and why?

Solution:

  • Filtrate (Acid-soluble pool): Contains only Compound W (glycine) because it is a water/acid-soluble micromolecule with molecular weight in the 18–800 Da range.
  • Retentate (Acid-insoluble fraction): Contains Compound Y (starch) and Compound Z (RuBisCO) because both are true polymeric biomacromolecules (\(\ge 10,000\,\mathrm{Da}\)). It also contains Compound X (phospholipid)—even though its MW is 750 Da—because disrupted cell membranes form water-insoluble vesicles that cannot pass through the filter.

Worked Example 2: Enzyme Kinetics & Competitive Inhibition

Assumed Experimental Scenario: An enzyme \(E\) catalysing \(S \rightarrow P\) has a maximum velocity \(V_{\max} = 80\,\mathrm{µmol\,min^{-1}}\) and reaches half-maximal velocity (\(40\,\mathrm{µmol\,min^{-1}}\)) at \([S] = 2.5\,\mathrm{mM}\). Now, a structural analogue \(I\) of substrate \(S\) is added at a fixed concentration, and the reaction velocity drops at low \([S]\). How can the original \(V_{\max}\) still be approached, and what happens if the temperature is raised from 37 °C to 75 °C (assuming \(E\) is a mammalian enzyme)?

Solution:

  • By definition, \(K_m = 2.5\,\mathrm{mM}\) (the \([S]\) giving \(\frac{V_{\max}}{2}\)).
  • Because \(I\) is a competitive inhibitor competing for the active site, increasing \([S]\) to very high levels outcompetes \(I\) and allows the enzyme to still approach the same \(V_{\max} = 80\,\mathrm{µmol\,min^{-1}}\).
  • Raising the temperature of a mammalian enzyme from 37 °C to 75 °C (well above 40 °C) destroys its tertiary structure through heat denaturation, causing permanent loss of catalytic activity.

हल किया गया उदाहरण 2: एंजाइम गतिकी एवं प्रतिस्पर्धी संदमन

हल:

  • परिभाषा के अनुसार, \(K_m = 2.5\,\mathrm{mM}\) है (जहां वेग \(\frac{V_{\max}}{2}\) होता है)।
  • चूंकि \(I\) सक्रिय स्थल के लिए प्रतिस्पर्धा करने वाला एक प्रतिस्पर्धी संदमक है, इसलिए \([S]\) को अत्यधिक बढ़ाने पर क्रियाधार \(I\) को पछाड़ देता है और एंजाइम पुनः वही \(V_{\max} = 80\,\mathrm{µmol\,min^{-1}}\) प्राप्त कर सकता है।
  • स्तनधारी एंजाइम का तापमान 37 °C से 75 °C (40 °C से काफी अधिक) करने पर ऊष्मा के कारण इसकी तृतीयक संरचना नष्ट (विकृतीकरण / denaturation) हो जाएगी, जिससे उत्प्रेरक सक्रियता स्थायी रूप से समाप्त हो जाएगी।

8. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist | सामान्य त्रुटियां एवं पुनरावृत्ति सूची

English
High-Frequency NEET Traps to Avoid:
  • Lipids ≠ True Macromolecules: Only proteins, nucleic acids, and polysaccharides are true polymeric macromolecules (\(\ge 10,000\,\mathrm{Da}\)). Lipids (< 800 Da) appear in the retentate only due to membrane vesicle formation.
  • Collagen vs. RuBisCO: Collagen is the most abundant protein in the animal world; RuBisCO is the most abundant protein in the entire biosphere.
  • Helix Handedness: Proteins contain only right-handed α-helices.
  • Glycogen Ends: Right end = Reducing; Left end = Non-reducing.
  • Enzymes & Thermodynamics: Enzymes lower activation energy (\(E_a\)), but they never alter the energy difference (\(\Delta G\)) between substrate and product.
हिन्दी
NEET में होने वाली सामान्य गलतियां जिनसे बचें:
  • लिपिड ≠ सच्चे वृहद अणु: केवल प्रोटीन, न्यूक्लिक अम्ल और पॉलीसैकेराइड ही सच्चे बहुलक वृहद अणु (\(\ge 10,000\,\mathrm{Da}\)) हैं। लिपिड (< 800 Da) केवल झिल्ली पुटिकाएं बनने के कारण अवशेष में आते हैं।
  • कोलेजन बनाम RuBisCO: कोलेजन जंतु जगत में सर्वाधिक प्रचुर प्रोटीन है; जबकि RuBisCO संपूर्ण जीवमंडल में सर्वाधिक प्रचुर प्रोटीन है।
  • कुंडलिनी की दिशा: प्रोटीन में केवल दक्षिणावर्ती (right-handed) α-कुंडलिनी पाई जाती है।
  • ग्लाइकोजन के सिरे: दायां सिरा = अपचायी (Reducing); बायां सिरा = अनअपचायी (Non-reducing)।
  • एंजाइम एवं ऊर्जा: एंजाइम सक्रियण ऊर्जा (\(E_a\)) को कम करते हैं, परंतु क्रियाधार और उत्पाद के बीच के ऊर्जा अंतर को कभी नहीं बदलते।

9. Concise Final Recap | संक्षिप्त अंतिम पुनरावलोकन

English

Living organisms share the same elements as non-living matter but are enriched in C, H, and O, with water (70–90%) as the most abundant cellular chemical and proteins (10–15%) as the most abundant organic macromolecule. Acid fractionation separates micromolecules (18–800 Da) in the filtrate from true polymeric macromolecules and membrane lipid vesicles in the retentate. Proteins rely on their 3D tertiary structure for biological activity, polysaccharides serve structural (cellulose, chitin) and storage (starch, glycogen, inulin) roles, nucleic acids store genetic information, and enzymes—classified into six functional groups and often aided by co-factors—accelerate cellular chemistry by lowering activation energy barriers.

हिन्दी

जीवित जीव निर्जीव पदार्थों के समान तत्वों से बने होते हैं, परंतु उनमें C, H और O अधिक प्रचुर होते हैं; जल (70–90%) सर्वाधिक प्रचुर कोशिकीय रसायन और प्रोटीन (10–15%) सर्वाधिक प्रचुर कार्बनिक वृहद अणु है। अम्ल प्रभाजन से सूक्ष्म अणु (18–800 Da) निस्पंद में तथा सच्चे बहुलक वृहद अणु व झिल्ली लिपिड पुटिकाएं अवशेष में अलग हो जाते हैं। प्रोटीन की जैविक सक्रियता उसकी तृतीयक 3D संरचना पर निर्भर करती है; पॉलीसैकेराइड संरचनात्मक (सेलुलोज, काइटिन) व संचयी (स्टार्च, ग्लाइकोजन, इनुलिन) कार्य करते हैं; न्यूक्लिक अम्ल आनुवंशिक सूचना वहन करते हैं; और छह वर्गों में विभाजित एंजाइम (प्रायः सह-कारकों की सहायता से) सक्रियण ऊर्जा को कम करके जैव रासायनिक अभिक्रियाओं को अत्यंत तीव्र बनाते हैं।
