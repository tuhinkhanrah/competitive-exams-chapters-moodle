1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

English

Multicellular organisms require an efficient internal transport system to deliver oxygen (\( \mathrm{O_2} \)), nutrients, and hormones to every living cell while continuously removing carbon dioxide (\( \mathrm{CO_2} \)) and metabolic wastes. While simple aquatic animals such as sponges and coelenterates circulate surrounding water through their body cavities, higher animals rely on specialized internal fluids—primarily blood and lymph.

By completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Differentiate between plasma, serum, and the formed elements of blood, including quantitative counts and functions.
  • Explain ABO and Rh blood group compatibility, erythroblastosis foetalis, and the enzymatic cascade of blood coagulation.
  • Compare open and closed circulatory systems and trace vertebrate heart evolution from two-chambered to four-chambered hearts.
  • Analyze the conducting system of the human heart, the \(0.8\,\mathrm{s}\) cardiac cycle, cardiac output calculations, heart sounds, and standard ECG waveforms.
  • Distinguish pulmonary, systemic, hepatic portal, and coronary circulations, along with neural/hormonal regulation and major cardiovascular disorders.

2. Blood: Fluid Matrix (Plasma) & Formed Elements

English

Blood is a specialized fluid connective tissue composed of a liquid matrix called plasma (~55% of total blood volume) and cellular components called formed elements (~45%).

2.1 Plasma & Serum

Plasma is a straw-coloured, viscous fluid. About 90–92% of plasma is water, and 6–8% consists of plasma proteins:

  • Fibrinogen: Essential for blood clotting (coagulation).
  • Globulins: Primarily involved in body defence mechanisms (immunoglobulins/antibodies).
  • Albumins: Maintain osmotic balance (colloidal osmotic pressure) of blood.

Plasma also carries dissolved minerals (\(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\), \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\)), nutrients in transit (glucose, amino acids, lipids), and inactive clotting factors. Serum is defined as plasma without clotting factors:

\[ \mathrm{Serum} = \mathrm{Plasma} - \mathrm{Clotting\ Factors\ (including\ Fibrinogen)} \]

3. Blood Grouping & Mechanism of Coagulation

English

3.1 ABO Blood Grouping

The ABO system depends on the presence or absence of two surface antigens (chemicals capable of inducing an immune response) on RBCs—A and B—and corresponding natural antibodies in the plasma. Mismatched transfusion causes clumping (agglutination) and destruction of RBCs.

Blood Group Antigens on RBCs Antibodies in Plasma Compatible Donor Groups
AAanti-BA, O
BBanti-AB, O
ABA, BNilAB, A, B, O (Universal Recipient)
ONilanti-A, anti-BO (Universal Donor)

3.2 Rh Grouping & Erythroblastosis Foetalis

Approximately 80% of humans possess the Rh antigen (similar to that in Rhesus monkeys) on their RBCs and are termed Rh-positive (\(\mathrm{Rh^+}\)); those lacking it are Rh-negative (\(\mathrm{Rh^-}\)). If an \(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) individual receives \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) blood, they produce specific anti-Rh antibodies.

Clinical Case — Erythroblastosis Foetalis: This occurs when an \(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) mother carries an \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) foetus:

  1. During the first pregnancy, the placenta separates maternal and foetal blood, so no significant mixing occurs.
  2. At the time of delivery of the first child, small amounts of \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) foetal blood may enter the mother's circulation, sensitizing her immune system to produce anti-Rh antibodies.
  3. In subsequent pregnancies with an \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) foetus, maternal anti-Rh antibodies cross the placenta and destroy foetal RBCs, causing severe anaemia, jaundice, or fatal outcomes.
  4. Prevention: Administering anti-Rh antibodies to the mother immediately after the delivery of the first child neutralizes any escaped foetal \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) cells before maternal sensitization occurs.

3.3 Coagulation of Blood (Clotting Cascade)

Coagulation prevents excessive blood loss after vascular injury. The dark reddish-brown clot is a meshwork of insoluble fibrin threads trapping dead and damaged formed elements.

Step 1 (Initiation): Trauma stimulates platelets and injured tissues to release clotting factors, activating a cascade of linked enzymatic reactions (requiring \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) ions) to form the enzyme complex thrombokinase.

\[ \mathrm{Prothrombin\ (inactive)} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Thrombokinase,\ Ca^{2+}}} \mathrm{Thrombin\ (active)} \] \[ \mathrm{Fibrinogen\ (soluble,\ inactive)} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{Thrombin}} \mathrm{Fibrin\ (insoluble\ meshwork)} \]

৩.২ Rh শ্রেণিবিভাগ ও এরিথ্রোব্লাস্টোসিস ফিটালিস

প্রায় ৮০% মানুষের RBC-র পৃষ্ঠে Rh অ্যান্টিজেন (রেসাস বানরের অনুরূপ) থাকে, এদের Rh-পজিটিভ (\(\mathrm{Rh^+}\)) এবং যাদের থাকে না তাদের Rh-নেগেটিভ (\(\mathrm{Rh^-}\)) বলে। কোনো \(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) ব্যক্তি \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) রক্তের সংস্পর্শে এলে তার দেহে anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি তৈরি হয়।

বিশেষ ক্ষেত্র — এরিথ্রোব্লাস্টোসিস ফিটালিস: যখন একজন \(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) মাতার গর্ভে \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) ভ্রূণ থাকে:

  1. প্রথম গর্ভাবস্থায় অমরা (প্ল্যাসেন্টা) মা ও ভ্রূণের রক্তকে পৃথক রাখায় সাধারণত রক্ত মেশে না।
  2. প্রথম সন্তান প্রসবের সময় ভ্রূণের সামান্য \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) রক্ত মায়ের রক্তে মিশে যেতে পারে, ফলে মায়ের দেহে anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি তৈরি শুরু হয়।
  3. পরবর্তী গর্ভাবস্থায় পুনরায় \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) ভ্রূণ এলে মায়ের দেহের anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি অমরা ভেদ করে ভ্রূণের RBC ধ্বংস করে। এতে শিশুর তীব্র রক্তাল্পতা, জন্ডিস বা মৃত্যু পর্যন্ত ঘটতে পারে।

4. Lymph (Tissue Fluid) & Its Role

English

As blood flows through tissue capillaries, water and small water-soluble solutes filter out into intercellular spaces, leaving larger plasma proteins and most formed elements inside the vessels. This fluid is called interstitial fluid or tissue fluid, and it has the same mineral distribution as plasma. All exchange of nutrients and gases between blood and cells occurs via this medium.

An elaborate network of vessels—the lymphatic system—collects this tissue fluid (now termed lymph) and drains it back into major veins. Lymph is colourless, contains specialized lymphocytes for immune responses, acts as a carrier for nutrients and hormones, and absorbs dietary fats through lacteals located in intestinal villi.

5.2 Human Heart & Conducting System

The human heart is mesodermally derived, located in the thoracic cavity between the two lungs, slightly tilted to the left, and enclosed by a double-walled pericardium containing pericardial fluid. Ventricular walls are much thicker than atrial walls because they pump blood over longer distances under higher pressure.

  • Septa: Thin muscular inter-atrial septum, thick-walled inter-ventricular septum, and thick fibrous atrio-ventricular septum.
  • Valves (ensure unidirectional flow): Tricuspid valve (3 muscular flaps between right atrium and right ventricle); Bicuspid / Mitral valve (between left atrium and left ventricle); Semilunar valves (guarding openings of right ventricle into pulmonary artery and left ventricle into aorta).
  • Nodal Tissue (Autoexcitable):
    • Sino-atrial node (SAN): Right upper corner of right atrium; generates 70–75 action potentials \(\mathrm{min^{-1}}\) (maximum rate), hence called the pacemaker (normal heart rate averages 72 beats \(\mathrm{min^{-1}}\)).
    • Atrio-ventricular node (AVN): Lower left corner of right atrium near the AV septum.
    • AV Bundle (Bundle of His) & Purkinje Fibres: Continues from AVN through the AV septum, divides into right and left bundle branches atop the inter-ventricular septum, and spreads as minute Purkinje fibres across ventricular musculature.

6. Cardiac Cycle, Cardiac Output, Heart Sounds & ECG

English

6.1 Sequential Events of the Cardiac Cycle

  1. Joint Diastole: All four chambers are relaxed; tricuspid and bicuspid valves are open, semilunar valves are closed. Blood flows passively from pulmonary veins and vena cava through atria into ventricles.
  2. Atrial Systole: SAN generates an action potential stimulating simultaneous contraction of both atria, increasing blood flow into ventricles by about 30%.
  3. Ventricular Systole: Action potential reaches ventricles via AVN, Bundle of His, and Purkinje fibres. Ventricular pressure rises sharply, closing tricuspid and bicuspid valves (preventing backflow into atria). Further pressure rise forces semilunar valves open, ejecting blood into the pulmonary artery and aorta. Meanwhile, atria undergo diastole.
  4. Ventricular Diastole: Ventricles relax and pressure falls, closing semilunar valves (preventing backflow into ventricles). As ventricular pressure drops below atrial pressure, AV valves are pushed open by blood accumulated in atria, returning the heart to joint diastole.
\[ \mathrm{Duration\ of\ 1\ Cardiac\ Cycle} = \frac{60\,\mathrm{s}}{72\ \mathrm{beats}} \approx 0.8\,\mathrm{s} \] \[ \mathrm{Cardiac\ Output\ (CO)} = \mathrm{Stroke\ Volume\ (SV)} \times \mathrm{Heart\ Rate\ (HR)} \]

For a healthy resting individual (\(\mathrm{SV} \approx 70\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{HR} = 72\,\mathrm{min^{-1}}\)): \(\mathrm{CO} \approx 70 \times 72 = 5040\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}} \approx 5\,\mathrm{L\,min^{-1}}\).

Heart Sounds: First sound ("Lub") is caused by closure of tricuspid and bicuspid (AV) valves at the start of ventricular systole. Second sound ("Dub") is caused by closure of semilunar valves at the start of ventricular diastole.

6.2 Electrocardiogram (ECG)

An ECG records the electrical activity of the heart during a cardiac cycle using three standard leads (one on each wrist, one on the left ankle):

  • P-wave: Electrical excitation (depolarisation) of atria, leading to atrial contraction.
  • QRS complex: Depolarisation of ventricles, initiating ventricular contraction (systole starts shortly after Q). Counting QRS complexes per minute gives the heart rate.
  • T-wave: Return of ventricles to normal state (repolarisation); the end of the T-wave marks the end of systole.

  • QRS জটিল (Complex): নিলয়ের ডিপোলারাইজেশন নির্দেশ করে, যা নিলয়ের সংকোচন শুরু করে (Q-এর পরপরই সিস্টোল শুরু হয়)। নির্দিষ্ট সময়ে QRS-এর সংখ্যা গুনে হৃদস্পন্দনের হার নির্ণয় করা যায়।
  • T-তরঙ্গ: নিলয়ের উত্তেজিত অবস্থা থেকে স্বাভাবিক অবস্থায় ফিরে আসাকে (রিপোলারাইজেশন) বোঝায়; T-তরঙ্গের সমাপ্তি সিস্টোলের সমাপ্তি নির্দেশ করে।

7. Double Circulation, Regulation & Circulatory Disorders

English

7.1 Blood Vessel Structure & Double Circulation

Arteries and veins share three histological layers: an inner squamous endothelium (tunica intima), a middle layer of smooth muscle and elastic fibres (tunica media—comparatively thinner in veins), and an outer fibrous connective tissue layer with collagen fibres (tunica externa).

  • Pulmonary Circulation: Right ventricle \(\rightarrow\) Pulmonary artery (deoxygenated) \(\rightarrow\) Lungs (oxygenation) \(\rightarrow\) Pulmonary veins (oxygenated) \(\rightarrow\) Left atrium.
  • Systemic Circulation: Left ventricle \(\rightarrow\) Aorta \(\rightarrow\) Arteries, arterioles, capillaries \(\rightarrow\) Body tissues \(\rightarrow\) Venules, veins, vena cava (deoxygenated) \(\rightarrow\) Right atrium.
  • Special Circuits: The hepatic portal system connects the digestive tract to the liver via the hepatic portal vein before systemic delivery. The coronary system exclusively supplies blood to and from cardiac muscle.

Tutorial Diagram 3 (English): Schematic of complete double circulation (Pulmonary & Systemic circuits).

7.2 Regulation of Cardiac Activity

The human heart is myogenic (auto-regulated by nodal tissue). A neural centre in the medulla oblongata moderates cardiac function via the autonomic nervous system (ANS):

  • Sympathetic nerves: Increase heart rate, strength of ventricular contraction, and cardiac output.
  • Parasympathetic nerves: Decrease heart rate, speed of action potential conduction, and cardiac output.
  • Hormonal control: Adrenal medullary hormones (adrenaline/noradrenaline) also increase cardiac output.

7.3 Disorders of the Circulatory System

  • Hypertension (High Blood Pressure): Normal BP is \(120/80\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (systolic/diastolic). Repeated readings of \(140/90\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) or higher indicate hypertension, damaging the heart, brain, and kidneys.
  • Coronary Artery Disease (CAD / Atherosclerosis): Deposition of calcium, fat, cholesterol, and fibrous tissue narrows the lumen of coronary arteries supplying the heart muscle.
  • Angina (Angina Pectoris): Acute chest pain occurring when insufficient oxygen reaches the heart muscle due to impaired blood flow.
  • Heart Failure: Heart fails to pump blood effectively enough to meet body demands (often called congestive heart failure due to lung congestion). Distinct from cardiac arrest (heart stops beating) and heart attack (sudden damage to heart muscle from inadequate blood supply).

Worked Example 1 (Cardiac Output & Cycle Duration — Assumed Athlete Data):
During moderate exercise, a trained runner (assumed values) exhibits a heart rate of \(120\,\mathrm{beats\,min^{-1}}\) and a stroke volume of \(95\,\mathrm{mL}\). Calculate (a) the duration of one cardiac cycle under this condition and (b) the cardiac output in \(\mathrm{L\,min^{-1}}\).

Solution:

(a) Duration of one cardiac cycle: \[ T = \frac{60\,\mathrm{s}}{120\ \mathrm{beats}} = 0.50\,\mathrm{s} \]

(b) Cardiac output: \[ \mathrm{CO} = \mathrm{SV} \times \mathrm{HR} = 95\,\mathrm{mL} \times 120\,\mathrm{min^{-1}} = 11{,}400\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}} = 11.4\,\mathrm{L\,min^{-1}} \]

Worked Example 2 (Transfusion Compatibility — Hypothetical Clinical Scenario):
A trauma patient has blood group B\(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) (assumed). Which blood groups from a hospital blood bank—\(\mathrm{O\,Rh^+}\), \(\mathrm{O\,Rh^-}\), \(\mathrm{B\,Rh^+}\), \(\mathrm{B\,Rh^-}\), \(\mathrm{AB\,Rh^-}\)—can be safely transfused to this patient?

Solution:

A group B recipient has anti-A antibodies in plasma, so donor RBCs must lack antigen A (ruling out \(\mathrm{AB}\)). Furthermore, an \(\mathrm{Rh^-}\) recipient lacks the Rh antigen and will form anti-Rh antibodies if exposed to \(\mathrm{Rh^+}\) blood. Therefore, only \(\mathrm{B\,Rh^-}\) and \(\mathrm{O\,Rh^-}\) are safe.

10. Common NEET Pitfalls, Revision Checklist & Final Recap

English

Common Exam Pitfalls to Avoid:

  • Heart Failure vs. Cardiac Arrest vs. Heart Attack: Heart failure = inadequate pumping (lung congestion); Cardiac arrest = heart stops beating; Heart attack = sudden myocardial damage due to blocked blood supply.
  • Crocodiles Exception: Reptiles generally have a 3-chambered heart, except crocodiles, which have a 4-chambered heart.
  • ECG Wave Timing: Ventricular contraction starts shortly after Q (not at P); the end of the T-wave marks the end of systole.
  • Tricuspid vs. Bicuspid: Tricuspid is on the right side (RA–RV); Bicuspid/Mitral is on the left side (LA–LV).

Concise Final Recap: Blood (55% plasma + 45% formed elements) and lymph transport gases, nutrients, and wastes. Human circulation is a closed, complete double circulation driven by a 4-chambered myogenic heart paced by the SAN (72 beats/min, 0.8 s cycle, ~5 L/min cardiac output), moderated by the medulla oblongata via ANS and adrenal medullary hormones.
