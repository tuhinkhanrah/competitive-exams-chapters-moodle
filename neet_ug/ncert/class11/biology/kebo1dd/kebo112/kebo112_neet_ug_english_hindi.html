1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview • अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं अध्याय अवलोकन

English

Every living cell requires a continuous supply of usable chemical energy to power active transport, biosynthesis, growth, and repair. In plants, photosynthesis traps solar energy in organic bonds, whereas cellular respiration systematically releases that energy through enzyme-controlled oxidation and stores it as adenosine triphosphate (ATP).

  • Master the mitochondrial stages of aerobic respiration: oxidative decarboxylation of pyruvate, the Tricarboxylic Acid (Krebs') Cycle, the Electron Transport System (ETS), and chemiosmotic ATP synthesis.

2. Gas Exchange in Plants & Principles of Cellular Respiration • पादपों में गैस विनिमय एवं कोशिकीय श्वसन के सिद्धांत

English

2.1 How Plants Manage Without Respiratory Organs

Unlike animals, plants lack lungs, gills, or circulatory blood for gas transport. Yet every living plant cell obtains \(\mathrm{O_2}\) and expels \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) through simple diffusion via stomata (in leaves and young stems) and lenticels (in woody bark). Three anatomical and physiological factors make this possible:

  • Local Autonomy: Each plant organ handles its own gas-exchange needs; long-distance gas transport between organs is minimal.
  • Low Metabolic Demand: Roots, stems, and leaves respire at much lower rates than animal tissues. During intense photosynthesis, chloroplasts generate \(\mathrm{O_2}\) internally inside the cell.
  • Short Diffusion Paths: Even in massive woody trees, living cells lie in thin layers just beneath the bark, while the deep interior consists of dead mechanical tissue. Loosely packed parenchyma cells create an interconnected network of intercellular air spaces so almost every living cell contacts air.

2.2 Cellular Respiration vs. Combustion

Direct burning (combustion) of glucose releases its entire chemical energy in a single uncontrolled step, mostly as heat:

\[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6\,O_2 \longrightarrow 6\,CO_2 + 6\,H_2O + Energy}\]

In contrast, cellular respiration breaks C–C bonds through slow, enzyme-catalysed oxidation steps. By releasing energy in discrete packets, cells couple exergonic steps to ATP synthesis rather than wasting energy as heat. Because ATP is hydrolysed wherever cellular work is needed, it serves as the universal energy currency of the cell. The compounds oxidised in respiration are called respiratory substrates—chiefly carbohydrates (derived from sucrose or starch), though fats, proteins, and organic acids can also be oxidised under specific conditions.

2.2 कोशिकीय श्वसन बनाम दहन

ग्लूकोज का प्रत्यक्ष दहन (combustion) एक ही अनियंत्रित चरण में सारी रासायनिक ऊर्जा को मुख्यतः ऊष्मा के रूप में मुक्त कर देता है:

\[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6\,O_2 \longrightarrow 6\,CO_2 + 6\,H_2O + \text{ऊर्जा (Energy)}}\]

इसके विपरीत, कोशिकीय श्वसन एंजाइमों द्वारा नियंत्रित धीमे ऑक्सीकरण चरणों के माध्यम से C–C बंधों को तोड़ता है। छोटे-छोटे पैकेटों में ऊर्जा मुक्त होने से कोशिका ऊष्मा के रूप में ऊर्जा नष्ट किए बिना इसे ATP संश्लेषण से जोड़ पाती है। आवश्यकता पड़ने पर ATP के जल-अपघटन से ऊर्जा मिलती है, इसलिए इसे कोशिका की ऊर्जा मुद्रा (energy currency) कहते हैं। श्वसन में ऑक्सीकृत होने वाले पदार्थ श्वसन क्रियाधार (respiratory substrates) कहलाते हैं—मुख्यतः कार्बोहाइड्रेट (सुक्रोज या स्टार्च से प्राप्त), यद्यपि विशेष परिस्थितियों में वसा, प्रोटीन और कार्बनिक अम्ल भी प्रयुक्त हो सकते हैं।

3. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway) • ग्लाइकोलिसिस (EMP पथ)

English

Named after Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof, and J. Parnas, glycolysis (Greek: glycos = sugar, lysis = splitting) is a universal ten-step pathway occurring in the cytoplasm of all living organisms—both anaerobes and aerobes. It requires no oxygen and partially oxidises one 6-carbon glucose molecule into two 3-carbon molecules of pyruvic acid (pyruvate).

In plants, glucose comes from storage carbohydrates or from sucrose (the translocated end-product of photosynthesis), which is cleaved into glucose and fructose by the enzyme invertase. Both hexoses enter glycolysis after phosphorylation by hexokinase.

Energy Investment vs. Payoff Ledger (Per Glucose Molecule)

  • Investment Phase (2 ATP consumed): (1) Glucose → Glucose-6-phosphate (uses 1 ATP, catalysed by hexokinase); (2) Fructose-6-phosphate → Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (uses 1 ATP).
  • Cleavage: Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (6C) splits into two interconvertible 3C triose phosphates: 3-phosphoglyceraldehyde (PGAL) and dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP). Both proceed forward as 2 molecules of PGAL.
  • Redox Step (2 NADH + H⁺ formed): Each PGAL is oxidised and phosphorylated using inorganic phosphate (\(\mathrm{P_i}\)) to form 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (BPGA), reducing \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) to \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) (2 molecules per glucose).
  • Payoff Phase (4 ATP synthesised by substrate-level phosphorylation): (1) 2 × BPGA → 2 × 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA) yields 2 ATP; (2) 2 × Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) → 2 × Pyruvic acid yields 2 ATP.
Net Direct Yield of Glycolysis: \(4\text{ ATP produced} - 2\text{ ATP consumed} = \mathbf{2\text{ ATP}}\) (net) plus \(\mathbf{2\text{ NADH + H}^+}\) and \(\mathbf{2\text{ Pyruvate}}\).

पादपों में ग्लूकोज संचित कार्बोहाइड्रेट या सुक्रोज (प्रकाश-संश्लेषण का अंतिम उत्पाद) से प्राप्त होता है। इन्वर्टेज (invertase) एंजाइम सुक्रोज को ग्लूकोज और फ्रक्टोज में तोड़ता है, जो हेक्सोकाइनेज (hexokinase) द्वारा फॉस्फोरिलीकृत होकर ग्लाइकोलिसिस में प्रवेश करते हैं।

ऊर्जा निवेश बनाम ऊर्जा लाभ (प्रति ग्लूकोज अणु)

  • निवेश चरण (2 ATP का उपयोग): (1) ग्लूकोज → ग्लूकोज-6-फॉस्फेट (1 ATP व्यय, हेक्सोकाइनेज द्वारा); (2) फ्रक्टोज-6-फॉस्फेट → फ्रक्टोज-1,6-बिसफॉस्फेट (1 ATP व्यय)।
  • विदलन (Cleavage): फ्रक्टोज-1,6-बिसफॉस्फेट (6C) दो परस्पर परिवर्तनीय 3C ट्रायोज फॉस्फेट में टूटता है: 3-फॉस्फोग्लिसरेल्डिहाइड (PGAL) तथा डाइहाइड्रॉक्सी एसीटोन फॉस्फेट (DHAP)। दोनों आगे 2 × PGAL के रूप में बढ़ते हैं।
  • रेडॉक्स चरण (2 NADH + H⁺ का निर्माण): प्रत्येक PGAL का ऑक्सीकरण और अकार्बनिक फॉस्फेट (\(\mathrm{P_i}\)) के साथ फॉस्फोरिलीकरण होकर 1,3-बिसफॉस्फोग्लिसरेट (BPGA) बनता है तथा \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) से \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) बनता है।
  • लाभ चरण (क्रियाधार-स्तर फॉस्फोरिलीकरण द्वारा 4 ATP का निर्माण): (1) 2 × BPGA → 2 × 3-फॉस्फोग्लिसरिक अम्ल (PGA) से 2 ATP; (2) 2 × फॉस्फोइनोलपायरुवेट (PEP) → 2 × पायरुविक अम्ल से 2 ATP।
ग्लाइकोलिसिस का शुद्ध प्रत्यक्ष लाभ: \(4\text{ ATP निर्मित} - 2\text{ ATP प्रयुक्त} = \mathbf{2\text{ ATP}}\) (शुद्ध), साथ ही \(\mathbf{2\text{ NADH + H}^+}\) और \(\mathbf{2\text{ पायरुवेट}}\)।

4. Fermentation (Anaerobic Fate of Pyruvate) • किण्वन (पायरुवेट का अवायवीय भविष्य)

English

When oxygen is absent or limiting, cells cannot oxidise \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) via the mitochondrial electron transport chain. To keep glycolysis running, \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) must be regenerated by transferring electrons from \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) back onto pyruvate or its derivative:

  • Alcoholic Fermentation (e.g., Yeast): Pyruvic acid is first decarboxylated by pyruvic acid decarboxylase to acetaldehyde and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), and then reduced to ethanol by alcohol dehydrogenase using \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\).
    NEET Alert: Yeasts poison themselves to death when alcohol concentration reaches about 13%; therefore, naturally fermented beverages cannot exceed ~13% alcohol without distillation.
  • Lactic Acid Fermentation (e.g., certain bacteria, exercising vertebrate muscles): Pyruvic acid is directly reduced to lactic acid by lactate dehydrogenase using \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\). Note that no \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) is released here.

Energy Efficiency & Hazard: Less than 7% of the total energy in glucose is released during fermentation, and only a fraction is trapped as 2 net ATP per glucose. Moreover, the end-products (alcohol or acid) are toxic to the cell.

5. Aerobic Respiration: Link Reaction & Tricarboxylic Acid (Krebs') Cycle • वायवीय श्वसन: लिंक अभिक्रिया एवं क्रेब्स चक्र

English

In eukaryotes, complete oxidation of pyruvate takes place inside the mitochondria in two spatially separated events: (1) stepwise removal of all hydrogen atoms in the mitochondrial matrix (leaving 3 \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) per pyruvate), and (2) electron transfer to \(\mathrm{O_2}\) with ATP synthesis on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

5.1 Oxidative Decarboxylation (Link Reaction)

Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix and is converted into 2-carbon Acetyl-CoA by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, requiring \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\), and Coenzyme A (CoA):

\[\mathrm{Pyruvic\ acid + CoA + NAD^+ \xrightarrow[\text{Pyruvate dehydrogenase}]{\text{Mg}^{2+}} Acetyl\text{-}CoA + CO_2 + NADH + H^+}\]

5.2 The Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA / Krebs') Cycle

Elucidated by Hans Krebs, the cycle begins when Acetyl-CoA (2C) condenses with oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C) and water—catalysed by citrate synthase—to form citric acid (6C), releasing CoA. Citrate isomerises to isocitrate, followed by two successive decarboxylations yielding α-ketoglutaric acid (5C) and then succinyl-CoA (4C). Subsequent oxidation regenerates OAA to sustain the cycle.

  • Substrate-level phosphorylation: Conversion of succinyl-CoA to succinic acid generates 1 GTP, which in a coupled reaction converts ADP to 1 ATP.
  • Reducing equivalents per turn (per Acetyl-CoA): 3 \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) and 1 \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) (formed during oxidation of succinic acid to malic acid/fumarate).
\[\mathrm{Pyruvic\ acid + 4\,NAD^+ + FAD^+ + 2\,H_2O + ADP + P_i \xrightarrow{\text{Matrix}} 3\,CO_2 + 4\,NADH + 4\,H^+ + FADH_2 + ATP}\]

Note: Since one glucose yields two pyruvates, multiply every product above by 2 to get the per-glucose yield from the Link Reaction + TCA cycle: 6 \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), 8 \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), 2 \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\), and 2 ATP.

English

The energy stored in \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) and \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) is harvested by passing their electrons through four multiprotein complexes embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane, ultimately reducing molecular \(\mathrm{O_2}\) to \(\mathrm{H_2O}\):

Complex I NADH dehydrogenase (FMN, Fe–S) Oxidises \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\); transfers electrons to Ubiquinone (UQ); pumps \(4\mathrm{H^+}\) to intermembrane space.
Complex II Succinate dehydrogenase (FAD, Fe–S) Receives reducing equivalents from succinate (\(\mathrm{FADH_2}\)) and transfers electrons to UQ (no proton pumping).

Role of Oxygen & Chemiosmotic ATP Synthesis

Although \(\mathrm{O_2}\) enters only at the terminal step, it is indispensable because it acts as the final hydrogen (electron) acceptor—removing spent electrons and driving the entire chain forward. Proton pumping creates an electrochemical proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane. Protons flow back from the intermembrane space into the matrix through the integral membrane channel \(\mathrm{F_0}\), driving conformational catalysis at the peripheral \(\mathrm{F_1}\) headpiece:

  • \(4\,\mathrm{H^+}\) passing through \(\mathrm{F_0}\) synthesise 1 ATP.
  • Oxidation of 1 \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) yields 3 ATP; oxidation of 1 \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) yields 2 ATP.

8. Amphibolic Pathway & Respiratory Quotient (RQ) • एम्फीबोलिक पथ एवं श्वसन गुणांक (RQ)

English

8.1 Why Respiration is Amphibolic

Although respiration breaks down substrates (catabolism), its intermediates are simultaneously withdrawn to build new biomolecules (anabolism):

  • Fats: Hydrolysed to glycerol (enters as PGAL) and fatty acids (degraded to Acetyl-CoA). Conversely, when fatty acids are synthesised, Acetyl-CoA is withdrawn from the pathway.
  • Proteins: Hydrolysed to amino acids, which after deamination enter at pyruvate, Acetyl-CoA, or Krebs' cycle intermediates; conversely, these intermediates act as carbon skeletons for amino acid synthesis.

Because it participates in both breakdown and synthesis, the respiratory pathway is accurately termed an amphibolic pathway.

8.2 Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

\[\mathrm{RQ = \frac{Volume\ of\ CO_2\ evolved}{Volume\ of\ O_2\ consumed}}\]
  • Carbohydrates: \(\mathrm{RQ = 1.0}\) (equal volumes of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) evolved and \(\mathrm{O_2}\) consumed, e.g., \(6/6 = 1.0\) for glucose).
  • Fats: \(\mathrm{RQ < 1}\) (for tripalmitin, \(2\,\mathrm{C_{51}H_{98}O_6} + 145\,\mathrm{O_2} \rightarrow 102\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 98\,\mathrm{H_2O}\), so \(\mathrm{RQ = 102/145 = 0.7}\)).
  • Proteins: \(\mathrm{RQ \approx 0.9}\). In living organisms, mixed substrates are usually respired rather than pure fats or proteins.

9. Original Worked Examples • मौलिक हल किए गए उदाहरण

English

Worked Example 1 (Assumed Scenario — Stoichiometry of Sucrose Oxidation)

Suppose a non-green root parenchyma cell imports 3 molecules of sucrose (assumed hypothetical value) and oxidises them completely via aerobic respiration under standard idealized conditions. Calculate: (a) total pyruvate molecules formed, and (b) total net ATP yield.

Solution:

  1. Invertase hydrolyses 1 sucrose into 1 glucose + 1 fructose (2 hexose molecules). Therefore, 3 sucrose molecules yield \(3 \times 2 = 6\) hexose molecules.
  2. Each hexose produces 2 pyruvate molecules in glycolysis, so \(6 \times 2 = \mathbf{12\text{ pyruvate molecules}}\) are formed.
  3. Under idealized aerobic conditions, 1 hexose yields 38 net ATP. Thus, 6 hexoses yield \(6 \times 38 = \mathbf{228\text{ ATP}}\).

In an isolated mitochondrial preparation (hypothetical assay), 4 molecules of \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) and 3 molecules of \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) are completely oxidised through the ETS.   1. ATP from \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\): \(4 \times 3 = 12\text{ ATP}\).

11. Common Pitfalls & NEET Revision Checklist • सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावृत्ति सूची

English

Common Exam Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Gross vs. Net ATP in Glycolysis: 4 ATP are synthesised directly, but 2 ATP are consumed in the investment phase, leaving a net gain of 2 ATP.
  • Per Pyruvate vs. Per Glucose: Always check whether a question asks per pyruvate (1 turn of TCA: 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP) or per glucose (2 turns: 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 GTP).
  • Complex II vs. Proton Pumping: Complex II (succinate dehydrogenase) transfers electrons from \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) to UQ without pumping protons, which is why \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) yields 2 ATP instead of 3 ATP.
  • Site of \(\mathrm{F_0}\) and \(\mathrm{F_1}\): \(\mathrm{F_0}\) is the integral membrane channel; \(\mathrm{F_1}\) is the peripheral headpiece facing the matrix where ATP catalysis occurs.

12. Concise Final Recap • संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Plant cells exchange gases via stomata and lenticels and oxidise substrates stepwise to trap energy as ATP. Glycolysis (cytoplasm) splits glucose into 2 pyruvates (+2 net ATP, +2 NADH). Without \(\mathrm{O_2}\), fermentation regenerates \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) yielding only 2 net ATP. With \(\mathrm{O_2}\), pyruvate enters mitochondria for the link reaction and Krebs' cycle (matrix), generating NADH and \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) whose electrons pass through ETS (inner membrane) to \(\mathrm{O_2}\), driving chemiosmotic ATP synthesis (theoretical 38 ATP/glucose). Because its intermediates serve both catabolism and anabolism, respiration is amphibolic, and its substrate identity is reflected by the Respiratory Quotient (\(\mathrm{RQ} = 1.0\) for carbohydrates, \(0.7\) for fats, \(\approx 0.9\) for proteins).

पादप कोशिकाएँ रंध्रों और वातरंध्रों द्वारा गैस विनिमय करती हैं तथा क्रमिक ऑक्सीकरण द्वारा ATP के रूप में ऊर्जा संचित करती हैं। ग्लाइकोलिसिस (कोशिकाद्रव्य) ग्लूकोज को 2 पायरुवेट (+2 शुद्ध ATP, +2 NADH) में तोड़ता है। \(\mathrm{O_2}\) की अनुपस्थिति में किण्वन \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) का पुनर्जनन करता है (केवल 2 शुद्ध ATP)। \(\mathrm{O_2}\) की उपस्थिति में पायरुवेट माइटोकॉन्ड्रिया की आधात्री में लिंक अभिक्रिया व क्रेब्स चक्र से गुजरता है, और बने NADH व \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) आंतरिक झिल्ली के ETS द्वारा \(\mathrm{O_2}\) तक इलेक्ट्रॉन पहुँचाकर रसोपरासरणी विधि से ATP (सैद्धांतिक 38 ATP/ग्लूकोज) बनाते हैं। अपचय व उपचय दोनों में भाग लेने के कारण श्वसन एक एम्फीबोलिक पथ है, और श्वसन गुणांक (\(\mathrm{RQ}\): कार्बोहाइड्रेट = \(1.0\), वसा = \(0.7\), प्रोटीन \(\approx 0.9\)) प्रयुक्त क्रियाधार को दर्शाता है।
