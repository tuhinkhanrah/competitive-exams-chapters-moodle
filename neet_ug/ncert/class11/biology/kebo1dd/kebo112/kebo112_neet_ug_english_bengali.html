Target NEET Competencies

  • Fundamental Respiration: Understand cellular respiration as a catabolic and amphibolic enzyme-controlled process releasing energy via step-wise oxidation of respiratory substrates (carbohydrates, fats, organic acids, proteins).
  • Plant Gas Exchange: Comprehend the anatomical and physiological reasons why plants can respire without specialized respiratory organs (stomata, lenticels, loose parenchyma, low metabolic demand, close proximity to surface).
  • Glycolysis (EMP Pathway): Master all 10 enzymatic reactions occurring in the cytoplasm, noting specific steps where ATP is consumed (2 ATP) and generated (4 ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation), and where \(2\text{ NADH} + \text{H}^+\) are synthesized.
  • Fermentation: Distinguish alcoholic fermentation (pyruvate decarboxylase, alcohol dehydrogenase; 13% ethanol toxicity) from lactic acid fermentation (lactate dehydrogenase; muscles and bacteria) under anaerobic conditions.
  • Mitochondrial Oxidation (Link Reaction & Krebs Cycle): Trace pyruvate entry into mitochondrial matrix, oxidative decarboxylation via pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (\(\text{Mg}^{2+}\), \(\text{CoA}\), \(\text{NAD}^+\)), and the cyclic tricarboxylic acid (TCA) reactions producing \(3\text{ CO}_2\), \(4\text{ NADH} + \text{H}^+\), \(1\text{ FADH}_2\), and \(1\text{ GTP/ATP}\) per pyruvate.
  • ETS & Oxidative Phosphorylation: Delineate electron transport Complexes I through IV in the inner mitochondrial membrane, mobile electron carriers (ubiquinone, cytochrome \(c\)), terminal reduction of \(\text{O}_2\) to \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\), and chemiosmotic ATP synthesis by Complex V (\(\text{F}_0\)-\(\text{F}_1\) ATP synthase) requiring \(4\text{ H}^+\) translocated per ATP.
  • Respiratory Balance Sheet: Evaluate the theoretical yield of \(38\text{ ATP}\) per glucose, recognizing critical biological assumptions and limitations.
  • Amphibolic Nature & Respiratory Quotient (RQ): Conceptualize respiratory pathways as both catabolic and anabolic hubs, and calculate substrate-specific RQ values (carbohydrates = 1.0, fats/tripalmitin = 0.7, proteins \(\approx\) 0.9).

2. Cellular Respiration & Do Plants Breathe? | কোষীয় শ্বসন ও উদ্ভিদের গ্যাসীয় বিনিময়

English Content

Cellular Respiration and Substrates

All living organisms require energy to sustain metabolic activities, transport, growth, and reproduction. Photosynthesis by green plant tissues and cyanobacteria traps solar energy, synthesizing carbohydrates such as glucose, sucrose, and starch. However, non-green plant tissues (roots, mature woody stems, flowers) and heterotrophic organisms cannot photosynthesize and rely entirely on the translocation and oxidation of organic food molecules.

Cellular Respiration is defined as the enzyme-controlled, step-wise breakdown (oxidation) of the carbon-carbon (\(\text{C}-\text{C}\)) bonds of complex organic compounds within the living cell, leading to the release of energy and its conservation as chemical energy in the form of Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP).

The organic compounds oxidised during respiration are called respiratory substrates.

  • Carbohydrates (Glucose, Fructose, Sucrose, Starch): The most common and preferred respiratory substrates.
  • Fats (Lipids): Stored primarily in oil seeds; mobilized as fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Proteins: Respired under specific physiological states, such as starvation or senescence, after being cleaved into amino acids and deaminated.
  • Organic acids: Oxidised directly in specific plants under specialized conditions.

Key NEET Principle: Energy in respiratory substrates is never liberated in a single, explosive reaction, because the vast majority would be dissipated uselessly as heat. Instead, it is released via a tightly controlled series of small, enzyme-catalyzed steps, enabling efficient coupling to ATP synthesis.

Overall Complete Combustion of Glucose:
\[ \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy (trapped as ATP + Heat)} \]

Why Plants Get Along Without Specialized Respiratory Organs

Unlike animals which possess gills, lungs, or tracheal networks, plants lack dedicated organs for gaseous exchange. They fulfill their respiratory gas-exchange requirements via stomata (in leaves and young green stems) and lenticels (in bark and woody periderm). Plants succeed without respiratory organs due to three fundamental anatomical and physiological reasons:

  1. Autonomous Gas Exchange: Each plant organ (leaf, stem, root) takes care of its own gaseous exchange requirements. There is negligible inter-organ transport of gases.
  2. Extremely Low Respiratory Demand: Plants possess much lower metabolic and respiratory rates than animals. Large volumes of gases are exchanged only during daytime photosynthesis, during which oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)) is generated directly inside the photosynthetic cells by photolysis of water.
  3. Short Diffusion Distances: In plant architecture, living cells are situated immediately adjacent to the surface. In bulky woody stems and roots, thick inner tissues consist predominantly of dead mechanical cells (xylem vessels, fibers). The living cells form thin peripheral mantles beneath the bark, and loosely packed parenchyma cells provide an interconnected, continuous meshwork of air spaces.

কোষীয় শ্বসন ও শ্বসন বস্তু

সমস্ত জীবদেহে জীবন ধারণ, চলন, পরিবহণ ও জননের জন্য প্রয়োজনীয় শক্তি খাদ্যের জারণ থেকে উৎপন্ন হয়। সবুজ উদ্ভিদের ক্লোরোপ্লাস্টে সালোকসংশ্লেষের মাধ্যমে সৌরশক্তি শর্করা অণুর রাসায়নিক বন্ধনে আবদ্ধ হয়। কিন্তু উদ্ভিদের অবর্ণ বা সবুজহীন অংশসমূহ (মূল, কাষ্ঠল কাণ্ড ইত্যাদি) সালোকসংশ্লেষে অক্ষম হওয়ায় তাদের শ্বসনের জন্য পাতা থেকে পরিবাহিত খাদ্য উপাদানের ওপর নির্ভর করতে হয়।

কোষীয় শ্বসন (Cellular Respiration): যে জটিল উৎসেচক-নিয়ন্ত্রিত জৈবরাসায়নিক প্রক্রিয়ায় কোষের ভেতরে জটিল জৈব যৌগের কার্বন-কার্বন (\(\text{C}-\text{C}\)) বন্ধন ভেঙে জারিত হয়ে প্রচুর শক্তি ধাপে ধাপে নির্গত হয় এবং রাসায়নিক শক্তি হিসেবে ATP অণুতে আবদ্ধ হয়, তাকে কোষীয় শ্বসন বলে।

শ্বসনে যে সমস্ত জৈব যৌগ জারিত হয়, তাদের শ্বসন বস্তু (Respiratory Substrates) বলে। প্রধান শ্বসন বস্তু হলো কার্বোহাইড্রেট (গ্লুকোজ), তবে কিছু বিশেষ শারীরবৃত্তীয় পরিস্থিতিতে ফ্যাট, প্রোটিন এমনকি জৈব অ্যাসিডও শ্বসনে ব্যবহৃত হতে পারে।

উদ্ভিদে কেন কোনো নির্দিষ্ট শ্বসন অঙ্গ থাকে না?

প্রাণীদের মতো ফুসফুস বা ফুলকা না থাকা সত্ত্বেও উদ্ভিদ খুব দক্ষতার সাথে গ্যাসীয় বিনিময় ঘটায়। এর মূল শারীরবৃত্তীয় কারণ তিনটি:

  1. স্বনির্ভর গ্যাসীয় বিনিময়: উদ্ভিদের প্রতিটি অংশ (পাতা, কাণ্ড, মূল) নিজের গ্যাসীয় চাহিদা নিজেই মেটায়। উদ্ভিদের এক অংশ থেকে অন্য অংশে গ্যাসের পরিবহণ প্রায় ঘটে না বললেই চলে।
  2. কম শ্বসন চাহিদা: প্রাণীদের তুলনায় উদ্ভিদের বিভিন্ন অঙ্গের শ্বসনের হার অনেক কম। কেবল দিনের বেলায় সালোকসংশ্লেষের সময় বিপুল গ্যাস বিনিময় ঘটে, আর তখন কোষের ভেতরেই জল বিভাজনের মাধ্যমে প্রচুর \(\text{O}_2\) উৎপন্ন হয়।
  3. স্বল্প ব্যাপন দূরত্ব: উদ্ভিদের সমস্ত সজীব কোষ পৃষ্ঠতলের খুব কাছে অবস্থান করে। স্থূল কাষ্ঠল কাণ্ডে ভেতরের কোষগুলি অধিকাংশ মৃত এবং যান্ত্রিক দৃঢ়তা দেয়; কেবল বাকলের নিচে সজীব কোষের একটি পাতলা স্তর থাকে, যেখানে লেন্টিসেল ও প্যারেনকাইমার আন্তঃকোষীয় ফাঁক দিয়ে সরাসরি বাতাস চলাচল করে।

3. Glycolysis (EMP Pathway) | গ্লাইকোলাইসিস (EMP পথ)

English Content

Biochemical Scheme of Glycolysis

The term glycolysis is derived from Greek words: glycos (sugar) and lysis (splitting). It was elucidated by Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof, and J. Parnas, hence it is designated as the EMP pathway.

  • Intracellular Location: Cytoplasm (Cytosol).
  • Universal Occurrence: Operates in all living cells (prokaryotes, eukaryotes, aerobes, and anaerobes). In obligate anaerobes, it is the sole energy-yielding pathway.
  • Oxygen Requirement: Independent of molecular oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)). Does not consume \(\text{O}_2\) and does not evolve \(\text{CO}_2\).
  • Substrate Entry in Plants: Sucrose (end product of photosynthesis) is cleaved by the enzyme invertase into glucose and fructose, which readily enter glycolysis.
  • Key End Product: Partial oxidation of one \(6\text{-carbon}\) molecule of glucose generates two \(3\text{-carbon}\) molecules of pyruvic acid (pyruvate).

গ্লাইকোলাইসিসের বিক্রিয়া ও শক্তি লাভ

গ্রিক শব্দ glycos (চিনি) ও lysis (ভাঙন) থেকে গ্লাইকোলাইসিস শব্দের উৎপত্তি। তিন বিজ্ঞানী গুস্তাভ এম্বডেন, অটো মেয়ারহফ এবং জে. পারনাস এই পথ আবিষ্কার করায় একে EMP পথ বলা হয়।

  • স্থান: কোষের সাইটোপ্লাজম (Cytoplasm)।
  • সর্বজনীনতা: এটি সমস্ত জীবের (সবাত ও অবাত) কোষে ঘটে। অবাত জীবে এটিই একমাত্র শ্বসন পথ।
  • উদ্ভিদে প্রারম্ভিক বিক্রিয়া: সালোকসংশ্লেষের চূড়ান্ত উৎপাদ সুক্রোজকে ইনভার্টেজ (Invertase) উৎসেচক গ্লুকোজ ও ফ্রুক্টোজে ভেঙে দেয়, যা সহজে গ্লাইকোলাইসিসে প্রবেশ করে।
  • ATP ব্যয়: ধাপ ১ (গ্লুকোজ \(\to\) গ্লুকোজ-৬-ফসফেট) এবং ধাপ ৩ (ফ্রুক্টোজ-৬-ফসফেট \(\to\) ফ্রুক্টোজ-১,৬-বিসফসফেট)। মোট ২টি ATP অণু ব্যবহৃত হয়।
  • অক্সিডেশন / NADH উৎপাদন: ধাপ ৬-এ ৩-ফসফোগ্লিসারালডিহাইড (PGAL) জারিত হয়ে ১,৩-বিসফসফোগ্লিসারিক অ্যাসিডে (BPGA) পরিণত হওয়ার সময় \(2\text{ NADH} + 2\text{H}^+\) উৎপন্ন হয়।
  • সাবস্ট্রেট স্তরে ফসফোরাইলেশন (ATP লাভ): ধাপ ৭ (১,৩-BPGA \(\to\) ৩-PGA) এবং ধাপ ১০ (PEP \(\to\) পাইরুভিক অ্যাসিড)-এ মোট ৪টি ATP উৎপন্ন হয়।
  • মোট লাভ (Net Gain): তৈরি হওয়া ৪টি ATP থেকে ব্যবহৃত ২টি ATP বিয়োগ করলে প্রত্যক্ষ লাভ হয় ২টি ATP এবং এর সাথে \(2\text{ NADH} + 2\text{H}^+\)।

English Content

Metabolic Fates of Pyruvic Acid

Pyruvic acid is the pivotal junctional metabolite of glycolysis. Its subsequent utilization depends upon oxygen availability and organismal cellular machinery:

  1. Lactic Acid Fermentation: Anaerobic reduction of pyruvate into lactic acid without \(\text{CO}_2\) release.
  2. Alcoholic Fermentation: Anaerobic decarboxylation followed by reduction to ethanol and \(\text{CO}_2\).
  3. Aerobic Respiration: Complete oxidation via the Krebs cycle and oxidative phosphorylation in mitochondria in the presence of \(\text{O}_2\).

Alcoholic and Lactic Acid Fermentation Mechanisms

Fermentation accounts for only partial breakdown of glucose under anaerobic conditions:

  • Alcoholic Fermentation (Yeast & Microorganisms): Pyruvate is converted to acetaldehyde and \(\text{CO}_2\) by the enzyme pyruvic acid decarboxylase. Acetaldehyde is subsequently reduced to ethanol by alcohol dehydrogenase, with \(\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+\) being reoxidized to \(\text{NAD}^+\).
    \[ \text{Pyruvic acid} \xrightarrow[\text{Decarboxylase}]{\text{Pyruvate}} \text{Acetaldehyde} + \text{CO}_2 \xrightarrow[\text{Dehydrogenase, NADH}+\text{H}^+\to\text{NAD}^+]{\text{Alcohol}} \text{Ethanol} \]
    Crucial NEET Fact: Yeast cells poison themselves to death when the concentration of naturally accumulated alcohol reaches approximately 13%. Hence, beverages with higher alcohol concentrations (whisky, brandy, rum) must be produced via distillation rather than natural fermentation alone.
  • Lactic Acid Fermentation (Lactic Acid Bacteria & Animal Skeletal Muscle): In bacteria (e.g., Lactobacillus) and during strenuous muscular exercise in animals when oxygen supply is insufficient, pyruvate is reduced directly to lactic acid by lactate dehydrogenase. No \(\text{CO}_2\) is released.
    \[ \text{Pyruvic acid} + \text{NADH} + \text{H}^+ \xrightarrow{\text{Lactate Dehydrogenase}} \text{Lactic acid} + \text{NAD}^+ \]

Energetics of Fermentation

  • Low Energy Yield: Less than 7% of the total chemical energy trapped within a glucose molecule is liberated during fermentation, and only a fraction of that is conserved in high-energy bonds of ATP.
  • Net ATP Generated: Exactly \(2\text{ ATP}\) per glucose molecule (derived solely from glycolytic substrate-level phosphorylation). No additional ATP is formed during the terminal conversion of pyruvate to ethanol or lactate.
  • Role of Terminal Steps: The sole vital biochemical function of reducing pyruvate to ethanol or lactate is to regenerate \(\text{NAD}^+\) from \(\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+\), sustaining the operation of glycolysis in the absence of mitochondrial electron transport.
  • Rate of NADH Reoxidation: In fermentation, \(\text{NADH}\) is reoxidised to \(\text{NAD}^+\) rather slowly; by contrast, in aerobic respiration, reoxidation via the electron transport system is extremely vigorous.

5. Aerobic Respiration: Link Reaction & Krebs Cycle | সবাত শ্বসন: লিঙ্ক বিক্রিয়া ও ক্রেবস চক্র

English Content

Crucial Events of Aerobic Respiration

In eukaryotes, aerobic respiration requires molecular oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)) and unfolds inside the mitochondria. It involves two distinct biochemical phases:

  1. Matrix Phase: Complete oxidation of pyruvate via step-wise removal of hydrogen atoms, liberating \(3\text{ molecules of CO}_2\) per pyruvate.
  2. Inner Membrane Phase: Transfer of electrons stripped from hydrogen atoms to molecular oxygen, coupled to simultaneous ATP synthesis (Oxidative Phosphorylation).

The Link Reaction (Oxidative Decarboxylation of Pyruvate)

Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix from the cytosol and undergoes oxidative decarboxylation catalyzed by the multienzyme complex pyruvate dehydrogenase. This reaction requires \(\text{Mg}^{2+}\) and several cofactors including Coenzyme A (CoA) and \(\text{NAD}^+\).

\[ \text{Pyruvic acid} + \text{CoA} + \text{NAD}^+ \xrightarrow[\text{Pyruvate dehydrogenase}]{\text{Mg}^{2+}} \text{Acetyl CoA} + \text{CO}_2 + \text{NADH} + \text{H}^+ \] Per Glucose molecule (\(2\times \text{Pyruvate}\)): Yields \(2\text{ Acetyl CoA} + 2\text{ CO}_2 + 2\text{ NADH} + 2\text{H}^+\).

The Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) / Krebs Cycle

Elucidated by Hans Krebs, the cycle operates in the mitochondrial matrix:

  1. Condensation: The \(2\text{-carbon}\) acetyl group of acetyl CoA condenses with a \(4\text{-carbon}\) acceptor molecule, oxaloacetic acid (OAA), and water to produce \(6\text{-carbon}\) citric acid. The reaction is catalyzed by citrate synthase, releasing CoA.
  2. Isomerisation: Citric acid is isomerised to isocitrate.
  3. First Oxidative Decarboxylation: Isocitrate is oxidised and decarboxylated to produce \(5\text{-carbon}\) \(\alpha\)-ketoglutaric acid, yielding \(1\text{ CO}_2\) and \(1\text{ NADH} + \text{H}^+\).
  4. Second Oxidative Decarboxylation: \(\alpha\)-ketoglutaric acid undergoes oxidative decarboxylation to form \(4\text{-carbon}\) succinyl-CoA, releasing \(1\text{ CO}_2\) and producing \(1\text{ NADH} + \text{H}^+\).
  5. Substrate-Level Phosphorylation: Succinyl-CoA is cleaved to succinic acid by succinate thiokinase. This energy-releasing step is coupled to the synthesis of guanosine triphosphate (GTP) from GDP and Pi. In a coupled reaction, GTP transfers its terminal phosphate to ADP, forming ATP.
  6. Dehydrogenation to Fumarate: Succinic acid is oxidised to fumaric acid by succinate dehydrogenase (Complex II, bound to inner membrane), reducing \(\text{FAD}^+\) to \(\text{FADH}_2\).
  7. Hydration & Regeneration of OAA: Fumaric acid is hydrated to malic acid, which is then oxidised by malate dehydrogenase to regenerate oxaloacetic acid (OAA), generating the third \(\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+\) of the cycle.
Overall Equation for Mitochondrial Oxidation (Link Reaction + Krebs Cycle per Pyruvate):
\[ \text{Pyruvic acid} + 4\text{NAD}^+ + \text{FAD}^+ + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{ADP} + \text{Pi} \xrightarrow{\text{Matrix}} 3\text{CO}_2 + 4\text{NADH} + 4\text{H}^+ + \text{FADH}_2 + \text{ATP} \] Per Glucose molecule (\(2\times \text{Pyruvate}\)): Yields \(6\text{ CO}_2 + 8\text{ NADH} + 8\text{H}^+ + 2\text{ FADH}_2 + 2\text{ ATP/GTP}\).

    4. সাকসিনিক অ্যাসিড থেকে ফিউমারিক অ্যাসিড তৈরি হওয়ার সময় \(\text{FADH}_2\) উৎপন্ন হয় (উৎসেচক: সাকসিনেট ডিহাইড্রোজিনেজ)।
    5. ম্যালিক অ্যাসিড থেকে পুনরায় OAA পুনরুৎপাদনের সময় তৃতীয় \(\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+\) তৈরি হয়।
  • প্রতি অণু গ্লুকোজের জন্য (২টি পাইরুভেট) মাইটোকনড্রিয়ায় মোট উৎপন্ন হয়: \(6\text{ CO}_2\), \(8\text{ NADH} + 8\text{H}^+\), \(2\text{ FADH}_2\), এবং \(2\text{ ATP/GTP}\)।

6. Electron Transport System (ETS) & Oxidative Phosphorylation | ইলেকট্রন পরিবহন তন্ত্র ও জারণীয় ফসফোরাইলেশন

English Content

Architecture of the Inner Mitochondrial Membrane ETS

The energy conserved during glycolysis, the link reaction, and the Krebs cycle as \(\text{NADH} + \text{H}^+\) and \(\text{FADH}_2\) is released and utilized only when these coenzymes are oxidised through the Electron Transport System (ETS) embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons are transported through a series of specialized multi-protein complexes to molecular oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)), resulting in the reduction of \(\text{O}_2\) to \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\).

Oxidative Phosphorylation & The Chemiosmotic Mechanism

  • Role of Oxygen: Although aerobic respiration operates only in the presence of oxygen, the direct utilization of \(\text{O}_2\) is restricted exclusively to the terminal step (Complex IV). Nonetheless, oxygen is absolutely vital because it drives the continuous flow of electrons through all upstream complexes by acting as the final hydrogen/electron acceptor, yielding metabolic water.
  • Distinction from Photophosphorylation: In photophosphorylation, light energy creates the proton gradient. In oxidative phosphorylation, the chemical energy of redox oxidation-reduction reactions generates the proton gradient.
  • ATP Yield per Reducing Equivalent:
    • Oxidation of \(1\text{ molecule of NADH}\) yields \(3\text{ ATP}\).
    • Oxidation of \(1\text{ molecule of FADH}_2\) yields \(2\text{ ATP}\) (because electrons from \(\text{FADH}_2\) enter at Complex II, bypassing the proton pumping of Complex I).
  • Structure of Complex V (\(\text{F}_0\)-\(\text{F}_1\) ATP Synthase):
    • \(\text{F}_1\) Headpiece: A peripheral membrane protein complex facing the mitochondrial matrix; contains the catalytic site for ATP synthesis from ADP and Pi.
    • \(\text{F}_0\) Basepiece: An integral transmembrane protein channel through which protons translocate across the inner mitochondrial membrane down their electrochemical gradient.
    • Proton Stoichiometry: For each ATP molecule synthesized, \(4\text{ H}^+\) pass through the \(\text{F}_0\) channel from the intermembrane space into the matrix.

The 4 Mandatory Assumptions for 38 ATP Gain

  1. Sequential Order: A strict sequential, orderly metabolic pipeline operates without interruption: Glycolysis \(\to\) Link Reaction \(\to\) Krebs Cycle \(\to\) ETS.
  2. Cytosolic NADH Transfer: The \(\text{NADH}\) synthesized during cytosolic glycolysis is transferred into mitochondria without energy loss and undergoes complete oxidative phosphorylation.
  3. No Siphoning of Intermediates: None of the metabolic intermediates in the entire pathway are siphoned off or withdrawn to synthesize other cellular molecules.
  4. Exclusive Substrate: Only glucose serves as the respiratory substrate; no alternative substrates (fatty acids, amino acids) enter at intermediary junctions.
Why These Assumptions Fail in Living Cells: Living cells never operate as closed sequential pipelines. All pathways function concurrently. Substrates enter and intermediates are routinely withdrawn for anabolism. Enzymatic reaction rates are modulated dynamically by feedback inhibition and cellular ATP/ADP charge.

8. Amphibolic Pathway & Respiratory Quotient (RQ) | উভমুখী পথ ও শ্বসন অনুপাত (RQ)

English Content

The Amphibolic Nature of Respiration

Respiration has historically been described strictly as a catabolic pathway because it involves the breakdown of respiratory substrates into \(\text{CO}_2\) and \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\). However, this characterization is fundamentally incomplete.

Whenever the cell requires specific biomolecules, key intermediate metabolites of the respiratory pathway are withdrawn to serve as starting precursors for biosynthesis (anabolism):

  • Fats: When broken down for energy, fats yield glycerol (which enters as PGAL / DHAP) and fatty acids (which are cleaved into acetyl CoA). Conversely, when the cell synthesizes fatty acids, acetyl CoA is withdrawn from the respiratory pathway. When synthesizing glycerol, DHAP/PGAL is withdrawn.
  • Proteins: Proteins are hydrolyzed to amino acids and deaminated. Depending on carbon chain length, their carbon skeletons enter as pyruvate, acetyl CoA, or Krebs cycle intermediates (e.g., \(\alpha\)-ketoglutarate, oxaloacetate). Conversely, when synthesizing amino acids and proteins, \(\alpha\)-ketoglutaric acid and oxaloacetic acid are withdrawn to undergo transamination.

Because respiratory pathways simultaneously mediate both catabolism (breakdown) and anabolism (biosynthesis), the respiratory pathway is correctly designated as an amphibolic pathway rather than solely a catabolic one.

Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

During aerobic respiration, \(\text{O}_2\) is consumed and \(\text{CO}_2\) is released. The Respiratory Quotient (RQ), or respiratory ratio, is defined as the volume ratio of carbon dioxide evolved to oxygen consumed over a given period:

\[ \text{RQ} = \frac{\text{Volume of } \text{CO}_2 \text{ evolved}}{\text{Volume of } \text{O}_2 \text{ consumed}} \]

The precise numerical value of RQ depends entirely upon the chemical nature of the respiratory substrate being oxidised:

  • Carbohydrates (Glucose, Fructose): \(\text{RQ} = 1.0\)

    Carbohydrates are rich in oxygen atoms, requiring equimolar \(\text{O}_2\) consumption and \(\text{CO}_2\) evolution upon complete oxidation.

    \[ \text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy} \implies \text{RQ} = \frac{6\text{ CO}_2}{6\text{ O}_2} = 1.0 \]
  • Fats (Tripalmitin): \(\text{RQ} = 0.7\)

    Fats contain a very low proportion of oxygen relative to carbon and hydrogen. Consequently, far more external \(\text{O}_2\) is consumed to oxidize the hydrogen atoms into water.

    \[ 2(\text{C}_{51}\text{H}_{98}\text{O}_6) + 145\text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 102\text{CO}_2 + 98\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Energy} \implies \text{RQ} = \frac{102\text{ CO}_2}{145\text{ O}_2} \approx 0.7 \]
  • Proteins: \(\text{RQ} \approx 0.9\)

    When proteins are metabolized as respiratory substrates, the calculated RQ is approximately 0.9.

  • Crucial Ecological & Physiological Fact: In living organisms, respiratory substrates are rarely oxidised in isolation; pure proteins or pure fats are never used as sole respiratory substrates in living systems. Cells always respire mixtures of substrates.

Example 1: ATP and Reducing Power Generation Accounting

Problem: Calculate the total number of ATP molecules generated exclusively by substrate-level phosphorylation when 3 molecules of sucrose undergo complete aerobic respiration.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Sucrose Inversion: Each sucrose molecule yields 1 glucose + 1 fructose = 2 hexose units. For 3 sucrose molecules: \(3 \times 2 = 6\text{ hexose molecules (glucose equivalents)}\).
  2. Substrate-Level ATP in Glycolysis: Each hexose produces 4 ATP gross by substrate-level phosphorylation (2 at 1,3-BPGA \(\to\) 3-PGA, and 2 at PEP \(\to\) Pyruvate). Gross substrate-level ATP per hexose = 4 ATP. Net substrate-level ATP per hexose = \(4 - 2 = 2\text{ ATP}\).
  3. Substrate-Level ATP in Krebs Cycle: Each hexose yields 2 pyruvates \(\to\) 2 turns of Krebs cycle \(\to\) 2 GTP (equivalent to 2 ATP) via substrate-level phosphorylation (Succinyl-CoA \(\to\) Succinate).
  4. Total Substrate-Level ATP Generated:
    • Gross substrate-level ATP formed = \(6 \times (4\text{ in glycolysis} + 2\text{ in Krebs}) = 6 \times 6 = \mathbf{36\text{ ATP}}\).
    • Net substrate-level ATP gained = \(6 \times (2\text{ net in glycolysis} + 2\text{ in Krebs}) = 6 \times 4 = \mathbf{24\text{ ATP}}\).

Example 2: Mixed Substrate RQ Calculation

Problem: A germinating castor seed utilizes a respiratory substrate mixture consisting of 60% tripalmitin and 40% glucose by molar ratio. What will be the expected Respiratory Quotient (RQ) of this germinating seed tissue?

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Recall the individual balanced oxidation equations:
    • For Glucose: \(\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\text{O}_2 \to 6\text{CO}_2 + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}\). (Ratio: \(6\text{ CO}_2 / 6\text{ O}_2\)).
    • For Tripalmitin: \(2(\text{C}_{51}\text{H}_{98}\text{O}_6) + 145\text{O}_2 \to 102\text{CO}_2 + 98\text{H}_2\text{O}\). Per mole of tripalmitin: \(\text{O}_2\text{ consumed} = 72.5\), \(\text{CO}_2\text{ evolved} = 51\).
  2. For a mixture of \(0.6\text{ moles of Tripalmitin}\) and \(0.4\text{ moles of Glucose}\):
    • Total \(\text{O}_2\text{ consumed} = (0.6 \times 72.5) + (0.4 \times 6) = 43.5 + 2.4 = 45.9\text{ moles}\).
    • Total \(\text{CO}_2\text{ evolved} = (0.6 \times 51) + (0.4 \times 6) = 30.6 + 2.4 = 33.0\text{ moles}\).
  3. Calculate combined RQ: \[ \text{RQ}_{\text{mixture}} = \frac{33.0}{45.9} \approx \mathbf{0.719} \]
  4. Conclusion: Because fats demand substantial oxygen for hydrogen oxidation, the RQ of lipid-rich germinating seeds remains substantially below 1 (close to 0.72).

11. Common Pitfalls & High-Yield Revision Checklist | সাধারণ ভুলভ্রান্তি ও রিভিশন তালিকা

English Content

High-Risk Exam Traps (NEET Pitfalls)

Trap 1: Gross vs. Net ATP in Glycolysis: Many students confuse gross synthesis (\(4\text{ ATP}\)) with net gain (\(2\text{ ATP}\)). Read the question wording carefully: "synthesized directly" vs. "net yield".

Trap 2: Complex II Does Not Pump Protons: Succinate dehydrogenase (Complex II) does not translocate protons into the intermembrane space. This is precisely why \(1\text{ FADH}_2\) yields only \(2\text{ ATP}\), whereas \(1\text{ NADH}\) yields \(3\text{ ATP}\).

Trap 3: Decarboxylation in Fermentation: Remember that alcoholic fermentation produces \(\text{CO}_2\) (via pyruvate decarboxylase), but lactic acid fermentation produces NO \(\text{CO}_2\) (direct reduction by lactate dehydrogenase).

Trap 4: Cytochrome \(c\) Location: Cytochrome \(c\) is not an integral inner membrane protein; it is a small peripheral protein mobile on the outer surface of the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Trap 5: Protons per ATP in ATP Synthase: Note the NCERT chemiosmotic value: \(4\text{ H}^+\) pass through \(\text{F}_0\) from intermembrane space to matrix for each ATP produced.

Quick Revision Checklist

✓ Glycolysis location: Cytoplasm | Krebs cycle: Matrix | ETS: Inner mitochondrial membrane.
✓ Enzyme converting sucrose to glucose & fructose: Invertase.
✓ Substrate-level phosphorylation steps in glycolysis: 1,3-BPGA \(\to\) 3-PGA, and PEP \(\to\) Pyruvate.
✓ Substrate-level phosphorylation in Krebs cycle: Succinyl-CoA \(\to\) Succinate (GTP/ATP).
✓ Terminal electron acceptor of ETS: Molecular oxygen (\(\text{O}_2\)), forming \(\text{H}_2\text{O}\).
✓ Standard RQ values: Glucose = 1.0; Tripalmitin = 0.7; Proteins \(\approx\) 0.9.
✓ Lethal alcohol concentration in natural yeast fermentation: ~13%.

12. Concise Final Recap | অধ্যায়ের সারসংক্ষেপ

English Content

Cellular respiration is the foundational metabolic engine of life, coupling the stepwise oxidation of organic substrates to the generation of a transmembrane electrochemical proton gradient and ATP synthesis. Respiration operates via cytoplasmic glycolysis (EMP pathway, yielding \(2\text{ pyruvate} + 2\text{ ATP} + 2\text{ NADH}\)), followed anaerobically by fermentation (lactic acid or ethanol, yielding net \(2\text{ ATP}\) and regenerating \(\text{NAD}^+\)) or aerobically in mitochondria via the link reaction, the tricarboxylic acid (Krebs) cycle, and the electron transport system (ETS). In the ETS, electrons from \(10\text{ NADH}\) and \(2\text{ FADH}_2\) per glucose are relayed across Complexes I through IV to the terminal acceptor \(\text{O}_2\), powering the pumping of protons into the intermembrane space. Complex V (\(\text{F}_0\)-\(\text{F}_1\) ATP synthase) harnesses this gradient, producing a theoretical net gain of up to \(38\text{ ATP}\). Finally, because its intermediates serve as dynamic junctions for both macromolecular catabolism and anabolism, respiration is fundamentally an amphibolic pathway, with respiratory quotients reflecting the specific carbon-to-oxygen ratios of the oxidised substrates.

বাংলা বিষয়বস্তু

কোষীয় শ্বসন হলো জীবের প্রধান শক্তি উৎপাদনকারী বিপাকীয় প্রক্রিয়া, যেখানে বিভিন্ন শ্বসন বস্তুর ধাপে ধাপে জারণ ঘটিয়ে রাসায়নিক শক্তি ATP রূপে আবদ্ধ করা হয়। সাইটোপ্লাজমে সংঘটিত গ্লাইকোলাইসিসে গ্লুকোজ আংশিক জারিত হয়ে ২টি পাইরুভিক অ্যাসিড, ২টি ATP ও \(2\text{ NADH}\) তৈরি করে। অক্সিজেনের অনুপস্থিতিতে সন্ধান ঘটে, যেখানে নিট লাভ হয় মাত্র ২টি ATP এবং \(\text{NAD}^+\) পুনরুৎপাদিত হয়। অক্সিজেনের উপস্থিতিতে মাইটোকনড্রিয়ায় লিঙ্ক বিক্রিয়া ও ক্রেবস চক্রের মাধ্যমে পাইরুভেটের সম্পূর্ণ জারণ ঘটে এবং প্রচুর \(\text{NADH}\) ও \(\text{FADH}_2\) তৈরি হয়। মাইটোকনড্রিয়ার অন্তঃপর্দার ইলেকট্রন পরিবহন তন্ত্রে (ETS) এই বিজারিত কো-এনজাইমগুলি জারিত হয়ে প্রোটন নতিমাত্রা সৃষ্টি করে এবং এটিপি সিন্থেজের মাধ্যমে তাত্ত্বিকভাবে সর্বমোট ৩৮টি ATP তৈরি করে। পরিশেষে, শ্বসনের মধ্যবর্তী যৌগগুলি একই সাথে অণু ভাঙন ও নতুন জৈব অণু সংশ্লেষে ব্যবহৃত হওয়ায় এটি একটি নিখুঁত অ্যাম্ফিবলিক (Amphibolic) পথ, যার শ্বসন অনুপাত (RQ) ব্যবহৃত শ্বসন বস্তুর রাসায়নিক গঠন অনুযায়ী পরিবর্তিত হয়।
