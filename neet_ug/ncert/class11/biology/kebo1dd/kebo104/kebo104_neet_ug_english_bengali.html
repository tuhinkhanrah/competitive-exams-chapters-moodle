English

With over a million described animal species exhibiting vast diversity in form and habitat, systematic classification provides a logical framework to group organisms and assign positions to newly discovered taxa. For NEET UG, mastery of this chapter depends on connecting anatomical criteria (body plan, symmetry, germ layers, coelom) to phylum-level and class-level diagnostic traits and memorising key taxonomic exceptions.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish animals by levels of cellular organisation, body symmetry, embryonic germ layers, coelom type, segmentation, and presence of a notochord.
  • Compare all major non-chordate phyla from Porifera to Hemichordata using diagnostic structures and physiological systems.
  • Differentiate protochordates from vertebrates and classify vertebrate classes (Cyclostomata through Mammalia) by endoskeleton, heart chambers, thermoregulation, and reproduction.
  • Avoid high-frequency NEET traps involving exceptions (such as larval vs. adult symmetry in echinoderms, four-chambered heart in crocodiles, and oviparity in platypus).

1. Architectural Foundations of Animal Classification / প্রাণি শ্রেণিবিন্যাসের গঠনগত ভিত্তি

English

1.1 Levels of Organisation & Organ-System Complexity

Although every animal is multicellular, cells interact at four progressive grades of complexity:

  • Cellular level: Cells form loose aggregates with limited division of labour, without forming true tissues (seen in Porifera).
  • Tissue level: Cells performing a shared function are organised into coordinated tissues (seen in Coelenterata and Ctenophora).
  • Organ level: Different tissues assemble into specialised organs (seen in Platyhelminthes).
  • Organ-system level: Multiple organs cooperate to perform integrated physiological functions (seen from Aschelminthes through Chordata).

Organ systems also vary in design. An incomplete digestive system has a single opening acting as both mouth and anus (as in coelenterates, ctenophores, and flatworms), whereas a complete digestive system has separate mouth and anus (from roundworms onward). Similarly, circulation is either open type—where blood is pumped into body spaces directly bathing tissues (e.g., arthropods, most molluscs, hemichordates)—or closed type—where blood remains confined within vessels of varying diameters (e.g., annelids, chordates).

1.2 Body Symmetry

  • Asymmetry: No plane passing through the centre yields two equal halves (characteristic of most sponges).
  • Radial symmetry: Any plane passing through the central longitudinal axis divides the organism into two identical halves (e.g., coelenterates, ctenophores, and adult echinoderms).
  • Bilateral symmetry: Only one specific sagittal plane divides the body into mirror-image left and right halves (e.g., platyhelminthes through chordates, excluding adult echinoderms).

1.3 Embryonic Germ Layers & Coelom

Animals whose embryos develop two germ layers—an outer ectoderm and an inner endoderm separated by a non-cellular mesoglea—are diploblastic (coelenterates and ctenophores). Animals possessing a third middle layer, the mesoderm, between ectoderm and endoderm are triploblastic (platyhelminthes to chordates).

The nature of the cavity between the body wall and gut wall defines three coelomic plans:

  • Acoelomates: No body cavity exists; the space is packed with mesodermal tissue (e.g., Platyhelminthes).
  • Pseudocoelomates: A body cavity is present, but it is not lined continuously by mesoderm; instead, mesoderm persists as scattered pouches between ectoderm and endoderm (e.g., Aschelminthes).
  • Coelomates (Eucoelomates): A true body cavity lined completely by mesoderm (e.g., Annelida through Chordata).

1.4 Segmentation & Notochord

Metameric segmentation (metamerism) is the serial repetition of external and internal body segments containing repeated organs, classically observed in earthworms (Annelida) and also present in Arthropoda and Chordata. The notochord is a mesoderm-derived, dorsal rod-like structure formed during embryonic development; its presence separates Chordates from Non-chordates (Porifera to Hemichordata).

2. Non-Chordate Phyla: Diagnostic Traits & Comparisons / অমেরুদণ্ডী পর্বসমূহ: স্বতন্ত্র বৈশিষ্ট্য ও তুলনা

English

2.1 Primitive Aquatic Phyla (Porifera, Coelenterata, Ctenophora)

  • Porifera (Sponges): Mostly marine, asymmetrical, cellular level. Water enters via ostia into the spongocoel and exits via the osculum. Flagellated choanocytes (collar cells) line the spongocoel and canals. Intracellular digestion; skeleton of calcareous/siliceous spicules or spongin fibres; hermaphrodite, internal fertilisation, indirect development. Examples: Sycon (Scypha), Spongilla (freshwater sponge), Euspongia (bath sponge).
  • Coelenterata (Cnidaria): Aquatic, mostly marine, radial, diploblastic, tissue level. Stinging cnidoblasts/cnidocytes (containing nematocysts) aid anchorage, defence, and prey capture. Central gastro-vascular cavity opens via a single mouth on the hypostome; digestion is both extracellular and intracellular. Corals secrete a calcium carbonate skeleton. Two body forms exist: sessile cylindrical polyp (Hydra, Adamsia) and free-swimming umbrella-shaped medusa (Aurelia). Species having both forms show alternation of generations (metagenesis): polyps produce medusae asexually, and medusae form polyps sexually (e.g., Obelia). Other examples: Physalia (Portuguese man-of-war), Pennatula (sea-pen), Gorgonia (sea-fan), Meandrina (brain coral).
  • Ctenophora (Sea walnuts / Comb jellies): Exclusively marine, radial, diploblastic, tissue level. Locomotion is powered by eight external rows of ciliated comb plates. Marked bioluminescence; digestion extracellular and intracellular; hermaphrodite; reproduction is strictly sexual with external fertilisation and indirect development. Examples: Pleurobrachia, Ctenoplana.

2.2 Worm Phyla (Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida)

  • Platyhelminthes (Flatworms): Dorso-ventrally flattened, bilateral, triploblastic, acoelomate, organ level. Mostly endoparasites with hooks and suckers (some absorb nutrients directly across the body surface). Flame cells perform osmoregulation and excretion. Hermaphrodite, internal fertilisation, multiple larval stages; Planaria exhibits high regeneration capacity. Examples: Taenia (tapeworm), Fasciola (liver fluke).
  • Aschelminthes (Roundworms): Circular in cross-section, bilateral, triploblastic, pseudocoelomate, organ-system level. Complete alimentary canal with a muscular pharynx. Excretory tube removes waste via an excretory pore. Dioecious (females often longer than males), internal fertilisation, direct or indirect development. Examples: Ascaris, Wuchereria (filarial worm), Ancylostoma (hookworm).
  • Annelida: Bilateral, triploblastic, true coelomate, metamerically segmented, closed circulatory system. Longitudinal and circular muscles aid movement; aquatic Nereis also bears lateral parapodia for swimming. Nephridia handle osmoregulation and excretion. Paired ganglia connect to a double ventral nerve cord. Nereis is dioecious, whereas earthworms (Pheretima) and leeches (Hirudinaria) are monoecious.

2.3 Advanced Coelomate Non-Chordates (Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Hemichordata)

  • Arthropoda: Largest phylum (over two-thirds of all named species). Chitinous exoskeleton, body divided into head, thorax, and abdomen, jointed appendages. Open circulation; respiration via gills, book gills, book lungs, or tracheal system; excretion via Malpighian tubules; sensory antennae, simple/compound eyes, and statocysts (balancing organs). Mostly dioecious and oviparous. Examples: Apis, Bombyx, Laccifer (economically useful); Anopheles, Culex, Aedes (vectors); Locusta (gregarious pest); Limulus (king crab, a living fossil).
  • Mollusca: Second largest phylum. Unsegmented soft body covered by a calcareous shell, differentiated into head, muscular foot, and visceral hump. A spongy mantle covers the hump, enclosing a mantle cavity with feather-like gills (respiratory and excretory). Mouth contains a rasping radula. Usually dioecious, oviparous, indirect development. Examples: Pila (apple snail), Pinctada (pearl oyster), Sepia (cuttlefish), Loligo (squid), Octopus (devil fish), Aplysia (sea-hare), Dentalium (tusk shell), Chaetopleura (chiton).
  • Echinodermata: Exclusively marine; endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles (spiny bodied). Adults are radially symmetrical, whereas larvae are bilaterally symmetrical. Complete digestive tract (ventral mouth, dorsal anus). Diagnostic water vascular system assists locomotion, food capture/transport, and respiration. Excretory system is absent. Sexes separate, external fertilisation, indirect development. Examples: Asterias (starfish), Echinus (sea urchin), Antedon (sea lily), Cucumaria (sea cucumber), Ophiura (brittle star).
  • Hemichordata: Now placed as a separate non-chordate phylum (formerly a subphylum of Chordata). Worm-like marine animals with anterior proboscis, collar (housing a rudimentary stomochord, similar to a notochord), and long trunk. Open circulation, gill respiration, excretion via proboscis gland, external fertilisation, indirect development. Examples: Balanoglossus, Saccoglossus.

২.১ আদিম জলজ পর্বসমূহ (পরিফেরা, সিলেনটারেটা, টিনোফোরা)

  • পরিফেরা (স্পঞ্জ): অধিকাংশই সামুদ্রিক, অপ্রতিসম, কোষীয় মাত্রার। দেহপ্রাচীরের অস্টিয়া (ostia) ছিদ্রপথে জল কেন্দ্রীয় স্পঞ্জোসিল (spongocoel) গহ্বরে প্রবেশ করে এবং অসকুলাম (osculum) দিয়ে বেরিয়ে যায়। ফ্ল্যাজেলাযুক্ত কোয়ানোসাইট বা কলার কোষ এই নালিতন্ত্রকে আবৃত রাখে। অন্তঃকোষীয় পরিপাক; স্পিকিউল বা স্পঞ্জিন তন্তুর কঙ্কাল; উভলিঙ্গ, অন্তঃনিষেক ও পরোক্ষ পরিস্ফুটন। উদাহরণ: Sycon, Spongilla (মিষ্টি জলের স্পঞ্জ), Euspongia (বাথ স্পঞ্জ)।

3.1 Fundamental Chordate Triad & Subphyla

Chordates are bilaterally symmetrical, triploblastic, coelomate animals characterised by a notochord, a dorsal hollow single nerve cord, and paired pharyngeal gill slits, along with a post-anal tail and a closed circulatory system.

Phylum Chordata comprises three subphyla:

  • Urochordata (Tunicata): Exclusively marine protochordate; notochord occurs only in the larval tail (e.g., Ascidia, Salpa, Doliolum).
  • Cephalochordata: Exclusively marine protochordate; notochord extends from head to tail and persists throughout life (e.g., Branchiostoma / Amphioxus).
  • Vertebrata: Notochord is present during the embryonic period and is replaced by a cartilaginous or bony vertebral column in the adult. Hence, all vertebrates are chordates, but all chordates are not vertebrates. Vertebrates also possess a ventral muscular heart (2, 3, or 4 chambers), kidneys for excretion/osmoregulation, and paired appendages (fins or limbs).

3.2 Classification of Vertebrata

Vertebrata is divided into Agnatha (jawless; Class Cyclostomata) and Gnathostomata (jawed; Superclasses Pisces and Tetrapoda).

  • Class Cyclostomata (Agnatha): Ectoparasites on fishes; elongated, scaleless body lacking paired fins; circular sucking mouth without jaws; 6–15 pairs of gill slits; cartilaginous cranium and vertebral column; closed circulation. Marine adults migrate to freshwater for spawning, die shortly after, and metamorphosed larvae return to the ocean (e.g., Petromyzon [lamprey], Myxine [hagfish]).
  • Class Chondrichthyes (Cartilaginous fishes): Marine, streamlined, cartilaginous endoskeleton, ventral mouth, persistent notochord, separate gill slits without operculum, tough skin with minute placoid scales (teeth are modified backwardly directed placoid scales). Air bladder is absent, forcing constant swimming to avoid sinking. Two-chambered heart, poikilothermous, internal fertilisation, males have pelvic claspers, many are viviparous. Examples: Scoliodon (dogfish), Pristis (sawfish), Carcharodon (great white shark), Trygon (sting ray with poison sting), Torpedo (electric organs).
  • Class Osteichthyes (Bony fishes): Marine and freshwater, bony endoskeleton, terminal mouth, 4 pairs of gills covered by an operculum, cycloid/ctenoid scales, air bladder present for buoyancy. Two-chambered heart, poikilothermous, external fertilisation, mostly oviparous, direct development. Examples: Marine—Exocoetus (flying fish), Hippocampus (sea horse); Freshwater—Labeo (rohu), Catla, Clarias (magur); Aquarium—Betta (fighting fish), Pterophyllum (angel fish).
  • Class Amphibia: Dual aquatic and terrestrial life; moist scaleless skin; eyelids; tympanum represents ear; cloaca receives alimentary, urinary, and reproductive tracts; respiration via gills, lungs, and skin; three-chambered heart (2 auricles, 1 ventricle); poikilothermous; external fertilisation, oviparous, indirect development. Examples: Bufo (toad), Rana (frog), Hyla (tree frog), Salamandra, Ichthyophis (limbless amphibian).
  • Class Reptilia: Creeping/crawling locomotion; dry cornified skin with epidermal scales or scutes; tympanum represents ear (no external ear opening); poikilothermous; internal fertilisation, oviparous, direct development. Heart is usually three-chambered, except in crocodiles (four-chambered). Snakes and lizards shed scales as skin cast. Examples: Chelone (turtle), Testudo (tortoise), Chameleon, Calotes, Crocodilus, Alligator, Hemidactylus; poisonous snakes—Naja (cobra), Bungarus (krait), Vipera.
  • Class Aves: Feathers, beak, forelimbs modified into wings, hind limbs with scales; dry skin without glands except the oil gland at the tail base; fully ossified pneumatic bones (hollow with air cavities); digestive tract has crop and gizzard; four-chambered heart; homoiothermous (warm-blooded); lungs supplemented by air sacs; internal fertilisation, oviparous, direct development. Examples: Corvus, Columba, Psittacula, Struthio (flightless ostrich), Pavo, Aptenodytes (penguin), Neophron (vulture).
  • Class Mammalia: Diagnostic mammary glands and hair on skin; external ears (pinnae); heterodont dentition (different types of teeth); four-chambered heart; homoiothermous; pulmonary respiration; internal fertilisation, direct development. Mostly viviparous, with the key oviparous exception Ornithorhynchus (platypus). Viviparous examples: Macropus (kangaroo), Pteropus (flying fox), Camelus, Macaca, Rattus, Canis, Felis, Elephas, Equus, Delphinus (dolphin), Balaenoptera (blue whale), Panthera tigris, Panthera leo.

  • শ্রেণি সাইক্লোস্টোমাটা (অ্যাগনাথা): মাছেদের দেহে বহিঃপরজীবী; লম্বা, আঁশবিহীন ও জোড় পাখনাবিহীন দেহ; চোয়ালহীন গোলাকার চোষক মুখ; ৬–১৫ জোড়া ফুলকা রন্ধ্র; তরুণাস্থিময় করোটি ও মেরুদণ্ড। সামুদ্রিক হলেও ডিম পাড়ার জন্য মিষ্টি জলে আসে, ডিম পাড়ার পর মারা যায় এবং লার্ভা রূপান্তরিত হয়ে সমুদ্রে ফিরে যায় (যেমন: Petromyzon, Myxine)।
  • শ্রেণি কনড্রিকথিস (তরুণাস্থিময় মাছ): সামুদ্রিক, মাকুর মতো দেহ, তরুণাস্থিময় অন্তঃকঙ্কাল, অঙ্কীয় মুখ, সারা জীবন স্থায়ী নোটোকর্ড, অপারকুলামবিহীন পৃথক ফুলকা রন্ধ্র, প্ল্যাকয়েড আঁশ (দাঁত পরিবর্তিত প্ল্যাকয়েড আঁশ)। পটকা (air bladder) অনুপস্থিত হওয়ায় ডুবে যাওয়া এড়াতে ক্রমাগত সাঁতার কাটতে হয়। ২-প্রকোষ্ঠযুক্ত হৃৎপিণ্ড, শীতল রক্তের, অন্তঃনিষেক, পুরুষের শ্রোণি পাখনায় ক্ল্যাসপার (claspers) থাকে। উদাহরণ: Scoliodon, Pristis, Carcharodon, Trygon (বিষাক্ত কাঁটা), Torpedo (বৈদ্যুতিক অঙ্গ)।

4. Worked Examples / মৌলিক সমাধানকৃত উদাহরণ

English

Worked Example 1 (Hypothetical Field Discovery)

Scenario: Suppose marine biologists collect a hypothetical unsegmented, soft-bodied animal from a rocky tide pool. Dissection reveals a true coelom, bilateral symmetry, a calcareous outer shell, a rasping feeding organ in the mouth, and feather-like gills housed in a cavity beneath a spongy skin fold.

Taxonomic Reasoning:

  1. True coelom + bilateral symmetry + unsegmented soft body: Narrows the candidate to Phylum Mollusca (unlike Annelida or Arthropoda, which are segmented).
  2. Calcareous shell + spongy skin fold (mantle) + feather-like gills in mantle cavity + rasping organ (radula): Uniquely confirms Phylum Mollusca.

Worked Example 2 (Hypothetical Comparative Anatomy)

Scenario: Two hypothetical marine vertebrates, Specimen X and Specimen Y, both possess a two-chambered heart and gills. Specimen X sinks whenever it stops swimming and has backwardly directed tooth-like scales without gill covers. Specimen Y hovers effortlessly at mid-depth and has four pairs of gills shielded by an operculum.

Taxonomic Reasoning:

  1. Both belong to Superclass Pisces (paired fins, 2-chambered heart, gills).
  2. Specimen X lacks an air bladder (forcing constant swimming) and lacks an operculum, with placoid scales → Class Chondrichthyes.
  3. Specimen Y possesses an air bladder for buoyancy and an operculum covering four pairs of gills → Class Osteichthyes.

সমাধানকৃত উদাহরণ ১ (কাল্পনিক ক্ষেত্র-পর্যবেক্ষণ)

পরিস্থিতি: মনে করো সামুদ্রিক জীববিজ্ঞানীরা উপকূলীয় জলাশয় থেকে একটি কাল্পনিক অখণ্ডিত, নরম দেহবিশিষ্ট প্রাণী সংগ্রহ করলেন। ব্যবচ্ছেদে দেখা গেল প্রাণীটি দ্বিপার্শ্বীয় প্রতিসম ও প্রকৃত সিলোমেট; এর দেহে চুনজাতীয় বহিঃখোলক, মুখে খাদ্য ঘষার জন্য একটি রেতিসদৃশ অঙ্গ এবং নরম চর্মভাজের নিচের গহ্বরে পালকের মতো ফুলকা রয়েছে।

শ্রেণিবিন্যাসগত যুক্তি:

  1. প্রকৃত সিলোম + দ্বিপার্শ্বীয় প্রতিসাম্য + অখণ্ডিত নরম দেহ: এটি অ্যানেলিডা বা আর্থ্রোপোডা নয় (কারণ তারা খণ্ডকায়িত), বরং মোলাস্কা পর্বের দিকে ইঙ্গিত করে।
  2. চুনজাতীয় খোলক + ম্যান্টল পর্দা + পালকের মতো ফুলকা + র‍্যাডুলা: এই সমস্ত স্বতন্ত্র বৈশিষ্ট্য নিশ্চিতভাবে মোলাস্কা (Mollusca) পর্বকে নির্দেশ করে।

সমাধানকৃত উদাহরণ ২ (কাল্পনিক তুলনামূলক শারীরস্থান)

পরিস্থিতি: দুটি কাল্পনিক সামুদ্রিক মেরুদণ্ডী প্রাণী—নমুনা X ও নমুনা Y—উভয়েরই ২-প্রকোষ্ঠযুক্ত হৃৎপিণ্ড ও ফুলকা রয়েছে। নমুনা X সাঁতার থামালেই ডুবে যায় এবং এর দেহে কানকোবিহীন ফুলকা রন্ধ্র ও পশ্চাৎমুখী দাঁতসদৃশ আঁশ রয়েছে। নমুনা Y জলের মাঝামাঝি স্থিরভাবে ভেসে থাকতে পারে এবং এর ৪ জোড়া ফুলকা অপারকুলাম দ্বারা ঢাকা।

শ্রেণিবিন্যাসগত যুক্তি:

  1. উভয়ই অধিশ্রেণি পিসেস-এর অন্তর্ভুক্ত।
  2. নমুনা X-এ পটকা ও অপারকুলাম অনুপস্থিত এবং প্ল্যাকয়েড আঁশ বিদ্যমান → শ্রেণি কনড্রিকথিস।
  3. নমুনা Y-এ প্লবতার জন্য পটকা এবং ৪ জোড়া ফুলকার ওপর অপারকুলাম উপস্থিত → শ্রেণি অস্টিকথিস।

Which of the following combinations correctly matches an animal phylum with its germ layer status, coelom type, and excretory structure?

7. Concise Final Recap / সংক্ষিপ্ত চূড়ান্ত সারসংক্ষেপ

English

Animal classification organises Kingdom Animalia along a clear evolutionary gradient: from cellular asymmetry (Porifera) and diploblastic radial tissue plans (Coelenterata, Ctenophora) to triploblastic bilateral organ systems progressing from acoelomates (Platyhelminthes) and pseudocoelomates (Aschelminthes) to true coelomates (Annelida to Chordata). Chordates are unified by a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, and pharyngeal gill slits, branching into protochordates and vertebrates (jawless Cyclostomata and jawed Pisces and Tetrapoda).

প্রাণিজগতের শ্রেণিবিন্যাস একটি সুস্পষ্ট বিবর্তনীয় ক্রম অনুসরণ করে: কোষীয় অপ্রতিসম দেহ (পরিফেরা) ও দ্বিস্তরী অরীয় কলাস্তর (সিলেনটারেটা, টিনোফোরা) থেকে শুরু করে ত্রিস্তরী দ্বিপার্শ্বীয় অঙ্গ-তন্ত্র স্তরে উন্নীত হয়—যা অ্যাসিলোমেট (প্ল্যাটিহেলমিনথেস) ও সিউডোসিলোমেট (অ্যাস্কেহেলমিনথেস) হয়ে প্রকৃত সিলোমেট (অ্যানেলিডা থেকে কর্ডাটা) পর্যন্ত বিস্তৃত। নোটোকর্ড, পৃষ্ঠীয় ফাঁপা স্নায়ুরজ্জু ও গলবিলীয় ফুলকা রন্ধ্র কর্ডাটদের একত্রিত করে, যা প্রোটোকর্ডাট এবং ভার্টিব্রেটায় (চোয়ালবিহীন সাইক্লোস্টোমাটা এবং চোয়ালযুক্ত পিসেস ও টেট্রাপোডা) বিভক্ত।
