Unit Foundation: Reductionism versus Systems Biology

Historically, biological investigation relied heavily on a reductionist approach—breaking down complex life forms into isolated physico-chemical models, surviving tissue slices, and cell-free biochemical extracts. While this yielded profound breakthroughs in molecular biology, biochemistry, and biophysics, modern physiology recognizes that neither a purely reductionist nor a purely organismic view can fully capture living phenomena.

Under systems biology, all living phenomena are understood as emergent properties arising from dynamic interactions among molecular regulatory networks, supramolecular complexes, organelles, cells, tissues, organs, and entire organisms. In human respiration, molecular kinetics (such as carbonic anhydrase catalysis and haemoglobin allostery) integrate seamlessly with biomechanical forces (such as pleural mechanics and thoracic expansion) to sustain systemic homeostasis.

Historical Pioneer in Human Physiology: Alfonso Corti (1822 – 1888)

Alfonso Corti was an eminent Italian anatomist born in 1822. Corti initiated his scientific investigations by examining the cardiovascular systems of reptiles. He subsequently shifted his anatomical focus to the mammalian auditory apparatus. In 1851, Corti published his seminal discovery describing a specialised neuroepithelial receptor organ situated upon the basilar membrane of the cochlea. This structure, rich in mechanical hair cells that transduce acoustic fluid vibrations into sensory nerve impulses, was permanently designated as the organ of Corti. Corti died in 1888, leaving an indelible foundation in sensory and microscopic physiology.

1. Diversity of Respiratory Organs across Animal Phyla

Cells continuously require oxygen (\(O_2\)) to catabolize nutrient molecules such as glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids to liberate metabolic energy (ATP). Concurrently, catabolism produces carbon dioxide (\(CO_2\)), an acidic and cytotoxic byproduct that must be swiftly removed. The exchange of atmospheric \(O_2\) for cellular \(CO_2\) constitutes breathing, commonly termed respiration.

Respiratory mechanisms vary across the animal kingdom depending primarily on habitat (aquatic vs. terrestrial) and level of body organisation (cellular, tissue, organ, or organ-system):

  • Lower Invertebrates (Sponges, Coelenterates, Flatworms): Lack specialised organs; exchange \(O_2\) and \(CO_2\) by simple diffusion across their entire moist body surface.
  • Earthworms (Annelids): Utilize their moist, vascularized cuticle for cutaneous gas exchange.
  • Insects (Arthropods): Possess an intricate network of internal air-conducting tracheal tubes that directly deliver atmospheric air to individual tissue cells through spiracles.
  • Aquatic Arthropods and Molluscs: Employ vascularised external or internal feathery structures called gills (branchial respiration).
  • Fishes (Aquatic Vertebrates): Rely entirely on vascularised internal gills for gas exchange in water.
  • Amphibians (e.g., Frogs): Exhibit dual modes: they can respire through their highly vascular moist skin (cutaneous respiration) as well as vascular bags called lungs (pulmonary respiration).
  • Reptiles, Birds, and Mammals (Terrestrial Vertebrates): Possess well-developed, highly vascularised bags called lungs for pulmonary respiration.

2. Human Respiratory System: Structural Organization

The human respiratory system comprises an air-conduction conduit leading into specialized pulmonary exchange parenchyma:

  • External Nostrils & Nasal Chambers: Atmospheric air enters through paired external nostrils situated above the upper lips, traversing the nasal passage into the nasal chamber.
  • Pharynx: The nasal chamber opens into the pharynx, a fibromuscular vestibule acting as the common passage for both ingested food and inhaled air.
  • Larynx & Epiglottis: The pharynx communicates through the glottis into the trachea via the larynx. The larynx is a specialized cartilaginous box facilitating sound generation and phonation, termed the sound box. During deglutition (swallowing), the glottis is sealed by a thin, flexible, elastic cartilaginous flap called the epiglottis, preventing accidental aspiration of food boluses into the tracheal lumen.
  • Trachea: A patent, straight cylindrical tube extending through the mid-thoracic cavity. At the anatomical level of the 5th thoracic vertebra (T5), the trachea bifurcates into the right and left primary bronchi.
  • Bronchial Arborization: Each primary bronchus enters its respective lung and undergoes repeated dichotomous branching into secondary (lobar) bronchi, tertiary (segmental) bronchi, and progressively smaller bronchioles, culminating in extremely delicate terminal bronchioles.
  • Cartilaginous Skeletal Support: To prevent mechanical collapse during ventilatory pressure fluctuations, the trachea, primary, secondary, and tertiary bronchi, and initial bronchioles are structurally fortified with incomplete C-shaped cartilaginous rings.
  • Alveolar Exchange Units: Every terminal bronchiole terminates in multiple thin, irregular-walled, and highly vascularized bag-like micro-chambers known as alveoli. The combined branching arbor of bronchi, bronchioles, and terminal alveoli forms the parenchyma of the lungs.

Functional Division of the Respiratory Tract

Functionally, the respiratory system is demarcated into two non-overlapping physiological zones:

  1. Conducting Part (External Nostrils up to Terminal Bronchioles): Performs four indispensable conditioning duties:
    • Conducts atmospheric air from the environment to the alveolar air sacs.
    • Purifies inhaled air by trapping dust, particulates, and inhaled foreign microbes via mucociliary filtration.
    • Humidifies dry atmospheric air with water vapor.
    • Thermoregulates the incoming air, equilibrating its temperature with internal core body temperature.
  2. Respiratory / Exchange Part (Alveoli and Alveolar Ducts): Represents the true functional diffusion site where rapid, gradient-dependent exchange of \(O_2\) and \(CO_2\) takes place between the alveolar gas space and the surrounding capillary bloodstream.

Pleural Envelopes and the Airtight Thoracic Chamber

Humans possess two lungs situated within the thoracic chamber, each encapsulated by a continuous, double-layered pleural membrane:

  • Outer Parietal Pleura: Lies in intimate anatomical contact with the internal thoracic cavity wall.
  • Inner Visceral Pleura: Tightly adheres to the pulmonary parenchymal surface.
  • Pleural Cavity & Pleural Fluid: The narrow potential space between the two pleural layers contains serous pleural fluid, which lubricates the opposing membranes, drastically minimizing frictional wear during continuous lung inflation and deflation.

The lungs are housed in an anatomically airtight thoracic chamber. This closed structural box is bounded:

  • Dorsally: By the vertebral column.
  • Ventrally: By the sternum (breastbone).
  • Laterally: By the arched ribs.
  • Inferiorly (on the lower side): By the muscular, dome-shaped diaphragm.
Crucial Physiological Concept: Human lung tissue lacks intrinsic voluntary skeletal muscle and cannot directly alter its own pulmonary volume. Because the thoracic cavity is strictly airtight, any change in thoracic chamber volume is instantaneously mirrored by an identical volumetric change in the pulmonary cavities. This anatomical coupling is the absolute physical prerequisite for pulmonary ventilation.

২. মানব শ্বসনতন্ত্র: গঠনগত সংগঠন

মানব শ্বসনতন্ত্র একটি নির্দিষ্ট বায়ু পরিবহন পথ এবং ফুসফুসের মধ্যস্থ বিশেষ গ্যাসীয় বিনিময় অঞ্চল নিয়ে গঠিত:

  • বহিঃনাসারন্ধ্র ও নাসা গহ্বর: ওপরের ঠোঁটের ওপরে অবস্থিত একজোড়া বহিঃনাসারন্ধ্রের মাধ্যমে বাতাস প্রবেশ করে নাসা পথের মধ্য দিয়ে নাসা গহ্বরে পৌঁছায়।
  • গলবিল (Pharynx): নাসা গহ্বরটি গলবিলে উন্মুক্ত হয়, যা খাদ্য এবং বায়ু উভয়েরই সাধারণ পথ হিসেবে কাজ করে।
  • ল্যারিংস বা স্বরযন্ত্র ও উপজিহ্বা (Epiglottis): গলবিলটি গ্লটিসের মাধ্যমে স্বরযন্ত্রে (Larynx) প্রবেশ করে শ্বাসনালীতে পৌঁছায়। ল্যারিংস হলো একটি তরুণাস্থি নির্মিত বাক্স যা শব্দ উৎপাদনে সহায়তা করে, তাই একে শব্দ বাক্স (Sound box) বলা হয়। খাদ্য গলাধঃকরণের সময় গ্লটিসটি এপিগ্লটিস (উপজিহ্বা) নামক একটি পাতলা, স্থিতিস্থাপক তরুণাস্থিময় ঢাকনা দ্বারা আবৃত হয়ে যায়, যাতে শ্বাসনালীতে খাদ্যকণা প্রবেশ করতে না পারে।
  • শ্বাসনালী বা ট্রাকিয়া (Trachea): এটি একটি সোজা নলাকার গঠন যা মধ্য-বক্ষ গহ্বর পর্যন্ত বিস্তৃত এবং পঞ্চম বক্ষদেশীয় কশেরুকা (5th Thoracic vertebra, T5)-র স্তরে ডান ও বাম দুটি প্রাথমিক ব্রঙ্কাসে (Primary bronchi) বিভক্ত হয়।
  • ব্রঙ্কিয়াল বৃক্ষ: প্রতিটি ব্রঙ্কাস ফুসফুসে প্রবেশ করে বারবার বিভক্ত হয়ে যথাক্রমে গৌণ ব্রঙ্কাস, প্রগৌণ ব্রঙ্কাস এবং শেষে অতি সূক্ষ্ম টার্মিনাল ব্রঙ্কিওলে (Terminal bronchioles) পরিণত হয়।
  • তরুণাস্থি বলয়ের অবস্থান: বায়ুচাপের পরিবর্তনে যাতে শ্বাসনালী চুপসে না যায়, সেজন্য ট্রাকিয়া, প্রাথমিক, গৌণ ও প্রগৌণ ব্রঙ্কাস এবং প্রাথমিক ব্রঙ্কিওলগুলি অসম্পূর্ণ তরুণাস্থি নির্মিত C-আকৃতির বলয় দ্বারা সুরক্ষিত থাকে।
  • অ্যালভিওলাই বা বায়ুতলী: প্রতিটি টার্মিনাল ব্রঙ্কিওল থেকে পাতলা, অনিয়মিত প্রাচীরযুক্ত এবং রক্তজালকপূর্ণ অসংখ্য থলির মতো গঠন উৎপন্ন হয়, যাদের অ্যালভিওলাই (Alveoli) বলে। ব্রঙ্কাই, ব্রঙ্কিওল এবং অ্যালভিওলাই সমবেতভাবে ফুসফুস গঠন করে।

শ্বসনতন্ত্রের কার্যগত বিভাজন

  1. পরিবহন অংশ (Conducting Part - বহিঃনাসারন্ধ্র থেকে টার্মিনাল ব্রঙ্কিওল): এর মূল কাজ চারটি:
    • বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় বাতাসকে অ্যালভিওলাই পর্যন্ত পরিবহন করা।
    • মিউকাস ও সিলিয়ার সাহায্যে ধূলিকণা ও জীবাণু আটকে বাতাস পরিষ্কার করা।
    • বাতাসকে আর্দ্র (Humidify) করা।
    • প্রবেশকারী বায়ুকে দেহের অভ্যন্তরীণ তাপমাত্রার সমতুল্য করা।
  2. বিনিময় অংশ (Respiratory/Exchange Part - অ্যালভিওলাই ও অ্যালভিওলার নালী): এটি প্রকৃত গ্যাস বিনিময়ের স্থান, যেখানে ব্যাপন প্রক্রিয়ায় রক্ত এবং অ্যালভিওলার বায়ুর মধ্যে \(O_2\) ও \(CO_2\)-এর আদানপ্রদান সম্পন্ন হয়।

প্লুরা এবং বায়ুরোধী বক্ষ গহ্বরের প্রাচীর

মানবদেহে দুটি ফুসফুস দ্বি-স্তরীয় প্লুরা (Pleura) নামক ঝিল্লি দ্বারা আবৃত থাকে:

  • বহিঃপ্লুরা (Parietal Pleura): বক্ষপ্রাচীরের অভ্যন্তরীণ আস্তরণের সংস্পর্শে থাকে।
  • অন্তঃপ্লুরা (Visceral Pleura): ফুসফুসের বহিপৃষ্ঠের সংস্পর্শে থাকে।
  • প্লুরাল তরল: এই দুই স্তরের মধ্যবর্তী স্থানে প্লুরাল তরল থাকে, যা ফুসফুসের সঞ্চালনের সময় ঘর্ষণ হ্রাস করে।

ফুসফুস দুটি একটি সম্পূর্ণরূপে বায়ুরোধী বক্ষ গহ্বরে (Thoracic chamber) অবস্থিত। এই গহ্বরটি গঠিত:

  • পৃষ্ঠদেশে (Dorsally): মেরুদণ্ড (Vertebral column) দ্বারা।
  • অঙ্কীয়দেশে (Ventrally): স্টার্নাম (উরোস্থি) দ্বারা।
  • পার্শ্বদেশে (Laterally): পাঁজর বা পরশুকা (Ribs) দ্বারা।
  • নিম্নদেশে (Lower side): গম্বুজাকৃতি পেশীবহুল ডায়াফ্রাম (মধ্যচ্ছদা) দ্বারা।
NEET-এর গুরুত্বপূর্ণ ধারণা: আমরা সরাসরি ফুসফুসের আয়তন পরিবর্তন করতে পারি না। কিন্তু বক্ষ গহ্বর বায়ুরোধী হওয়ায়, বক্ষ গহ্বরের আয়তনে যেকোনো পরিবর্তন সরাসরি ফুসফুসের (পালমোনারি) আয়তনে প্রতিফলিত হয়—যা শ্বাসকার্য সম্পাদনের জন্য একান্ত অপরিহার্য।

3. Biomechanics of Ventilation: Inspiration and Expiration

Pulmonary ventilation operates by establishing an alternating pressure gradient between the atmospheric environment and the pulmonary cavities.

  • Inspiration: Occurs when the pressure within the alveolar cavities (termed intra-pulmonary pressure) drops below ambient atmospheric pressure, establishing a negative pressure gradient.
  • Expiration: Occurs when intra-pulmonary pressure rises above atmospheric pressure, generating a positive pressure gradient that forces alveolar gas out of the respiratory tree.

The Dual-Axis Muscular Engine

Pressure gradients are generated by the coordinated actions of the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles (external and internal) located between adjacent ribs:

Ventilatory Phase Muscular Movement Thoracic Axis of Expansion Pressure Dynamics & Airflow
Inspiration
(Active process, ~2 sec)		 1. Contraction of the muscular diaphragm, which flattens inferiorly.
2. Contraction of external intercostal muscles, lifting the ribs and sternum upward and outward. 		• Diaphragm expands thoracic volume in the antero-posterior axis.
• External intercostals expand thoracic volume in the dorso-ventral axis. 		Total thoracic volume increases → Pulmonary volume increases proportionally → Intra-pulmonary pressure falls below atmospheric pressure → Air rushes in.
Expiration
(Passive process at rest, ~3 sec)		 1. Relaxation of the diaphragm, allowing it to spring upward into its resting dome shape.
2. Relaxation of external intercostals, allowing ribs and sternum to return downward and inward. 		• Diaphragm reduces thoracic volume in the antero-posterior axis.
• Rib cage recoil reduces volume in the dorso-ventral axis. 		Thoracic and pulmonary volumes decrease → Intra-pulmonary pressure rises slightly above atmospheric pressure → Air is driven out.

Active Forced Breathing & Spirometry

Under physical exertion or voluntary hyperventilation, humans can amplify the force and depth of both inspiration and expiration by recruiting accessory abdominal muscles (rectus abdominis, transversus abdominis) and internal intercostal muscles.

  • Resting Breathing Rate: A healthy adult breathes at an average frequency of 12 to 16 breaths per minute.
  • Clinical Assessment (Spirometry): The pulmonary volumes inhaled and exhaled are clinically quantified using a spirometer. Spirometric assessment serves as a critical diagnostic index for evaluating ventilatory impairment.
NEET High-Yield Pitfall: Which volume cannot be measured by a simple spirometer? Residual Volume (RV) cannot be exhaled into a spirometer; consequently, any pulmonary capacity containing RV (i.e., FRC and TLC) cannot be measured by conventional spirometry!

৩. শ্বাসকার্যের কৌশল বা মেকানিজম (Inspiration & Expiration)

ফুসফুস এবং বায়ুমণ্ডলের মধ্যে একটি নির্দিষ্ট চাপের নতিমাত্রা বা গ্রেডিয়েন্ট (Pressure gradient) তৈরি করার মাধ্যমেই ফুসফুসে বায়ুর প্রবেশ ও নির্গমন নিয়ন্ত্রিত হয়:

  • প্রশ্বাস (Inspiration): যখন ফুসফুসের মধ্যস্থ চাপ (অন্তঃফুসফুসীয় চাপ বা Intra-pulmonary pressure) বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপের চেয়ে কম হয়, অর্থাৎ ফুসফুসে ঋণাত্মক চাপের সৃষ্টি হয়, তখন বাতাস ফুসফুসে প্রবেশ করে।
  • নিঃশ্বাস (Expiration): যখন অন্তঃফুসফুসীয় চাপ বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপের চেয়ে বেশি হয়, তখন ফুসফুস থেকে বাতাস বাইরে বেরিয়ে যায়।

দ্বি-অক্ষীয় পেশীীয় সঞ্চালন

চাপের এই নতিমাত্রা সৃষ্টিতে ডায়াফ্রাম (মধ্যচ্ছদা) এবং পাঁজরের মধ্যবর্তী ইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশী (বহিঃ ও অন্তঃ ইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশী) প্রধান ভূমিকা পালন করে:

শ্বাসকার্যের পর্যায় পেশীর ক্রিয়া বক্ষ গহ্বরের প্রসারণের অক্ষ চাপের পরিবর্তন ও বায়ুপ্রবাহ
প্রশ্বাস (Inspiration)
(সক্রিয় প্রক্রিয়া)		 ১. ডায়াফ্রামের সংকোচন ঘটে, ফলে এটি সমতল হয়ে নিচের দিকে নামে।
২. বহিঃইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশীর সংকোচন পাঁজর ও স্টার্নামকে ওপরের ও বাইরের দিকে তোলে। 		• ডায়াফ্রামের ফলে বক্ষ গহ্বরের আয়তন অগ্র-পশ্চাৎ অক্ষে (Antero-posterior axis) বৃদ্ধি পায়।
• ইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশীর ফলে আয়তন পৃষ্ঠ-অঙ্কীয় অক্ষে (Dorso-ventral axis) বৃদ্ধি পায়। 		বক্ষ গহ্বরের মোট আয়তন বৃদ্ধি → ফুসফুসীয় আয়তন বৃদ্ধি → অন্তঃফুসফুসীয় চাপ বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপের চেয়ে কমে যায় → বাইরে থেকে বায়ু ফুসফুসে প্রবেশ করে।
নিঃশ্বাস (Expiration)
(বিশ্রামকালে নিষ্ক্রিয়)		 ১. ডায়াফ্রামের শিথিলকরণ ঘটে, ফলে এটি পুনরায় গম্বুজাকৃতি ধারণ করে ওপরে ওঠে।
২. বহিঃইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশীর শিথিলকরণ পাঁজর ও স্টার্নামকে স্বাভাবিক অবস্থানে নামিয়ে আনে। 		• ডায়াফ্রামের শিথিলতায় বক্ষ গহ্বর অগ্র-পশ্চাৎ অক্ষে সংকুচিত হয়।
• পাঁজরের প্রত্যাবর্তনে পৃষ্ঠ-অঙ্কীয় অক্ষে আয়তন হ্রাস পায়। 		ফুসফুসের আয়তন হ্রাস পায় → অন্তঃফুসফুসীয় চাপ বায়ুমণ্ডলীয় চাপের চেয়ে কিছুটা বৃদ্ধি পায় → ফুসফুস থেকে বায়ু বাইরে নির্গত হয়।

সক্রিয় বলপূর্বক শ্বাসকার্য ও স্পাইরোমিটার

আমরা উদরীয় পেশীর (Abdominal muscles) সহায়তায় বলপূর্বক প্রশ্বাস ও নিঃশ্বাসের ক্ষমতা বৃদ্ধি করতে পারি।

  • স্বাভাবিক শ্বাসগতি: একজন সুস্থ প্রাপ্তবয়স্ক মানুষ গড়ে প্রতি মিনিটে ১২ থেকে ১৬ বার শ্বাসকার্য সম্পন্ন করে।
  • স্পাইরোমিটার (Spirometer): শ্বাসকার্যে জড়িত বায়ুর আয়তন পরিমাপ করার জন্য স্পাইরোমিটার যন্ত্র ব্যবহার করা হয়, যা ফুসফুসের কার্যকারিতা মূল্যায়নে অত্যন্ত সহায়ক।

4. Pulmonary Volumes and Capacities

Clinical spirometry quantifies ventilation into four distinct non-overlapping lung volumes and five derived physiological capacities (formed by summing specific volume combinations):

Parameter Standard Definition Average Normal Value Diagnostic / Clinical Significance
Tidal Volume (TV) Volume of air inspired or expired during a normal resting respiration. ~500 mL
(6,000 – 8,000 mL/min)		 Minute ventilation = \(\mathrm{TV} \times \text{respiratory rate}\) (e.g., \(500\,\mathrm{mL} \times 12\text{--}16 = 6000\text{--}8000\,\mathrm{mL/min}\)).
Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) Additional volume of air a person can forcibly inspire beyond the normal tidal volume. 2,500 – 3,000 mL Reflects inspiratory muscle reserve and lung compliance.
Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) Additional volume of air a person can forcibly expire beyond the normal resting expiration. 1,000 – 1,100 mL Assesses expiratory muscle strength and active airway patency.
Residual Volume (RV) Volume of air permanently remaining inside the lungs even after the most strenuous, forced expiration. 1,100 – 1,200 mL Prevents complete alveolar collapse (atelectasis); maintains continuous gas exchange between breaths. Cannot be cleared.
Inspiratory Capacity (IC) Total volume of air a person can inspire following a normal resting expiration: \(\mathrm{IC} = \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV}\). 3,000 – 3,500 mL Assesses maximal inspiratory capability.
Expiratory Capacity (EC) Total volume of air a person can expire following a normal resting inspiration: \(\mathrm{EC} = \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{ERV}\). 1,500 – 1,600 mL Assesses maximal expiratory ability.
Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) Volume of air remaining in the lungs at the end of a normal, unforced expiration: \(\mathrm{FRC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV}\). 2,100 – 2,300 mL Acts as a physiological oxygen buffer preventing blood desaturation during the expiratory pause.
Vital Capacity (VC) The maximum volume of air a person can breathe in after a forced expiration (or breathe out after a forced inspiration): \(\mathrm{VC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV}\). 3,500 – 4,600 mL Crucial index of cardiopulmonary reserve and physical fitness.
Total Lung Capacity (TLC) Total volume of air accommodated inside the pulmonary tree at the conclusion of a maximal, forced inspiration: \(\mathrm{TLC} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV} = \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} + \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV}\). 5,000 – 6,000 mL Represents total anatomic volumetric capacity of both lungs.

5. Exchange of Gases at Alveolar and Tissue Boundaries

Gas exchange across biological membranes operates purely via simple physical diffusion governed by four decisive biophysical parameters:

  1. Pressure / Concentration Gradient: Gases move passively from higher to lower partial pressures.
  2. Solubility of the Gas: Determines how many gas molecules can partition into and diffuse across the aqueous membrane.
  3. Thickness of the Diffusion Barrier: Inversely proportional to diffusion velocity (Fick's law).
  4. Functional Surface Area: The massive alveolar surface area (~70–100 m² in humans) promotes rapid equilibrium.

Partial Pressure Gradients: Quantitative Data

Partial pressure is the pressure exerted by an individual gas in a gas mixture, denoted \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) for oxygen and \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) for carbon dioxide.

Respiratory Gas Atmospheric Air (mm Hg) Alveoli (mm Hg) Deoxygenated Blood (mm Hg) Oxygenated Blood (mm Hg) Body Tissues (mm Hg)
Oxygen (\(O_2\)) 159 104 40 95 40
Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\)) 0.3 40 45 40 45

Differential Gas Solubilities and Diffusion Kinetics

Comparing the gradients:

  • For \(O_2\): The pressure gradient from alveoli (\(104\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) to deoxygenated pulmonary capillary blood (\(40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) is steep: \(\Delta p = 104 - 40 = 64\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\). At tissues, \(O_2\) diffuses from oxygenated blood (\(95\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) into tissue cells (\(40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)), driven by a \(\Delta p\) of \(55\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\).
  • For \(CO_2\): The gradient from tissues (\(45\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) into deoxygenated blood (\(40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) is only \(\Delta p = 5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\). At the alveoli, \(CO_2\) diffuses from deoxygenated blood (\(45\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) into alveolar air (\(40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)), driven by a gradient of only \(5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\).
The Solubility Factor: The solubility of \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) is 20 to 25 times higher than that of \(\mathrm{O}_2\)! Consequently, despite having a much smaller partial pressure difference (\(5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) vs \(64\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)), \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) diffuses across the respiratory membrane with exceptional speed, achieving full equilibrium during the brief capillary transit time.

Histological Architecture of the Alveolo-Capillary Diffusion Membrane

The diffusion membrane across which gas diffusion occurs consists of exactly three microscopic layers:

  1. The thin squamous epithelium of the alveolar wall (one-celled thick).
  2. The endothelium of the alveolar blood capillaries (single endothelial cell layer).
  3. The intervening basement substance (formed by the fused basement membrane of the alveolar epithelium and the basement membrane surrounding the capillary endothelium).

Crucially, the total thickness of this three-layered diffusion barrier is much less than a millimetre (typically between \(0.2\text{--}0.5\,\mathrm{µm}\)). This microscopic thinness, combined with high gas solubility and an expansive capillary network, ensures optimal diffusive flux.

6. Transport of Gases in Blood

Blood is the primary transport vehicle for respiratory gases between the pulmonary alveoli and systemic metabolizing tissues:

6.1 Transport of Oxygen (\(O_2\))

Oxygen is transported via two distinct physical pathways:

  • Oxyhaemoglobin in RBCs (97%): Haemoglobin (\(\mathrm{Hb}\)) is an iron-containing (\(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\)) metalloprotein localized inside red blood cells. Oxygen binds reversibly to haemoglobin to form oxyhaemoglobin. Each individual tetrameric haemoglobin molecule can bind a maximum of four molecules of \(O_2\)).
  • Dissolved in Blood Plasma (3%): Transported in simple physical solution through the aqueous plasma.

The Oxygen-Haemoglobin Dissociation Curve

When the percentage saturation of haemoglobin with \(O_2\) is plotted against partial pressure (\(p\mathrm{O}_2\)), a characteristic sigmoid (S-shaped) curve is generated. This sigmoidal profile is the clinical manifestation of cooperative binding (binding of the first oxygen molecule increases the affinity of the remaining subunits for subsequent oxygen molecules).

Binding and unloading of \(O_2\) are governed by four physiological determinants:

Physiological Factor Alveolar Conditions (Favours Oxyhaemoglobin Formation) Tissue Conditions (Favours Oxygen Dissociation / Unloading)
Partial Pressure of \(O_2\) (\(p\mathrm{O}_2\)) High (\(\approx 104\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) Low (\(\approx 40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\))
Partial Pressure of \(CO_2\) (\(p\mathrm{CO}_2\)) Low (\(\approx 40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) High (\(\approx 45\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\))
Hydrogen Ion Concentration (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) & pH Lesser \(\mathrm{H}^+\) (Higher pH / Alkaline) High \(\mathrm{H}^+\) (Lower pH / Acidic)
Temperature Lower Temperature Higher Temperature (Active metabolic heat)
Curve Displacement Left Shift (Increased \(O_2\) affinity) Right Shift (Bohr Effect – Decreased affinity)
Physiological Delivery Constant:
Every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of oxygenated blood delivers approximately \(5\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(O_2\) to the tissues under normal resting physiological conditions.

6.2 Transport of Carbon Dioxide (\(CO_2\))

Carbon dioxide produced by cellular catabolism is transported from the tissues to the lungs via three physiological mechanisms:

  1. Dissolved in Blood Plasma (7%): Carried in simple physical solution.
  2. As Carbamino-haemoglobin in RBCs (20 – 25%): \(CO_2\) binds reversibly directly to the free amino groups of the globin chains: \[ \mathrm{Hb} + \mathrm{CO}_2 \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{HbCO}_2 \quad (\text{Carbamino-haemoglobin}) \] This binding depends heavily on \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\). In tissues, high \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) and low \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) promote binding; in alveoli, low \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) and high \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) stimulate dissociation of \(CO_2\).
  3. As Bicarbonate Ions (\(\mathrm{HCO}_3^-\)) (70%): The predominant mode of \(CO_2\) transport.

The Carbonic Anhydrase Enzyme Mechanism

Red blood cells contain an extremely high concentration of the zinc-containing metalloenzyme carbonic anhydrase, whereas only minute trace quantities are found in blood plasma. Carbonic anhydrase catalyzes the following bidirectional equilibrium with extraordinary kinetic velocity:

\[ \mathrm{CO}_2 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \xrightleftharpoons[\text{Carbonic anhydrase}]{\text{Carbonic anhydrase}} \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{CO}_3 \xrightleftharpoons[\text{Carbonic anhydrase}]{\text{Carbonic anhydrase}} \mathrm{HCO}_3^- + \mathrm{H}^+ \]
  • At the Tissue Level: Active catabolism generates high \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\). \(CO_2\) diffuses into RBCs, where carbonic anhydrase converts it into carbonic acid (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\)), which swiftly dissociates into bicarbonate (\(\mathrm{HCO}_3^-\)) and hydrogen ions (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)). Bicarbonate diffuses into the plasma.
  • At the Alveolar Level: In the pulmonary capillaries, where \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) is low, the equilibrium completely reverses: \(\mathrm{HCO}_3^-\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) recombine into \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\), which is catalytically cleaved back into gaseous \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\). The liberated \(CO_2\) diffuses into the alveoli and is exhaled.
Physiological Elimination Constant:
Every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of deoxygenated blood delivers approximately \(4\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(CO_2\) to the alveoli for exhalation.

7. Neural and Chemical Regulation of Respiration

The human body possesses a sophisticated autonomic control system capable of dynamically adjusting the respiratory rhythm to meet the fluctuating metabolic demands of somatic tissues:

  1. Respiratory Rhythm Centre (Medulla Oblongata): Located in the medulla oblongata of the brainstem, this specialized neural centre acts as the primary pacemaker responsible for generating and maintaining the basal rhythm of breathing.
  2. Pneumotaxic Centre (Pons Varolii): Situated dorsally in the pons region of the brain, the pneumotaxic centre exerts moderating inhibitory control over the medullary rhythm centre. Neural discharge from this centre transmits a 'switch-off' signal for inspiration, reducing the duration of inspiration. By curtailing inspiratory duration, it reflexively increases the respiratory rate.
  3. Central Chemosensitive Area (Medulla): Situated immediately adjacent to the respiratory rhythm centre in the medulla, this region is intensely sensitive to elevated concentrations of carbon dioxide (\(CO_2\)) and hydrogen ions (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)). Accumulation of \(CO_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) activates this area, which directly signals the rhythm centre to accelerate ventilatory rate and depth, thereby purging these metabolic toxins.
  4. Peripheral Arterial Chemoreceptors (Aortic Arch & Carotid Arteries): Sensory receptor clusters located in the aortic arch and carotid bodies continually monitor systemic arterial blood chemistry. They specifically detect rising concentrations of \(CO_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) ions, rapidly dispatching sensory afferent impulses to the medullary rhythm centre to initiate corrective hyperventilation.
Paramount NEET Concept: The role of oxygen (\(O_2\)) in the regular neural regulation of respiratory rhythm is quite insignificant! Normal breathing is driven primarily by arterial \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) levels, not by oxygen deficit.

8. Disorders of the Respiratory System

Ventilatory pathology encompasses allergic, degenerative, and occupational conditions:

  • Asthma: A chronic respiratory disorder characterized by acute dyspnea (difficulty in breathing) and distinctive high-pitched wheezing sounds during expiration, caused by localized allergic spasm and inflammation of the bronchi and bronchioles.
  • Emphysema: A debilitating chronic obstructive condition in which the delicate alveolar walls undergo progressive destruction. This anatomical damage causes irreversible enlargement of air spaces and a severe permanent reduction of the respiratory exchange surface area. One of the single major etiological causes of emphysema is chronic cigarette smoking.
  • Occupational Respiratory Disorders: Pathologies arising in heavy industrial workplaces, particularly industries involving stone-grinding, silica handling, or rock-quarrying. Chronic inhalation of crystalline dust particles generates massive particulate loads that overwhelm the pulmonary alveolar macrophages. Protracted exposure triggers persistent inflammation culminating in fibrosis (pathological proliferation and scarring of fibrous connective tissues), leading to permanent loss of lung elasticity, severe parenchymal destruction, and respiratory failure. Strict adherence to protective industrial dust masks is mandatory.

Solution & Physiological Analysis:

(a) Minute Pulmonary Ventilation: Total air moved into the respiratory tract per minute:

\[ \dot{V}_E = \mathrm{TV} \times \text{Breathing Rate} = 500\,\mathrm{mL/breath} \times 14\,\text{breaths/min} = 7,000\,\mathrm{mL/min} \; (7.0\,\mathrm{L/min}) \]

(b) Effective Alveolar Ventilation: Only the fresh air that enters the alveoli participates in gas exchange. The air trapped in the conducting part (\(150\,\mathrm{mL}\)) is expired without exchange:

\[ \mathrm{Alveolar\;Tidal\;Volume} = \mathrm{TV} - \text{Dead Space} = 500\,\mathrm{mL} - 150\,\mathrm{mL} = 350\,\mathrm{mL/breath} \] \[ \dot{V}_A = 350\,\mathrm{mL/breath} \times 14\,\text{breaths/min} = 4,900\,\mathrm{mL/min} \; (4.9\,\mathrm{L/min}) \]

Significance: Shows why rapid shallow breathing is inefficient: dead space ventilation remains constant, drastically compromising alveolar gas renewal.

Example 2: Systemic Gas Delivery from Cardiac Output

Problem Statement: An individual has a resting cardiac output of \(5.0\,\mathrm{L/min}\) (\(5,000\,\mathrm{mL/min}\)). Using established NCERT physiological constants:

  1. Determine the total volume of \(O_2\) delivered to systemic tissues per minute.
  2. Determine the total volume of \(CO_2\) transferred from venous blood to the alveolar air sacs per minute.
Solution & Quantitative Derivation:

(a) Oxygen Delivery to Tissues: NCERT establishes that every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of oxygenated blood delivers approximately \(5\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(O_2\) under normal physiological conditions:

\[ \text{Total } O_2 \text{ Delivered} = \frac{5,000\,\mathrm{mL}\text{ blood/min}}{100\,\mathrm{mL}\text{ blood}} \times 5\,\mathrm{mL}\;O_2 = 50 \times 5\,\mathrm{mL} = 250\,\mathrm{mL}\;O_2\text{/min} \]

(b) Carbon Dioxide Elimination at Alveoli: NCERT establishes that every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of deoxygenated blood delivers approximately \(4\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(CO_2\) to the alveoli:

\[ \text{Total } CO_2 \text{ Delivered} = \frac{5,000\,\mathrm{mL}\text{ blood/min}}{100\,\mathrm{mL}\text{ blood}} \times 4\,\mathrm{mL}\;CO_2 = 50 \times 4\,\mathrm{mL} = 200\,\mathrm{mL}\;CO_2\text{/min} \]

Respiratory Exchange Ratio (\(R\)): \(\frac{200\,\mathrm{mL}\;CO_2}{250\,\mathrm{mL}\;O_2} = 0.8\), the typical baseline resting metabolic quotient.

Example 3: Spirometric Capacity Derivations

Problem Statement: A spirometric evaluation of a subject yields: \(\mathrm{TV} = 500\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{IRV} = 2800\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{ERV} = 1100\,\mathrm{mL}\), and \(\mathrm{RV} = 1200\,\mathrm{mL}\). Derive the values of (i) \(\mathrm{IC}\), (ii) \(\mathrm{FRC}\), (iii) \(\mathrm{VC}\), and (iv) \(\mathrm{TLC}\).

Solution & Formula Evaluation:
  • Inspiratory Capacity (\(\mathrm{IC}\)): \(\mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} = 500\,\mathrm{mL} + 2800\,\mathrm{mL} = \mathbf{3300\,\mathrm{mL}}\).
  • Functional Residual Capacity (\(\mathrm{FRC}\)): \(\mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV} = 1100\,\mathrm{mL} + 1200\,\mathrm{mL} = \mathbf{2300\,\mathrm{mL}}\).
  • Vital Capacity (\(\mathrm{VC}\)): \(\mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} = 1100 + 500 + 2800 = \mathbf{4400\,\mathrm{mL}}\).
  • Total Lung Capacity (\(\mathrm{TLC}\)): \(\mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV} = 4400\,\mathrm{mL} + 1200\,\mathrm{mL} = \mathbf{5600\,\mathrm{mL}}\).

Practice Question 1 • Biomechanics of Pulmonary Ventilation
During normal resting inspiration in a healthy human, contraction of the diaphragm and the external intercostal muscles increases the volume of the thoracic chamber respectively along which anatomical axes?
(A) Dorso-ventral axis and Antero-posterior axis
(B) Antero-posterior axis and Dorso-ventral axis
(C) Antero-posterior axis and Longitudinal axis
(D) Dorso-ventral axis and Transverse axis
Correct Answer: (B) Antero-posterior axis and Dorso-ventral axis

Worked Explanation:

Inspiration is initiated by the contraction of the muscular diaphragm, which flattens downward, thereby increasing the thoracic volume in the antero-posterior axis. Concurrently, the contraction of the external intercostal muscles elevates the ribs and sternum upward and outward, expanding the thoracic chamber in the dorso-ventral axis. The cumulative volumetric expansion across both axes lowers intra-pulmonary pressure below atmospheric pressure, drawing air into the lungs.

Practice Question 2 • Spirometric Capacities & Incalculable Volumes
Which of the following pulmonary capacities CANNOT be determined directly using a conventional simple spirometer, and what is the underlying physiological reason?
(A) Vital Capacity (\(\mathrm{VC}\)), because it requires maximal forced expiration.
(B) Inspiratory Capacity (\(\mathrm{IC}\)), because inspiratory reserve volume cannot be exhaled.
(C) Functional Residual Capacity (\(\mathrm{FRC}\)) and Total Lung Capacity (\(\mathrm{TLC}\)), because they include Residual Volume (\(\mathrm{RV}\)).
(D) Expiratory Capacity (\(\mathrm{EC}\)), because tidal volume fluctuates between breaths.
Correct Answer: (C) Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) and Total Lung Capacity (TLC), because they include Residual Volume (RV).

Worked Explanation:

A simple spirometer records only the volume of air that actually moves into and out of the lungs during respiratory maneuvers. Residual Volume (\(\mathrm{RV}\)) is the volume permanently retained inside the pulmonary alveoli even after the most strenuous, maximal expiration; it can never be expired into a spirometer. Because \(\mathrm{FRC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV}\) and \(\mathrm{TLC} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV}\), neither capacity can be determined directly by simple spirometry.

Practice Question 3 • Gas Solubility & Diffusion Gradients
The partial pressure gradient of \(O_2\) from the alveoli to deoxygenated pulmonary blood is \(64\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (\(104 - 40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)), whereas the gradient for \(CO_2\) from deoxygenated blood to alveoli is merely \(5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (\(45 - 40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)). Despite this stark difference, \(CO_2\) diffuses rapidly across the alveolar membrane. This is primarily because:
(A) Alveolar capillary endothelium is selectively permeable only to \(CO_2\).
(B) The molecular weight of \(CO_2\) is substantially lower than that of \(O_2\).
(C) The solubility of \(CO_2\) in the diffusion medium is 20 to 25 times higher than that of \(O_2\).
(D) Carbonic anhydrase present in the alveolar wall actively pumps \(CO_2\) into the alveolus.
Correct Answer: (C) The solubility of CO2 in the diffusion medium is 20 to 25 times higher than that of O2.

Worked Explanation:

According to Fick's law of diffusion, the diffusive flux across a membrane is directly proportional to both the partial pressure difference and the solubility coefficient of the diffusing gas. Because the solubility of \(CO_2\) is 20 to 25 times greater than that of \(O_2\), a much smaller partial pressure gradient (\(5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) for \(CO_2\) versus \(64\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) for \(O_2\)) is fully sufficient to drive rapid equimolar gas exchange across the thin respiratory membrane.

অ্যালভিওলাই থেকে রক্তে \(O_2\)-এর আংশিক চাপের পার্থক্য হলো \(৬৪\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (\(১০৪ - ৪০\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)), পক্ষান্তরে রক্ত থেকে অ্যালভিওলাইতে \(CO_2\)-এর চাপের পার্থক্য মাত্র \(৫\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (\(৪৫ - ৪০\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\))। এই ব্যাপক বৈষম্য সত্ত্বেও \(CO_2\) অতি দ্রুত ব্যাপিত হয়, কারণ:

(C) ব্যাপন মাধ্যমে \(CO_2\)-এর দ্রাব্যতা \(O_2\)-এর তুলনায় ২০ থেকে ২৫ গুণ বেশি।

বিশ্লেষণ ও ব্যাখ্যা:

ব্যাপনের হার শুধু আংশিক চাপের পার্থক্যের ওপর নির্ভর করে না, বরং সংশ্লিষ্ট গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতার ওপরও নির্ভরশীল। যেহেতু \(CO_2\)-এর দ্রাব্যতা \(O_2\)-এর তুলনায় ২০ থেকে ২৫ গুণ বেশি, তাই মাত্র \(৫\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) আংশিক চাপের পার্থক্য থাকা সত্ত্বেও রক্ত থেকে অ্যালভিওলাইতে অত্যন্ত দ্রুত ও কার্যকরভাবে \(CO_2\)-এর ব্যাপন সম্পন্ন হয়।

(A) High \(p\mathrm{O}_2\), Low \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\), Low \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentration, Low temperature
(B) Low \(p\mathrm{O}_2\), High \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\), High \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentration, High temperature
(C) High \(p\mathrm{O}_2\), High \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\), Low \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentration, High temperature

Assertion (A) is TRUE: The pneumotaxic centre in the pons moderates the medullary rhythm centre by sending an inhibitory signal that turns off inspiration prematurely, shortening inspiratory duration and elevating respiratory rate.
Reason (R) is FALSE: The central chemosensitive area and peripheral chemoreceptors (in the carotid bodies and aortic arch) are primarily sensitive to increases in \(CO_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) ions, not a decrease in \(O_2\). NCERT explicitly emphasizes: "The role of oxygen in the regulation of respiratory rhythm is quite insignificant."

11. High-Yield NEET Revision Checklist & Common Pitfalls

  • Muscular Axis Distinction: Remember that diaphragm contraction expands the thoracic cavity in the antero-posterior axis, while external intercostal contraction lifts the ribs and expands it in the dorso-ventral axis. Confusing these two axes is the single most common examiner trap.
  • Incalculable Spirometric Volumes: Residual Volume (RV) cannot be exhaled. Consequently, any capacity formula containing RV—namely Functional Residual Capacity (\(\mathrm{FRC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV}\)) and Total Lung Capacity (\(\mathrm{TLC} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV}\))—CANNOT be directly measured with a simple spirometer.
  • CO2 vs. O2 Solubility Differential: \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) is 20 to 25 times more soluble than \(\mathrm{O}_2\). Hence, a tiny partial pressure difference of only \(5\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) (\(45 - 40\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\)) drives sufficient \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) diffusion to balance the large \(64\,\mathrm{mm\,Hg}\) gradient of \(\mathrm{O}_2\).
  • Binding Sites on Haemoglobin: Oxygen binds reversibly to the \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\) haem prosthetic group (forming oxyhaemoglobin). In contrast, \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) binds reversibly to the free amino terminal groups of the globin protein chains (forming carbamino-haemoglobin). \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) does NOT bind to haem iron!
  • Physiological Delivery Constants:
    • Every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of oxygenated blood delivers \(5\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(O_2\) to somatic tissues.
    • Every \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of deoxygenated blood delivers \(4\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(CO_2\) to the alveoli.
  • Regulation Drivers: The role of oxygen in regulating respiratory rhythm is insignificant. Normal neural regulation is governed predominantly by central medullary and peripheral arterial chemoreceptors monitoring \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentrations.
  • Distinct Anatomical Lesions in Respiratory Pathology:
    • Asthma: Localized inflammation of bronchi and bronchioles, producing expiratory wheezing.
    • Emphysema: Destruction of alveolar walls, resulting in decreased respiratory surface area (strongly linked to cigarette smoking).
    • Occupational Disorders: Long-term dust inhalation causing fibrosis (pathological proliferation of fibrous tissues).

12. Concise Final Chapter Recap

Respiration integrates pulmonary ventilation, diffusion, gas transport, and tissue respiration. Atmospheric air is cyclically inhaled through the conducting zone (nostrils to terminal bronchioles) into the thin, vascularized alveoli. The generation of alternating negative and positive intra-pulmonary pressures is powered by the rhythmic contractions of the diaphragm and intercostal muscles within an airtight thoracic container. Exchange of gases across the microscopic, three-layered diffusion membrane (< 1 mm thick) proceeds passively down partial pressure gradients, aided by the high aqueous solubility of \(\mathrm{CO}_2\). In the blood, 97% of \(O_2\) is ferried as oxyhaemoglobin, characterized by a sigmoidal dissociation curve whose right shift (Bohr effect) ensures unloading at acidotic, hypercapnic tissues. Concurrently, 70% of \(CO_2\) is converted by erythrocytic carbonic anhydrase into plasma bicarbonate. Respiration is autonomously modulated by the medullary rhythm centre and pontine pneumotaxic centre, driven by \(CO_2\) and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) sensitivity, while \(O_2\) plays an insignificant role. Pathologies such as asthma, emphysema, and occupational fibrosis disrupt this precision apparatus, underscoring the vital relationship between structure, biomechanics, and molecular physiology.

১২. সংক্ষিপ্ত অধ্যায় সারসংক্ষেপ (Final Recap)

শ্বাসকার্য ও গ্যাসীয় বিনিময় প্রক্রিয়াটি মূলত ফুসফুসীয় বায়ু চলাচল, ব্যাপন, রক্তে পরিবহন এবং কলা শ্বসনের একটি সামগ্রিক সমন্বয়। বহিরাগত বাতাস পরিবহন অংশের মধ্য দিয়ে আর্দ্র, উষ্ণ ও পরিশোধিত হয়ে ফুসফুসের অ্যালভিওলাইতে পৌঁছায়। বায়ুরোধী বক্ষ গহ্বরের ভেতর ডায়াফ্রাম ও ইন্টারকোস্টাল পেশীর সুনির্দিষ্ট সঞ্চালনে সৃষ্ট ঋণাত্মক ও ধনাত্মক চাপের দ্বারাই বাতাস ফুসফুসে প্রবেশ ও নির্গত হয়। এক মিলিমিটারের চেয়েও কম পুরু তিন স্তরবিশিষ্ট ব্যাপন পর্দার মাধ্যমে আংশিক চাপের নতিমাত্রা ও উচ্চ দ্রাব্যতার সমন্বয়ে \(O_2\) ও \(CO_2\)-এর দ্রুত বিনিময় ঘটে। রক্তে ৯৭% অক্সিজেন লোহিত রক্তকণিকায় অক্সিহিমোগ্লোবিন রূপে পরিবাহিত হয় এবং সিগময়েড বিয়োজন বক্ররেখার মাধ্যমে কলায় অক্সিজেন ত্যাগ করে। অপরদিকে কার্বনিক অ্যানহাইড্রেজ উৎসেচকের সহায়তায় ৭০% \(CO_2\) বাইকার্বনেট রূপে এবং ২০-২৫% কার্বামিনো-হিমোগ্লোবিন রূপে ফুসফুসে পরিবাহিত হয়ে নির্গত হয়। শ্বাস ছন্দ কেন্দ্র ও নিউমোট্যাক্সিক কেন্দ্র রক্তে \(CO_2\) ও \(\mathrm{H}^+\) আয়নের ঘনত্বের ওপর নির্ভর করে নিখুঁতভাবে শ্বাসকার্যের গতি নিয়ন্ত্রণ করে, যেখানে অক্সিজেনের ভূমিকা নগণ্য। ব্রঙ্কাই-এর প্রদাহে হাঁপানি, অ্যালভিওলার প্রাচীর বিনষ্টে এমফাইসিমা এবং দীর্ঘস্থায়ী ধূলিকণায় ফুসফুসে ফাইব্রোসিস দেখা দেয়।
