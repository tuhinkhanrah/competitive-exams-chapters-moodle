1. Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

English

Cellular metabolism continuously consumes molecular oxygen (O2) to oxidize nutrients such as glucose, amino acids, and fatty acids for energy, while generating carbon dioxide (CO2) as a potentially harmful catabolic by-product. Organisms must therefore maintain an uninterrupted exchange of atmospheric O2 with cellular CO2—a physiological process termed breathing (external respiration).

Key NEET UG Learning Goals

  • Compare respiratory organs across major animal phyla and vertebrate classes.
  • Map the conducting and exchange zones of the human respiratory tract and understand pleural mechanics.
  • Explain pulmonary ventilation through pressure gradients, muscle actions, and spirometric lung volumes/capacities.
  • Analyze alveolar and tissue gas exchange using partial pressure gradients, gas solubility, and the three-layered diffusion membrane.
  • Master O2 and CO2 transport mechanisms, the sigmoid oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve, and carbonic anhydrase equilibria.
  • Describe neural and chemical control of breathing and major respiratory disorders.

3. Functional Anatomy of the Human Respiratory System

English

The human respiratory tract is organized into two functional divisions: the conducting part and the respiratory (exchange) part.

  • Airway Pathway: External nostrils → nasal chamber → pharynx (common passage for food and air) → larynx (cartilaginous sound box; its opening, the glottis, is covered during swallowing by the elastic cartilaginous epiglottis to prevent food entry) → trachea.
  • Bronchial Tree: The trachea extends to the mid-thoracic cavity and bifurcates at the level of the 5th thoracic vertebra into right and left primary bronchi. These divide repeatedly into secondary bronchi, tertiary bronchi, bronchioles, and thin terminal bronchioles.
  • Cartilaginous Support: Incomplete C-shaped cartilaginous rings support the trachea, primary, secondary, and tertiary bronchi, and initial bronchioles to prevent airway collapse.
  • Conducting Part (External nostrils up to terminal bronchioles): Transports atmospheric air to the alveoli, clears foreign particles, humidifies the air, and warms it to body temperature.
  • Exchange Part (Alveoli and their ducts): Very thin, irregular-walled, richly vascularized bag-like alveoli serve as the actual site of O2 and CO2 diffusion between blood and alveolar air.
  • Pleural & Thoracic Setup: Two lungs are enclosed by a double-layered pleura containing pleural fluid to minimize friction. The outer pleura contacts the thoracic lining, while the inner pleura adheres to the lung surface. The thoracic chamber is anatomically airtight—bounded dorsally by the vertebral column, ventrally by the sternum, laterally by ribs, and inferiorly by the dome-shaped diaphragm. Because pulmonary volume cannot be altered directly, any change in thoracic volume automatically changes lung volume.

4. Mechanics of Pulmonary Ventilation

English

Air moves into and out of the lungs strictly along a pressure gradient between the atmosphere and the alveoli, driven by volume changes in the thoracic cavity.

  • Inspiration (Active process):
    1. Diaphragm contracts and flattens, increasing thoracic volume along the antero-posterior axis.
    2. External intercostal muscles contract, lifting the ribs and sternum upward and outward, increasing thoracic volume along the dorso-ventral axis.
    3. Increased thoracic volume increases pulmonary volume, dropping intra-pulmonary pressure below atmospheric pressure (negative pressure). Air rushes into the lungs.
  • Expiration (Passive under normal resting conditions):
    1. Diaphragm and external intercostal muscles relax, returning the diaphragm, ribs, and sternum to their original positions.
    2. Thoracic and pulmonary volumes decrease, raising intra-pulmonary pressure slightly above atmospheric pressure. Air is expelled.
  • Forced Breathing: Additional abdominal muscles (and internal intercostals) can increase the strength of inspiration and expiration.
  • Normal Breathing Rate: A healthy adult breathes 12–16 times/minute. Respiratory air volumes are clinically measured using a spirometer.

  • নিঃশ্বাস (Expiration — স্বাভাবিক অবস্থায় নিষ্ক্রিয় প্রক্রিয়া):
    1. মধ্যচ্ছদা ও বহিঃস্থ ইন্টারকস্টাল পেশি শিথিল হয়ে পূর্বাবস্থায় ফিরে আসে।

5. Respiratory Volumes and Pulmonary Capacities

English

Four primary respiratory volumes are combined to define five clinically important pulmonary capacities. Note that a simple spirometer cannot directly measure Residual Volume (RV), and therefore cannot directly measure capacities containing RV (FRC and TLC).

Volume / Capacity Definition / Formula Standard Value (mL)
Tidal Volume (TV) Air inspired or expired during normal quiet breathing ~500 mL (6000–8000 mL/min)
Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV) Additional air inspired by forcible inspiration 2500–3000 mL
Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) Additional air expired by forcible expiration 1000–1100 mL
Residual Volume (RV) Air remaining in lungs even after forcible expiration 1100–1200 mL
Inspiratory Capacity (IC) Total air inspired after normal expiration: \(\mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV}\) 3000–3500 mL
Expiratory Capacity (EC) Total air expired after normal inspiration: \(\mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{ERV}\) 1500–1600 mL
Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) Air remaining in lungs after normal expiration: \(\mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV}\) 2100–2300 mL
Vital Capacity (VC) Max air breathed in after forced expiration (or out after forced inspiration): \(\mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV}\) 4000–4600 mL
Total Lung Capacity (TLC) Total air in lungs at end of forced inspiration: \(\mathrm{RV} + \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV}\) 5100–5800 mL

6. Gas Exchange: Partial Pressures & Diffusion Membrane

English

Alveoli are the primary sites of gas exchange, followed by exchange between systemic blood and body tissues. Gases move across membranes by simple diffusion governed by three key factors:

  1. Partial Pressure Gradient (\(p\mathrm{O}_2\) and \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\)): Pressure contributed by an individual gas in a mixture.
  2. Solubility of Gases: CO2 is 20–25 times more soluble than O2, so far more CO2 diffuses per unit partial pressure difference.
  3. Thickness of the Diffusion Membrane: The respiratory membrane has three major layers—(a) thin squamous epithelium of alveoli, (b) endothelium of alveolar capillaries, and (c) basement substance between them—with a total thickness of much less than 1 mm.

7. Transport of Oxygen and Carbon Dioxide in Blood

English

Transport of Oxygen (O2)

  • 97% of O2 is transported by RBCs bound reversibly to the iron-containing pigment haemoglobin (Hb) as oxyhaemoglobin; 3% is carried dissolved in plasma.
  • Each haemoglobin molecule binds a maximum of 4 molecules of O2.
  • Oxygen Dissociation Curve (ODC): Plotting percentage saturation of Hb against \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) yields a sigmoid curve.
  • Alveolar Conditions (Favour Association — Left Shift): High \(p\mathrm{O}_2\), low \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\), low \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentration (high pH), and lower temperature favour oxyhaemoglobin formation.
  • Tissue Conditions (Favour Dissociation — Right Shift): Low \(p\mathrm{O}_2\), high \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\), high \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentration (low pH), and higher temperature promote O2 release to tissues.
  • Under normal physiological conditions, every 100 mL of oxygenated blood delivers ~5 mL of O2 to tissues.
Tutorial Diagram 4: Sigmoid Oxygen Dissociation Curve and right-shift factors in tissues.

Transport of Carbon Dioxide (CO2)

  • 70% as Bicarbonate (\(\mathrm{HCO}_3^-\)): Catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase (present in very high concentration in RBCs and minute quantities in plasma).
  • 20–25% as Carbamino-haemoglobin: CO2 binds to haemoglobin; high \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) and low \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) (in tissues) favour binding, whereas low \(p\mathrm{CO}_2\) and high \(p\mathrm{O}_2\) (in alveoli) cause dissociation.
  • 7% dissolved in plasma.

Every 100 mL of deoxygenated blood delivers ~4 mL of CO2 to the alveoli.

8. Regulation of Respiration & Respiratory Disorders

English

Neural and Chemical Control

  • Respiratory Rhythm Centre (Medulla): Primarily responsible for generating and maintaining the basic respiratory rhythm.
  • Pneumotaxic Centre (Pons): Moderates the medullary rhythm centre; neural signals from here reduce the duration of inspiration and thereby alter the respiratory rate.
  • Chemosensitive Area (adjacent to rhythm centre in Medulla): Highly sensitive to CO2 and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) ions; increased levels activate this area to stimulate the rhythm centre for elimination of CO2.
  • Peripheral Chemoreceptors (Aortic arch & Carotid artery): Detect changes in blood CO2 and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) concentrations and signal the medullary rhythm centre.
  • NEET High-Yield Fact: The role of oxygen (O2) in regulating respiratory rhythm is quite insignificant.

Disorders of the Respiratory System

  • Asthma: Difficulty in breathing accompanied by wheezing due to inflammation of bronchi and bronchioles.
  • Emphysema: Chronic disorder in which alveolar walls are damaged, decreasing the respiratory surface area; cigarette smoking is a major cause.
  • Occupational Respiratory Disorders: Prolonged exposure to heavy dust (e.g., grinding or stone-breaking industries) overwhelms lung defenses, causing inflammation and fibrosis (proliferation of fibrous tissues) and severe lung damage. Protective masks are essential.

9. Worked Examples (NEET UG Level)

English

Worked Example 1 (Respiratory Capacities)

Problem: In a hypothetical clinical spirometry assessment (assumed values for calculation), a healthy subject records: \(\mathrm{TV} = 520\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{IRV} = 2800\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{ERV} = 1050\,\mathrm{mL}\), and \(\mathrm{RV} = 1150\,\mathrm{mL}\). Calculate the subject's (a) Vital Capacity (VC), (b) Functional Residual Capacity (FRC), and (c) Total Lung Capacity (TLC).

Solution:

  • (a) \(\mathrm{VC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} = 1050 + 520 + 2800 = 4370\,\mathrm{mL}\).
  • (b) \(\mathrm{FRC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV} = 1050 + 1150 = 2200\,\mathrm{mL}\).
  • (c) \(\mathrm{TLC} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV} = 4370 + 1150 = 5520\,\mathrm{mL}\).

সমাধানকৃত উদাহরণ ১ (ফুসফুসীয় ধারকত্ব)

সমস্যা: একটি কাল্পনিক স্পাইরোমেট্রি পরীক্ষায় (গণনার জন্য ধরে নেওয়া মান) একজন সুস্থ ব্যক্তির ক্ষেত্রে পাওয়া গেল: \(\mathrm{TV} = 520\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{IRV} = 2800\,\mathrm{mL}\), \(\mathrm{ERV} = 1050\,\mathrm{mL}\) এবং \(\mathrm{RV} = 1150\,\mathrm{mL}\)। ব্যক্তিটির (ক) ভাইটাল ক্যাপাসিটি (VC), (খ) কার্যকরী অবশিষ্ট ধারকত্ব (FRC) এবং (গ) মোট ফুসফুসীয় ধারকত্ব (TLC) নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

  • (ক) \(\mathrm{VC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{TV} + \mathrm{IRV} = 1050 + 520 + 2800 = 4370\,\mathrm{mL}\)।
  • (খ) \(\mathrm{FRC} = \mathrm{ERV} + \mathrm{RV} = 1050 + 1150 = 2200\,\mathrm{mL}\)।
  • (গ) \(\mathrm{TLC} = \mathrm{VC} + \mathrm{RV} = 4370 + 1150 = 5520\,\mathrm{mL}\)।

Worked Example 2 (Oxygen & CO2 Delivery by Blood)

Problem: Assume a resting cardiac output of \(5\,\mathrm{L/min}\) (hypothetical physiological assumption). Under normal conditions, how much O2 is delivered to tissues per minute and how much CO2 is delivered to alveoli per minute?

Solution:

  • Blood flow per minute \(= 5\,\mathrm{L} = 5000\,\mathrm{mL}\).
  • Since \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of oxygenated blood delivers \(5\,\mathrm{mL}\) of O2 to tissues: \(\text{O}_2\text{ delivered} = \frac{5000}{100} \times 5 = 250\,\mathrm{mL/min}\).
  • Since \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) of deoxygenated blood delivers \(4\,\mathrm{mL}\) of CO2 to alveoli: \(\text{CO}_2\text{ delivered} = \frac{5000}{100} \times 4 = 200\,\mathrm{mL/min}\).

সমাধানকৃত উদাহরণ ২ (রক্ত দ্বারা O2 ও CO2 সরবরাহ)

সমস্যা: বিশ্রামরত অবস্থায় একজন মানুষের কার্ডিয়াক আউটপুট \(5\,\mathrm{L/min}\) ধরে নেওয়া হলো (কাল্পনিক শারীরবৃত্তীয় অনুমান)। স্বাভাবিক অবস্থায় প্রতি মিনিটে কলাকোষে কতটা O2 এবং অ্যালভিওলাইয়ে কতটা CO2 সরবরাহ করা হয়?

সমাধান:

  • প্রতি মিনিটে রক্ত প্রবাহ \(= 5\,\mathrm{L} = 5000\,\mathrm{mL}\)।
  • যেহেতু \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) অক্সিজেনযুক্ত রক্ত কলাকোষে \(5\,\mathrm{mL}\) O2 দেয়: প্রতি মিনিটে প্রদত্ত O2 \(= \frac{5000}{100} \times 5 = 250\,\mathrm{mL/min}\)।
  • যেহেতু \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) অক্সিজেনবিহীন রক্ত অ্যালভিওলাইয়ে \(4\,\mathrm{mL}\) CO2 দেয়: প্রতি মিনিটে প্রদত্ত CO2 \(= \frac{5000}{100} \times 4 = 200\,\mathrm{mL/min}\)।

3. Which pulmonary capacity cannot be measured using a simple spirometer, and what is its formula?

11. Common NEET UG Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

English
Common Misconceptions to Avoid:
  • O2 vs. CO2 in Breathing Control: Students often assume low O2 is the primary driver of breathing; in reality, medullary and peripheral chemoreceptors respond primarily to CO2 and \(\mathrm{H}^+\), while O2 plays an insignificant role in rhythm regulation.
  • Cartilage Rings Extent: Cartilaginous rings stop at the initial bronchioles; terminal bronchioles and alveoli lack cartilage.
  • Diffusion Membrane Layers: Although there are 3 layers (squamous epithelium, basement substance, capillary endothelium), the total thickness is < 1 mm, not > 1 mm.
  • 100 mL Blood Delivery: Remember 5 mL O2 (from oxygenated blood to tissues) vs. 4 mL CO2 (from deoxygenated blood to alveoli).
বাংলা
সাধারণ ভুল ও সতর্কতা:
  • শ্বসন নিয়ন্ত্রণে O2 বনাম CO2: শ্বসন ছন্দ নিয়ন্ত্রণে অক্সিজেনের ভূমিকা নগণ্য; মূল নিয়ন্ত্রক হলো CO2 এবং \(\mathrm{H}^+\) ঘনত্ব।
  • তরুণাস্থি বলয়ের বিস্তৃতি: তরুণাস্থির বলয় প্রারম্ভিক ব্রঙ্কিওল পর্যন্তই থাকে; প্রান্তীয় ব্রঙ্কিওল ও অ্যালভিওলাইয়ে থাকে না।
  • ব্যাপন পর্দার পুরুত্ব: তিনটি স্তর থাকা সত্ত্বেও পর্দাটির মোট পুরুত্ব ১ mm-এর চেয়ে কম।
  • ১০০ mL রক্তের গ্যাস সরবরাহ: ১০০ mL অক্সিজেনযুক্ত রক্ত ৫ mL O2 দেয় এবং ১০০ mL অক্সিজেনবিহীন রক্ত ৪ mL CO2 দেয়।

12. Concise Final Recap

English

Breathing exchanges atmospheric O2 for cellular CO2 across a 3-layered alveolar membrane (< 1 mm) via pressure gradients created by the diaphragm and intercostal muscles. O2 travels mainly as oxyhaemoglobin (97%) yielding a sigmoid dissociation curve, whereas CO2 travels mainly as bicarbonate (70%) via carbonic anhydrase and carbamino-haemoglobin (20–25%). Respiratory rhythm is governed by the medulla and moderated by the pons in response to CO2 and \(\mathrm{H}^+\) levels.

বাংলা

মধ্যচ্ছদা ও ইন্টারকস্টাল পেশির দ্বারা সৃষ্ট চাপের নতিমাত্রার মাধ্যমে বায়ু ফুসফুসে প্রবেশ ও নির্গত হয় এবং ১ mm-এর কম পুরু তিন-স্তরীয় অ্যালভিওলার পর্দার মধ্য দিয়ে O2 ও CO2-এর বিনিময় ঘটে। O2 প্রধানত অক্সিহিমোগ্লোবিন (৯৭%) রূপে এবং CO2 প্রধানত বাইকার্বোনেট (৭০%) ও কার্বামিনো-হিমোগ্লোবিন (২০–২৫%) রূপে পরিবাহিত হয়। CO2 ও \(\mathrm{H}^+\) মাত্রার সাপেক্ষে মেডুলা ও পন্স শ্বাসকার্যের ছন্দ নিয়ন্ত্রণ করে।
