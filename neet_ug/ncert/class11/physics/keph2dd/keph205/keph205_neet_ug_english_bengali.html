1. Learning Objectives & Exam Scope | শিখন উদ্দেশ্য ও পরীক্ষার পরিধি

English

Kinetic theory bridges the microscopic world of rapidly moving atoms and molecules with macroscopic thermodynamic quantities such as pressure, temperature, internal energy, and specific heat capacity. For NEET UG, this topic tests both conceptual clarity (especially temperature dependence of molecular speeds and degrees of freedom) and quick numerical facility with gas laws, root-mean-square speeds, specific heat ratios, and mean free path.

  • Understand the molecular model of matter and the conditions under which real gases approximate ideal gas behaviour.
  • Apply the ideal gas equation in molar, molecular, and density forms, along with Boyle’s, Charles’, and Dalton’s laws.
  • Derive and interpret the kinetic pressure relation \(P = \frac{1}{3}\rho v_{\mathrm{rms}}^2\) and the kinetic meaning of absolute temperature.
  • Use the law of equipartition of energy to determine degrees of freedom, internal energy, \(C_V\), \(C_P\), and \(\gamma\) for monatomic, diatomic, and polyatomic gases, as well as molar heat capacity of solids.
  • Calculate the mean free path and collision frequency of gas molecules in terms of molecular diameter, number density, pressure, and temperature.

2. Molecular Picture & Behaviour of Gases | পদার্থের আণবিক প্রকৃতি ও গ্যাসের আচরণ

English

2.1 Intermolecular Forces and Scales

All matter is composed of atoms and molecules (typical atomic size \(\sim 1\,\mathrm{Å} = 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)) in perpetual random motion. In solids and liquids, neighbouring molecules are separated by only a few ångströms (around \(2\,\mathrm{Å}\)), so short-range repulsive forces and attractive intermolecular forces strongly govern their behaviour. In gases at ordinary temperature and pressure, the average intermolecular spacing is roughly ten times larger (tens of ångströms), and the average distance travelled between successive collisions—the mean free path—is on the order of thousands of ångströms. Because molecules spend almost all their time far apart, intermolecular interactions are negligible except during brief collisions.

2.2 Ideal Gas Equation and Gas Laws

An ideal gas is a theoretical model in which molecules are treated as point masses with zero intermolecular attraction or repulsion except during perfectly elastic collisions. Real gases closely follow ideal gas behaviour at low pressure and high temperature, because molecules are far apart and their kinetic energy dominates over weak intermolecular attractions.

\[ PV = \mu R T = N k_{\mathrm{B}} T \qquad \text{and} \qquad P = n k_{\mathrm{B}} T = \frac{\rho R T}{M_0} \]
  • \(\mu = \frac{M}{M_0} = \frac{N}{N_{\mathrm{A}}}\) is the number of moles, \(M\) is sample mass, \(M_0\) is molar mass, and \(N\) is the number of molecules.
  • \(n = \frac{N}{V}\) is the molecular number density, and \(\rho = \frac{M}{V}\) is the mass density.
  • Universal gas constant \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\), Avogadro’s number \(N_{\mathrm{A}} = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\), and Boltzmann constant \(k_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{R}{N_{\mathrm{A}}} = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}}\).
  • At STP (\(T = 273\,\mathrm{K}\), \(P = 1\,\mathrm{atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}\)), \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of any ideal gas occupies \(22.4\,\mathrm{L}\).

Key Gas Laws:

  • Boyle’s Law: At constant temperature \(T\) and fixed \(\mu\), \(PV = \text{constant}\) (\(P \propto \frac{1}{V}\)).
  • Charles’ Law: At constant pressure \(P\) and fixed \(\mu\), \(V \propto T\) (where \(T\) is absolute temperature in kelvin).
  • Avogadro’s Hypothesis: Equal volumes of all gases at the same \(P\) and \(T\) contain equal numbers of molecules (\(N\)).
  • Dalton’s Law of Partial Pressures: For a non-reacting mixture of ideal gases in volume \(V\) at temperature \(T\), total pressure is \(P = P_1 + P_2 + \dots = (\mu_1 + \mu_2 + \dots)\frac{RT}{V}\).

3. Kinetic Pressure & Interpretation of Temperature | গ্যাসের চাপ ও তাপমাত্রার গতিতত্ত্বীয় ব্যাখ্যা

English

3.1 Origin of Gas Pressure

Consider \(N\) identical molecules of mass \(m\) moving randomly inside a cubical vessel of volume \(V\) (number density \(n = N/V\)). All collisions with the walls and among molecules are perfectly elastic. When a molecule with velocity \((v_x, v_y, v_z)\) strikes a wall perpendicular to the \(x\)-axis (area \(A\)), it rebounds with velocity \((-v_x, v_y, v_z)\). The momentum transferred to the wall in one collision is \(2 m v_x\).

In a small interval \(\Delta t\), molecules within distance \(v_x \Delta t\) moving toward the wall hit it. Since on average half of the molecules move toward the wall, the number of hits is \(\frac{1}{2} n A v_x \Delta t\). Summing over all molecular speeds and using isotropy (\(\overline{v_x^2} = \overline{v_y^2} = \overline{v_z^2} = \frac{1}{3}\overline{v^2}\)):

\[ P = \frac{1}{3} n m \overline{v^2} = \frac{1}{3} \rho v_{\mathrm{rms}}^2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad PV = \frac{2}{3} E_{\mathrm{trans}} \]

Here \(E_{\mathrm{trans}} = N \left(\frac{1}{2} m \overline{v^2}\right)\) is the total translational kinetic energy of the gas molecules, and \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\overline{v^2}}\) is the root-mean-square speed.

Tutorial Diagram 2: Elastic rebound of a gas molecule from a wall normal to the x-axis reverses only the x-component of velocity.

3.2 Kinetic Interpretation of Temperature

Equating \(PV = \frac{2}{3} E_{\mathrm{trans}}\) with the ideal gas law \(PV = N k_{\mathrm{B}} T\) yields:

\[ \bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{trans}} = \frac{1}{2} m \overline{v^2} = \frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T \qquad \text{and} \qquad v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 R T}{M_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 P}{\rho}} \]
  • The average translational kinetic energy per molecule (\(\frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T\)) depends only on absolute temperature \(T\)—it is independent of pressure, volume, and the chemical nature or mass of the gas.
  • At the same temperature \(T\), lighter molecules move faster: \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M_0}}\).
  • Note the mathematical inequality: the mean of squares \(\overline{v^2}\) is always greater than the square of the mean \((\bar{v})^2\) whenever speeds are distributed.

৩.১ গ্যাসের চাপের উৎপত্তি

ধরা যাক, \(V\) আয়তনের একটি ঘনকাকৃতি পাত্রে \(m\) ভরের \(N\) সংখ্যক অভিন্ন অণু এলোমেলোভাবে গতিশীল (সংখ্যা ঘনত্ব \(n = N/V\))। পাত্রের দেয়াল ও অণুগুলোর মধ্যকার সকল সংঘর্ষ সম্পূর্ণ স্থিতিস্থাপক। \((v_x, v_y, v_z)\) বেগের একটি অণু \(x\)-অক্ষের সাথে লম্বভাবে থাকা \(A\) ক্ষেত্রফলের দেয়ালে আঘাত করে \((-v_x, v_y, v_z)\) বেগে ফিরে আসে। প্রতিটি সংঘর্ষে দেয়ালে প্রদত্ত ভরবেগ হলো \(2 m v_x\)।

ক্ষুদ্র সময় \(\Delta t\)-তে দেয়াল থেকে \(v_x \Delta t\) দূরত্বের মধ্যে থাকা অণুগুলোর গড়ে অর্ধেক দেয়ালের দিকে ধাবিত হয়ে আঘাত করে (সংখ্যা \(\frac{1}{2} n A v_x \Delta t\))। সকল বেগের ওপর সমষ্টি করে এবং গ্যাসের সমদৈশিকতা (\(\overline{v_x^2} = \overline{v_y^2} = \overline{v_z^2} = \frac{1}{3}\overline{v^2}\)) ব্যবহার করে পাই:

\[ P = \frac{1}{3} n m \overline{v^2} = \frac{1}{3} \rho v_{\mathrm{rms}}^2 \qquad \text{এবং} \qquad PV = \frac{2}{3} E_{\mathrm{trans}} \]

এখানে \(E_{\mathrm{trans}} = N \left(\frac{1}{2} m \overline{v^2}\right)\) হলো গ্যাসের অণুগুলোর মোট রৈখিক গতিশক্তি এবং \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\overline{v^2}}\) হলো গড় বর্গবেগের বর্গমূল।

টিউটোরিয়াল চিত্র ২: x-অক্ষের সাথে লম্ব দেয়ালে স্থিতিস্থাপক সংঘর্ষের ফলে অণুর বেগের কেবল x-উপাংশের দিক পরিবর্তিত হয়।

৩.২ তাপমাত্রার গতিতত্ত্বীয় তাৎপর্য

\(PV = \frac{2}{3} E_{\mathrm{trans}}\) এবং আদর্শ গ্যাস সমীকরণ \(PV = N k_{\mathrm{B}} T\) তুলনা করে পাওয়া যায়:

\[ \bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{trans}} = \frac{1}{2} m \overline{v^2} = \frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T \qquad \text{এবং} \qquad v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 R T}{M_0}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 P}{\rho}} \]
  • প্রতি অণুর গড় রৈখিক গতিশক্তি (\(\frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T\)) কেবল পরম তাপমাত্রা \(T\)-এর ওপর নির্ভর করে—তা চাপ, আয়তন বা গ্যাসের প্রকৃতির ওপর নির্ভরশীল নয়।
  • একই তাপমাত্রায় হালকা অণু দ্রুত চলে: \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M_0}}\)।
  • বেগের বণ্টন থাকলে সর্বদা \(\overline{v^2} > (\bar{v})^2\) হয়।

4. Law of Equipartition of Energy & Specific Heats | শক্তির সমবিভাজন নীতি ও আপেক্ষিক তাপ

English

4.1 Degrees of Freedom and Equipartition

The law of equipartition of energy states that for any dynamical system in thermal equilibrium at absolute temperature \(T\), the total energy is equally shared among all quadratic energy modes (degrees of freedom), with each mode having an average energy of \(\frac{1}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T\) per molecule.

  • Translational modes: Every molecule moving freely in 3D space has 3 translational degrees of freedom (\(\frac{1}{2}mv_x^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_y^2 + \frac{1}{2}mv_z^2\)), contributing \(3 \times \frac{1}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T = \frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T\).
  • Rotational modes: A linear (e.g., diatomic) molecule has 2 independent rotational axes perpendicular to the internuclear axis (rotation about the bond axis has negligible moment of inertia and is not excited thermally), contributing \(2 \times \frac{1}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T = k_{\mathrm{B}} T\). A non-linear polyatomic molecule has 3 rotational axes, contributing \(\frac{3}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T\).
  • Vibrational modes: Each vibrational mode consists of two quadratic terms—kinetic energy \(\frac{1}{2} m \left(\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{\mathrm{d}t}\right)^2\) and potential energy \(\frac{1}{2} k y^2\)—so each vibrational mode contributes \(2 \times \frac{1}{2} k_{\mathrm{B}} T = k_{\mathrm{B}} T\).

4.2 Molar Specific Heat Capacities of Gases and Solids

For \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of an ideal gas with internal energy \(U\), \(C_V = \frac{\mathrm{d}U}{\mathrm{d}T}\), Mayer’s relation \(C_P - C_V = R\) holds universally, and \(\gamma = \frac{C_P}{C_V}\).

Rigid diatomic gas (e.g., \(\mathrm{N}_2, \mathrm{O}_2\) at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\)) \(5\) (\(3\) trans + \(2\) rot) \(\frac{5}{2}R\) \(\frac{7}{2}R\) \(\frac{7}{5} = 1.40\)

Specific Heat of Solids: Each atom in a crystal lattice vibrates in 3 independent directions. With \(3\) vibrational modes, average energy per atom is \(3 k_{\mathrm{B}} T\), so for \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\), \(U = 3 R T\). Since \(\Delta V\) is negligible for solids, \(C \approx \frac{\mathrm{d}U}{\mathrm{d}T} = 3R \approx 24.9\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) at room temperature (most metals obey this, though light stiff solids like carbon are exceptions at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\)).

5. Mean Free Path & Collision Frequency | গড় মুক্ত পথ ও সংঘর্ষ কম্পাঙ্ক

English

Although gas molecules travel at hundreds of metres per second, diffusion is slow because molecules undergo frequent collisions. Modelling molecules as hard spheres of diameter \(d\), a moving molecule with average speed \(\langle v \rangle\) collides with any molecule whose centre lies within distance \(d\) of its path, sweeping out an effective collision cylinder of cross-sectional area \(\pi d^2\) and length \(\langle v \rangle \Delta t\) in time \(\Delta t\).

Accounting for the relative motion of all moving molecules introduces a factor of \(\sqrt{2}\), giving the standard kinetic theory expressions:

\[ l = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\, n \pi d^2} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{\sqrt{2}\, \pi d^2 P} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \tau = \frac{l}{\langle v \rangle}, \qquad \nu_{\mathrm{coll}} = \frac{1}{\tau} = \sqrt{2}\, n \pi d^2 \langle v \rangle \]

Thus, mean free path \(l\) increases with temperature at constant pressure (\(l \propto T\)), decreases with increasing pressure at constant temperature (\(l \propto 1/P\)), and is inversely proportional to molecular cross-section (\(\pi d^2\)).

Worked Example 1: Ideal Gas Mixture & RMS Speed Ratio

Problem: A rigid insulated tank (assumed ideal gas behaviour) contains a mixture of \(4.0\,\mathrm{g}\) of helium (\(\mathrm{He}\), molar mass \(4.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) and \(32.0\,\mathrm{g}\) of oxygen (\(\mathrm{O}_2\), molar mass \(32.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) at \(127\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\). Find (a) the ratio of partial pressures \(P_{\mathrm{He}} : P_{\mathrm{O}_2}\), and (b) the ratio of rms speeds \(v_{\mathrm{rms}}(\mathrm{He}) : v_{\mathrm{rms}}(\mathrm{O}_2)\).

Solution:

(a) Number of moles: \(\mu_{\mathrm{He}} = \frac{4.0}{4.0} = 1.0\,\mathrm{mol}\), and \(\mu_{\mathrm{O}_2} = \frac{32.0}{32.0} = 1.0\,\mathrm{mol}\). Since both gases share the same volume \(V\) and temperature \(T = 127 + 273 = 400\,\mathrm{K}\), partial pressure \(P_i = \frac{\mu_i R T}{V} \propto \mu_i\). Thus, \(P_{\mathrm{He}} : P_{\mathrm{O}_2} = 1 : 1\).

(b) At the same temperature \(T\), \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M_0}}\). Therefore:

\[ \frac{v_{\mathrm{rms}}(\mathrm{He})}{v_{\mathrm{rms}}(\mathrm{O}_2)} = \sqrt{\frac{M_{\mathrm{O}_2}}{M_{\mathrm{He}}}} = \sqrt{\frac{32.0}{4.0}} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83 \]

Worked Example 2: Specific Heat of a Gas Mixture

Problem: Suppose \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a monatomic ideal gas are mixed with \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a rigid diatomic ideal gas in a rigid container. Calculate the effective molar specific heat at constant volume \(C_{V,\mathrm{mix}}\) and the effective adiabatic index \(\gamma_{\mathrm{mix}}\) of the mixture.

Solution: For the monatomic gas, \(C_{V1} = \frac{3}{2}R\); for the rigid diatomic gas, \(C_{V2} = \frac{5}{2}R\). Using energy conservation for the mixture:

\[ C_{V,\mathrm{mix}} = \frac{\mu_1 C_{V1} + \mu_2 C_{V2}}{\mu_1 + \mu_2} = \frac{2\left(\frac{3}{2}R\right) + 2\left(\frac{5}{2}R\right)}{2 + 2} = \frac{8R}{4} = 2R \]

By Mayer’s relation, \(C_{P,\mathrm{mix}} = C_{V,\mathrm{mix}} + R = 3R\). Hence:

\[ \gamma_{\mathrm{mix}} = \frac{C_{P,\mathrm{mix}}}{C_{V,\mathrm{mix}}} = \frac{3R}{2R} = 1.5 \]

Worked Example 3: Scaling of Mean Free Path

Problem: At a pressure of \(1.0\,\mathrm{atm}\) and temperature \(300\,\mathrm{K}\), an ideal gas has a mean free path \(l_1 = 9.0 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}\). If the gas is compressed so that its pressure becomes \(3.0\,\mathrm{atm}\) and its temperature rises to \(450\,\mathrm{K}\), find the new mean free path \(l_2\).

Solution: Since \(l = \frac{k_{\mathrm{B}} T}{\sqrt{2}\pi d^2 P}\), for a given gas (\(d\) constant) we have \(l \propto \frac{T}{P}\). Therefore:

\[ l_2 = l_1 \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = (9.0 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}) \times \left(\frac{450}{300}\right) \times \left(\frac{1.0}{3.0}\right) = 9.0 \times 10^{-8} \times 1.5 \times \frac{1}{3} = 4.5 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m} \]

\[ l_2 = l_1 \left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right)\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right) = (9.0 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m}) \times \left(\frac{450}{300}\right) \times \left(\frac{1.0}{3.0}\right) = 9.0 \times 10^{-8} \times 1.5 \times \frac{1}{3} = 4.5 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m} \]

Common Exam Pitfalls to Avoid
  • Celsius vs. Kelvin: Always convert temperature to kelvin (\(T(\mathrm{K}) = t(^\circ\mathrm{C}) + 273\)) before using \(PV = \mu RT\), \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} \propto \sqrt{T}\), or \(l \propto T/P\).
  • Vibrational Mode Counting: Each vibrational mode counts as 2 quadratic energy terms (kinetic + potential), contributing \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) per molecule, not \(\frac{1}{2}k_{\mathrm{B}}T\).
  • \(\overline{v^2}\) vs. \((\bar{v})^2\): Never equate \(v_{\mathrm{rms}}\) to the average speed \(\bar{v}\); for a Maxwellian speed distribution, \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} > \bar{v}\).
  • Molar Mass Units: When computing \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{3RT/M_0}\) in SI units (\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)), express \(M_0\) in \(\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}\) (e.g., \(32 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}\) for \(\mathrm{O}_2\)), not \(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\).

Quick Revision Recap:

  • \(PV = \mu RT = N k_{\mathrm{B}}T = \frac{2}{3}E_{\mathrm{trans}}\)
  • \(P = \frac{1}{3}\rho v_{\mathrm{rms}}^2\), \(\quad v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M_0}}\)
  • \(\bar{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{trans}} = \frac{3}{2}k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) (depends only on \(T\))
  • \(C_V = \frac{f_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2}R\), \(\quad C_P = C_V + R\), \(\quad \gamma = 1 + \frac{2}{f_{\mathrm{eff}}}\)
  • \(l = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\,n\pi d^2} = \frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\sqrt{2}\,\pi d^2 P}\)
বাংলা
পরীক্ষায় এড়িয়ে চলার মতো সাধারণ ভুল
  • সেলসিয়াস বনাম কেলভিন: \(PV = \mu RT\), \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} \propto \sqrt{T}\), বা \(l \propto T/P\) সূত্র ব্যবহারের আগে সর্বদা তাপমাত্রাকে কেলভিনে (\(T(\mathrm{K}) = t(^\circ\mathrm{C}) + 273\)) রূপান্তর করো।
  • কম্পন মোডের হিসাব: প্রতিটি কম্পন মোডে ২টি দ্বিঘাত পদ (গতিশক্তি + স্থিতিশক্তি) থাকে, তাই এটি \(\frac{1}{2}k_{\mathrm{B}}T\)-এর বদলে \(k_{\mathrm{B}}T\) শক্তি যোগ করে।
  • \(\overline{v^2}\) বনাম \((\bar{v})^2\): \(v_{\mathrm{rms}}\) এবং গড় বেগ \(\bar{v}\) এক নয়; সর্বদা \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} > \bar{v}\) হয়।
  • মোলার ভরের একক: SI এককে (\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)) \(v_{\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{3RT/M_0}\) নির্ণয়ের সময় \(M_0\)-কে \(\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}\) এককে (যেমন \(\mathrm{O}_2\)-এর জন্য \(32 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{kg\,mol^{-1}}\)) বসাবে।
