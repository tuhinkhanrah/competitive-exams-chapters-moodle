2. Position, Path Length, Displacement, and Point Object Approximation

ENGLISH

Point Object Approximation: An object in motion can be treated as a point object if its linear dimensions are negligible compared to the total distance travelled over a given duration. For example, a railway carriage travelling several hundred kilometers between two stations is safely approximated as a point mass, whereas a tumbling beaker falling from a desk cannot be treated as a point object since its dimensions are comparable to the fall height.

Frame of Reference & Position: Rectilinear motion is motion along a single straight axis (say, the \(x\)-axis). The position of a particle is specified by its coordinate relative to a chosen origin \(O\). Position values to the right of \(O\) are conventionally taken as positive (\(+x\)) and to the left as negative (\(-x\)).

Path Length (Distance) vs. Displacement:

  • Path Length: The total length of the actual trajectory traversed by an object between initial and final instants. It is a strictly non-negative scalar quantity and never decreases with time for a moving particle.
  • Displacement (\(\Delta x\)): The vector representing the change in position: \(\Delta x = x_2 - x_1\). In one dimension, direction is indicated completely by the algebraic sign (\(+\) or \(-\)). Displacement can be positive, negative, or zero.
Fundamental Relation • মৌলিক সম্পর্ক:
\[\text{Path Length} \ge |\text{Displacement}| \implies \frac{\text{Distance}}{|\text{Displacement}|} \ge 1\] Equality holds strictly if and only if the particle moves along a straight line without reversing its direction.

অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব (Path Length) বনাম সরণ (Displacement):

  • অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব: কোনো গতিশীল কণা দ্বারা নির্দিষ্ট সময় ব্যবধানে অতিক্রান্ত মোট গতিপথের দৈর্ঘ্য। এটি একটি স্কেলার রাশি এবং সর্বদা ধনাত্মক (বা শূন্য); সময়ের সাথে এটি কখনোই হ্রাস পায় না।

সর্বদা মনে রাখতে হবে: অতিক্রান্ত দূরত্ব \(\ge |\text{সরণ}|\)। কণাটি সরলরেখা বরাবর দিক পরিবর্তন না করে চললে কেবল তখনই দুটি রাশি সমান হয়।

3. Instantaneous Velocity, Speed, and the Calculus Limiting Process

ENGLISH

Average Velocity (\(\bar{v}\)): The displacement divided by the corresponding time interval: \[\bar{v} = \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{x_2 - x_1}{t_2 - t_1}\]

Average Speed: The total path length divided by the total time taken: \[\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Path Length}}{\Delta t} \ge |\bar{v}|\]

Instantaneous Velocity (\(v\)): Average velocity conveys how rapidly position changes over a finite duration, but provides no information about the velocity at any specific instant. Instantaneous velocity is the mathematical limit of the average velocity as the time interval approaches zero: \[v = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t} = \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\] Geometrically, on a position-time (\(x-t\)) graph, the ratio \(\frac{\Delta x}{\Delta t}\) represents the slope of the secant (chord) connecting points \(P_1\) and \(P_2\). As \(\Delta t \to 0\), the chord converges to the tangent line at point \(P(t)\). Thus:
The slope of the tangent to the position-time curve at any instant equals the instantaneous velocity.

Instantaneous Speed: Instantaneous speed is strictly the magnitude of instantaneous velocity: \[\text{Instantaneous Speed} = |v| = \left|\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|\] Crucial conceptual note: While average speed over a finite interval is greater than or equal to the magnitude of average velocity (\(\text{Average Speed} \ge |\bar{v}|\)), at any infinitesimal instant \(\mathrm{d}t\), the path length \(\mathrm{d}s\) equals \(|\mathrm{d}x|\). Therefore, instantaneous speed is identically equal to the magnitude of instantaneous velocity.

4. Acceleration: Average, Instantaneous, and Graphical Signatures

ENGLISH

Historical Context: In the seventeenth century, Galileo investigated whether change in velocity should be quantified with respect to distance or time. Through inclined plane and free-fall experiments, he established that in free fall, the change in velocity with distance decreases as distance increases, whereas the rate of change of velocity with respect to time remains constant. Hence, acceleration was defined as the time-derivative of velocity.

Definitions: \[\text{Average Acceleration: } \bar{a} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_2 - v_1}{t_2 - t_1}\] \[\text{Instantaneous Acceleration: } a = \lim_{\Delta t \to 0}\frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{\mathrm{d}^2x}{\mathrm{d}t^2} = v\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x}\]

5. Kinematic Equations for Uniform Acceleration

ENGLISH

For rectilinear motion with constant acceleration \(a\), the velocity-time relationship is linear.

Rigorous Derivation via Definite Calculus:

1. Velocity-Time Relation: \[a = \frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t} \implies \int_{v_0}^v \mathrm{d}v = \int_0^t a\,\mathrm{d}t \implies v - v_0 = at \implies v = v_0 + at \tag{1}\]

2. Position-Time Relation: \[v = \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} \implies \int_{x_0}^x \mathrm{d}x = \int_0^t (v_0 + at)\,\mathrm{d}t \implies x - x_0 = v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \tag{2}\]

3. Velocity-Position Relation: \[a = v\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}x} \implies \int_{v_0}^v v\,\mathrm{d}v = \int_{x_0}^x a\,\mathrm{d}x \implies \frac{v^2 - v_0^2}{2} = a(x - x_0) \implies v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(x - x_0) \tag{3}\]

4. Average Velocity Form & \(n^{\text{th}}\) Second Displacement: \[x - x_0 = \bar{v}t = \left(\frac{v_0 + v}{2}\right)t \tag{4}\] \[S_{n^{\text{th}}} = S_n - S_{n-1} = v_0 + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1) \tag{5}\]

6. Free Fall, Galileo's Law of Odd Numbers, Stopping Distance, and Reaction Time

ENGLISH

A. Motion Under Gravity (Free Fall)

An object released near the Earth's surface experiences downward gravitational acceleration \(g \approx 9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) (or \(10\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) when approximated). Choosing the upward direction as \(+y\), acceleration is \(a = -g\). If dropped from rest (\(v_0 = 0\)) at \(y = 0\): \[v = -gt\] \[y = -\frac{1}{2}gt^2\] \[v^2 = -2gy \implies |v| = \sqrt{2g|y|}\]

B. Galileo's Law of Odd Numbers

For a body released from rest under constant acceleration, the distances traversed in successive equal time intervals \(\tau\) stand to one another in the ratio of consecutive odd integers beginning with unity: \[y_1 : y_2 : y_3 : y_4 : \dots = 1 : 3 : 5 : 7 : \dots\]

\(0\) \(0\) \(0\) — —
\(\tau\) \(-\frac{1}{2}g\tau^2\) \(-y_0\) \(y_0\) 1
\(2\tau\) \(-\frac{1}{2}g(2\tau)^2 = -4y_0\) \(-4y_0\) \(4y_0 - y_0 = 3y_0\) 3
\(3\tau\) \(-\frac{1}{2}g(3\tau)^2 = -9y_0\) \(-9y_0\) \(9y_0 - 4y_0 = 5y_0\) 5
\(4\tau\) \(-\frac{1}{2}g(4\tau)^2 = -16y_0\) \(-16y_0\) \(16y_0 - 9y_0 = 7y_0\) 7

C. Stopping Distance of Vehicles

When brakes are applied, producing a constant deceleration \(-a\), the distance \(d_s\) traversed before coming to rest (\(v = 0\)) is obtained from \(0^2 = v_0^2 - 2ad_s\): \[d_s = \frac{v_0^2}{2a}\] Direct NEET Consequence: The stopping distance is directly proportional to the square of initial velocity (\(d_s \propto v_0^2\)). Doubling the initial speed quadruples the required stopping distance for the same braking capacity.

D. Reaction Time (\(t_r\))

Reaction time is the time elapsed between the perception of an event and the physical execution of a response. If a ruler dropped from rest slips through fingers by a distance \(d\) before being caught: \[d = \frac{1}{2}gt_r^2 \implies t_r = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{g}}\] The total distance travelled by a vehicle before coming to rest when an obstacle is spotted is: \[d_{\text{total}} = d_{\text{reaction}} + d_s = v_0 t_r + \frac{v_0^2}{2a}\]

৬. মুক্ত পতন, গ্যালিলিওর বিজোড় সংখ্যার সূত্র, থামার দূরত্ব ও প্রতিক্রিয়া কাল

১. মুক্ত পতন (Free Fall): অভিকর্ষজ ত্বরণ \(g\)-এর প্রভাবে নিম্নমুখী গতি। ওপরের দিক ধনাত্মক ধরলে \(a = -g\)। স্থির অবস্থা থেকে পতনের ক্ষেত্রে: \[v = -gt,\quad y = -\frac{1}{2}gt^2,\quad v^2 = 2g|y|\]

7. Relative Velocity in One Dimension

ENGLISH

All motion is relative. The velocity of body \(B\) relative to body \(A\) moving along the same straight line is: \[v_{BA} = v_B - v_A\] Similarly, the velocity of body \(A\) relative to body \(B\) is: \[v_{AB} = v_A - v_B = -v_{BA}\]

  • Bodies moving in the same direction: The relative speed is the difference between their individual speeds: \(|v_{BA}| = |v_B - v_A|\).
  • Bodies moving in opposite directions: Let \(v_A = +u\) and \(v_B = -v\). Then \(v_{BA} = -v - (+u) = -(u + v)\). The relative speed is the sum of their individual speeds: \(|v_{BA}| = u + v\).

৭. একমাত্রিক আপেক্ষিক বেগ

কোনো সরলরেখা বরাবর গতিশীল দুটি বস্তু \(A\) এবং \(B\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে, \(A\)-এর সাপেক্ষে \(B\)-এর আপেক্ষিক বেগ: \[v_{BA} = v_B - v_A\] এবং \(B\)-এর সাপেক্ষে \(A\)-এর আপেক্ষিক বেগ: \[v_{AB} = v_A - v_B = -v_{BA}\]

  • উভয় বস্তু একই দিকে গতিশীল হলে আপেক্ষিক দ্রুতি তাদের বেগের বিয়োগফলের সমান হয়।
  • বস্তু দুটি পরস্পর বিপরীত দিকে গতিশীল হলে আপেক্ষিক দ্রুতি তাদের বেগের যোগফলের সমান হয়।

Worked Example 1: Calculus Differentiation of Kinematic Functions
ENGLISH

The position of a particle along the \(x\)-axis is given by \(x(t) = a + bt^2\), where \(a = 8.5\,\mathrm{m}\), \(b = 2.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\), and \(t\) is in seconds.
(a) Find the instantaneous velocity at \(t = 0\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}\).
(b) What is the average velocity between \(t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}\) and \(t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}\)?

Solution:

(a) The instantaneous velocity is the derivative of position with respect to time: \[v(t) = \frac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t} = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(a + bt^2) = 2bt = 2(2.5)t = 5.0\,t\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] At \(t = 0\,\mathrm{s}\): \(v(0) = 5.0(0) = 0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
At \(t = 2.0\,\mathrm{s}\): \(v(2.0) = 5.0(2.0) = 10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).

(b) The average velocity over \([t_1, t_2]\) is given by: \[\bar{v} = \frac{x(t_2) - x(t_1)}{t_2 - t_1} = \frac{[a + b(4.0)^2] - [a + b(2.0)^2]}{4.0 - 2.0} = \frac{16b - 4b}{2.0} = 6.0\,b\] Substituting \(b = 2.5\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\): \[\bar{v} = 6.0 \times 2.5 = 15.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\]

Worked Example 2: Vertical Motion from a Building (Global Coordinate Approach)
ENGLISH

A ball is thrown vertically upward with a speed of \(20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) from the edge of the roof of a building \(25.0\,\mathrm{m}\) high. Taking \(g = 10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\):
(a) Find the maximum height reached above the ground.
(b) Find the total time taken before the ball strikes the ground at the base of the building.

Solution:

Let the ground be the origin (\(y = 0\)) and the upward vertical direction be positive (\(+y\)).
Initial position: \(y_0 = +25.0\,\mathrm{m}\).
Initial velocity: \(v_0 = +20.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Acceleration: \(a = -g = -10.0\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\).

(a) At the highest point, instantaneous velocity \(v = 0\): \[v^2 = v_0^2 + 2a(y_{\text{max}} - y_0) \implies 0 = (20.0)^2 + 2(-10.0)(y_{\text{max}} - 25.0)\] \[20.0(y_{\text{max}} - 25.0) = 400 \implies y_{\text{max}} - 25.0 = 20.0\,\mathrm{m} \implies y_{\text{max}} = 45.0\,\mathrm{m}\] The ball rises \(20.0\,\mathrm{m}\) above the roof, reaching \(45.0\,\mathrm{m}\) above the ground.

(b) When the ball hits the ground, final coordinate \(y = 0\). Using the general displacement formula: \[y = y_0 + v_0 t + \frac{1}{2}at^2 \implies 0 = 25.0 + 20.0\,t - \frac{1}{2}(10.0)t^2\] \[5.0t^2 - 20.0t - 25.0 = 0 \implies t^2 - 4t - 5 = 0 \implies (t - 5)(t + 1) = 0\] Since time \(t > 0\), we discard the negative root: \[t = 5.0\,\mathrm{s}\]

Worked Example 3: Relative Velocity - Police Chase
ENGLISH

A police van moving on a straight highway at \(30\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) fires a bullet at a thief's car speeding away in the same direction at \(192\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\). If the muzzle speed of the bullet is \(150\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), with what relative speed does the bullet strike the thief's car?

Solution:

First convert all speeds systematically to SI units (\(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)): \[v_P = 30 \times \frac{5}{18} = \frac{25}{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] \[v_T = 192 \times \frac{5}{18} = \frac{160}{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] Muzzle speed of the bullet relative to the police van: \(v_{B,P} = 150\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Velocity of the bullet relative to the ground: \[v_B = v_{B,P} + v_P = 150 + \frac{25}{3} = \frac{475}{3}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] The speed relevant for damaging the thief's car is the bullet's speed relative to the thief's car: \[v_{B,T} = v_B - v_T = \frac{475}{3} - \frac{160}{3} = \frac{315}{3} = 105\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\]

A car travels the first one-third distance of a straight track with speed \(v_1 = 10\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\), the second one-third distance with speed \(v_2 = 20\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\), and the final one-third distance with speed \(v_3 = 60\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\). Practice Question 3 (Galileo's Ratios for Equal Consecutive Distances)
ENGLISH

A small lead pellet is dropped from rest from the top of a cliff. It covers three successive equal vertical distances \(h\) in times \(t_1\), \(t_2\), and \(t_3\) respectively. The ratio \(t_1 : t_2 : t_3\) is:

(A) \(1 : \sqrt{2} : \sqrt{3}\)
(B) \(1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\)
(C) \(1 : 3 : 5\)
(D) \(1 : 4 : 9\)
Answer: (B) \(1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\)

Reasoning: For a body falling freely from rest, total distance fallen from the start in time \(t\) is \(y = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \implies t = \sqrt{\frac{2y}{g}}\).
Time to fall distance \(h\): \(t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}\).
Time to fall cumulative distance \(2h\): \(T_2 = \sqrt{\frac{2(2h)}{g}} = \sqrt{2}t_1\). Hence \(t_2 = T_2 - t_1 = (\sqrt{2} - 1)t_1\).
Time to fall cumulative distance \(3h\): \(T_3 = \sqrt{\frac{2(3h)}{g}} = \sqrt{3}t_1\). Hence \(t_3 = T_3 - T_2 = (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})t_1\).
Therefore, \(t_1 : t_2 : t_3 = 1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\).

(A) \(1 : \sqrt{2} : \sqrt{3}\)
(B) \(1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\)
(C) \(1 : 3 : 5\)
(D) \(1 : 4 : 9\)
সঠিক উত্তর: (B) \(1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\)

বিশদ সমাধান: স্থিরাবস্থা থেকে মোট পতনের সময় \(t = \sqrt{\frac{2y}{g}}\)।
প্রথম \(h\) দূরত্বের জন্য: \(t_1 = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}\)।
প্রথম \(2h\) দূরত্বের জন্য মোট সময়: \(T_2 = \sqrt{\frac{4h}{g}} = \sqrt{2}t_1 \implies t_2 = T_2 - t_1 = (\sqrt{2} - 1)t_1\)।
প্রথম \(3h\) দূরত্বের জন্য মোট সময়: \(T_3 = \sqrt{\frac{6h}{g}} = \sqrt{3}t_1 \implies t_3 = T_3 - T_2 = (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})t_1\)।
অনুপাত: \(1 : (\sqrt{2} - 1) : (\sqrt{3} - \sqrt{2})\)।

Practice Question 4 (Braking and Stopping Distance Scaling)
ENGLISH

A vehicle travelling at a speed of \(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) is brought to rest in a distance of \(40\,\mathrm{m}\) by applying brakes that produce constant retarding acceleration. If the same vehicle were moving at \(108\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\), what would be its stopping distance under the same retarding force?

(A) \(60\,\mathrm{m}\)
(B) \(80\,\mathrm{m}\)
(C) \(90\,\mathrm{m}\)
(D) \(120\,\mathrm{m}\)
Answer: (C) \(90\,\mathrm{m}\)

Reasoning: The stopping distance is given by \(d_s = \frac{v_0^2}{2a}\).
Since the braking deceleration \(a\) is constant: \(\frac{d_{s2}}{d_{s1}} = \left(\frac{v_{02}}{v_{01}}\right)^2\).
Given: \(v_{01} = 72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) and \(v_{02} = 108\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\).
Ratio of velocities: \(\frac{v_{02}}{v_{01}} = \frac{108}{72} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5\).
Hence, \(d_{s2} = d_{s1} \times (1.5)^2 = 40 \times 2.25 = 90\,\mathrm{m}\).

(C) \(90\,\mathrm{m}\)
(D) \(120\,\mathrm{m}\)
সঠিক উত্তর: (C) \(90\,\mathrm{m}\)

Practice Question 6 (Assertion & Reason • Kinematics)
ENGLISH

Assertion (A): A particle can have zero instantaneous velocity at an instant and yet possess a non-zero acceleration at that very instant.
Reason (R): Acceleration is defined as the rate of change of velocity with respect to time, not the ratio of velocity to time.

(A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
(B) Both (A) and (R) are true but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
(C) (A) is true but (R) is false.
(D) Both (A) and (R) are false.
Answer: (A) Both (A) and (R) are true and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).

Reasoning: When a projectile is thrown vertically upwards, at its uppermost trajectory point, its instantaneous velocity is momentarily zero (\(v = 0\)). However, the Earth continues to exert a downward gravitational force, causing a constant downward acceleration \(a = -g = -9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). This occurs precisely because acceleration measures the time-derivative of velocity (\(\frac{\mathrm{d}v}{\mathrm{d}t}\)), which can be non-zero even at an isolated instant where \(v = 0\).

Practice Question 7 (Displacement in n-th Second)
ENGLISH

A body starting from rest moves with a uniform acceleration along a straight line. The ratio of the distance traversed by the body during the \(5^{\text{th}}\) second to that traversed in \(5\) seconds is:

(A) \(9 : 25\)
(B) \(1 : 5\)
(C) \(9 : 50\)
(D) \(11 : 25\)
Answer: (A) \(9 : 25\)

Reasoning:
Distance travelled in the \(n^{\text{th}}\) second starting from rest (\(v_0 = 0\)): \[S_{n^{\text{th}}} = \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1) \implies S_{5^{\text{th}}} = \frac{a}{2}(2(5) - 1) = \frac{9a}{2}\] Total distance traversed in \(n = 5\) seconds: \[S_5 = \frac{1}{2}a(5)^2 = \frac{25a}{2}\] Taking the ratio: \[\frac{S_{5^{\text{th}}}}{S_5} = \frac{\frac{9a}{2}}{\frac{25a}{2}} = \frac{9}{25}\]

Practice Question 8 (Relative Motion of Oppositely Moving Trains)
ENGLISH

Two trains \(A\) and \(B\), each of length \(150\,\mathrm{m}\), are moving on parallel straight tracks in opposite directions with uniform speeds of \(54\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) and \(72\,\mathrm{km\,h^{-1}}\) respectively. The time of completely passing each other is:

(A) \(12.0\,\mathrm{s}\)
(B) \(8.57\,\mathrm{s}\)
(C) \(15.0\,\mathrm{s}\)
(D) \(6.0\,\mathrm{s}\)
Answer: (B) \(8.57\,\mathrm{s}\)

Reasoning:
Convert speeds to \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\): \[v_A = 54 \times \frac{5}{18} = 15\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}},\quad v_B = -72 \times \frac{5}{18} = -20\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\] Relative speed between the trains: \(v_{\text{rel}} = |v_A - v_B| = 15 - (-20) = 35\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
Total relative distance required to cross each other completely: \[D_{\text{rel}} = L_A + L_B = 150 + 150 = 300\,\mathrm{m}\] Time taken: \(t = \frac{D_{\text{rel}}}{v_{\text{rel}}} = \frac{300}{35} = \frac{60}{7} \approx 8.57\,\mathrm{s}\).

10. Common Pitfalls and Rapid Revision Points

NEET Exam Pitfall Alert • পরীক্ষার্থীদের জন্য সতর্কবার্তা
ENGLISH
  • Confusing Negative Acceleration with Deceleration: A negative sign on acceleration does not automatically imply retardation (slowing down). If a particle moves in the negative direction (\(v < 0\)) and has negative acceleration (\(a < 0\)), its speed is actually increasing! Speeding up occurs whenever \(v\) and \(a\) have the same algebraic sign. Deceleration occurs only when \(v\) and \(a\) have opposite signs.
  • Misapplying Kinematic Equations to Variable Acceleration: The equations \(v = v_0 + at\) and \(x = v_0t + \frac{1}{2}at^2\) are strictly valid only for constant acceleration. If acceleration is a function of time (e.g., \(a = 3t\)) or position (e.g., \(a = -kx\)), one must integrate from first principles: \(\int \mathrm{d}v = \int a\,\mathrm{d}t\) or \(\int v\,\mathrm{d}v = \int a\,\mathrm{d}x\).
  • Average Speed as Arithmetic Mean of Speeds: Average speed is defined strictly as \(\frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}\). The arithmetic mean \(\frac{v_1 + v_2}{2}\) applies only if the object travels with these speeds for equal intervals of time. If it travels over equal distances, the average speed is the harmonic mean: \(\frac{2v_1v_2}{v_1 + v_2}\).
বাংলা
  • ঋণাত্মক ত্বরণ মানেই মন্দন নয়: ত্বরণের চিহ্ন অক্ষের ধনাত্মক বা ঋণাত্মক দিকের ওপর নির্ভর করে। যদি বেগ ঋণাত্মক (\(v < 0\)) হয় এবং ত্বরণও ঋণাত্মক (\(a < 0\)) হয়, তবে কণার দ্রুতি বৃদ্ধি পাবে। বেগ ও ত্বরণ বিপরীতমুখী (বিপরীত চিহ্নের) হলেই কেবল মন্দন সৃষ্টি হয়।
  • পরিবর্তনশীল ত্বরণে সূত্রের ভুল প্রয়োগ: গতির ৩টি সাধারণ সমীকরণ শুধুমাত্র ধ্রুবক ত্বরণের ক্ষেত্রেই প্রযোজ্য। ত্বরণ সময়ের সাথে পরিবর্তনশীল হলে অবশ্যই ক্যালকুলাসের সমাকলন (integration) পদ্ধতি ব্যবহার করতে হবে।
  • গড় দ্রুতি নির্ণয়ে ভ্রান্তি: সর্বদা মনে রাখতে হবে: গড় দ্রুতি \(= \frac{\text{মোট দূরত্ব}}{\text{মোট সময়}}\)। সমান দূরত্বের ক্ষেত্রে গড় দ্রুতি হলো গুণোত্তর বা বিপরীত গড় (Harmonic Mean), সাধারণ পাটিগণিতীয় গড় নয়।
