English

For NEET UG, this chapter bridges cellular physiology (cytoskeleton and contractile proteins) with gross human anatomy (muscles, bones, joints, and clinical disorders).

  • Distinguish between general movement and locomotion, and classify cellular modes of movement in humans.
  • Compare the structural, functional, and regulatory features of skeletal, visceral (smooth), and cardiac muscles.
  • Analyze the ultrastructure of a sarcomere and explain the biochemical steps of the sliding filament theory.
  • Memorize the exact bone distribution of the axial (80) and appendicular (126) human skeleton (total 206 bones).
  • Classify joints structurally and identify key musculoskeletal disorders tested in medical entrance exams.

2. Movement vs. Locomotion & Cellular Modes  |  ২. চলন বনাম গমন এবং কোষীয় চলন

English

Movement refers to any change in posture, orientation, or position of a cell, organ, or body part. Locomotion is a specific subset of voluntary movement that results in a net change of place or location of the entire organism (for example, walking, running, swimming, or flying).

Core Principle: All locomotions are movements, but all movements are not locomotions. Furthermore, the same anatomical structure can serve both roles: cilia in Paramoecium propel food into the cytopharynx (movement) and drive swimming (locomotion); tentacles in Hydra capture prey and assist in somersaulting locomotion; human limbs change posture and enable walking.

Organisms perform locomotion to find food, shelter, mates, suitable breeding grounds, favorable climates, or to escape predators. At the cellular level, human cells exhibit three primary types of movement (alongside flagellar swimming in spermatozoa):

Mode of Movement Cellular Mechanism Human & Biological Examples
Amoeboid Protoplasmic streaming forming pseudopodia, driven by cytoskeletal microfilaments (actin). Human macrophages and leucocytes (white blood cells); unicellular Amoeba.
Ciliary Coordinated beating of hair-like membrane outgrowths (cilia) lining tubular tracts. Human trachea (clearing inhaled dust/foreign particles) and female reproductive tract/oviduct (passage of ova).
Flagellar Whip-like propulsion by flagella (cell membrane outgrowths). Swimming of human spermatozoa; water current in sponge canal system; locomotion in Euglena.
Muscular Active contraction and relaxation of specialized mesodermal muscle fibres. Movement of human limbs, jaws, tongue, and eyelids; requires coordinated muscular, skeletal, and neural activity.

3. Muscle Types & Sarcomere Ultrastructure  |  ৩. পেশির প্রকারভেদ ও সারকোমিয়ারের গঠন

English

Muscle is a specialized tissue of mesodermal origin contributing roughly 40–50% of adult human body weight. Its four defining physiological properties are excitability (response to stimuli), contractility (ability to shorten), extensibility (ability to stretch), and elasticity (recoil to original length).

Feature Skeletal Muscle Visceral (Smooth) Muscle Cardiac Muscle
Location Attached to skeletal bones. Inner walls of hollow visceral organs (alimentary canal, reproductive tract). Walls of the heart.
Appearance Striated (alternate dark and light bands). Non-striated (smooth). Striated; cells assemble in a branching pattern.
Neural Control Voluntary. Involuntary. Involuntary (not directly controlled by nervous system).
Primary Role Locomotion and posture changes. Transport of food in gut and gametes in genital tract. Rhythmic pumping of blood.

Structural Hierarchy of a Skeletal Muscle

  • Fascia & Fascicles: An organized skeletal muscle consists of numerous muscle bundles (fascicles) bound together by a common collagenous connective tissue layer called fascia.
  • Muscle Fibre (Anatomical Unit): Each fascicle contains many elongated muscle fibres. The plasma membrane is the sarcolemma, enclosing the sarcoplasm. Because sarcoplasm holds multiple nuclei, a muscle fibre is a syncytium. Its specialized endoplasmic reticulum—the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)—acts as the intracellular reservoir of \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) ions.
  • Myofibrils & Sarcomere (Functional Unit): Parallel rod-like filaments called myofibrils run inside the sarcoplasm and display alternating bands:
    • I-band (Isotropic / Light band): Contains only thin (actin) filaments and is bisected at its center by an elastic fibre called the Z-line.
    • A-band (Anisotropic / Dark band): Contains thick (myosin) filaments held centrally by a thin fibrous membrane called the M-line.
    • H-zone: The central portion of the A-band where thin filaments do not overlap thick filaments in a resting sarcomere.
    • Sarcomere: The segment between two successive Z-lines—specifically comprising one complete A-band plus two half I-bands.

4. Contractile Proteins & Sliding Filament Mechanism  |  ৪. সংকোচনশীল প্রোটিন ও স্লাইডিং ফিলামেন্ট কৌশল

English

Molecular Architecture of Thin and Thick Filaments

  • Actin (Thin) Filament: Built from two helically wound F-actin (filamentous) strands, each being a polymer of globular G-actin monomers. Two strands of tropomyosin run along the F-actin grooves. A trimeric regulatory protein, troponin, sits at regular intervals on tropomyosin; in the resting state, a subunit of troponin masks the myosin-binding active sites on actin.
  • Myosin (Thick) Filament: A polymer of monomeric units called meromyosins. Each meromyosin has two domains:
    • Heavy Meromyosin (HMM): Globular head + short arm, projecting outward at regular distances and angles as the cross arm. The globular head functions as an active ATPase enzyme and carries both ATP-binding sites and actin-binding sites.
    • Light Meromyosin (LMM): The coiled-coil tail forming the backbone of the thick filament.

Sequence of Excitation–Contraction Coupling

  1. A motor signal from the Central Nervous System (CNS) travels via a motor neuron (a motor neuron plus all muscle fibres it innervates forms a motor unit).
  2. At the neuromuscular junction (motor end plate), the neurotransmitter acetylcholine (ACh) is released, generating an action potential across the sarcolemma.
  3. The impulse spreads inward, triggering the sarcoplasmic reticulum to release \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) ions into the sarcoplasm.
  4. \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) binds to a subunit of troponin, causing a conformational shift that unmasks the active sites on actin.
  5. Using energy from ATP hydrolysis (\(\mathrm{ATP} \rightarrow \mathrm{ADP} + \mathrm{P_i}\)), the myosin head binds to actin to form a cross-bridge.
  6. The myosin head rotates, pulling the attached thin filaments toward the center of the A-band (M-line). The Z-lines are pulled inward, shortening the sarcomere.
  7. Cross-bridge detachment: Release of \(\mathrm{ADP}\) and \(\mathrm{P_i}\) returns myosin to its low-energy state; binding of a new ATP molecule breaks the cross-bridge. (Lack of ATP after death prevents detachment, causing rigidity/rigor mortis).
  8. Relaxation: When neural stimulation stops, \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) is actively pumped back into the sarcoplasmic cisternae, troponin re-masks actin, and Z-lines return to their resting positions.

  • Red Fibres (Aerobic): High myoglobin, abundant mitochondria, lower sarcoplasmic reticulum; sustained aerobic ATP production.
  • White Fibres (Anaerobic): Very low myoglobin (pale), few mitochondria, high sarcoplasmic reticulum; fast bursts via anaerobic glycolysis.

  3. এই উদ্দীপনা সারকোপ্লাজমিক রেটিকুলাম থেকে সারকোপ্লাজমে \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) আয়ন মুক্ত করে।
  4. \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) আয়ন ট্রপোনিনের সাথে যুক্ত হয়ে অ্যাকটিনের ওপর মায়োসিন বাঁধার সক্রিয় স্থানগুলি উন্মুক্ত করে।

  6. মায়োসিন মস্তক ঘুরে অ্যাকটিন তন্তুকে A-পট্টির কেন্দ্রের দিকে টানে; ফলে Z-রেখা ভিতরের দিকে সরে আসে ও সারকোমিয়ার সংকুচিত হয়।

  8. প্রসারণ (Relaxation): উদ্দীপনা বন্ধ হলে \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) পুনরায় সারকোপ্লাজমিক রেটিকুলামে পাম্প হয়ে ফিরে যায়, ট্রপোনিন অ্যাকটিনের সক্রিয় স্থান ঢেকে দেয় এবং Z-রেখা পূর্বের অবস্থানে ফিরে আসে।

The human skeletal framework consists of 206 bones and a few cartilages. Both are specialized connective tissues: bone has a very hard matrix enriched with calcium salts, whereas cartilage has a slightly pliable matrix due to chondroitin salts.

6. Joints & Musculoskeletal Disorders  |  ৬. অস্থিসন্ধি ও পেশি-কঙ্কালতন্ত্রের রোগব্যাধি

English

Joints are points of contact between bones, or between bones and cartilages, acting as fulcrums for muscle-generated force.

Joint Type Structural Feature & Mobility NEET High-Yield Examples
Fibrous Dense fibrous connective tissue; immovable. Sutures between flat skull bones forming the cranium.
Cartilaginous Joined by cartilage; permits limited movement. Between adjacent vertebrae; pubic symphysis.
Synovial Fluid-filled synovial cavity between articulating surfaces; allows considerable movement. Ball & socket: Humerus–glenoid / Femur–acetabulum; Hinge: Knee, phalanges; Pivot: Atlas–Axis; Gliding: Carpals; Saddle: Carpal–metacarpal of thumb.

Disorders of the Muscular & Skeletal System

  • Myasthenia gravis: Autoimmune disorder targeting the neuromuscular junction, causing progressive fatigue, weakening, and paralysis of skeletal muscles.
  • Muscular dystrophy: Progressive degeneration of skeletal muscle, mostly due to a genetic disorder.
  • Tetany: Rapid spasms (wild contractions) in muscles triggered by low \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\) levels in body fluids.
  • Arthritis: Inflammation of joints.
  • Osteoporosis: Age-related decrease in bone mass with elevated fracture risk; decreased estrogen levels are a common cause.
  • Gout: Joint inflammation caused by accumulation of uric acid crystals.

  • অস্টিওপোরোসিস (Osteoporosis): বয়সজনিত কারণে অস্থির ঘনত্ব হ্রাস ও ভঙ্গুরতা বৃদ্ধি; ইস্ট্রোজেন হরমোনের ঘাটতি এর একটি প্রধান কারণ।
  • গাউট (Gout): অস্থিসন্ধিতে ইউরিক অ্যাসিডের কেলাস জমে প্রদাহ সৃষ্টি।

English — Worked Example 1

Scenario (Assumed Values): Suppose a single resting sarcomere has an A-band length of \(1.6\,\mathrm{µm}\), a total I-band contribution (two half I-bands) of \(0.8\,\mathrm{µm}\), and an H-zone of \(0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\). During maximal contraction, each Z-line moves inward by \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\). Calculate the new sarcomere length, A-band length, and H-zone width.

Solution:

1. Resting Sarcomere Length: \(L_{\text{rest}} = \text{A-band} + \text{two half I-bands} = 1.6\,\mathrm{µm} + 0.8\,\mathrm{µm} = 2.4\,\mathrm{µm}\).

2. Total Shortening: Since both Z-lines move inward by \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\) each, total shortening \(= 2 \times 0.2\,\mathrm{µm} = 0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\). Thus, \(L_{\text{contracted}} = 2.4\,\mathrm{µm} - 0.4\,\mathrm{µm} = 2.0\,\mathrm{µm}\).

3. A-band Length: Thick filaments do not shorten; hence the A-band remains unchanged at \(1.6\,\mathrm{µm}\).

4. H-zone Width: Thin filaments slide inward by \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\) from each side, reducing the H-zone by \(2 \times 0.2\,\mathrm{µm} = 0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\). New H-zone \(= 0.4\,\mathrm{µm} - 0.4\,\mathrm{µm} = 0\,\mathrm{µm}\) (disappears).

বাংলা — সমাধানসহ উদাহরণ ১

প্রেক্ষাপট (অনুমিত মান): ধরা যাক, বিশ্রাম অবস্থায় একটি সারকোমিয়ারের A-পট্টির দৈর্ঘ্য \(1.6\,\mathrm{µm}\), মোট I-পট্টির (দুটি অর্ধেক I-পট্টি) দৈর্ঘ্য \(0.8\,\mathrm{µm}\) এবং H-অঞ্চলের প্রস্থ \(0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\)। সর্বোচ্চ সংকোচনের সময় প্রতিটি Z-রেখা \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\) ভিতরের দিকে সরে আসে। নতুন সারকোমিয়ারের দৈর্ঘ্য, A-পট্টির দৈর্ঘ্য এবং H-অঞ্চলের প্রস্থ নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

১. বিশ্রাম অবস্থায় সারকোমিয়ারের দৈর্ঘ্য: \(L_{\text{rest}} = 1.6\,\mathrm{µm} + 0.8\,\mathrm{µm} = 2.4\,\mathrm{µm}\)।

২. মোট সংকোচন: উভয় Z-রেখা \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\) করে ভিতরে সরে আসায় মোট দৈর্ঘ্য কমে \(= 2 \times 0.2\,\mathrm{µm} = 0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\)। সুতরাং সংকুচিত সারকোমিয়ারের দৈর্ঘ্য \(= 2.4 - 0.4 = 2.0\,\mathrm{µm}\)।

৩. A-পট্টির দৈর্ঘ্য: মোটা তন্তু সংকুচিত হয় না, তাই A-পট্টি \(1.6\,\mathrm{µm}\)-ই থাকবে।

৪. H-অঞ্চল: দুই দিক থেকে পাতলা তন্তু \(0.2\,\mathrm{µm}\) করে সরে আসায় H-অঞ্চল \(0.4\,\mathrm{µm}\) কমে যায়। নতুন H-অঞ্চল \(= 0.4 - 0.4 = 0\,\mathrm{µm}\) (অদৃশ্য হয়)।

English — Worked Example 2

Scenario: An anatomy student counts all bones in one complete right upper extremity (right forelimb plus right half of the pectoral girdle) and compares it with the total number of ribs in the human thoracic cage. Find the ratio of bones in the right upper extremity to total ribs.

Solution:

• Right forelimb bones: Humerus (1) + Radius (1) + Ulna (1) + Carpals (8) + Metacarpals (5) + Phalanges (14) \(= 30\) bones.

• Right pectoral girdle half: Clavicle (1) + Scapula (1) \(= 2\) bones.

• Total in right upper extremity \(= 30 + 2 = 32\) bones.

• Total ribs in human thoracic cage \(= 12\text{ pairs} = 24\) bones.

• Required ratio \(= 32 : 24 = 4 : 3\).

প্রেক্ষাপট: একজন ছাত্র একটি সম্পূর্ণ ডান ঊর্ধ্বাঙ্গের (ডান অগ্রপদ + বক্ষ-অস্থিচক্রের ডান অর্ধাংশ) মোট অস্থি গণনা করে এবং তা মানব বক্ষপিঞ্জরের মোট পঞ্জরাস্থির সংখ্যার সাথে তুলনা করে। ডান ঊর্ধ্বাঙ্গের অস্থি ও মোট পঞ্জরাস্থির অনুপাত নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

• ডান অগ্রপদের অস্থি: হিউমেরাস (১) + রেডিয়াস (১) + আলনা (১) + কার্পাল (৮) + মেটাকার্পাল (৫) + ফ্যালাঞ্জেস (১৪) \(= 30\) টি।

• বক্ষ-অস্থিচক্রের ডান অর্ধাংশ: ক্ল্যাভিকল (১) + স্ক্যাপুলা (১) \(= 2\) টি।

• ডান ঊর্ধ্বাঙ্গে মোট অস্থি \(= 30 + 2 = 32\) টি।

• নির্ণেয় অনুপাত \(= 32 : 24 = 4 : 3\)।

English
Common NEET Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Carpals vs. Tarsals: Each wrist has 8 carpals, while each ankle has 7 tarsals (the 30th leg bone is the patella).
  • A-band vs. I-band during contraction: The A-band NEVER shortens during contraction; only the I-band and H-zone shorten.
  • ATP role: ATP hydrolysis drives the power stroke, and binding of a new ATP is required to break the cross-bridge.
  • Skull count: Cranial (8) + Facial (14) = 22; adding 1 Hyoid + 6 Ear Ossicles gives 29 bones in the head region.

Final Recap: Locomotion is voluntary displacement powered by coordinated muscular, skeletal, and neural activity. Skeletal muscle contraction follows the sliding filament mechanism triggered by \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\)-troponin binding and ATPase-driven cross-bridge cycling. The 206 bones (80 axial + 126 appendicular) articulate via fibrous, cartilaginous, and synovial joints to enable precise movement.
