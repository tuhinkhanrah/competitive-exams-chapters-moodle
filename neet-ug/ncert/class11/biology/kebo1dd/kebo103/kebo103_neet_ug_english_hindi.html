2. Evolution of Plant Classification Systems | पादप वर्गीकरण प्रणालियों का विकास

English

Under the Five Kingdom framework proposed by R. H. Whittaker (1969), Kingdom Plantae encompasses eukaryotic, multicellular, chlorophyll-containing autotrophs: Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, and Angiosperms. Earlier classification schemes grouped Fungi, cell-walled Protista, and prokaryotic cyanobacteria (once called blue-green algae) with plants; modern taxonomy excludes all three from Plantae.

Botanical classification has progressed through three major paradigms, supplemented by modern analytical branches:

System / Branch Core Basis & Proponents Key Features & Limitations
Artificial System Superficial morphological traits (habit, leaf shape/count, colour) or androecium structure (Carolus Linnaeus). Separated closely related species; gave equal weight to vegetative and sexual traits even though vegetative traits are strongly modified by environment.
Natural System Natural affinities combining external morphology with ultrastructure, anatomy, embryology, and phytochemistry (George Bentham & Joseph Dalton Hooker for flowering plants). Reflects broader structural and developmental similarity among taxa.
Phylogenetic System Evolutionary relationships and shared common ancestry. Currently accepted standard; assumes members of a taxon descend from a common ancestor.
Numerical Taxonomy Computer-assisted processing of all observable characters assigned numerical codes. Evaluates hundreds of characters simultaneously with equal weightage; especially useful when fossil records are absent.
Cytotaxonomy Cytological data: chromosome number, structure, and meiotic behaviour. Resolves taxonomic ambiguities at cellular and chromosomal levels.
Chemotaxonomy Chemical constituents of plants (secondary metabolites, pigments, storage compounds). Clarifies relationships when morphological or fossil evidence is inconclusive.

3. Algae: Thalloid Photoautotrophs | शैवाल: थैलसाभ प्रकाश-स्वपोषी

English

General Nature & Habitats: Algae are chlorophyll-bearing, simple, thalloid, autotrophic organisms that are predominantly aquatic (freshwater and marine). They also inhabit moist stones, soils, and wood, and form symbiotic associations with fungi (lichens) or animals (for example, growing on the fur of the sloth bear). Their body organization spans microscopic unicells, motile colonies (Volvox), unbranched or branched filaments (Ulothrix, Spirogyra), and gigantic marine kelps.

Modes of Reproduction:

  • Vegetative: Fragmentation, where each broken segment regenerates a complete thallus.
  • Asexual: Production of spores, most commonly motile, flagellated zoospores that germinate into new plants.
  • Sexual: Fusion of two gametes, classified into three patterns:
    • Isogamous: Fusion of morphologically similar gametes—either flagellated and motile (Ulothrix) or non-flagellated and non-motile (Spirogyra).
    • Anisogamous: Fusion of two gametes that differ in size (certain species of Eudorina).
    • Oogamous: Fusion between one large, static (non-motile) female gamete and a smaller, motile male gamete (Volvox, Fucus).

Ecological & Commercial Importance: Algae perform at least half of Earth's total CO₂ fixation via photosynthesis, elevate dissolved O₂ in aquatic habitats, and act as primary producers for aquatic food webs. Around 70 marine species—including Porphyra, Laminaria, and Sargassum—are consumed as food. Marine brown and red algae yield water-holding hydrocolloids: algin (from brown algae) and carrageen (from red algae). Agar, extracted from Gelidium and Gracilaria, is used to culture microbes and prepare ice-creams and jellies. The unicellular protein-rich green alga Chlorella serves as a dietary supplement, including for space travellers.

Rhodophyceae thrive in warmer marine waters from sunlit surface layers to deep ocean zones with low light penetration, and exhibit oogamous sexual reproduction with complex post-fertilization events.

4. Bryophytes: Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom | ब्रायोफाइटा: पादप जगत के उभयचर

English

Why "Amphibians"? Bryophytes (liverworts and mosses) grow on damp, shaded soil in hills, yet they strictly require external water for sexual reproduction so male gametes can swim to the egg. Their plant body is more differentiated than algae—prostrate or erect and anchored by unicellular or multicellular rhizoids—but lacks true roots, stems, leaves, and vascular tissues.

Life Cycle & Alternation of Generations: The dominant, photosynthetic plant body is the haploid gametophyte (\(n\)). It bears multicellular sex organs: the antheridium (producing biflagellate antherozoids) and the flask-shaped archegonium (containing a single egg). Fertilization yields a diploid zygote (\(2n\)) that does not undergo immediate meiosis; instead, it divides mitotically to form a multicellular sporophyte (\(2n\)) differentiated into foot, seta, and capsule. The sporophyte is not free-living—it remains attached to and nourished by the photosynthetic gametophyte. Inside the capsule, spore mother cells undergo meiosis to release haploid spores (\(n\)), which germinate into new gametophytes.

Ecological & Economic Roles: Mosses alongside lichens are pioneer organisms that colonize bare rocks and decompose them, initiating ecological succession. Dense moss mats cushion falling rain and prevent soil erosion. Species of Sphagnum form peat (used as fuel) and serve as packing material for trans-shipment of living plants due to high water-holding capacity.

5. Pteridophytes: First Vascular Terrestrial Plants | टेरिडोफाइटा: प्रथम संवहनी स्थलीय पादप

English

Dominant Sporophyte & Vascularization: Pteridophytes (horsetails and ferns) are the earliest terrestrial plants to evolve true vascular tissues—xylem and phloem. Unlike bryophytes, the dominant phase is the diploid sporophyte (\(2n\)), clearly differentiated into true roots, stem, and leaves. Leaves may be small (microphylls, as in Selaginella) or large (macrophylls, as in ferns). They are valued as ornamentals, soil-binders, and medicinal plants.

Sporophylls, Prothallus & Restricted Distribution: Sporangia are borne on leaf-like appendages called sporophylls, which in genera like Selaginella and Equisetum compact into cone-like strobili. Meiosis inside sporangia produces haploid spores (\(n\)) that germinate into an inconspicuous, small, multicellular, free-living, mostly photosynthetic thalloid gametophyte—the prothallus. Because the prothallus requires cool, damp, shady habitats and water is essential for antherozoids to swim to the archegonial mouth, living pteridophytes remain restricted to narrow geographical zones.

Homospory vs. Heterospory & Seed Habit Precursor: Most pteridophytes produce uniform spores (homosporous). However, genera such as Selaginella and Salvinia are heterosporous, producing large megaspores (giving rise to female gametophytes) and small microspores (giving rise to male gametophytes). In heterosporous pteridophytes, female gametophytes are retained on the parent sporophyte for variable periods, and zygote development into young embryos occurs within the female gametophyte—an evolutionary milestone regarded as a precursor to the seed habit.

Four Classes: Psilopsida (Psilotum), Lycopsida (Selaginella, Lycopodium), Sphenopsida (Equisetum), and Pteropsida (Dryopteris, Pteris, Adiantum).

6. Gymnosperms & Angiosperms: Seed-Bearing Plants | जिम्नोस्पर्म एवं एंजियोस्पर्म: बीजधारी पादप

English

Gymnosperms ("Naked Seeds")

Morphology & Adaptations: Gymnosperms are shrubs and medium-to-tall trees (including the giant redwood Sequoia, among the tallest tree species) whose ovules are not enclosed by an ovary wall and remain exposed before and after fertilization—producing naked seeds. Roots are generally tap roots: Pinus forms fungal mycorrhiza, whereas Cycas develops specialized coralloid roots harbouring N₂-fixing cyanobacteria. Stems are unbranched in Cycas and branched in Pinus and Cedrus. Leaves withstand extreme temperature, humidity, and wind; pinnate leaves in Cycas persist for years, while conifers possess needle-like leaves with reduced surface area, thick cuticle, and sunken stomata to curb water loss.

Reproduction & Dependent Gametophytes: All gymnosperms are heterosporous. Sporophylls arrange spirally along an axis into lax or compact strobili (cones): microsporangiate (male) strobili bear microsporophylls and microsporangia, while macrosporangiate (female) strobili bear megasporophylls with ovules (megasporangia). In Pinus, male and female cones occur on the same tree; in Cycas, male cones and megasporophylls are borne on different trees. Microspores develop inside microsporangia into highly reduced, few-celled male gametophytes called pollen grains. Inside the ovule (nucellus protected by envelopes), one nucellar cell differentiates as the megaspore mother cell, dividing meiotically into four megaspores; one megaspore develops into a multicellular female gametophyte bearing two or more archegonia, retained within the megasporangium. Crucially, unlike bryophytes and pteridophytes, gymnosperm male and female gametophytes have no independent free-living existence. Wind-borne pollen reaches the ovule opening, and a pollen tube delivers male gametes near the archegonial mouth; the zygote forms an embryo and the ovule matures into an uncovered seed.

Angiosperms (Flowering Plants)

In angiosperms, pollen grains and ovules develop inside specialized reproductive structures called flowers, and seeds remain enclosed within fruits (developed from the ovary). Occurring across diverse habitats, angiosperms span from tiny aquatic Wolffia to towering Eucalyptus exceeding 100 m, supplying food, fodder, fuel, and medicines. They are classified into dicotyledons and monocotyledons.

एंजियोस्पर्म (पुष्पी पादप / आवृतबीजी)

Worked Example 1: Ploidy & Chromosome Count Across Plant Groups

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): Suppose a botanist studies four distinct plant species—a moss (Funaria), a liverwort (Marchantia), a fern (Dryopteris), and a gymnosperm (Cycas)—and assumes for calculation purposes that each species has a haploid chromosome number of \(n = 12\). Determine the chromosome number in: (a) moss protonemal cell, (b) moss spore mother cell, (c) liverwort gemma cell, (d) fern prothallus cell, and (e) Cycas nucellus cell.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  • (a) Moss protonemal cell: The protonema develops directly from a haploid spore and belongs to the gametophyte generation (\(n\)). Therefore, \(12\) chromosomes.
  • (b) Moss spore mother cell: Located inside the sporophyte capsule prior to meiosis, it is diploid (\(2n\)). Therefore, \(2 \times 12 = 24\) chromosomes.
  • (c) Liverwort gemma cell: Gemmae are asexual buds produced on the haploid thalloid gametophyte (\(n\)). Therefore, \(12\) chromosomes.
  • (d) Fern prothallus cell: The prothallus is the free-living haploid gametophyte (\(n\)) formed by spore germination. Therefore, \(12\) chromosomes.
  • (e) Cycas nucellus cell: The nucellus is somatic tissue of the ovule borne on the diploid sporophyte (\(2n\)). Therefore, \(2 \times 12 = 24\) chromosomes.

Q1. Which of the following statements accurately distinguishes modern phylogenetic classification from the artificial system proposed by Linnaeus?

  • (A) The artificial system relied on ultrastructure and embryology, whereas phylogenetic systems use only floral morphology.
  • (B) The artificial system gave equal weightage to vegetative and sexual characters, whereas phylogenetic systems are based on evolutionary relationships and common ancestry.
  • (C) Numerical taxonomy is identical to the artificial system because it ignores observable characters.
  • (D) Bentham and Hooker proposed an artificial system for flowering plants based solely on chromosome number.

Answer: (B)

Explanation: Linnaeus's artificial system used superficial morphological traits (vegetative or androecium structure) and gave equal weightage to vegetative and sexual characters, even though vegetative traits are easily altered by environment. Bentham and Hooker gave a natural system (not artificial), and numerical taxonomy processes all observable characters.

9. Common Pitfalls & NEET UG Revision Checklist | सामान्य त्रुटियां एवं पुनरावृत्ति सूची

English

High-Frequency NEET UG Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Blue-green algae (Cyanobacteria): Despite the historical name, they are prokaryotes (Kingdom Monera) and are not classified under Algae / Plantae.
  • Flagella in Algae: Red algae (Rhodophyceae) have zero flagellated stages. Brown algae have 2 unequal, lateral flagella; green algae have 2–8 equal, apical flagella.
  • Bryophyte vs. Pteridophyte Dominance: In bryophytes, the gametophyte is dominant and the sporophyte is nutritionally dependent; in pteridophytes, the sporophyte is dominant and the gametophyte (prothallus) is small and free-living.
  • Cycas vs. Pinus: Cycas is dioecious (male cones and megasporophylls on different trees) with coralloid roots and unbranched stem; Pinus is monoecious with mycorrhiza and branched stem.

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Verify pigment + reserve food + cell wall + flagellar profile for all three algal classes.
  • Confirm ploidy of every structure: protonema (\(n\)), prothallus (\(n\)), gemma (\(n\)), sporophyll (\(2n\)), nucellus (\(2n\)), megaspore (\(n\)).
  • Recall heterosporous pteridophyte examples: Selaginella and Salvinia.
हिन्दी

NEET UG में बचने योग्य प्रमुख त्रुटियां

  • नील-हरित शैवाल (सायनोबैक्टीरिया): पारंपरिक नाम के बावजूद ये प्रोकैरियोट (जगत मोनेरा) हैं और शैवाल / प्लांटी में शामिल नहीं हैं।
  • शैवाल में कशाभिका: लाल शैवाल (रोडोफाइसी) में कशाभिका-युक्त अवस्थाएं पूर्णतः अनुपस्थित होती हैं। भूरे शैवाल में 2 असमान, पार्श्व कशाभिकाएं; हरित शैवाल में 2–8 समान, शीर्षस्थ कशाभिकाएं होती हैं।
  • ब्रायोफाइटा बनाम टेरिडोफाइटा की प्रमुख अवस्था: ब्रायोफाइटा में युग्मकोद्भिद प्रभावी व बीजाणुद्भिद आश्रित होता है; टेरिडोफाइटा में बीजाणुद्भिद प्रभावी व युग्मकोद्भिद (प्रोथैलस) छोटा व स्वतंत्रजीवी होता है।
  • Cycas बनाम Pinus: Cycas एकलिंगाश्रयी (नर शंकु व गुरुबीजाणुपर्ण भिन्न वृक्षों पर), प्रवाल मूल व अशाखित तने वाला है; Pinus उभयलिंगाश्रयी, माइकोराइजा व शाखित तने वाला है।

त्वरित पुनरावृत्ति सूची

  • तीनों शैवाल वर्गों के वर्णक + संचित भोजन + कोशिका भित्ति + कशाभिका विवरण दोहराएं।
  • प्रत्येक संरचना की गुणितता सुनिश्चित करें: प्रोटोनिमा (\(n\)), प्रोथैलस (\(n\)), जेमा (\(n\)), बीजाणुपर्ण (\(2n\)), बीजांडकाय (\(2n\)), गुरुबीजाणु (\(n\))।
  • विषमबीजाणुक टेरिडोफाइटा के उदाहरण याद रखें: Selaginella और Salvinia।

10. Concise Final Recap | संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Kingdom Plantae exhibits a clear evolutionary trajectory: from thalloid, water-dependent Algae (classified by pigments and food reserves into Chlorophyceae, Phaeophyceae, and Rhodophyceae) to amphibious Bryophytes with dominant gametophytes and dependent sporophytes; onward to Pteridophytes, the first vascular plants with dominant sporophytes where heterospory laid the foundation for the seed habit; and finally to seed-bearing Gymnosperms (naked ovules, dependent gametophytes) and Angiosperms (flowers and fruit-enclosed seeds).

हिन्दी

पादप जगत एक स्पष्ट विकासीय क्रम दर्शाता है: थैलसाभ व जल-आश्रित शैवाल (वर्णक व संचित भोजन के आधार पर क्लोरोफाइसी, फियोफाइसी और रोडोफाइसी में विभाजित) से लेकर प्रभावी युग्मकोद्भिद व आश्रित बीजाणुद्भिद वाले उभयचर ब्रायोफाइटा; उसके बाद प्रभावी बीजाणुद्भिद वाले प्रथम संवहनी पादप टेरिडोफाइटा (जहां विषमबीजाणुता ने बीज प्रकृति की नींव रखी); और अंततः बीजधारी जिम्नोस्पर्म (नग्न बीजांड, आश्रित युग्मकोद्भिद) तथा एंजियोस्पर्म (पुष्प एवं फल के भीतर बंद बीज)।
