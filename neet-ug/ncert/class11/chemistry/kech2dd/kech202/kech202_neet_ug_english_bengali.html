2. Tetravalence of Carbon, Hybridisation & \(\pi\)-Bond Features

English

Historical Note & Catenation: Carbon atoms link with one another through strong covalent bonds to form long chains and rings—a unique property called catenation. In 1828, Friedrich Wöhler disproved the "vital force" theory by synthesising an organic compound, urea (\(\mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}\)), simply by heating inorganic ammonium cyanate (\(\mathrm{NH_4CNO}\)):

\[\mathrm{NH_4CNO} \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}\]

Later, Kolbe synthesised acetic acid (1845) and Berthelot synthesised methane (1856) from inorganic precursors, establishing modern synthetic organic chemistry.

Hybridisation and Molecular Properties

Carbon's tetravalence and molecular shapes are governed by the mixing of its \(2s\) and \(2p\) orbitals:

Hybridisation % \(s\)-Character Geometry & Bond Angle Relative Electronegativity Example
\(sp^3\) 25% Tetrahedral, 109.5° Lowest among the three Methane (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\))
\(sp^2\) 33.3% Trigonal planar, 120° Intermediate Ethene (\(\mathrm{C_2H_4}\))
\(sp\) 50% Linear, 180° Highest (electrons held closest to nucleus) Ethyne (\(\mathrm{C_2H_2}\))

Key Principle: Greater \(s\)-character pulls the hybrid orbital closer to the nucleus, making the C–H and C–C bonds shorter, stronger (higher bond enthalpy), and the carbon atom more electronegative.

Characteristics of \(\pi\) Bonds

  • A \(\pi\) bond forms by lateral (sideways) overlap of two mutually parallel unhybridised \(p\) orbitals perpendicular to the molecular plane (e.g., all six atoms of \(\mathrm{H_2C=CH_2}\) lie in one plane).
  • Rotating one \(\mathrm{CH_2}\) group relative to the other breaks this parallel overlap; hence, rotation about a \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bond is restricted.
  • The \(\pi\)-electron cloud lies above and below the internuclear plane, making \(\pi\) electrons loosely held and easily accessible to attacking electrophilic reagents. Thus, \(\pi\) bonds act as reactive centres.

  • \(\pi\) ইলেকট্রন মেঘ বন্ধন তলের উপরে ও নিচে অবস্থান করায় আক্রমণকারী বিকারকের কাছে সহজেই লভ্য হয়, ফলে বহুবন্ধনযুক্ত অণুতে \(\pi\) বন্ধন সক্রিয় কেন্দ্র হিসেবে কাজ করে।

3. Structural & Three-Dimensional Representations

English

Organic structures are written at varying levels of detail:

  • Complete Structural Formula: Shows every atom and every covalent bond as a dash (– for single, = for double, ≡ for triple). Lone pairs on heteroatoms (O, N, S, X) may be shown or omitted.
  • Condensed Structural Formula: Omits most bond dashes and groups identical attached units using subscripts, e.g., \(\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_4CH_3}\) for hexane.
  • Bond-Line Structural Formula: C and H atoms attached to carbon are omitted. Carbon–carbon bonds are drawn as zig-zag lines; every vertex (junction) and terminal represents a carbon atom satisfied with the required number of hydrogens. Heteroatoms (O, N, Cl, Br) and hydrogens attached to them (like –OH, –NH₂) are explicitly written.
  • 3-D Wedge-and-Dash Formula: Represents tetrahedral geometry on 2-D paper:
    • Normal line (—): bond lying in the plane of the paper.
    • Solid wedge (▲): bond projecting out of the plane towards the observer (broad end towards observer).
    • Dashed wedge (┆): bond projecting into the plane away from the observer.
  • Molecular Models: (1) Framework model shows only bond patterns; (2) Ball-and-stick model (or ball-and-spring for double bonds) shows both atoms and bonds; (3) Space-filling model emphasises the relative van der Waals volume of each atom without showing sticks.

  • ত্রিমাত্রিক ওয়েজ-ড্যাশ সংকেত (3-D Wedge-Dash Formula):

  • আণবিক মডেল: (১) ফ্রেমওয়ার্ক মডেল কেবল বন্ধনের বিন্যাস দেখায়; (২) বল-অ্যান্ড-স্টিক মডেল পরমাণু ও বন্ধন উভয়ই দেখায়; (৩) স্পেস-ফিলিং মডেল ভ্যান ডার ওয়ালস ব্যাসার্ধ অনুযায়ী প্রতিটি পরমাণুর আপেক্ষিক আয়তন তুলে ধরে।

4. Classification, Functional Groups & Homologous Series

English

Organic compounds are broadly classified by carbon skeleton into Acyclic (open-chain / aliphatic) and Cyclic (closed-chain / ring) compounds:

  • Alicyclic Compounds: Ring compounds with properties resembling aliphatic compounds. They can be homocyclic/carbocyclic (ring of C atoms only, e.g., cyclopropane, cyclohexane) or heterocyclic non-aromatic (ring contains O, N, or S, e.g., tetrahydrofuran).
  • Aromatic Compounds: Special conjugated planar ring systems. They include benzenoid compounds (benzene, aniline, naphthalene), non-benzenoid compounds (tropone), and heterocyclic aromatic compounds (furan, thiophene, pyridine).

Functional Group: An atom or group of atoms bonded to a carbon chain that dictates the characteristic chemical reactivity of the molecule (e.g., –OH, –CHO, –COOH).

Homologous Series: A family of compounds sharing the same functional group and general formula, where consecutive members differ by a \(-\mathrm{CH_2}-\) unit (14 u in molecular mass). Members show a regular gradation in physical properties and similar chemical reactions.

5. IUPAC System of Nomenclature

English

Rules for Branched & Cyclic Alkanes

  1. Longest Chain Rule: Identify the longest continuous carbon chain as the parent chain. If two chains have equal length, choose the one with the greater number of side chains (substituents).
  2. Lowest Locant Rule: Number the parent chain from the end that gives the lowest set of locants at the first point of difference (e.g., 2,5,6 is preferred over 3,4,7).
  3. Alphabetical Order: List different alkyl substituents alphabetically (ignoring multiplying prefixes like di-, tri-, sec-, tert-, but considering iso- and neo- as part of the fundamental name).
  4. Tie-breaker at Equivalent Positions: If two different substituents are at equivalent positions from opposite ends, give the lower number to the one that comes first alphabetically (e.g., 3-ethyl-6-methyloctane, not 6-ethyl-3-methyloctane).
  5. Branched Substituents & Cyclic Rings: Number the carbon of the branch attached to the parent chain as 1 and enclose the complex substituent in parentheses. For substituted cycloalkanes, a more-branched ring carbon gets the lower number if there is a tie.

Polyfunctional Compounds & Priority Order

Only one functional group is chosen as the principal functional group (indicated by the class suffix); all others become prefix substituents. Decreasing order of priority:

\[-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{SO_3H} > -\mathrm{COOR} > -\mathrm{COCl} > -\mathrm{CONH_2} > -\mathrm{CN} > -\mathrm{CHO} > >\mathrm{C=O} > -\mathrm{OH} > -\mathrm{NH_2} > >\mathrm{C=C}< > -\mathrm{C\equiv C}- \]

Always prefix substituents: –R (alkyl), –C₆H₅ (phenyl), –X (halo), –NO₂ (nitro), –OR (alkoxy).

Substituted Benzene Compounds

  • For disubstituted benzenes, 1,2-, 1,3-, and 1,4- positions correspond to ortho (o-), meta (m-), and para (p-) in trivial names.
  • For trisubstituted or higher derivatives, use the lowest locant rule (do not use o, m, p). If a common base name is used (e.g., aniline, phenol, anisole, toluene), its principal group gets position 1.

7. Fundamental Concepts in Organic Reaction Mechanisms

English

Covalent Bond Fission & Reactive Intermediates

  • Heterolytic Cleavage (Ionic/Polar): Both shared electrons stay with one fragment.
    • Carbocation: Carbon has a sextet of electrons and a positive charge (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH_3^+}\)). Carbon is \(sp^2\) hybridised with a trigonal planar shape and an empty perpendicular \(p\) orbital. Stability order: \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \mathrm{CH_3^+}\).
    • Carbanion: Carbon retains the electron pair and carries a negative charge (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_3C:^-}\)). Carbon is \(sp^3\) hybridised with a pyramidal (distorted tetrahedral) shape.
  • Homolytic Cleavage (Nonpolar/Free Radical): Each atom takes one electron from the shared pair (shown by half-headed fish-hook arrows), forming neutral free radicals with an unpaired electron. Stability order: \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > \dot{\mathrm{C}}\mathrm{H_3}\).

Nucleophiles vs. Electrophiles

  • Nucleophile (\(\mathrm{Nu:}\)) — "nucleus-seeking", electron-rich species that donate an electron pair to an electrophilic centre. Examples: \(\mathrm{HO^-}\), \(\mathrm{NC^-}\), \(\mathrm{HS^-}\), \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{R_3N:}\).
  • Electrophile (\(\mathrm{E^+}\)) — "electron-seeking", electron-deficient species that accept an electron pair from a nucleophilic centre. Examples: \(\mathrm{CH_3^+}\), \(\mathrm{NO_2^+}\), \(\mathrm{BF_3}\), \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\), carbonyl carbon (\(>\mathrm{C=O}\)), alkyl halide carbon (\(\mathrm{R_3C-X}\)).

Curved-Arrow Notation: A full-headed curved arrow shows the movement of an electron pair (from source to sink); a half-headed (fish-hook) arrow shows the movement of a single electron.

8. Electronic Displacement Effects & Reaction Types

English
  1. Inductive Effect (I effect — Permanent, \(\sigma\)-bond): Polarisation of a \(\sigma\)-bond due to an adjacent electronegative or electropositive group. It diminishes rapidly with distance and becomes negligible after 3 bonds.
    • Electron-withdrawing (\(-I\)): \(-\mathrm{NO_2}\), \(-\mathrm{CN}\), \(-\mathrm{COOH}\), \(-\mathrm{COOR}\), \(-\mathrm{OAr}\), halogens (F, Cl, Br, I).
    • Electron-donating (\(+I\)): Alkyl groups (\(-\mathrm{CH_3}\), \(-\mathrm{CH_2CH_3}\), \(-\mathrm{C(CH_3)_3}\)).
  2. Resonance / Mesomeric Effect (R or M effect — Permanent, \(\pi\)-bond): Delocalisation of \(\pi\) electrons or lone pairs in a conjugated system.
    • Canonical structures are hypothetical; the real molecule is a resonance hybrid with lower energy than any canonical form. The energy difference is called resonance stabilisation energy.
    • Stability rules for canonical forms: More covalent bonds > complete octets > less charge separation > negative charge on more electronegative atom.
    • \(+R\) groups (donate to ring/chain): –X, –OH, –OR, –OCOR, –NH₂, –NHR, –NR₂, –NHCOR.
    • \(-R\) groups (withdraw from ring/chain): –COOH, –CHO, >C=O, –CN, –NO₂.
  3. Electromeric Effect (E effect — Temporary): Complete transfer of a \(\pi\)-electron pair to one atom of a multiple bond only in the presence of an attacking reagent.
    • \(+E\) effect: \(\pi\) electrons shift toward the atom where the reagent attaches (e.g., \(\mathrm{H^+}\) adding to alkene).
    • \(-E\) effect: \(\pi\) electrons shift away from the atom where the reagent attaches (e.g., \(\mathrm{CN^-}\) adding to \(>\mathrm{C=O}\)).
  4. Hyperconjugation ("No-bond resonance" — Permanent): Delocalisation of \(\sigma\) electrons of an \(\alpha\)-\(\mathrm{C-H}\) bond of an alkyl group into an adjacent empty \(p\) orbital (carbocation) or \(\pi\) system (alkene/alkylarene). Greater the number of \(\alpha\)-hydrogens, greater the stability (e.g., \((\mathrm{CH_3)_3C^+}\) has 9 \(\alpha\)-H, while \(\mathrm{CH_3^+}\) has 0).

Types of Organic Reactions: (1) Substitution, (2) Addition, (3) Elimination, and (4) Rearrangement reactions.

9. Methods of Purification of Organic Compounds

English

Purity of an organic compound is verified by a sharp melting or boiling point, or via chromatographic/spectroscopic methods.

Purification Method Underlying Principle & Application
Sublimation Separates sublimable solids (solid → vapour directly, e.g., camphor) from non-sublimable impurities (e.g., \(\mathrm{CaSO_4}\)).
Crystallisation Based on solubility differences in a suitable solvent (sparingly soluble at room temp, highly soluble when hot). Activated charcoal removes coloured impurities.
Simple Distillation Separates volatile liquids from non-volatile impurities, or liquids with a large boiling point difference (e.g., chloroform b.p. 334 K & aniline b.p. 457 K).
Fractional Distillation Used when boiling points are close; uses a fractionating column where each condensation–vaporisation cycle is a theoretical plate (e.g., crude oil refining).
Distillation under Reduced Pressure For high-boiling liquids that decompose at or below their normal b.p.; lowering external pressure lowers the boiling point (e.g., glycerol from spent-lye).
Steam Distillation For steam-volatile, water-immiscible liquids. Mixture boils when \(p = p_1 + p_2\) (just below 373 K), e.g., aniline–water mixture.
Differential Extraction Shaking an aqueous mixture with an immiscible organic solvent in a separating funnel where the compound is more soluble.

Chromatography

  • Adsorption Chromatography: Based on differential adsorption on a solid stationary phase (silica gel or alumina) as a liquid mobile phase (eluant) moves over it. Includes Column Chromatography and Thin Layer Chromatography (TLC).
  • Retardation Factor (\(R_f\)) in TLC:
    \[R_f = \frac{\text{Distance moved by the substance from base line } (x)}{\text{Distance moved by the solvent front from base line } (y)}\]
    Colourless TLC spots are detected using UV light, iodine vapour (brown spots), or ninhydrin spray (for amino acids).
  • Partition Chromatography (e.g., Paper Chromatography): Based on continuous differential partitioning between a stationary liquid phase (water trapped in chromatography paper) and a mobile solvent phase.

কেলাসন (Crystallisation) নির্দিষ্ট দ্রাবকে যৌগ ও অশুদ্ধির দ্রাব্যতার পার্থক্যের ওপর ভিত্তি করে (কক্ষ তাপমাত্রায় কম দ্রাব্য, কিন্তু উত্তপ্ত অবস্থায় বেশি দ্রাব্য)। রঙিন অশুদ্ধি দূর করতে সক্রিয় চারকোল ব্যবহৃত হয়।
সাধারণ পাতন (Simple Distillation) স্ফুটনাঙ্কের যথেষ্ট পার্থক্যযুক্ত দুটি তরল পৃথক করতে (যেমন ক্লোরোফর্ম 334 K ও অ্যানিলিন 457 K)।
আংশিক পাতন (Fractional Distillation) দুটি তরলের স্ফুটনাঙ্কের পার্থক্য কম হলে অংশক স্তম্ভ (fractionating column) ব্যবহার করা হয় (যেমন পেট্রোলিয়াম শোধন)।
নিম্নচাপ পাতন (Reduced Pressure) উচ্চ স্ফুটনাঙ্কযুক্ত তরল যা স্ফুটনাঙ্কের আগে বিয়োজিত হয় (যেমন স্পেন্ট-লাই থেকে গ্লিসারল পৃথকীকরণ)।
বাষ্প পাতন (Steam Distillation) জলে অদ্রবণীয় কিন্তু বাষ্প-উদ্বায়ী যৌগের জন্য; \(p = p_1 + p_2\) শর্তে 373 K-এর নিচেই তরল ফোটে (যেমন অ্যানিলিন–জল মিশ্রণ)।
পৃথকীকরণ নিষ্কাশন (Differential Extraction) জলীয় মাধ্যম থেকে অমিশ্রণীয় জৈব দ্রাবকের সাহায্যে সেপারেটিং ফানেলের মাধ্যমে যৌগ পৃথক করা।

11. Quantitative Analysis (Estimation Formulas)

English

Let \(m\) be the mass (in g) of the organic compound taken in each experiment:

1. Carbon & Hydrogen (Liebig's Combustion Method): \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) (\(m_1\) g) absorbed in anhydrous \(\mathrm{CaCl_2}\); \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) (\(m_2\) g) absorbed in \(\mathrm{KOH}\) solution.

\[\%\mathrm{C} = \frac{12 \times m_2 \times 100}{44 \times m} \qquad \%\mathrm{H} = \frac{2 \times m_1 \times 100}{18 \times m}\]

2. Nitrogen — Dumas Method: \(\mathrm{N_2}\) collected over \(\mathrm{KOH}\) at pressure \(p_1 = p_{\text{atm}} - \text{aqueous tension}\) and temperature \(T_1\). Volume at STP, \(V = \frac{p_1 V_1 \times 273}{760 \times T_1}\).

\[\%\mathrm{N} = \frac{28 \times V(\text{in mL}) \times 100}{22400 \times m}\]

3. Nitrogen — Kjeldahl's Method: \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) absorbed in \(V\text{ mL}\) of \(M\text{ molar }\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\); excess acid titrated with \(V_1\text{ mL}\) of \(M\text{ molar }\mathrm{NaOH}\).

\[\%\mathrm{N} = \frac{1.4 \times M \times 2\left(V - \frac{V_1}{2}\right)}{m}\]

Limitation: Kjeldahl's method is not applicable to nitro (\(-\mathrm{NO_2}\)), azo (\(-\mathrm{N=N}-\)), and ring-nitrogen compounds (e.g., pyridine).

4. Halogens (Carius Method): Mass of \(\mathrm{AgX}\) formed = \(m_1\text{ g}\).

\[\%\mathrm{X} = \frac{\text{Atomic mass of X} \times m_1 \times 100}{\text{Molar mass of AgX} \times m}\]

5. Sulphur (Carius Method): Mass of \(\mathrm{BaSO_4}\) (molar mass = \(233\text{ g mol}^{-1}\)) formed = \(m_1\text{ g}\).

\[\%\mathrm{S} = \frac{32 \times m_1 \times 100}{233 \times m}\]

6. Phosphorus: As \(\mathrm{(NH_4)_3PO_4\cdot 12MoO_3}\) (molar mass = \(1877\text{ g mol}^{-1}\)) or \(\mathrm{Mg_2P_2O_7}\) (molar mass = \(222\text{ g mol}^{-1}\)):

\[\%\mathrm{P} = \frac{31 \times m_1 \times 100}{1877 \times m} \quad \text{or} \quad \%\mathrm{P} = \frac{62 \times m_1 \times 100}{222 \times m}\]

7. Oxygen: Usually by difference (\(100 - \sum\%\text{ other elements}\)), or directly via \(\mathrm{O_2} \xrightarrow{\text{C, 1373 K}} \mathrm{CO} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{I_2O_5}} \mathrm{CO_2}\) (\(m_1\) g): \(\%\mathrm{O} = \frac{32 \times m_1 \times 100}{88 \times m}\).

১. কার্বন ও হাইড্রোজেন (দহন পদ্ধতি): অনার্দ্র \(\mathrm{CaCl_2}\)-এ শোষিত \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) (\(m_1\text{ g}\)) এবং \(\mathrm{KOH}\) দ্রবণে শোষিত \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) (\(m_2\text{ g}\))।

৪. হ্যালোজেন (ক্যারিয়াস পদ্ধতি): উৎপন্ন \(\mathrm{AgX}\)-এর ভর \(m_1\text{ g}\) হলে, \(\%\mathrm{X} = \frac{\text{X-এর পারমাণবিক ভর} \times m_1 \times 100}{\mathrm{AgX}\text{-এর আণবিক ভর} \times m}\)

Worked Example 1: Hybridisation & Bond Counting
English

Problem: Determine the hybridisation of each carbon atom (from left to right) and the total number of \(\sigma\) and \(\pi\) bonds in \(\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-C\equiv N}\).

Solution:

  • C-1 (\(\mathrm{CH_3}-\)): 4 single bonds → \(sp^3\)
  • C-2 (\(-\mathrm{CH}=\)): 1 double bond, 2 single bonds → \(sp^2\)
  • C-3 (\(=\mathrm{CH}-\)): 1 double bond, 2 single bonds → \(sp^2\)
  • C-4 (\(-\mathrm{C\equiv N}\)): 1 triple bond, 1 single bond → \(sp\)
  • Bond count: Total \(\sigma\) bonds = \(5\text{ (C–H)} + 3\text{ (C–C)} + 1\text{ (C–N)} = 9\,\sigma\) bonds. Total \(\pi\) bonds = \(1\text{ (C=C)} + 2\text{ (C}\equiv\text{N)} = 3\,\pi\) bonds.
বাংলা (Bengali)

সমস্যা: \(\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH-C\equiv N}\) যৌগটিতে বাম দিক থেকে প্রতিটি কার্বন পরমাণুর সংকরায়ণ এবং মোট \(\sigma\) ও \(\pi\) বন্ধনের সংখ্যা নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

  • C-1 (\(\mathrm{CH_3}-\)): ৪টি একক বন্ধন → \(sp^3\)
  • C-2 (\(-\mathrm{CH}=\)): ১টি দ্বিবন্ধন, ২টি একক বন্ধন → \(sp^2\)
  • C-3 (\(=\mathrm{CH}-\)): ১টি দ্বিবন্ধন, ২টি একক বন্ধন → \(sp^2\)
  • C-4 (\(-\mathrm{C\equiv N}\)): ১টি ত্রিবন্ধন, ১টি একক বন্ধন → \(sp\)
  • বন্ধন সংখ্যা: মোট \(\sigma\) বন্ধন = \(5\text{ (C–H)} + 3\text{ (C–C)} + 1\text{ (C–N)} = 9\)টি। মোট \(\pi\) বন্ধন = \(1\text{ (C=C)} + 2\text{ (C}\equiv\text{N)} = 3\)টি।
Worked Example 2: IUPAC Naming of Polyfunctional Compound
English

Problem: Write the systematic IUPAC name for \(\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CO-CH_2-COOH}\).

Solution:

The principal functional group (highest priority) is \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) (suffix: -oic acid). Numbering starts from the carboxylic carbon (C-1):

\(\overset{6}{\mathrm{CH_3}}-\overset{5}{\mathrm{CH}}(\mathrm{OH})-\overset{4}{\mathrm{CH_2}}-\overset{3}{\mathrm{CO}}-\overset{2}{\mathrm{CH_2}}-\overset{1}{\mathrm{COOH}}\)

Subordinate groups: 5-hydroxy and 3-oxo (alphabetically: hydroxy before oxo). Parent chain = 6 carbons (hexane).

IUPAC Name: 5-Hydroxy-3-oxohexanoic acid.

বাংলা (Bengali)

সমস্যা: \(\mathrm{CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_2-CO-CH_2-COOH}\) যৌগটির IUPAC নাম লেখো।

সমাধান:

এখানে সর্বোচ্চ অগ্রাধিকারপ্রাপ্ত প্রধান কার্যকরী মূলক হলো \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) (অনুলগ্ন: -oic acid)। কার্বক্সিলিক কার্বনকে ১ নম্বর ধরে গণনা করলে ৩ নম্বরে কিটো (oxo) এবং ৫ নম্বরে হাইড্রক্সি (hydroxy) মূলক রয়েছে।

Worked Example 3: Quantitative Estimation — Combustion (Hypothetical Sample)
English

Problem: On complete combustion, \(0.30\,\mathrm{g}\) of a hypothetical organic compound gave \(0.44\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) and \(0.18\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{H_2O}\). Calculate the percentage of carbon and hydrogen.

14. Common Pitfalls & NEET UG Revision Checklist

English

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

  • Forgetting to include the principal group carbon in the parent chain: Carbon of \(-\mathrm{COOH}\), \(-\mathrm{CHO}\), and \(-\mathrm{CN}\) must always be counted as C-1 of the parent chain.
  • Alphabetical prefix trap: Ignore di-, tri-, sec-, tert- when alphabetising substituents, but do alphabetise iso- and neo- under 'i' and 'n'.
  • Acetic acid vs. \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) in Sulphur test: Always acidify Lassaigne's extract with acetic acid (never \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)) before adding lead acetate, otherwise \(\mathrm{PbSO_4}\) precipitates.
  • Boiling with conc. \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) before \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\): Essential when N or S is present, to decompose \(\mathrm{NaCN}\) and \(\mathrm{Na_2S}\) which otherwise form \(\mathrm{AgCN}\) or \(\mathrm{Ag_2S}\) precipitates.
  • Forgetting aqueous tension in Dumas method: Always subtract aqueous tension from total pressure before converting \(V_1\) to STP.

Revision Checklist

  • [ ] Hybridisation & % \(s\)-character vs. electronegativity & bond length.
  • [ ] Functional group priority order from \(-\mathrm{COOH}\) down to alkynes.
  • [ ] Stability orders: Carbocations & Free Radicals (\(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ\)), Inductive (\(-I/+I\)), Resonance (\(-R/+R\)), Hyperconjugation (\(\alpha\)-H count).
  • [ ] All qualitative colours (Prussian blue, Blood-red, Violet, Black \(\mathrm{PbS}\), Yellow phosphomolybdate) & quantitative formulas.
