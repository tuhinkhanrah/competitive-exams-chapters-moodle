1. Learning Objectives & Overview / अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं संक्षिप्त परिचय

English

Mendelian genetics established that discrete hereditary factors pass from parents to offspring, but their chemical identity remained unknown until the mid-twentieth century. Living cells contain two nucleic acids—deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA). While DNA serves as the primary repository of genetic information in nearly all organisms, RNA performs messenger, adapter, structural, catalytic, and (in certain viruses) genetic roles.

After studying this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Analyze the chemical polarity, base-pairing geometry, and packaging of DNA into nucleosomes and chromatin.
  • Evaluate classical experiments proving DNA as the genetic material and semiconservative replication.
  • Trace the flow of genetic information from DNA to mRNA (transcription) and polypeptide (translation).
  • Interpret genetic code properties, point/frameshift mutations, and negative regulation of the lac operon.
  • Apply key numerical findings of the Human Genome Project and VNTR-based DNA fingerprinting to NEET UG problems.

2. Nucleic Acid Architecture & DNA Packaging / न्यूक्लिक अम्ल संरचना एवं डीएनए पैकेजिंग

English

2.1 Chemical Structure of a Polynucleotide Chain

A nucleotide consists of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar (ribose in RNA; 2'-deoxyribose in DNA), and a phosphate group. Nitrogenous bases belong to two classes: purines (Adenine, A; Guanine, G) and pyrimidines (Cytosine, C; Thymine, T; Uracil, U). DNA contains A, G, C, and T (chemically 5-methyl uracil), whereas RNA contains U instead of T.

  • Nucleoside formation: A nitrogenous base attaches to the 1'-carbon of the pentose sugar via an N-glycosidic linkage (e.g., adenosine, deoxyadenosine, deoxythymidine).
  • Nucleotide formation: A phosphate group esterifies to the 5'-OH of a nucleoside via a phosphoester linkage.
  • Chain elongation: Adjacent nucleotides join through 3'–5' phosphodiester linkages, creating a sugar-phosphate backbone with projecting bases. One terminus has a free 5'-phosphate (5'-end) and the opposite terminus has a free 3'-OH group (3'-end).
  • Chemical reactivity difference: Every ribonucleotide in RNA possesses a reactive 2'-OH group, making RNA chemically labile compared to DNA.

2.2 Double-Helix Geometry & Central Dogma

Friedrich Meischer first isolated nuclear acidic material ('nuclein') in 1869. In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick proposed the right-handed B-DNA double helix using X-ray diffraction data from Maurice Wilkins and Rosalind Franklin alongside Erwin Chargaff's base-equivalence rules.

  • Antiparallel polarity: One strand runs \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) and its partner runs \(3' \rightarrow 5'\).
  • Complementary H-bonding: Adenine pairs with Thymine via two hydrogen bonds (\(A = T\)), while Guanine pairs with Cytosine via three hydrogen bonds (\(G \equiv C\)). Always pairing a two-ring purine with a single-ring pyrimidine maintains a nearly uniform inter-strand diameter.
  • Helical dimensions: Pitch = \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)) with roughly \(10\,\mathrm{bp}\) per turn; axial rise per base pair = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\)). Base-pair stacking and H-bonds together stabilize the helix.
  • Central Dogma: Proposed by Francis Crick, genetic information flows as \(\text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{transcription}} \text{RNA} \xrightarrow{\text{translation}} \text{Protein}\). In retroviruses, reverse transcription synthesizes DNA from RNA.

2.3 Packaging of DNA into Chromatin

A diploid human cell holds \(6.6 \times 10^9\,\mathrm{bp}\), giving a total DNA contour length of \(6.6 \times 10^9 \times 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.2\,\mathrm{m}\)—far exceeding a nucleus of diameter \(\approx 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\).

  • Prokaryotes (e.g., E. coli): Negatively charged DNA is folded into large loops stabilized by positively charged proteins within a region called the nucleoid.
  • Eukaryotes: Negatively charged DNA wraps around a positively charged histone octamer (rich in basic amino acids lysine and arginine) to form a nucleosome. Each typical nucleosome encloses ~\(200\,\mathrm{bp}\) of DNA and is sealed by linker histone H1, appearing as 'beads-on-string' under electron microscopy.
  • Higher-order folding: Chromatin fibers further condense during metaphase with the aid of Non-Histone Chromosomal (NHC) proteins.
  • Euchromatin vs. Heterochromatin: Loosely packed, lightly staining, transcriptionally active chromatin is euchromatin; densely packed, darkly staining, transcriptionally inactive chromatin is heterochromatin.

2.2 द्विकुंडलिनी ज्यामिति एवं केंद्रीय सिद्धांत (Central Dogma)

1869 में फ्रेडरिक मीशर ने केंद्रकीय अम्लीय पदार्थ ('न्यूक्लिन') की खोज की। 1953 में जेम्स वॉटसन तथा फ्रांसिस क्रिक ने विल्किंस व फ्रैंकलिन के एक्स-रे विवर्तन आँकड़ों तथा इरविन चारगाफ के नियमों के आधार पर दक्षिणहस्त (right-handed) B-DNA द्विकुंडलिनी मॉडल प्रस्तुत किया।

  • प्रतिसमानांतर ध्रुवता: एक रज्जुक \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) तथा दूसरा \(3' \rightarrow 5'\) दिशा में होता है।
  • पूरक हाइड्रोजन बंधन: A और T के बीच दो हाइड्रोजन बंध (\(A = T\)) तथा G और C के बीच तीन हाइड्रोजन बंध (\(G \equiv C\)) बनते हैं। सदैव प्यूरीन के सामने पिरिमिडीन आने से दोनों रज्जुकों के बीच की दूरी लगभग समान रहती है।
  • कुंडलिनी माप: पिच = \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)), प्रति चक्कर लगभग \(10\,\mathrm{bp}\), तथा दो क्रमागत क्षार-युग्मों के बीच की दूरी = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\))।
  • केंद्रीय सिद्धांत: फ्रांसिस क्रिक के अनुसार आनुवंशिक सूचना का प्रवाह \(\text{DNA} \rightarrow \text{RNA} \rightarrow \text{Protein}\) होता है। कुछ विषाणुओं में यह विपरीत दिशा (RNA से DNA) में होता है।

2.3 क्रोमैटिन में डीएनए की पैकेजिंग

मानव द्विगुणित कोशिका में \(6.6 \times 10^9\,\mathrm{bp}\) होते हैं, जिनकी कुल लंबाई लगभग \(2.2\,\mathrm{m}\) होती है, जबकि केंद्रक का व्यास लगभग \(10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\) होता है।

  • प्रोकैरियोट्स (जैसे E. coli): ऋणावेशित DNA धनावेशित प्रोटीनों के साथ बड़े लूप में न्यूक्लियॉइड क्षेत्र में व्यवस्थित रहता है।
  • यूकैरियोट्स: ऋणावेशित DNA, क्षारीय अमीनो अम्ल लाइसीन व आर्जीनीन से समृद्ध धनावेशित हिस्टोन अष्टक (histone octamer) के चारों ओर लिपटकर न्यूक्लियोसोम बनाता है। एक न्यूक्लियोसोम में ~\(200\,\mathrm{bp}\) DNA होता है, जो इलेक्ट्रॉन सूक्ष्मदर्शी में 'डोरी पर मोती' जैसा दिखता है।
  • उच्च-स्तरीय संघनन: मेटाफेज में गुणसूत्र बनाने के लिए गैर-हिस्टोन गुणसूत्री (NHC) प्रोटीन आवश्यक होते हैं।

3. Search for Genetic Material & RNA World / आनुवंशिक पदार्थ की खोज एवं आरएनए जगत

English

3.1 Key Experimental Proofs

  • Griffith's Transforming Principle (1928): Working with Streptococcus pneumoniae, Frederick Griffith observed that virulent S-strain (smooth polysaccharide coat) killed mice, whereas non-virulent R-strain (rough, no coat) did not. Heat-killed S-strain did not kill mice, but a mixture of heat-killed S + live R killed mice and yielded live S bacteria—proving transfer of a 'transforming principle' (biochemical nature not yet identified).
  • Avery, MacLeod & McCarty (1933–44): Using purified fractions from heat-killed S cells, they found that proteases and RNases did not block transformation, whereas DNase completely inhibited transformation, establishing DNA as the transforming substance.
  • Hershey & Chase (1952) — Unequivocal Proof: They grew T2 bacteriophages in two separate media: one with radioactive phosphorus (\(^{32}\mathrm{P}\), labels DNA because DNA contains P, not S) and one with radioactive sulfur (\(^{35}\mathrm{S}\), labels protein coat because proteins contain S, not P). After infection of E. coli, blending (to shear viral coats) and centrifugation (to pellet heavier bacteria):
    • \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) radioactivity was detected inside the bacterial pellet.
    • \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) radioactivity remained in the supernatant.
    This proved DNA enters the host and directs viral synthesis.

3.2 Criteria for Genetic Material & RNA World

An ideal genetic material must: (1) replicate accurately, (2) remain chemically and structurally stable, (3) allow slow mutations for evolution, and (4) express itself as Mendelian characters. Both DNA and RNA replicate and mutate, yet DNA is superior for long-term storage because it lacks the reactive 2'-OH group and uses thymine (conferring repair advantages) instead of uracil. RNA, being catalytic (ribozymes) and labile, mutates faster (as seen in RNA viruses like TMV and QB bacteriophage) and excels at information transmission. Evidence suggests RNA was the first genetic material (RNA World), around which translation and splicing evolved before DNA emerged as a more stable archive.

    • \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) जीवाणु कोशिकाओं (pellet) के भीतर पाया गया।
    • \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) केवल अधिप्लवी (supernatant) में पाया गया।
    इससे सिद्ध हुआ कि प्रोटीन नहीं, बल्कि DNA ही आनुवंशिक पदार्थ है।

3.2 आनुवंशिक पदार्थ के गुण एवं आरएनए जगत

आनुवंशिक पदार्थ को (1) प्रतिकृति बनाने में सक्षम, (2) रासायनिक व संरचनात्मक रूप से स्थायी, (3) धीमे उत्परिवर्तन की संभावना वाला, तथा (4) मेंडेलियन लक्षणों के रूप में अभिव्यक्त होने योग्य होना चाहिए। 2'-OH की अनुपस्थिति व थायमीन की उपस्थिति के कारण DNA भंडारण के लिए अधिक स्थायी है, जबकि RNA (राइबोजाइम) अधिक क्रियाशील होने से तीव्र उत्परिवर्तन करता है (जैसे TMV, QB बैक्टिरियोफेज)। RNA प्रथम आनुवंशिक पदार्थ था (RNA जगत), जिससे बाद में अधिक स्थायी DNA विकसित हुआ।

4. Semiconservative DNA Replication / अर्ध-संरक्षी डीएनए प्रतिकृति

English

4.1 Meselson & Stahl Experiment (1958)

Watson and Crick predicted that each parental strand serves as a template for a new complementary strand, producing daughter duplexes with one old and one new strand (semiconservative replication). Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl proved this in E. coli (division time = \(20\,\mathrm{min}\)):

  • Bacteria were grown in \(^{15}\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\) (heavy, non-radioactive nitrogen isotope) for many generations until all DNA was heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)), then shifted to normal \(^{14}\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\) medium.
  • DNA densities were separated by CsCl density gradient centrifugation:
    • Generation I (\(20\,\mathrm{min}\)): 100% intermediate/hybrid density (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
    • Generation II (\(40\,\mathrm{min}\)): 50% hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) and 50% light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
  • In eukaryotes, Taylor and colleagues (1958) confirmed semiconservative chromosome replication in Vicia faba using radioactive thymidine.

4.2 Replication Machinery & Polarity

  • Main enzyme: DNA-dependent DNA polymerase polymerizes deoxynucleotides strictly in the \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) direction. In E. coli (\(4.6 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{bp}\)), replication completes in ~\(18\,\mathrm{min}\) at an average rate of ~\(2000\,\mathrm{bp/s}\).
  • Dual role of dNTPs: Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dATP, dGTP, dCTP, dTTP) serve both as substrates and as energy sources (via their two terminal high-energy phosphate bonds).
  • Replication fork: Because separating the entire duplex at once is energetically prohibitive, unwinding occurs locally at a Y-shaped replication fork starting at a specific origin of replication (ori).
  • Continuous vs. Discontinuous synthesis: On the template strand with \(3' \rightarrow 5'\) polarity, synthesis is continuous (leading strand). On the \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) template strand, synthesis is discontinuous in short fragments (lagging strand) later joined by DNA ligase.
  • Cell-cycle coordination: In eukaryotes, DNA replication occurs in the S-phase; failure of cell division following replication leads to polyploidy.

5. Transcription & RNA Processing / अनुलेखन एवं आरएनए प्रक्रमण

English

5.1 Transcription Unit & Strand Conventions

Transcription copies only one strand of a defined DNA segment into RNA (A pairs with U). Both strands are not copied simultaneously because: (1) two different proteins would be coded from one locus, and (2) complementary RNA transcripts would anneal into double-stranded RNA, blocking translation.

  • Three regions: Promoter (5'-upstream of coding strand; binds RNA polymerase and defines template/coding strands), Structural gene, and Terminator (3'-downstream of coding strand).
  • Template vs. Coding strand: Because DNA-dependent RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA only in \(5' \rightarrow 3'\), the \(3' \rightarrow 5'\) DNA strand acts as the template strand. The \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) strand—whose sequence matches the RNA (with T instead of U)—is called the coding strand.
  • Cistron & Split genes: A cistron is a DNA segment coding for a polypeptide. Prokaryotes typically have polycistronic structural genes, whereas eukaryotes have monocistronic split genes containing coding exons interrupted by non-coding introns.

5.2 Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Transcription

  • Prokaryotes: A single DNA-dependent RNA polymerase transcribes mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA. Core enzyme catalyzes elongation; it transiently binds sigma (\(\sigma\)) factor for initiation and rho (\(\rho\)) factor for termination. Because there is no nuclear membrane and mRNA needs no processing, transcription and translation are coupled.
  • Eukaryotes: Three nuclear RNA polymerases show division of labor:
    • RNA Polymerase I: transcribes rRNAs (\(28\mathrm{S}\), \(18\mathrm{S}\), \(5.8\mathrm{S}\)).
    • RNA Polymerase II: transcribes precursor of mRNA, i.e., hnRNA (heterogeneous nuclear RNA).
    • RNA Polymerase III: transcribes tRNA, \(5\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{rRNA}\), and snRNAs.
  • Post-transcriptional processing of hnRNA: (1) Capping: addition of methyl guanosine triphosphate (\({}^m\mathrm{G}_{\text{ppp}}\)) at the 5'-end; (2) Splicing: removal of introns and joining of exons in defined order; (3) Tailing: template-independent addition of 200–300 adenylate residues (poly-A tail) at the 3'-end.

6. Genetic Code, tRNA & Translation / आनुवंशिक कूट, tRNA एवं स्थानांतरण

English

6.1 Genetic Code & Deciphering

Physicist George Gamow proposed a triplet code (\(4^3 = 64\) codons) to specify 20 amino acids. Har Gobind Khorana (chemical synthesis of defined RNA homopolymers/copolymers), Marshall Nirenberg (cell-free protein synthesis), and Severo Ochoa's enzyme (polynucleotide phosphorylase for template-independent RNA synthesis) helped decipher the code.

  • Triplet & Contiguous: 61 codons specify amino acids; 3 stop (nonsense) codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) terminate translation. Codons are read contiguously without punctuation.
  • Degenerate: Most amino acids are encoded by multiple codons (exceptions: Methionine = AUG, Tryptophan = UGG).
  • Nearly Universal: Same codon specifies the same amino acid from bacteria to humans, with minor exceptions in mitochondria and some protozoans.
  • Dual role of AUG: Codes for Methionine (Met) and acts as the initiator codon.

6.2 Mutations & tRNA (Adapter Molecule)

  • Point mutation: Single base substitution, e.g., sickle-cell anemia (glutamate \(\rightarrow\) valine in \(\beta\)-globin chain).
  • Frameshift mutation: Insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases shifts the entire downstream reading frame; insertion/deletion of 3 (or multiples of 3) bases adds/removes whole amino acids without shifting the frame.
  • tRNA: Francis Crick's predicted adapter molecule. Secondary structure resembles a clover-leaf; actual 3D structure is an inverted L. It carries an anticodon loop complementary to the mRNA codon and a 3'-amino acid acceptor end. No tRNA exists for stop codons.

6.3 Mechanism of Translation

  • Aminoacylation (Charging of tRNA): Amino acids are activated using ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA.
  • Ribosome: Consists of structural RNAs and ~80 proteins in two subunits (small binds mRNA; large has two sites for aminoacyl-tRNAs and acts as a ribozyme—specifically 23S rRNA in bacteria catalyzes peptide bond formation).
  • UTRs: Untranslated regions at both 5'- and 3'-ends of mRNA enhance translation efficiency.
  • Termination: A release factor binds the stop codon, releasing the completed polypeptide.

7. Gene Regulation, HGP & DNA Fingerprinting / जीन नियमन, मानव जीनोम परियोजना एवं डीएनए अंगुलीछापी

7.1 Regulation of Gene Expression & The lac Operon

In eukaryotes, regulation occurs at transcriptional, processing (splicing), mRNA transport, and translational levels. In prokaryotes, transcriptional initiation is the primary control point. Francois Jacob and Jacque Monod elucidated the inducible lac operon in E. coli:

  • Components: Regulator gene i (inhibitor; constitutively produces repressor protein), promoter (p), operator (o), and three structural genes: z (\(\beta\)-galactosidase: hydrolyzes lactose into galactose + glucose), y (permease: increases cell permeability to \(\beta\)-galactosides), and a (transacetylase).
  • In absence of inducer (OFF): Repressor binds the operator (o), blocking RNA polymerase from transcribing z, y, a.
  • In presence of inducer—lactose/allolactose (ON): Inducer binds and inactivates the repressor, allowing RNA polymerase to transcribe polycistronic lac mRNA. (Note: glucose and galactose cannot act as inducers; regulation by repressor is negative regulation.)

7.2 Human Genome Project (HGP: 1990–2003)

  • Methodologies: (1) ESTs (Expressed Sequence Tags—identifying genes expressed as RNA); (2) Sequence Annotation (sequencing the entire genome and assigning functions). DNA fragments were cloned in bacteria/yeast using BAC and YAC vectors and sequenced via automated Sanger sequencers. Chromosome 1 was completed in May 2006.
  • Salient Features: \(3164.7\,\mathrm{million\,bp}\); ~\(30{,}000\) genes; average gene = \(3000\,\mathrm{bases}\); largest gene = dystrophin (\(2.4\,\mathrm{million\,bases}\)); \(99.9\%\) base identity among humans; \(<2\%\) codes for proteins; Chromosome 1 has most genes (\(2968\)), Y has fewest (\(231\)); ~\(1.4\,\mathrm{million}\) SNPs (single nucleotide polymorphisms).

7.3 DNA Fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys)

Based on high polymorphism in non-coding repetitive satellite DNA (specifically mini-satellite VNTRs — Variable Number of Tandem Repeats, \(0.1\text{–}20\,\mathrm{kb}\)). DNA polymorphism is an inheritable mutation occurring in a population at frequency \(>0.01\). Steps: (1) DNA isolation, (2) restriction digestion, (3) gel electrophoresis, (4) Southern blotting onto nitrocellulose/nylon membrane, (5) hybridization with radiolabeled VNTR probe, (6) autoradiography. PCR amplification allows analysis from a single cell. Identical (monozygotic) twins share identical banding patterns.

Worked Example 1: Chargaff's Rule & DNA Dimensions (Hypothetical Assumption)

Problem: Assume a hypothetical double-stranded eukaryotic DNA molecule contains \(18\%\) guanine (G) and has a total contour length of \(6.8 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}\). Calculate: (a) the percentage of adenine (A), and (b) the approximate number of nucleosomes required to package this DNA.

Solution:

  1. By Chargaff's rule for dsDNA, \([C] = [G] = 18\%\). Thus, \([G] + [C] = 36\%\).
  2. The remaining \(100\% - 36\% = 64\%\) is shared equally between A and T: \([A] = [T] = \frac{64\%}{2} = 32\%\).
  3. Total base pairs: \[ N_{\text{bp}} = \frac{6.8 \times 10^{-5}\,\mathrm{m}}{0.34 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m/bp}} = 2.0 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{bp} \]
  4. Approximate nucleosomes (at \(200\,\mathrm{bp}\) per nucleosome): \[ \text{Nucleosomes} = \frac{2.0 \times 10^5}{200} = 1000 \]

Worked Example 2: Meselson-Stahl Generations (Hypothetical Assumption)

Problem: Assume E. coli cells with completely heavy DNA (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{15}\mathrm{N}\)) are transferred to a \(^{14}\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\) medium and allowed to divide synchronously for \(60\,\mathrm{minutes}\) (generation time = \(20\,\mathrm{min}\)). What percentage of DNA molecules will have hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) density and light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) density?

Solution:

  1. Number of generations \(n = \frac{60\,\mathrm{min}}{20\,\mathrm{min}} = 3\).
  2. Total dsDNA molecules formed per original duplex = \(2^3 = 8\).
  3. In semiconservative replication, the two original \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) strands remain conserved in exactly 2 hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) molecules; the remaining \(8 - 2 = 6\) molecules are completely light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)).
  4. Ratio = \(2 : 6 = 1 : 3\), i.e., 25% hybrid and 75% light.

Worked Example 3: Transcription & Nonsense Mutation (Hypothetical Sequence)

Problem: Consider a hypothetical DNA coding strand sequence: \(5'\text{-ATGGCTCAATTAGGATAA-}3'\). (a) Write the transcribed mRNA sequence. (b) If the 10th base (T) on the coding strand mutates to A, how many amino acids will the resulting polypeptide contain?

Solution:

  1. The mRNA sequence is identical to the \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) coding strand except U replaces T: \(5'\text{-AUGGCUCAAUUAGGAUAA-}3'\).
  2. Reading in triplets: \(\text{AUG - GCU - CAA - UUA - GGA - UAA}\) (5 amino acids before stop codon UAA).
  3. If the 10th base on the coding strand mutates from T to A, the 4th codon changes from \(\text{UUA}\) (Leu) to \(\text{UAA}\) (Stop codon).
  4. Translation terminates prematurely after the first 3 amino acids (Met–Ala–Gln).

11. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरीक्षण सूची

English
Common Exam Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) vs. \(^{32}\mathrm{P}/^{35}\mathrm{S}\): \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) in Meselson–Stahl is a heavy, non-radioactive isotope separated by CsCl density gradient; \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) and \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) in Hershey–Chase are radioactive isotopes.
  • Template vs. Coding strand: The template strand is \(3' \rightarrow 5'\); the coding strand is \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) and has the same sequence as mRNA (with T instead of U), even though it does not code!
  • Lac operon inducer: Lactose/allolactose is the inducer; glucose or galactose cannot induce the lac operon. The i gene stands for inhibitor, not inducer.
  • Chargaff's rule scope: Applies strictly to double-stranded DNA, not to single-stranded DNA (\(\phi \times 174\)) or single-stranded RNA.
हिंदी
परीक्षा में बचने योग्य सामान्य त्रुटियाँ:
  • \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) बनाम \(^{32}\mathrm{P}/^{35}\mathrm{S}\): मेसेल्सन-स्टाल प्रयोग में \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) एक भारी, गैर-विकिरणधर्मी समस्थानिक है, जबकि हर्शे-चेज में \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) व \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) विकिरणधर्मी हैं।
  • टेम्पलेट बनाम कूटलेखन रज्जुक: टेम्पलेट \(3' \rightarrow 5'\) होता है; कूटलेखन रज्जुक \(5' \rightarrow 3'\) होता है और इसका अनुक्रम mRNA जैसा (U की जगह T) होता है।

12. Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

DNA is a double-stranded antiparallel polymer stabilized by complementary H-bonding (\(A=T\), \(G \equiv C\)) and base stacking, packaged into nucleosomes (~200 bp around a lysine/arginine-rich histone octamer). Classical experiments (Griffith, Avery–MacLeod–McCarty, Hershey–Chase) proved DNA as the genetic material, while Meselson–Stahl proved semiconservative replication. Genetic information flows from DNA to RNA (via \(3' \rightarrow 5'\) template transcription; split-gene splicing in eukaryotes) and then to protein (via contiguous triplet codons read by tRNA adapters on ribosomes catalyzed by 23S rRNA). Gene expression in prokaryotes is exemplified by negative regulation of the inducible lac operon, while genomics (HGP) and VNTR-based DNA fingerprinting translate molecular sequence variation into medicine and forensics.

हिंदी

DNA एक द्विरज्जुकी प्रतिसमानांतर बहुलक है जो पूरक हाइड्रोजन बंधन (\(A=T\), \(G \equiv C\)) से स्थायी रहता है तथा हिस्टोन अष्टक पर लिपटकर न्यूक्लियोसोम (~200 bp) बनाता है। ग्रिफिथ, एवरी व हर्शे-चेज के प्रयोगों ने DNA को आनुवंशिक पदार्थ और मेसेल्सन-स्टाल ने अर्ध-संरक्षी प्रतिकृति को सिद्ध किया। आनुवंशिक सूचना अनुलेखन (यूकैरियोट्स में आच्छादन, संबधन व पुच्छन सहित) द्वारा mRNA में और स्थानांतरण (राइबोसोम व tRNA अनुकूलक द्वारा) द्वारा प्रोटीन में बदलती है। प्रोकैरियोट्स में lac ओपेरॉन ऋणात्मक नियमन का उदाहरण है, जबकि मानव जीनोम परियोजना तथा VNTR-आधारित DNA अंगुलीछापी आधुनिक जीनोमिक्स व फॉरेंसिक विज्ञान के आधार हैं।
