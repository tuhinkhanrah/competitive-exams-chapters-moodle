Cell: The Unit of Life

Comprehensive conceptual tutorial, structural comparisons, worked examples, and NEET UG practice assessment

Learning Objectives and Conceptual Overview

Every living organism—whether a single-celled bacterium or a multicellular mammal—relies on the cell as its fundamental structural and functional unit. Understanding cellular architecture explains how life sustains metabolism, transport, heredity, and reproduction. In competitive medical entrance examinations such as NEET UG, questions from this chapter test precise structural facts, organelle interactions, size scales, and exceptions across prokaryotes and eukaryotes.

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:
  • Trace the historical milestones of cell discovery and the modern formulation of Cell Theory.
  • Distinguish prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells by envelope layers, genetic organisation, ribosomes, and specialised membrane extensions.
  • Explain the Fluid Mosaic Model of the plasma membrane and compare passive versus active transport mechanisms.
  • Map the coordinated roles of the Endomembrane System and contrast them with semi-autonomous double-membrane organelles.
  • Classify chromosomes by centromere position and identify cytoskeletal, ciliary, flagellar, and nuclear structures.

1. Foundations of Cell Theory and Cellular Diversity

The study of cells bridges morphology with biochemistry—a physico-chemical perspective often termed reductionist biology, in which molecular interactions inside cell-free or intact systems explain gross physiological processes.

  • Anton Von Leeuwenhoek: First observed and described a living cell under a simple microscope.
  • Robert Brown (1831): Discovered and named the nucleus.
  • Matthias Schleiden (1838): A German botanist who concluded from extensive plant studies that all plants are composed of varied cell types forming tissues.
  • Theodore Schwann (1839): A British zoologist who identified the thin outer boundary of animal cells (now called the plasma membrane) and noted that the cell wall is a distinctive feature of plant cells. Together, Schleiden and Schwann proposed the initial Cell Theory.
  • Rudolf Virchow (1855): Resolved how new cells arise by demonstrating that cells divide from pre-existing cells (Omnis cellula-e cellula), completing the modern Cell Theory.
Modern Cell Theory Postulates:
  1. All living organisms are composed of cells and products of cells.
  2. All cells arise from pre-existing cells.

2. Prokaryotic Cell Architecture

Prokaryotes—comprising bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), mycoplasma, and PPLO—lack a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles. Despite their small size and rapid multiplication, they share a remarkably consistent structural plan.

The Three-Layered Cell Envelope

Most bacterial cells possess a tightly integrated, three-layered protective envelope:

  1. Glycocalyx (Outermost): Varies in thickness and chemical composition. It may form a loose sheath called a slime layer or a dense, tough capsule.
  2. Cell Wall (Middle): Provides mechanical rigidity, determines cell shape, and prevents osmotic bursting or collapse. (Absent in Mycoplasma.)
  3. Plasma Membrane (Innermost): Selectively permeable barrier structurally similar to eukaryotic membranes.

Based on cell envelope differences and response to Christian Gram's staining procedure, bacteria are grouped into Gram-positive (retain the Gram stain) and Gram-negative (do not retain the stain).

Specialised Prokaryotic Structures

  • Mesosomes: Infoldings of the plasma membrane appearing as vesicles, tubules, and lamellae. They assist in cell wall formation, DNA replication and distribution to daughter cells, respiration, secretion, and increasing membrane surface area and enzymatic content.
  • Chromatophores: Pigment-bearing membranous extensions into the cytoplasm found in photosynthetic prokaryotes like cyanobacteria.
  • Motility & Attachment Appendages: Bacterial flagella (for motility) consist of three parts: basal body, hook, and filament (longest part). In contrast, pili (elongated protein tubes) and fimbriae (bristle-like fibres for attachment to rocks or host tissues) do not mediate motility.
  • Genetic Material: A single circular genomic chromosome lies naked in the cytoplasm (nucleoid). Many bacteria also carry small circular extrachromosomal plasmids that confer traits such as antibiotic resistance.
  • 70S Ribosomes & Inclusion Bodies: Prokaryotic ribosomes (`15 nm × 20 nm`) comprise `50S` and `30S` subunits and often form polysomes along a single mRNA. Reserve materials (phosphate, cyanophycean, and glycogen granules) are stored as non-membrane-bound inclusion bodies; photosynthetic bacteria also possess gas vacuoles.

3. Eukaryotic Cells: Plasma Membrane and Cell Wall

Eukaryotes (protists, plants, fungi, and animals) compartmentalise metabolic pathways inside membrane-bound organelles and house linear chromosomes within a nuclear envelope. Plant cells possess a rigid cell wall, plastids, and a large central vacuole, whereas animal cells contain centrioles (absent in almost all plant cells).

Fluid Mosaic Model (Singer and Nicolson, 1972)

Biochemical studies on human erythrocyte (RBC) membranes revealed an approximate composition of 52% protein and 40% lipid. The lipid matrix is a phospholipid bilayer with hydrophilic polar heads facing outward toward the aqueous surroundings and hydrophobic saturated hydrocarbon tails tucked inward, stabilised by cholesterol.

  • Integral Proteins: Partially or completely embedded within the lipid bilayer.
  • Peripheral Proteins: Loosely attached to the inner or outer membrane surfaces.
  • Membrane Fluidity: The quasi-fluid nature of lipids permits lateral movement of proteins within the bilayer—vital for cell growth, intercellular junction formation, secretion, endocytosis, and cell division.

Tutorial Diagram 2: Fluid Mosaic Model illustrating the amphipathic phospholipid bilayer, embedded integral proteins, surface peripheral proteins, cholesterol, and carbohydrate chains.

Membrane Transport Mechanisms

  • Passive Transport (No ATP): Movement along the concentration gradient (higher to lower). Neutral solutes cross by simple diffusion; water moves by osmosis; polar molecules require membrane carrier proteins (facilitated diffusion).
  • Active Transport (ATP-Dependent): Movement of ions or molecules against their concentration gradient (lower to higher), such as the \(\mathrm{Na}^+/\mathrm{K}^+\) pump.

Cell Wall

The cell wall is a non-living, rigid outer covering in fungi and plants that maintains shape, prevents mechanical damage/infection, mediates cell-to-cell interaction, and blocks undesirable macromolecules.

  • Composition: In algae, it contains cellulose, galactans, mannans, and minerals such as \(\mathrm{CaCO}_3\). In other plants, it comprises cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins, and proteins.
  • Growth & Layers: Young plant cells possess a growing primary wall, which diminishes as maturity sets in and a secondary wall is deposited on the inner side (towards the plasma membrane).
  • Middle Lamella & Plasmodesmata: Neighbouring cells are cemented together by the middle lamella (chiefly calcium pectate). Both the cell wall and middle lamella are traversed by plasmodesmata, forming cytoplasmic bridges between adjacent cells.

4. The Endomembrane System

Organelles whose functions are tightly coordinated form the endomembrane system: the Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER), Golgi apparatus, Lysosomes, and Vacuoles. Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes are not part of this system because their functions are not coordinated with it.

Organelle Key Structural Features Primary Functions
Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER) Tubular network dividing intracellular space into luminal (inside ER) and extra-luminal (cytoplasm) compartments. RER (ribosomes attached, continuous with outer nuclear membrane): protein synthesis and secretion.
SER (ribosome-free): lipid synthesis; steroidal hormones in animal cells.
Golgi Apparatus
(Camillo Golgi, 1898)		 Parallel stacks of flat, disc-shaped cisternae (\(0.5\text{–}1.0\,\mathrm{µm}\) diameter) near the nucleus; convex cis (forming) face and concave trans (maturing) face. Modifies ER-derived proteins, packages materials into vesicles, and synthesises glycoproteins and glycolipids.
Lysosomes Single-membrane vesicles packaged by the Golgi apparatus. Contain hydrolytic enzymes (hydrolases: lipases, proteases, carbohydrases) active at acidic pH to digest macromolecules.
Vacuoles Bounded by a single membrane called the tonoplast; occupies up to 90% of plant cell volume. Tonoplast actively transports ions into the vacuole against gradients. Contractile vacuole in Amoeba (osmoregulation/excretion); food vacuoles in protists.

5. Semi-Autonomous Organelles: Mitochondria and Plastids

Both mitochondria and chloroplasts are double-membrane organelles that contain their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes, enabling limited autonomous protein synthesis.

Feature Mitochondria Chloroplasts (Plastids)
Occurrence All aerobic eukaryotic cells Plant cells and euglenoids (20–40/mesophyll cell; 1/cell in Chlamydomonas)
Dimensions Diameter \(0.2\text{–}1.0\,\mathrm{µm}\) (avg \(0.5\,\mathrm{µm}\)); length \(1.0\text{–}4.1\,\mathrm{µm}\) Length \(5\text{–}10\,\mathrm{µm}\); width \(2\text{–}4\,\mathrm{µm}\)
Membrane Organisation Outer smooth membrane; inner membrane folds into cristae enclosing the matrix. Outer membrane; less permeable inner membrane enclosing the stroma, which houses stacked thylakoids (grana) linked by stroma lamellae.
Genetic & Ribosomal Content Single circular DNA, RNA, \(70\mathrm{S}\) ribosomes; divides by fission. Small double-stranded circular DNA, \(70\mathrm{S}\) ribosomes.
Core Function Aerobic respiration and ATP synthesis (oxidative phosphorylation). Photosynthesis: light reactions in grana (chlorophyll), dark reactions (carbohydrate synthesis) in stroma.

Other Plastid Types: Chromoplasts contain fat-soluble carotenoids (carotene, xanthophylls) imparting yellow, orange, or red colours. Leucoplasts are colourless nutrient-storage plastids: amyloplasts (starch, e.g., potato), elaioplasts (oils/fats), and aleuroplasts (proteins).

6. Ribosomes, Cytoskeleton, Cilia, Flagella, and Centrioles

Ribosomes (George Palade, 1953)

Ribosomes are non-membrane-bound ribonucleoprotein granules (RNA + protein) that synthesise proteins. Their sedimentation coefficient is measured in Svedberg's units (\(\mathrm{S}\)), an indirect measure of density and size:

\[\text{Prokaryotic / Mitochondrial / Chloroplast Ribosome: } 70\mathrm{S} = 50\mathrm{S} + 30\mathrm{S}\] \[\text{Eukaryotic Cytoplasmic Ribosome: } 80\mathrm{S} = 60\mathrm{S} + 40\mathrm{S}\]

Cytoskeleton, Cilia, and Flagella

The cytoskeleton is a proteinaceous network of microtubules, microfilaments, and intermediate filaments providing mechanical support, motility, and shape maintenance. Eukaryotic cilia (oar-like) and flagella (longer, cell locomotion) are covered by the plasma membrane and emerge from centriole-like basal bodies. Their core axoneme displays a \(9 + 2\) microtubule array:

  • 9 peripheral microtubule doublets interconnected by interdoublet linkers.
  • 2 central singlet microtubules connected by a central bridge and enclosed by a central sheath.
  • 9 radial spokes linking one tubule of each peripheral doublet to the central sheath.

Tutorial Diagram 3: Transverse section of a eukaryotic cilium or flagellum depicting the characteristic \(9 + 2\) axonemal microtubule arrangement.

Centrosome and Centrioles

A centrosome contains two cylindrical centrioles oriented perpendicular to each other within amorphous pericentriolar material. Each centriole exhibits a cartwheel organisation: nine evenly spaced peripheral triplets of tubulin protein linked to a central proteinaceous hub via nine radial spokes (a \(9 + 0\) pattern). Centrioles form the basal bodies of cilia/flagella and generate spindle fibres during animal cell division.

7. Nucleus, Chromosomes, and Microbodies

The interphase nucleus is enclosed by a double-membraned nuclear envelope separated by a \(10\text{–}50\,\mathrm{nm}\) perinuclear space. Its outer membrane is continuous with the ER and bears ribosomes. Nuclear pores, formed by fusion of the two membranes, permit bidirectional transport of RNA and proteins.

  • Nucleolus: A spherical, non-membrane-bound body within the nucleoplasm that serves as the active site of ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis. Cells engaged in active protein synthesis have larger and more numerous nucleoli.
  • Chromatin: Nucleoprotein fibres (named by Flemming) composed of DNA, basic histone proteins, non-histone proteins, and RNA. A single human cell packs roughly \(2\,\mathrm{m}\) of DNA across 46 (23 pairs) chromosomes.
  • Centromere & Kinetochores: Dividing chromosomes display a primary constriction (centromere) flanked by disc-shaped kinetochores that bind spindle fibres.
  • Secondary Constriction & Satellite: Certain chromosomes bear non-staining secondary constrictions at fixed positions, giving the appearance of a small terminal fragment called a satellite.

Tutorial Diagram 4: Classification of chromosomes into four types based on the position of the centromere (teal circle).

Microbodies: Minute, single-membrane-bound vesicles containing diverse oxidative and metabolic enzymes, present in both plant and animal cells.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Estimating Membrane Protein and Lipid Proportions

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): Suppose a biochemical assay isolates \(250\,\mathrm{mg}\) of pure plasma membrane from human erythrocytes. Using the established erythrocyte membrane composition (52% protein and 40% lipid by mass), calculate the expected masses of membrane proteins and lipids in this sample.

Solution:

  1. Mass of membrane proteins: \[m_{\text{protein}} = 0.52 \times 250\,\mathrm{mg} = 130\,\mathrm{mg}\]
  2. Mass of membrane lipids: \[m_{\text{lipid}} = 0.40 \times 250\,\mathrm{mg} = 100\,\mathrm{mg}\]
  3. The remaining \(8\%\) (\(20\,\mathrm{mg}\)) corresponds primarily to membrane-associated carbohydrates.
Example 2: Tracing a Secretory Glycoprotein Through the Endomembrane System

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): A pancreatic acinar cell is pulsed with radiolabelled amino acids for \(3\,\mathrm{min}\) to track a secretory glycoprotein from synthesis to exocytosis. Predict the correct sequential order of organelles traversed.

Solution:

  1. Polypeptide synthesis occurs on ribosomes bound to the Rough Endoplasmic Reticulum (RER), entering the ER lumen.
  2. Transport vesicles bud from the ER and fuse with the convex cis face of the Golgi apparatus.
  3. Inside the Golgi cisternae, the protein is glycosylated into a glycoprotein and moves toward the concave trans face.
  4. Secretory vesicles bud from the trans face and fuse with the plasma membrane for exocytosis.
Example 3: Counting Microtubule Protofilaments in Axonemes vs. Centrioles

Scenario: Compare the total number of peripheral microtubule units in a transverse section of a eukaryotic flagellar axoneme with that of a basal body (centriole).

Solution:

  • Eukaryotic Flagellum (Axoneme): Follows the \(9 + 2\) rule—9 peripheral doublets (\(9 \times 2 = 18\) tubules) plus 2 central singlets, totalling \(20\) microtubules.
  • Centriole / Basal Body: Follows the \(9 + 0\) cartwheel organisation—9 peripheral triplets (\(9 \times 3 = 27\) tubules) and \(0\) central tubules, totalling \(27\) microtubules.

NEET UG Practice Questions

Practice Question 1 Single-Correct MCQ

Which of the following combinations correctly pairs a non-membrane-bound structure with its function in eukaryotic cells?

  • (a) Lysosome — Intracellular macromolecular digestion
  • (b) Nucleolus — Active ribosomal RNA (rRNA) synthesis
  • (c) Peroxisome — Lipid and steroidal hormone synthesis
  • (d) Tonoplast — Active ion transport into the vacuole

Answer: (b)

Explanation: The nucleolus is a non-membrane-bound spherical structure in the nucleoplasm responsible for active rRNA synthesis. Lysosomes, peroxisomes, and the vacuolar tonoplast are all single-membrane-bound structures.

Practice Question 2 Statement-Based MCQ

Read the two statements carefully and select the correct option:

Statement I: Mycoplasma are the smallest living cells and lack a cell wall.
Statement II: All prokaryotic cells possess a cell wall surrounding the plasma membrane.

  • (a) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
  • (b) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
  • (c) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
  • (d) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct.

Answer: (c)

Explanation: Mycoplasma (\(0.3\,\mathrm{µm}\)) are the smallest known living cells and are prokaryotes that naturally lack a cell wall. Therefore, Statement I is true, while Statement II ("all prokaryotes possess a cell wall") is false.

Practice Question 3 Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): Mitochondria and chloroplasts are excluded from the endomembrane system.
Reason (R): Both mitochondria and chloroplasts lack double membranes and do not contain ribosomes.

  • (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  • (c) A is true, but R is false.
  • (d) Both A and R are false.

Answer: (c)

Explanation: Mitochondria and chloroplasts are excluded from the endomembrane system because their functions are not coordinated with ER, Golgi, lysosomes, and vacuoles. Reason (R) is false because both organelles are double-membrane bound and contain \(70\mathrm{S}\) ribosomes.

Practice Question 5 Single-Correct MCQ

During plant cell maturation, where is the secondary cell wall deposited relative to the primary cell wall?

  • (a) Outside the middle lamella
  • (b) Between the middle lamella and the primary wall
  • (c) On the inner side of the primary wall, towards the plasma membrane
  • (d) Within the plasmodesmata channels

Answer: (c)

Explanation: As a plant cell matures, the growth capability of the primary wall diminishes and the secondary wall is laid down on the inner surface of the primary wall, facing the plasma membrane.

Practice Question 6 Single-Correct MCQ

Which bacterial surface structure is made of a special protein, forms elongated tubules, and does not participate in motility?

  • (a) Flagellum
  • (b) Pili
  • (c) Mesosome
  • (d) Slime layer

Answer: (b)

Explanation: Pili are elongated tubular structures composed of a special protein (pilin) on the bacterial surface. Along with fimbriae, pili do not play any role in bacterial motility (which is mediated by flagella).

Practice Question 7 Single-Correct MCQ

In the Fluid Mosaic Model, which property of the lipid bilayer directly enables the lateral movement of proteins within the membrane?

  • (a) Rigid covalent bonding between phospholipid heads
  • (b) Quasi-fluid nature of the lipid bilayer
  • (c) Presence of calcium pectate in the bilayer
  • (d) Hydrophilic hydrocarbon tails

Answer: (b)

Explanation: According to Singer and Nicolson's Fluid Mosaic Model, the quasi-fluid nature of lipids allows lateral diffusion of proteins within the overall bilayer, defining membrane fluidity.

Practice Question 8 Single-Correct MCQ

A chromosome has its centromere positioned very close to one end, forming one extremely short arm and one very long arm. This chromosome is classified as:

  • (a) Metacentric
  • (b) Sub-metacentric
  • (c) Acrocentric
  • (d) Telocentric

Answer: (c)

Explanation: An acrocentric chromosome has its centromere situated close to one end, producing one extremely short arm and one very long arm. A telocentric chromosome has a terminal centromere at the very tip.

Practice Question 9 Single-Correct MCQ

Which statement accurately describes the Golgi apparatus?

  • (a) Vesicles from the ER fuse with the concave trans face.
  • (b) It is the primary site for synthesis of phospholipids and steroidal hormones.
  • (c) It modifies proteins from the ER and synthesises glycoproteins and glycolipids.
  • (d) Its cisternae are unconnected and lack polarity.

Answer: (c)

Explanation: ER vesicles fuse with the convex cis (forming) face and exit from the concave trans (maturing) face. The Golgi apparatus modifies proteins and is the major site for glycoprotein and glycolipid formation. Steroidal hormones are synthesised in SER.

Practice Question 10 Single-Correct MCQ

How many radial spokes and peripheral microtubule doublets are present in a single transverse section of a eukaryotic cilium?

  • (a) 9 radial spokes and 9 peripheral doublets
  • (b) 9 radial spokes and 9 peripheral triplets
  • (c) 2 radial spokes and 9 peripheral doublets
  • (d) 18 radial spokes and 9 peripheral doublets

Answer: (a)

Explanation: The eukaryotic ciliary axoneme has a \(9 + 2\) array: 9 peripheral microtubule doublets, each connected to the central sheath by one radial spoke, making 9 radial spokes in total. (9 peripheral triplets occur in centrioles, not ciliary axonemes.)

Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist

High-Yield NEET UG Traps to Avoid:
  • Ribosome Subunit Addition: \(50\mathrm{S} + 30\mathrm{S} = 70\mathrm{S}\) (and \(60\mathrm{S} + 40\mathrm{S} = 80\mathrm{S}\)) is not arithmetic mass addition; \(\mathrm{S}\) measures sedimentation rate, which depends on both mass and shape.
  • Non-Membrane-Bound Structures: Ribosomes, centrosomes/centrioles, nucleolus, and prokaryotic inclusion bodies all lack a bounding lipid membrane.
  • Endomembrane Exclusions: Mitochondria, chloroplasts, and peroxisomes are excluded from the endomembrane system.
  • Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Flagella: Bacterial flagella are made of flagellin (basal body, hook, filament), whereas eukaryotic flagella are membrane-covered \(9 + 2\) tubulin axonemes.
  • Calcium Pectate vs. Calcium Carbonate: Middle lamella is chiefly calcium pectate; algal cell walls contain minerals like calcium carbonate.

Concise Final Recap

The cell is the irreducible unit of independent living. Prokaryotes rely on a three-layered envelope, mesosomes, plasmids, and \(70\mathrm{S}\) ribosomes, whereas eukaryotes compartmentalise metabolism using a fluid-mosaic plasma membrane, the coordinated endomembrane system (ER, Golgi, lysosomes, vacuoles), semi-autonomous \(70\mathrm{S}\)-containing mitochondria and plastids, \(80\mathrm{S}\) cytoplasmic ribosomes, a \(9 + 2\) ciliary/flagellar cytoskeleton, \(9 + 0\) centrioles, and a double-membraned nucleus housing histone-packaged chromatin.
