2. Defining Characteristics of Living Organisms | জীবের নির্ণায়ক বৈশিষ্ট্যসমূহ

English

When classifying an object as living, NEET UG tests a crucial distinction: a characteristic feature is shown by living organisms, whereas a defining feature is exhibited by all living organisms without exception and by no non-living object.

2.1 Growth (Non-Defining Feature)

  • Twin characteristics of growth: Increase in body mass and increase in number of individuals.
  • Plants vs. Animals: Plants grow continuously throughout life by cell division (indeterminate growth); animals grow only up to a certain age, though cell division continues in specific tissues to replace lost cells.
  • Why not defining? Non-living objects like mountains, boulders, and sand dunes also increase in mass by external accumulation of material (extrinsic growth). Living organisms grow from within (intrinsic growth). A dead organism does not grow.

2.2 Reproduction (Non-Defining Feature)

  • Modes: Sexual reproduction produces progeny similar to parents. Asexual modes include spores (fungi), budding (yeast, Hydra), true regeneration (Planaria—a fragmented flatworm regenerates lost parts to form a new organism), and fragmentation (fungi, filamentous algae, protonema of mosses).
  • Unicellular organisms: In bacteria, unicellular algae, and Amoeba, reproduction is synonymous with growth (increase in cell number).
  • Why not defining? Several living organisms cannot reproduce, such as mules, sterile worker bees, and infertile human couples. However, no non-living object can replicate by itself.

2.3 Metabolism & Cellular Organisation (Defining Features)

  • Metabolism: The sum total of all chemical reactions occurring inside a living body. Every plant, animal, fungus, and microbe exhibits metabolism; no non-living object does.
  • Crucial NEET Distinction: Isolated metabolic reactions carried out in vitro (in a test tube/cell-free system) are neither living things nor non-living things—they are living reactions. Therefore, cellular organisation of the body is the defining feature of life forms.

2.4 Consciousness (Defining Feature)

  • All organisms—from simple prokaryotes to complex eukaryotes—can sense environmental stimuli (physical, chemical, or biological) and respond. For example, photoperiod affects reproduction in seasonal breeders (both plants and animals).
  • Self-consciousness: Awareness of oneself is unique to human beings.
  • Brain-dead / Coma patients: Patients on life-support machines (replacing heart and lungs) lack self-consciousness, representing a complex clinical boundary, yet overall responsiveness/consciousness remains a defining property of living organisms.

2.5 Emergent Properties & Evolutionary Unity

Properties of tissues are not present in constituent cells alone, and properties of organelles are not present in their constituent molecules; they arise from interactions at each level (emergent properties). All living organisms—past, present, and future—share common genetic material to varying degrees, reflecting evolutionary unity.

3. Biodiversity, Binomial Nomenclature, and Systematics | জীববৈচিত্র্য, দ্বিপদ নামকরণ ও সিস্টেমেটিক্স

English

Biodiversity refers to the number and types of organisms present on Earth. Currently, the number of known and described species ranges between \(1.7\text{–}1.8\text{ million}\). Because local names vary across regions, scientists use standardized naming.

Key Scientist: Ernst Mayr (1904–2004)

Known as the "Darwin of the 20th century", Harvard evolutionary biologist Ernst Mayr pioneered the currently accepted Biological Species Concept and won the Triple Crown of Biology: the Balzan Prize (1983), International Prize for Biology (1994), and Crafoord Prize (1999).

3.1 Nomenclature Codes & Binomial Rules

  • Identification: Correctly describing an organism so that its name attaches unambiguously to that organism.
  • ICBN: International Code for Botanical Nomenclature (for plants).
  • ICZN: International Code of Zoological Nomenclature (for animals).
  • Binomial Nomenclature (Carolus Linnaeus): Each scientific name has two components—the Generic name and the specific epithet.
  1. Biological names are generally in Latin and printed in italics (Latinised irrespective of origin).
  2. The first word denotes the genus (starts with a Capital letter); the second word denotes the specific epithet (starts with a small letter).
  3. When handwritten, both words are separately underlined; when printed, they are italicized to indicate Latin origin.
  4. The author's name appears after the specific epithet in abbreviated form (roman, not italicized), e.g., Mangifera indica Linn. (first described by Linnaeus).

3.2 Taxonomy vs. Systematics

  • Taxonomy: The science of classifying organisms into taxa based on external/internal structure, cell structure, developmental processes, and ecological information. Its four basic processes are characterisation, identification, classification, and nomenclature.
  • Systematics: Derived from the Latin word systema (systematic arrangement of organisms). Linnaeus titled his publication Systema Naturae. Modern systematics includes identification, nomenclature, classification, and evolutionary relationships among organisms.

  • সিস্টেমেটিক্স (Systematics): ল্যাটিন শব্দ systema (জীবের সুশৃঙ্খল বিন্যাস) থেকে উদ্ভূত। লিনিয়াসের গ্রন্থের নাম ছিল Systema Naturae। আধুনিক সিস্টেমেটিক্স শনাক্তকরণ, নামকরণ ও শ্রেণিবিন্যাসের পাশাপাশি জীবের মধ্যকার বিবর্তনীয় সম্পর্ক বিবেচনা করে।

4. Taxonomic Categories and Hierarchical Arrangement | ট্যাক্সোনমিক ক্যাটাগরি ও হায়ারার্কিক্যাল বিন্যাস

English

Classification involves a hierarchy of steps where each step represents a rank or taxonomic category. All categories together form the taxonomic hierarchy. Each rank/unit of classification is a biological entity called a taxon (plural: taxa)—for example, 'Dogs', 'Mammals', and 'Animals' are taxa at different levels.

4.1 The Seven Obligate Categories (Ascending Order)

  • Species: Lowest obligate category; group of individual organisms with fundamental similarities (e.g., Solanum tuberosum, Panthera leo, Homo sapiens).
  • Genus: Group of closely related species with more common characters than species of other genera. E.g., Solanum includes potato (S. tuberosum), makoi (S. nigrum), and brinjal (S. melongena); Panthera includes lion (P. leo), leopard (P. pardus), and tiger (P. tigris), distinct from Felis (cats).
  • Family: Group of related genera with fewer similarities; characterised by both vegetative and reproductive features in plants. E.g., Solanaceae (Solanum, Petunia, Datura); Felidae (Panthera, Felis) vs. Canidae (dogs).
  • Order: Assemblage of families exhibiting a few similar characters. E.g., Polymoniales (Convolvulaceae + Solanaceae, mainly based on floral characters); Carnivora (Felidae + Canidae).
  • Class: Includes related orders. E.g., Mammalia includes orders Primata (monkey, gorilla, gibbon) and Carnivora (tiger, cat, dog).
  • Phylum (Animals) / Division (Plants): Classes sharing common features such as a notochord and dorsal hollow neural system (fishes, amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals) form Phylum Chordata.
  • Kingdom: Highest category—Kingdom Animalia (all animals) and Kingdom Plantae (all plants).
Golden Hierarchy Rule for NEET UG

As we move upwards from Species to Kingdom: the number of common characteristics decreases, specificity decreases, and the difficulty of determining relationships with other taxa at the same level increases.

৪.১ সাতটি বাধ্যতামূলক ক্যাটাগরি (আরোহী ক্রম)

  • প্রজাতি (Species): সর্বনিম্ন বাধ্যতামূলক ধাপ; মৌলিক সাদৃশ্যযুক্ত জীবগোষ্ঠী (যেমন— Solanum tuberosum, Panthera leo, Homo sapiens)।
  • গণ (Genus): ঘনিষ্ঠভাবে সম্পর্কিত প্রজাতির সমষ্টি, যাদের মধ্যে অন্যান্য গণের প্রজাতির তুলনায় বেশি সাধারণ বৈশিষ্ট্য থাকে। যেমন— Solanum গণে আলু (S. tuberosum), কাকমাছি (S. nigrum) ও বেগুন (S. melongena) অন্তর্ভুক্ত; Panthera গণে সিংহ (P. leo), চিতাবাঘ (P. pardus) ও বাঘ (P. tigris) রয়েছে, যা Felis (বিড়াল) গণ থেকে পৃথক।
  • গোত্র (Family): সম্পর্কিত গণের সমষ্টি, যেখানে সাদৃশ্য আরও কম; উদ্ভিদের ক্ষেত্রে অঙ্গজ ও জনন উভয় বৈশিষ্ট্যের ভিত্তিতে গোত্র নির্ধারিত হয়। যেমন— Solanaceae (Solanum, Petunia, Datura); Felidae (Panthera, Felis) বনাম Canidae (কুকুর)।
  • বর্গ (Order): কয়েকটি সাধারণ বৈশিষ্ট্যযুক্ত গোত্রের সমষ্টি। যেমন— Polymoniales (Convolvulaceae + Solanaceae, মূলত পুষ্পবৈশিষ্ট্যের ভিত্তিতে); Carnivora (Felidae + Canidae)।
  • শ্রেণি (Class): সম্পর্কিত বর্গের সমষ্টি। যেমন— Mammalia শ্রেণিতে Primata (বানর, গরিলা, গিবন) ও Carnivora (বাঘ, বিড়াল, কুকুর) অন্তর্ভুক্ত।
  • পর্ব / বিভাগ (Phylum / Division): নটোকর্ড ও পৃষ্ঠীয় ফাঁপা স্নায়ুতন্ত্রের মতো সাধারণ বৈশিষ্ট্যযুক্ত মৎস্য, উভচর, সরীসৃপ, পক্ষী ও স্তন্যপায়ী শ্রেণি নিয়ে Chordata পর্ব গঠিত। উদ্ভিদের ক্ষেত্রে 'Division' ব্যবহৃত হয়।
  • রাজ্য (Kingdom): সর্বোচ্চ ধাপ—Animalia (সকল প্রাণী) এবং Plantae (সকল উদ্ভিদ)।

5. Taxonomical Aids and Identification Tools | ট্যাক্সোনমিক সহায়ক ও শনাক্তকরণ পদ্ধতি

English

Taxonomic studies rely on collecting and preserving actual specimens as well as recording descriptive information. These aids are essential for agriculture, forestry, industry, and bio-resource conservation.

Taxonomical Aid Specimen State Key Features & Notable Examples
Herbarium Dead, dried, pressed, preserved plants on sheets Arranged by a universally accepted classification system; serves as a quick referral system. Sheet labels carry date & place of collection, English, local, and botanical names, family, and collector's name.
Botanical Gardens Living plants Grown for identification; each plant is labelled with its botanical name and family. Famous examples: Royal Botanical Gardens (Kew, England), Indian Botanical Garden (Howrah, India), National Botanical Research Institute (NBRI, Lucknow).
Museum Preserved plants & animals Set up in schools/colleges. Specimens kept in preservative jars or dried; insects are collected, killed, and pinned in insect boxes; birds/mammals are stuffed; skeletons are also preserved.
Zoological Parks (Zoos) Living wild animals Kept in protected environments under human care in conditions similar to natural habitats; helps study food habits and behaviour.
Taxonomic Key Analytical tool (non-specimen) Based on contrasting characters generally in a pair called a couplet; each statement in the key is a lead. Choice accepts one option and rejects the other. Separate keys are needed for each category (family, genus, species).

  • Manuals: Useful in providing information for identification of names of species found in an area.
  • Monographs: Contain comprehensive information on any one taxon.
  • Catalogues: Alphabetical or systematic listings of species/specimens for reference.

Scenario: A biology student records the scientific name of a newly studied plant (hypothetical species discovered in a field survey) in four ways: (i) solanum Bengalensis, (ii) Solanum bengalensis Linn., (iii) Solanum bengalensis (handwritten), and (iv) Solanum Bengalensis. Identify which formats obey ICBN rules and explain why the others fail.

Example 2: Comparing Taxonomic Ranks and Shared Characters

Scenario: Consider three organisms: a lion (Panthera leo), a domestic cat (Felis catus), and a dog (Canis familiaris). Which pair shares the highest number of common characters, and at which lowest taxonomic rank do all three converge?

Solution:

  1. Panthera leo and Felis catus belong to two different genera (Panthera and Felis) but share the same family, Felidae.
  2. Canis familiaris belongs to family Canidae.
  3. Both Felidae and Canidae are placed under the same order, Carnivora.
  4. Since lower taxonomic ranks have more shared characters, the lion and domestic cat (sharing Family Felidae) share more common characters than either shares with the dog. All three converge at the rank of Order (Carnivora).

Example 3: Selecting the Appropriate Taxonomical Aid

9. Concise Final Recap | সংক্ষিপ্ত চূড়ান্ত সারসংক্ষেপ

English
  • Living organisms are self-replicating, evolving, self-regulating interactive systems defined by cellular organisation, metabolism, and consciousness.
  • Ernst Mayr ("Darwin of the 20th century") formulated the biological species concept.
  • Linnaeus's binomial nomenclature (ICBN/ICZN) assigns each species a Latinised Genus (capitalized) + specific epithet (lowercase), italicized or separately underlined.
  • Systematics integrates taxonomy (characterisation, identification, classification, nomenclature) with evolutionary relationships.
  • The seven obligate ranks ascend from Species → Genus → Family → Order → Class → Phylum/Division → Kingdom, with decreasing shared characters.
  • Taxonomical aids include Herbaria, Botanical Gardens, Museums, Zoological Parks, analytical Keys (couplets & leads), Flora, Manuals, Monographs, and Catalogues.
