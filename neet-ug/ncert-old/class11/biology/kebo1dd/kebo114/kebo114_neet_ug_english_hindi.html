Cellular Respiration & Gaseous Exchange in Plants

Cellular respiration is the enzyme-controlled, step-wise oxidation of complex organic molecules (breaking C–C bonds) inside living cells to release chemical energy and trap it as adenosine triphosphate (ATP). The organic molecules oxidized during this process are called respiratory substrates. Carbohydrates (especially glucose) are the primary substrates, though fats, proteins, and organic acids can also be oxidized under specific physiological conditions.

Why Plants Manage Without Respiratory Organs

Unlike animals, plants lack lungs, gills, or circulatory blood for gas transport. They rely on simple diffusion through stomata (in leaves and young stems) and lenticels (in woody bark) for three main reasons:

  1. Local autonomy: Each plant organ meets its own gas-exchange needs; long-distance gas transport is negligible.
  2. Low respiratory demand: Roots, stems, and leaves respire much more slowly than active animal tissues. During active photosynthesis, leaf cells generate O2 internally.
  3. Short diffusion paths: Even in massive trees, living cells are arranged in thin layers near the surface (just beneath the bark in stems), while interior woody tissues are largely dead mechanical elements. Loose parenchyma packing creates an interconnected network of intercellular air spaces.

Step-Wise Oxidation vs. Uncontrolled Combustion

Direct burning of glucose releases all stored energy at once, almost entirely as heat. Living cells avoid thermal damage by oxidizing glucose through small, enzyme-regulated steps so that discrete packets of energy can be coupled to ATP synthesis:

\[\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6} + 6\,\mathrm{O_2} \longrightarrow 6\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 6\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \text{Energy (ATP + Heat)}\]

Glycolysis (EMP Pathway)

Glycolysis (Greek: glycos = sugar, lysis = splitting), elucidated by Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof, and J. Parnas (EMP pathway), occurs in the cytoplasm of all living organisms—both aerobes and anaerobes. It does not require oxygen and partially oxidizes one 6-carbon glucose molecule through a sequence of 10 enzyme-catalyzed reactions into two 3-carbon molecules of pyruvic acid (pyruvate).

In plants, glucose is commonly derived from sucrose (the translocated end product of photosynthesis) via the enzyme invertase, which splits sucrose into glucose and fructose. Both monosaccharides are phosphorylated by hexokinase using ATP and enter the same pathway.

Key Energy Steps in Glycolysis

  • ATP Investment (2 ATP consumed per glucose):
    Step 1: Glucose → Glucose-6-phosphate (catalyzed by hexokinase; 1 ATP used).
    Step 3: Fructose-6-phosphate → Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (1 ATP used).
  • Cleavage & Isomerization: Fructose-1,6-bisphosphate (6C) splits into two 3C triose phosphates: 3-phosphoglyceraldehyde (PGAL) and dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP), which are interconvertible.
  • Oxidation / Reducing Power (2 NADH + H+ formed per glucose): PGAL is oxidized using inorganic phosphate (Pi) and NAD+ to form 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (BPGA) and NADH + H+.
  • Substrate-Level Phosphorylation (4 ATP formed per glucose):
    BPGA → 3-phosphoglyceric acid (PGA) yields 2 ATP (1 per 3C molecule).
    Phosphoenolpyruvate (PEP) → Pyruvic acid yields 2 ATP (1 per 3C molecule).

Net Direct Yield per Glucose: \(4 - 2 = 2\,\mathrm{ATP}\) and \(2\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), plus \(2\) molecules of pyruvic acid.

ग्लाइकोलिसिस (EMP पथ)

ग्लाइकोलिसिस (ग्रीक: glycos = शर्करा, lysis = विखंडन), जिसे गुस्ताव एम्बडेन, ओटो मेयरहोफ और जे. पार्नास ने प्रस्तुत किया (EMP पथ), सभी जीवित कोशिकाओं (ऑक्सी व अनॉक्सी दोनों) के कोशिकाद्रव्य (cytoplasm) में संपन्न होता है। इसमें ऑक्सीजन की आवश्यकता नहीं होती और यह 10 एंजाइम-उत्प्रेरित अभिक्रियाओं द्वारा 6-कार्बन ग्लूकोज अणु का आंशिक ऑक्सीकरण कर 3-कार्बन पायरुविक अम्ल (पायरुवेट) के दो अणु बनाता है।

पादपों में ग्लूकोज प्रायः सुक्रोज (प्रकाश-संश्लेषण का अंतिम उत्पाद) से प्राप्त होता है, जिसे इनवर्टेज (invertase) एंजाइम ग्लूकोज और फ्रक्टोज में तोड़ता है। ये दोनों मोनोसैकेराइड हेक्सोकाइनेज (hexokinase) द्वारा फॉस्फोरिलीकृत होकर समान पथ में प्रवेश करते हैं।

ग्लाइकोलिसिस के प्रमुख ऊर्जा चरण

  • ATP निवेश (प्रति ग्लूकोज 2 ATP व्यय):
    चरण 1: ग्लूकोज → ग्लूकोज-6-फॉस्फेट (हेक्सोकाइनेज द्वारा उत्प्रेरित; 1 ATP व्यय)।
    चरण 3: फ्रक्टोज-6-फॉस्फेट → फ्रक्टोज-1,6-बिसफॉस्फेट (1 ATP व्यय)।
  • विखंडन एवं समावयवीकरण: फ्रक्टोज-1,6-बिसफॉस्फेट (6C) दो 3C ट्राइओज फॉस्फेट में टूटता है: 3-फॉस्फोग्लिसरैल्डिहाइड (PGAL) तथा डाइहाइड्रॉक्सीएसीटोन फॉस्फेट (DHAP), जो परस्पर परिवर्तनीय हैं।
  • ऑक्सीकरण / अपचयन शक्ति (प्रति ग्लूकोज 2 NADH + H+ निर्माण): अकार्बनिक फॉस्फेट (Pi) और NAD+ की सहायता से PGAL का ऑक्सीकरण होकर 1,3-बिसफॉस्फोग्लिसरेट (BPGA) और NADH + H+ बनता है।
  • क्रियाधार-स्तरीय फॉस्फोरिलीकरण (प्रति ग्लूकोज 4 ATP निर्माण):
    BPGA → 3-फॉस्फोग्लिसरिक अम्ल (PGA) से 2 ATP प्राप्त होते हैं।
    फॉस्फोईनॉलपायरुवेट (PEP) → पायरुविक अम्ल से 2 ATP प्राप्त होते हैं।

प्रति ग्लूकोज शुद्ध प्रत्यक्ष लाभ: \(4 - 2 = 2\,\mathrm{ATP}\) तथा \(2\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), और पायरुविक अम्ल के \(2\) अणु।

Fermentation: Anaerobic Fate of Pyruvate

When oxygen is absent or insufficient, cells cannot run mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation. To keep glycolysis running, the \(2\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) formed during PGAL oxidation must be re-oxidized to \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\). This is achieved by reducing pyruvic acid via two major anaerobic routes:

  • Alcoholic Fermentation (e.g., Yeast): Pyruvic acid is first decarboxylated to acetaldehyde and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) by pyruvic acid decarboxylase, and acetaldehyde is then reduced to ethanol by alcohol dehydrogenase using \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\). Yeasts poison themselves to death when ethanol concentration reaches about 13%.
  • Lactic Acid Fermentation (e.g., Lactic acid bacteria; animal skeletal muscles during strenuous exercise): Pyruvic acid is directly reduced to lactic acid by lactate dehydrogenase using \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), with no release of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\).
Energy Efficiency & Hazard: Less than 7% of the total energy in glucose is released during fermentation, and only a fraction of that is trapped as 2 net ATP per glucose. Moreover, the end products (acid or alcohol) are toxic to the cell.

Aerobic Respiration: Link Reaction & Krebs' Cycle (TCA Cycle)

In eukaryotes, aerobic respiration takes place inside mitochondria and comprises two spatially distinct phases: (1) complete oxidation of pyruvate in the mitochondrial matrix releasing \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), and (2) electron transfer to \(\mathrm{O_2}\) coupled with ATP synthesis on the inner mitochondrial membrane.

1. Oxidative Decarboxylation (Link Reaction)

Pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix and undergoes oxidative decarboxylation catalyzed by the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (requiring \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\), and Coenzyme A):

\[\text{Pyruvic acid (3C)} + \mathrm{CoA} + \mathrm{NAD^+} \xrightarrow[\text{Pyruvate dehydrogenase}]{\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}} \text{Acetyl CoA (2C)} + \mathrm{CO_2} + \mathrm{NADH + H^+}\]

Because one glucose yields two pyruvates, the link reaction produces \(2\,\mathrm{Acetyl\ CoA}\), \(2\,\mathrm{CO_2}\), and \(2\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) per glucose.

2. Tricarboxylic Acid (TCA) / Citric Acid Cycle

Elucidated by Hans Krebs, the TCA cycle begins when Acetyl CoA (2C) condenses with oxaloacetic acid (OAA, 4C) and water to form citric acid (6C), catalyzed by citrate synthase (releasing CoA). Citrate isomerizes to isocitrate, followed by two successive decarboxylations forming α-ketoglutaric acid (5C) and succinyl-CoA (4C). During conversion of succinyl-CoA to succinic acid, one GTP is formed by substrate-level phosphorylation, which transfers its phosphate to ADP to form 1 ATP. Subsequent oxidations regenerate OAA.

Note: Including the link reaction, each pyruvate yields \(3\,\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(4\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(1\,\mathrm{FADH_2}\), and \(1\,\mathrm{ATP}\). Thus, per glucose (2 pyruvates), the matrix reactions yield \(6\,\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(8\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(2\,\mathrm{FADH_2}\), and \(2\,\mathrm{ATP}\).

Electron Transport System (ETS) & Oxidative Phosphorylation

The energy stored in \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) and \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) is harvested by passing their electrons through an ordered series of carriers embedded in the inner mitochondrial membrane, known as the Electron Transport System (ETS):

  • Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase): Oxidizes matrix \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\) (via FMN and Fe–S centres) and transfers electrons to ubiquinone (UQ) within the inner membrane.
  • Complex II (Succinate dehydrogenase): Receives reducing equivalents from succinate oxidation via \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) (FAD, Fe–S) and transfers them to ubiquinone.
  • Complex III (Cytochrome bc1 complex): Oxidizes reduced ubiquinol (\(\mathrm{UQH_2}\)) and passes electrons to cytochrome c.
  • Cytochrome c: A small mobile peripheral protein on the outer surface of the inner membrane that shuttles electrons from Complex III to Complex IV.
  • Complex IV (Cytochrome c oxidase): Contains cytochromes a and a3 and two copper centres (\(\mathrm{Cu_A}\), \(\mathrm{Cu_B}\)). It transfers electrons to molecular oxygen (\(\mathrm{O_2}\)), the final hydrogen/electron acceptor, forming \(\mathrm{H_2O}\).

Chemiosmotic ATP Synthesis (Complex V)

As electrons flow through Complexes I, III, and IV, protons (\(\mathrm{H^+}\)) are pumped from the matrix into the intermembrane space, creating an electrochemical proton gradient. Protons flow back down this gradient through ATP synthase (Complex V), which has two parts: F0 (an integral membrane proton channel) and F1 (a peripheral headpiece facing the matrix that catalyzes \(\mathrm{ADP} + \mathrm{P_i} \rightarrow \mathrm{ATP}\)). For every ATP synthesized, \(2\,\mathrm{H^+}\) pass through F0. Oxidation of \(1\,\mathrm{NADH}\) yields 3 ATP, whereas \(1\,\mathrm{FADH_2}\) yields 2 ATP.

Respiratory Balance Sheet & Amphibolic Pathway

Under idealized theoretical assumptions—(i) sequential operation of glycolysis, TCA, and ETS; (ii) full transfer of cytosolic NADH into mitochondria for oxidative phosphorylation; (iii) zero withdrawal of intermediates for biosynthesis; and (iv) glucose as the sole substrate—the complete aerobic oxidation of one glucose molecule yields a theoretical net gain of 38 ATP:

Stage Direct ATP (Net) NADH + H+ (× 3 ATP) FADH2 (× 2 ATP) Total ATP
Glycolysis (Cytoplasm) 2 ATP 2 NADH → 6 ATP 0 8 ATP
Link Reaction (2 × Pyruvate) 0 2 NADH → 6 ATP 0 6 ATP
TCA Cycle (2 × Acetyl CoA) 2 ATP (via GTP) 6 NADH → 18 ATP 2 FADH2 → 4 ATP 24 ATP
Grand Total (1 Glucose) 4 ATP 10 NADH → 30 ATP 2 FADH2 → 4 ATP 38 ATP

In living cells, these pathways operate simultaneously rather than strictly sequentially, and intermediates are continuously withdrawn or fed in according to cellular needs.

Why Respiration is an Amphibolic Pathway

Traditionally viewed as purely catabolic (breakdown), the respiratory pathway actually serves both breakdown (catabolism) and synthesis (anabolism), making it an amphibolic pathway:

  • Fats: Broken down into fatty acids (enter as acetyl CoA) and glycerol (enters as PGAL). Conversely, when the cell synthesizes fatty acids, acetyl CoA is withdrawn from the respiratory pathway.
  • Proteins: Hydrolyzed by proteases into amino acids, which after deamination enter at pyruvate, acetyl CoA, or TCA intermediates; conversely, respiratory intermediates serve as carbon skeletons to synthesize amino acids.

Respiratory Quotient (RQ)

The Respiratory Quotient (RQ), or respiratory ratio, is the ratio of the volume of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) evolved to the volume of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) consumed during aerobic respiration:

\[\mathrm{RQ} = \frac{\text{Volume of }\mathrm{CO_2}\text{ evolved}}{\text{Volume of }\mathrm{O_2}\text{ consumed}}\]
  • Carbohydrates: \(\mathrm{RQ} = 1.0\) (equal volumes of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) evolved and \(\mathrm{O_2}\) consumed: \(6\,\mathrm{CO_2} / 6\,\mathrm{O_2} = 1.0\)).
  • Fats: \(\mathrm{RQ} < 1\) (e.g., for tripalmitin, \(2\,\mathrm{C_{51}H_{98}O_6} + 145\,\mathrm{O_2} \rightarrow 102\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 98\,\mathrm{H_2O}\), so \(\mathrm{RQ} = 102 / 145 = 0.7\)).
  • Proteins: \(\mathrm{RQ} \approx 0.9\).

Worked Examples

1. Calculating Net ATP Yield in Aerobic Respiration

Problem (Assumed Scenario): Suppose a plant cell completely oxidizes 3 molecules of sucrose through aerobic respiration under idealized balance-sheet conditions. Calculate the total net ATP molecules generated.

Solution:

  1. Invertase hydrolyzes 1 sucrose molecule into 1 glucose + 1 fructose (2 hexose units). Therefore, 3 sucrose molecules yield \(3 \times 2 = 6\) hexose units entering glycolysis.
  2. Each hexose unit yields a theoretical net gain of \(38\,\mathrm{ATP}\) upon complete aerobic oxidation (8 ATP from glycolysis + 6 ATP from link reaction + 24 ATP from TCA cycle).
  3. Total net ATP = \(6 \times 38 = 228\,\mathrm{ATP}\).

2. Determining Respiratory Quotient for a Lipid Substrate

Problem (Assumed Scenario): A hypothetical saturated lipid molecule has the formula \(\mathrm{C_{39}H_{74}O_6}\) and undergoes complete aerobic oxidation according to the balanced equation:

\[2\,\mathrm{C_{39}H_{74}O_6} + 109\,\mathrm{O_2} \longrightarrow 78\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 74\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \text{Energy}\]

Calculate its Respiratory Quotient (RQ) and state whether it is higher or lower than that of glucose.

Solution:

\[\mathrm{RQ} = \frac{\text{Volume of }\mathrm{CO_2}\text{ evolved}}{\text{Volume of }\mathrm{O_2}\text{ consumed}} = \frac{78}{109} \approx 0.716\]

Since \(0.716 < 1.0\), its RQ is lower than that of glucose (\(1.0\)), which is characteristic of oxygen-poor lipid substrates.

3. Tracing Reducing Equivalents and Substrate-Level ATP from Pyruvate

Problem (Assumed Scenario): If 4 molecules of pyruvic acid enter the mitochondrial matrix under aerobic conditions, how many \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), and direct ATP (via GTP) are produced before ETS?

Solution:

From the combined link reaction and TCA cycle, 1 pyruvic acid molecule yields \(3\,\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(4\,\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(1\,\mathrm{FADH_2}\), and \(1\,\mathrm{ATP}\). Multiplying by 4 pyruvic acid molecules:

  • \(\mathrm{CO_2} = 4 \times 3 = 12\) molecules
  • \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+} = 4 \times 4 = 16\) molecules
  • \(\mathrm{FADH_2} = 4 \times 1 = 4\) molecules
  • Direct ATP (via GTP) = \(4 \times 1 = 4\) molecules

  2. प्रत्येक हेक्सोज इकाई के पूर्ण ऑक्सीकरण से सैद्धांतिक रूप से \(38\,\mathrm{ATP}\) प्राप्त होते हैं (ग्लाइकोलिसिस से 8 ATP + लिंक अभिक्रिया से 6 ATP + TCA चक्र से 24 ATP)।
  3. कुल शुद्ध ATP = \(6 \times 38 = 228\,\mathrm{ATP}\)।

2. लिपिड क्रियाधार के लिए श्वसन गुणांक (RQ) का निर्धारण

प्रश्न (कल्पित परिदृश्य): एक कल्पित संतृप्त लिपिड अणु का सूत्र \(\mathrm{C_{39}H_{74}O_6}\) है और यह निम्नलिखित संतुलित समीकरण के अनुसार पूर्ण ऑक्सी श्वसन करता है:

\[2\,\mathrm{C_{39}H_{74}O_6} + 109\,\mathrm{O_2} \longrightarrow 78\,\mathrm{CO_2} + 74\,\mathrm{H_2O} + \text{Energy}\]

इसका श्वसन गुणांक (RQ) ज्ञात कीजिए तथा बताइए कि यह ग्लूकोज के RQ से अधिक है या कम।

हल:

\[\mathrm{RQ} = \frac{\text{मुक्त }\mathrm{CO_2}\text{ का आयतन}}{\text{उपभोग }\mathrm{O_2}\text{ का आयतन}} = \frac{78}{109} \approx 0.716\]

चूँकि \(0.716 < 1.0\), इसका RQ ग्लूकोज (\(1.0\)) से कम है, जो ऑक्सीजन-न्यून वसीय क्रियाधारों की विशेषता है।

हल:

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

  • Pitfall 1: Confusing total ATP made in glycolysis (4 ATP) with net ATP gain (2 ATP). Always subtract the 2 ATP invested in the preparatory steps.
  • Pitfall 2: Assuming \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) is released in all fermentations. Only alcoholic fermentation releases \(\mathrm{CO_2}\); lactic acid fermentation does not.
  • Pitfall 3: Forgetting to double TCA cycle outputs per glucose. One glucose produces two pyruvates, so multiply single-turn TCA products by 2.
  • Pitfall 4: Misplacing \(\mathrm{O_2}\)'s role. Oxygen acts only at the terminal step (Complex IV) as the final electron/hydrogen acceptor, yet it drives the entire ETS by removing electrons.

Concise Final Recap

Plants exchange gases locally via stomata and lenticels. Cellular respiration begins in the cytoplasm with glycolysis (glucose → 2 pyruvic acid, net 2 ATP + 2 NADH + H+). Without \(\mathrm{O_2}\), fermentation regenerates \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) with a net yield of only 2 ATP. With \(\mathrm{O_2}\), pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix for the link reaction and TCA cycle, generating \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\), and GTP (ATP). Electrons then pass through ETS (Complexes I–IV) on the inner membrane to \(\mathrm{O_2}\), driving chemiosmotic ATP synthesis via Complex V (F0–F1) for a theoretical yield of 38 ATP per glucose. The pathway is amphibolic, and substrate identity determines the Respiratory Quotient (RQ).

पादप रंध्रों और वातरंध्रों द्वारा स्थानीय स्तर पर गैसीय विनिमय करते हैं। कोशिकीय श्वसन कोशिकाद्रव्य में ग्लाइकोलिसिस (ग्लूकोज → 2 पायरुविक अम्ल, शुद्ध 2 ATP + 2 NADH + H+) से प्रारंभ होता है। \(\mathrm{O_2}\) के बिना किण्वन द्वारा \(\mathrm{NAD^+}\) का पुनर्जनन होता है और केवल 2 ATP प्राप्त होते हैं। \(\mathrm{O_2}\) की उपस्थिति में पायरुवेट माइटोकॉन्ड्रियल मैट्रिक्स में लिंक अभिक्रिया एवं TCA चक्र में प्रवेश कर \(\mathrm{CO_2}\), \(\mathrm{NADH + H^+}\), \(\mathrm{FADH_2}\) व GTP (ATP) उत्पन्न करता है। इसके पश्चात आंतरिक झिल्ली पर ETS (संकुल I–IV) से इलेक्ट्रॉन \(\mathrm{O_2}\) तक जाते हैं, जिससे संकुल V (F0–F1) द्वारा रसोपरासरणी विधि से प्रति ग्लूकोज सैद्धांतिक रूप से 38 ATP संश्लेषित होते हैं। यह पथ एम्फिबोलिक है और क्रियाधार की प्रकृति श्वसन गुणांक (RQ) निर्धारित करती है।
