  • Identify invertebrate and vertebrate excretory structures across major phyla.
  • Trace blood flow and filtrate processing through the gross anatomy of the human kidney and the microscopic nephron.
  • Explain ultrafiltration, selective tubular reabsorption, tubular secretion, and the counter-current mechanism.
  • Analyze homeostatic regulation of Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) via ADH, the Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS), and Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF).
  • Connect clinical disorders such as uremia, renal calculi, and glomerulonephritis with diagnostic urine parameters and hemodialysis principles.

Nitrogenous Wastes & Comparative Excretory Organs

Protein and nucleic acid catabolism produces ammonia (\(\mathrm{NH_3}\)), which is highly toxic and soluble. Depending on habitat and water economy, animals excrete nitrogen primarily in one of three chemical forms:

  • Ammonotelism: Excretion of ammonia. Because ammonia is extremely toxic, it must be diluted in large volumes of water. It diffuses readily across body surfaces or gill epithelia as ammonium ions (\(\mathrm{NH_4^+}\)); kidneys play minimal role. Examples: many bony fishes, aquatic amphibians, and aquatic insects.
  • Ureotelism: Excretion of urea. Terrestrial adaptation favored converting toxic ammonia into much less toxic urea in the liver, which enters blood and is filtered by kidneys. Some animals retain a small fraction of urea in the kidney medullary matrix to maintain osmolarity. Examples: mammals, many terrestrial amphibians, and marine fishes.
  • Uricotelism: Excretion of uric acid as a semi-solid paste or pellet. Uric acid is the least toxic and practically insoluble in water, allowing maximum water conservation. Examples: reptiles, birds, land snails, and insects.

Ureotelic Urea Moderate toxicity; moderate water needed Mammals, terrestrial amphibians, marine fishes

Human Excretory System & Nephron Architecture

The human urinary system comprises a pair of kidneys, a pair of ureters, a muscular urinary bladder, and a urethra. The reddish-brown, bean-shaped kidneys lie against the dorsal inner wall of the abdominal cavity between the 12th thoracic (T12) and 3rd lumbar (L3) vertebrae. Each adult kidney measures approximately 10–12 cm in length, 5–7 cm in width, and 2–3 cm in thickness, weighing 120–170 g.

Along the medial concave border sits a notch called the hilum, through which the ureter, renal blood vessels, and nerves enter or exit. Internally, a tough fibrous capsule encloses two distinct zones: an outer cortex and an inner medulla. The medulla is organized into conical medullary pyramids whose apices project into funnel-shaped calyces leading into the renal pelvis. Cortical tissue extends between adjacent pyramids as renal columns (Columns of Bertini).

Functional Unit: The Nephron

Each kidney houses nearly one million nephrons. Every nephron consists of two main components:

  1. Glomerulus: A high-pressure capillary tuft supplied by a wider afferent arteriole (branch of renal artery) and drained by a narrower efferent arteriole.
  2. Renal Tubule: Begins as the double-walled cup-like Bowman's capsule enclosing the glomerulus (together forming the Malpighian body or renal corpuscle). It continues into the coiled Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT), dips into the medulla as the hairpin Henle's loop (with descending and ascending limbs), rises back into the cortex as the Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT), and empties into a shared collecting duct. Multiple collecting ducts converge through medullary pyramids into the calyces.

Cortical vs. Juxtamedullary Nephrons: In most nephrons (cortical nephrons), the loop of Henle is short and barely enters the medulla, and the vasa recta (a U-shaped capillary loop arising from peritubular capillaries) is absent or highly reduced. In juxtamedullary nephrons, the loop of Henle plunges deep into the inner medulla alongside a well-developed vasa recta, enabling concentrated urine formation.

Urine Formation & Segmental Tubular Functions

Urine formation integrates three sequential processes: glomerular filtration, tubular reabsorption, and tubular secretion.

1. Glomerular Filtration (Ultrafiltration)

On average, the kidneys receive 1100–1200 mL of blood per minute, roughly one-fifth of cardiac output per ventricle. Glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure forces plasma across a three-layered filtration membrane:

  1. Endothelium of glomerular blood vessels
  2. Basement membrane (acellular middle layer)
  3. Epithelium of Bowman's capsule composed of specialized cells called podocytes, arranged with minute spaces called filtration slits (slit pores)

Because almost all plasma solutes except plasma proteins pass into Bowman's capsule, this non-selective process is termed ultrafiltration. In a healthy adult, the Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) is:

\[\mathrm{GFR} \approx 125\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}} = 180\,\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\]

2. Tubular Reabsorption and Secretion

Comparing 180 L of daily filtrate with 1.5 L of daily urine output reveals that nearly 99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed by renal tubular epithelial cells. Active transport recovers glucose, amino acids, and \(\mathrm{Na^+}\); passive transport reabsorbs nitrogenous wastes (like urea) and water (in initial segments). Simultaneously, tubular cells secrete \(\mathrm{H^+}\), \(\mathrm{K^+}\), and \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) into the filtrate to regulate blood pH and ionic balance.

मूत्र निर्माण एवं नलिकाओं के खंडवार कार्य

मूत्र निर्माण तीन प्रमुख प्रक्रियाओं के समन्वय से पूर्ण होता है: गुच्छीय निस्यंदन, नलिकीय पुनरावशोषण तथा नलिकीय स्रवण।

1. गुच्छीय निस्यंदन (परानिस्यंदन / Ultrafiltration)

वृक्कों द्वारा प्रति मिनट औसतन 1100–1200 mL रक्त का निस्यंदन होता है, जो हृदय के प्रत्येक निलय द्वारा प्रति मिनट पंप किए गए रक्त का लगभग 1/5 भाग है। केशिकागुच्छीय रक्त दाब के कारण प्लाज्मा तीन परतों की निस्यंदन झिल्ली से छनता है:

  1. केशिकागुच्छीय रक्त वाहिकाओं की अंतःकला (एंडोथीलियम)
  2. आधार झिल्ली (बेसमेंट मेम्ब्रेन)
  3. बोमन संपुट की उपकला, जिसमें पोडोसाइट्स (पदाणु) नामक विशिष्ट कोशिकाएँ सूक्ष्म निस्यंदन खाँच (स्लिट पोर्स) बनाती हैं

प्लाज्मा प्रोटीन को छोड़कर रक्त प्लाज्मा के लगभग सभी घटक बोमन संपुट की गुहा में आ जाते हैं, इसलिए इसे परानिस्यंदन कहते हैं। एक स्वस्थ वयस्क में गुच्छीय निस्यंदन दर (GFR) होती है:

\[\mathrm{GFR} \approx 125\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}} = 180\,\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\]

Mechanism of Concentration: The Counter-Current System

Mammals can excrete urine roughly four times more concentrated than the initial glomerular filtrate—raising osmolarity from \(300\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) in the cortex to \(1200\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) in the inner medulla. This steep corticomedullary osmotic gradient depends on the counter-current mechanism formed by two anatomical loops running in opposite flow directions:

  • Loop of Henle: Filtrate flows downward in the descending limb and upward in the ascending limb.
  • Vasa Recta: Blood flows downward in its descending limb and upward in its ascending limb, closely paralleling Henle's loop.

How the Gradient Is Built and Maintained:

  1. NaCl transported out of the ascending limb of Henle's loop enters the descending limb of the vasa recta and is returned to the medullary interstitium by the ascending vasa recta, preventing solute washout.
  2. Small amounts of urea diffuse out of the collecting duct into the inner medullary interstitium and enter the thin ascending limb of Henle's loop, recycling urea to sustain high inner-medullary osmolarity.
  3. As filtrate travels down the collecting duct through this hyperosmotic interstitium (\(300 \rightarrow 1200\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\)), water exits osmotically into the interstitium, concentrating the urine.

Regulation of Kidney Function, Micturition & Other Organs

Kidney output is tightly governed by hormonal feedback loops involving the hypothalamus, Juxtaglomerular Apparatus (JGA), and the heart:

  • ADH (Vasopressin) Mechanism: Excessive fluid loss activates osmoreceptors, stimulating the hypothalamus to release ADH from the neurohypophysis. ADH increases water reabsorption from the distal tubule and collecting duct (preventing diuresis) and constricts blood vessels, raising blood pressure and GFR. Rising body fluid volume switches off osmoreceptors and suppresses ADH.
  • Renin–Angiotensin–Aldosterone Mechanism (JGA): The JGA forms where the DCT contacts the afferent arteriole. A drop in glomerular blood flow or GFR triggers JG cells to secrete renin, converting plasma angiotensinogen into angiotensin I and then angiotensin II. Angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor (raising glomerular blood pressure and GFR) and stimulates the adrenal cortex to release aldosterone, promoting \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) and water reabsorption from distal tubules.
  • ANF (Atrial Natriuretic Factor): Increased blood flow to the cardiac atria triggers ANF release, causing vasodilation and lowering blood pressure. ANF thus acts as an inhibitory check on the renin-angiotensin system.

Micturition & Urine Characteristics

Stretching of the filling urinary bladder activates wall stretch receptors that signal the Central Nervous System (CNS). The CNS sends motor signals causing contraction of bladder smooth muscles and simultaneous relaxation of the urethral sphincter to release urine—a neural process called the micturition reflex. An adult excretes 1–1.5 L of light-yellow, slightly acidic urine (pH 6.0) containing 25–30 g of urea per day. Presence of glucose (glycosuria) and ketone bodies (ketonuria) indicates diabetes mellitus.

Accessory Excretory Organs

  • Lungs: Eliminate approximately 200 mL/minute of \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) plus significant water vapor daily.
  • Liver: Secretes bile containing bilirubin, biliverdin, cholesterol, degraded steroid hormones, vitamins, and drugs, which exit with feces.
  • Skin: Sweat glands excrete watery sweat (NaCl, small urea, lactic acid) primarily for cooling; sebaceous glands eliminate sterols, hydrocarbons, and waxes via sebum. Small amounts of nitrogenous wastes also exit in saliva.

वृक्क क्रिया का नियमन, मूत्रण एवं अन्य उत्सर्जी अंग

वृक्कों की कार्यप्रणाली का नियमन हाइपोथैलेमस, जक्सटाग्लोमेरुलर उपकरण (JGA) तथा हृदय से जुड़े हार्मोनल प्रतिपुष्टि तंत्रों द्वारा होता है:

  • ADH (वैसोप्रेसिन) तंत्र: शरीर से अत्यधिक द्रव हानि होने पर परासरणग्राही (ऑस्मोरिसेप्टर्स) सक्रिय होकर हाइपोथैलेमस को न्यूरोहाइपोफाइसिस से ADH स्रावित करने हेतु प्रेरित करते हैं। ADH नलिका के अंतिम भागों से जल पुनरावशोषण बढ़ाकर मूत्रलता (डायूरेसिस) रोकता है और रक्त वाहिकाओं को संकुचित कर रक्त दाब व GFR बढ़ाता है। द्रव आयतन बढ़ने पर ADH स्रवण रुक जाता है।
  • रेनिन-एंजियोटेन्सिन-एल्डोस्टेरोन तंत्र (JGA): DCT और अभिवाही धमनिका के संपर्क स्थल पर JGA स्थित होता है। गुच्छीय रक्त दाब या GFR गिरने पर JG कोशिकाएँ रेनिन स्रावित करती हैं, जो रक्त के एंजियोटेन्सिनोजन को एंजियोटेन्सिन I और फिर एंजियोटेन्सिन II में बदलता है। यह शक्तिशाली वाहिकासंकीर्णक है (जो रक्त दाब व GFR बढ़ाता है) और अधिवृक्क वल्कुट को एल्डोस्टेरोन स्रावित करने हेतु प्रेरित करता है, जिससे दूरस्थ नलिकाओं में \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) और जल का पुनरावशोषण बढ़ता है।
  • ANF (एट्रियल नेट्रियूरेटिक कारक): हृदय के आलिंद में रक्त प्रवाह बढ़ने पर ANF स्रावित होता है, जो वाहिकाविस्फारण (वैसोडाइलेशन) द्वारा रक्त दाब कम करता है और इस प्रकार रेनिन-एंजियोटेन्सिन तंत्र पर नियंत्रण रखता है।

Disorders of the Excretory System

  • Uremia & Hemodialysis: Kidney malfunction causes toxic urea accumulation in blood (uremia). In hemodialysis, blood drawn from an artery is mixed with an anticoagulant (heparin) and pumped through a coiled porous cellophane tube immersed in dialysing fluid. The dialysing fluid matches plasma composition except it lacks nitrogenous wastes, allowing urea and wastes to diffuse out down their concentration gradient. Cleared blood is mixed with anti-heparin and returned via a vein.
  • Renal Failure & Transplantation: Acute renal failure is ultimately corrected by kidney transplantation, preferably from a close relative to minimize host immune rejection.
  • Renal Calculi: Stones or insoluble masses of crystallized salts (such as oxalates) formed within the kidney.
  • Glomerulonephritis: Inflammation of the glomeruli of the kidney.

Worked Examples

1. Calculating Daily Filtrate and Reabsorption Percentage

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): Suppose a patient's Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) drops to \(100\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}}\) due to mild dehydration, and their daily urine output is measured at \(1.2\,\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\). Calculate the total filtrate formed per day and the percentage of filtrate reabsorbed.

Solution:

First, convert GFR from \(\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}}\) to \(\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\) (using \(1440\,\mathrm{min\,day^{-1}}\)):

\[\text{Daily Filtrate} = 100\,\mathrm{mL\,min^{-1}} \times 1440\,\mathrm{min\,day^{-1}} = 144{,}000\,\mathrm{mL\,day^{-1}} = 144\,\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\]

Next, compute the volume reabsorbed and its percentage:

\[\text{Reabsorbed Volume} = 144 - 1.2 = 142.8\,\mathrm{L\,day^{-1}}\] \[\text{Reabsorption Percentage} = \frac{142.8}{144} \times 100\% = 99.17\%\]

Even with reduced GFR, over 99% of the filtrate is conserved.

2. Predicting Filtrate Osmolarity Along Henle's Loop

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): Consider a juxtamedullary nephron where cortical interstitium is \(300\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) and inner medullary interstitium reaches \(1200\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\). Describe the osmolarity change as filtrate moves from the end of PCT, down the descending limb, and up the ascending limb to the start of DCT.

Solution:

  • End of PCT: Iso-osmotic with plasma at \(300\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) because water and solutes are reabsorbed in equal proportions.
  • Descending Limb: Permeable only to water; water exits into the hyperosmotic medulla, concentrating the filtrate progressively up to \(1200\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) at the hairpin bend.
  • Ascending Limb: Impermeable to water; NaCl is transported out into the interstitium, diluting the filtrate back down to approximately \(100\text{–}200\,\mathrm{mOsmol\,L^{-1}}\) (hypo-osmotic) as it enters the DCT.

3. Diagnosing Hormonal Response to Hemorrhage

Scenario (Hypothetical Assumption): An athlete loses significant blood volume after an injury, causing a sharp fall in arterial blood pressure and GFR. Which hormonal pathways are activated, and which hormone is suppressed?

Solution:

  • Activated: (1) RAAS: Low GFR triggers JG cells to release renin, generating angiotensin II (vasoconstriction) and aldosterone (\(\mathrm{Na^+}\)/water retention). (2) ADH: Reduced blood volume activates osmoreceptors/volume receptors, increasing ADH release to conserve water and constrict vessels.
  • Suppressed: Atrial Natriuretic Factor (ANF) is suppressed because atrial stretch decreases during low blood volume.

Common Pitfalls & NEET UG Revision Checklist

High-Yield Exam Traps to Avoid
  • Marine fishes vs. Bony freshwater fishes: Bony fishes are generally ammonotelic, whereas marine fishes are ureotelic.
  • Amphioxus exception: Flame cells (protonephridia) are mostly invertebrate structures, but they also occur in the chordate Amphioxus.

Concise Final Recap

Excretory systems balance metabolic waste removal with water and electrolyte homeostasis. Aquatic animals excrete toxic ammonia directly, whereas terrestrial vertebrates conserve water by converting nitrogen into urea (ureotelism) or insoluble uric acid (uricotelism). In humans, each kidney contains about one million nephrons that filter 180 L of plasma daily across the three-layered glomerular membrane, reabsorb ~99% of water and solutes along the PCT, Henle's loop, DCT, and collecting duct, and secrete excess \(\mathrm{H^+}\), \(\mathrm{K^+}\), and \(\mathrm{NH_3}\). The counter-current interplay between Henle's loop and the vasa recta builds a 300 to 1200 mOsmol/L medullary gradient, enabling ADH-mediated urine concentration. Overall fluid balance and blood pressure are fine-tuned by ADH, RAAS, and ANF, with lungs, liver, and skin providing essential accessory excretion.
