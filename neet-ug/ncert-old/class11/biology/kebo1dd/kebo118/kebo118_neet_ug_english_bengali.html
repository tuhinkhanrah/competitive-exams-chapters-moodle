2. Internal Transport Fluids: Blood and Lymph

Blood is a specialised fluid connective tissue composed of a liquid matrix called plasma (~55% of blood volume) and cellular components called formed elements (~45% of blood volume).

2.1 Plasma and Serum

Plasma is a straw-coloured, viscous fluid containing 90–92% water and 6–8% proteins. The three major plasma proteins perform distinct physiological tasks:

  • Fibrinogen: Essential for blood coagulation (clotting).
  • Globulins: Primarily involved in body defence mechanisms (immunoglobulins).
  • Albumins: Maintain osmotic balance of the blood.

Plasma also carries dissolved minerals (\(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\), \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\)), nutrients in transit (glucose, amino acids, lipids), and inactive clotting factors. Serum is plasma without its clotting factors.

2.2 Formed Elements

Component Normal Count / Features Origin & Lifespan Primary Function
Erythrocytes (RBCs) 5.0–5.5 million \(\mathrm{mm^{-3}}\); biconcave, enucleated in most mammals; 12–16 g haemoglobin per 100 mL blood Red bone marrow (adults); ~120 days; destroyed in spleen (graveyard of RBCs) Transport of respiratory gases (\(\mathrm{O_2}\) and \(\mathrm{CO_2}\))
Leucocytes (WBCs) 6000–8000 \(\mathrm{mm^{-3}}\); nucleated, colourless (lack haemoglobin) Bone marrow & lymphoid organs; generally short-lived Immune defence and phagocytosis
Platelets (Thrombocytes) 1,50,000–3,50,000 \(\mathrm{mm^{-3}}\); non-nucleated cell fragments Fragmented from megakaryocytes in bone marrow Release clotting factors for blood coagulation

2.3 Classification of Leucocytes (WBCs)

WBCs are grouped into granulocytes (neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils) and agranulocytes (lymphocytes, monocytes):

  • Neutrophils (60–65%): Most abundant WBCs; phagocytic cells that engulf and destroy foreign pathogens.
  • Lymphocytes (20–25%): Two major types—B and T lymphocytes—responsible for specific immune responses.
  • Monocytes (6–8%): Large phagocytic cells that destroy foreign organisms.
  • Eosinophils (2–3%): Resist infections and are associated with allergic reactions.
  • Basophils (0.5–1%): Least abundant WBCs; secrete histamine, serotonin, and heparin during inflammatory reactions.

2.4 Lymph (Tissue Fluid)

When blood flows through tissue capillaries, water and small water-soluble solutes filter out into intercellular spaces, leaving behind larger plasma proteins and most formed elements. This interstitial (tissue) fluid shares the same mineral distribution as plasma and mediates all nutrient and gas exchange between blood and cells. Collected by the lymphatic system and drained back into major veins, it is termed lymph—a colourless fluid rich in specialised lymphocytes for immunity and responsible for transporting hormones and absorbing fats via lacteals in intestinal villi.

২.১ প্লাজমা ও সিরাম

প্লাজমা হলো হালকা খড়ের রঙের সান্দ্র তরল, যার ৯০–৯২% জল এবং ৬–৮% প্রোটিন। প্রধান তিনটি প্লাজমা প্রোটিন হলো:

  • ফাইব্রিনোজেন (Fibrinogen): রক্ত তঞ্চন বা জমাট বাঁধার জন্য অপরিহার্য।
  • গ্লোবিউলিন (Globulins): প্রধানত দেহের প্রতিরক্ষা ব্যবস্থায় অংশ নেয়।
  • অ্যালবুমিন (Albumins): রক্তের অভিস্রবণিক ভারসাম্য (Osmotic balance) বজায় রাখে।

প্লাজমায় খনিজ আয়ন (\(\mathrm{Na^+}\), \(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{Mg^{2+}}\), \(\mathrm{HCO_3^-}\), \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\)), পরিবাহিত খাদ্যবস্তু (গ্লুকোজ, অ্যামিনো অ্যাসিড, লিপিড) এবং নিষ্ক্রিয় তঞ্চন ফ্যাক্টর থাকে। তঞ্চন ফ্যাক্টরবিহীন প্লাজমাকে সিরাম (Serum) বলে।

২.৪ লসিকা (কলারস বা Tissue Fluid)

3. Blood Grouping & Coagulation Cascade

3.1 ABO Blood Grouping

ABO grouping depends on the presence or absence of two surface antigens (A and B) on RBC membranes and corresponding natural antibodies (anti-A and anti-B) in plasma. Mismatched transfusion triggers clumping (agglutination and RBC destruction).

BBanti-AB, O
ABA, BNilAB, A, B, O (Universal Recipient)
ONilanti-A, anti-BO (Universal Donor)

3.2 Rh Grouping & Erythroblastosis Foetalis

Nearly 80% of humans possess the Rh antigen (similar to that in Rhesus monkeys) on their RBCs and are Rh+ve; those lacking it are Rh−ve. An Rh−ve individual exposed to Rh+ve blood develops anti-Rh antibodies.

Clinical Case: Erythroblastosis Foetalis

When an Rh−ve mother carries an Rh+ve foetus, the placenta prevents mixing during the first pregnancy. However, during delivery of the first child, foetal Rh+ve blood may enter maternal circulation, stimulating maternal anti-Rh antibody production. In subsequent pregnancies with an Rh+ve foetus, maternal anti-Rh antibodies cross the placenta and destroy foetal RBCs, causing severe anaemia, jaundice, or foetal death. Prevention: Administer anti-Rh antibodies to the mother immediately after delivery of the first child.

3.3 Mechanism of Blood Coagulation

Upon injury, platelets and damaged tissues release factors that initiate a linked enzymatic cascade in plasma. Calcium ions (\(\mathrm{Ca^{2+}}\)) are indispensable for this cascade.

৩.২ Rh শ্রেণিবিভাগ ও এরিথ্রোব্লাস্টোসিস ফিটালিস

প্রায় ৮০% মানুষের RBC-তে Rh অ্যান্টিজেন (রেসাস বানরের অনুরূপ) থাকে, এদের Rh+ve বলে; আর যাদের থাকে না, তারা Rh−ve। কোনো Rh−ve ব্যক্তি Rh+ve রক্তের সংস্পর্শে এলে তার দেহে anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি তৈরি হয়।

বিশেষ অবস্থা: এরিথ্রোব্লাস্টোসিস ফিটালিস (Erythroblastosis Foetalis)

Rh−ve মায়ের গর্ভে Rh+ve ভ্রূণ থাকলে প্রথম গর্ভাবস্থায় অমরা (Placenta) উভয় রক্তকে পৃথক রাখে। কিন্তু প্রথম প্রসবের সময় ভ্রূণের সামান্য Rh+ve রক্ত মায়ের রক্তে মিশে মায়ের দেহে anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি তৈরি করতে পারে। পরবর্তী গর্ভাবস্থায় ভ্রূণ Rh+ve হলে মায়ের anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি ভ্রূণের রক্তে প্রবেশ করে RBC ধ্বংস করে, যা ভ্রূণের তীব্র রক্তাল্পতা, জন্ডিস বা মৃত্যুর কারণ হয়। প্রতিরোধ: প্রথম সন্তান প্রসবের পরপরই মাকে anti-Rh অ্যান্টিবডি প্রয়োগ করতে হয়।

4. Circulatory Pathways & The Human Heart

4.1 Open vs. Closed Circulation & Vertebrate Hearts

  • Open Circulatory System: Found in arthropods and molluscs; blood pumped by the heart enters open body cavities called sinuses.
  • Closed Circulatory System: Found in annelids and chordates; blood remains confined within a closed network of vessels, allowing precise flow regulation.
  • Fishes (2-chambered heart): 1 atrium + 1 ventricle; pumps deoxygenated blood to gills for oxygenation and then directly to body tissues (single circulation).
  • Amphibians & Reptiles except Crocodiles (3-chambered heart): 2 atria + 1 ventricle; oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mix in the single ventricle (incomplete double circulation).
  • Crocodiles, Birds & Mammals (4-chambered heart): 2 atria + 2 ventricles; complete separation of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood (complete double circulation).

4.2 Human Heart Anatomy & Nodal Conduction System

The mesodermally derived human heart sits in the thoracic cavity between the two lungs, slightly tilted to the left, protected by a double-walled pericardium containing pericardial fluid. Ventricular walls are much thicker than atrial walls.

  • Septa: Thin muscular inter-atrial septum; thick inter-ventricular septum; thick fibrous atrio-ventricular (AV) septum.
  • Valves (ensure unidirectional flow): Tricuspid valve (3 cusps) guards the right AV opening; Bicuspid (Mitral) valve guards the left AV opening; Semilunar valves guard the openings of the right ventricle into the pulmonary artery and the left ventricle into the aorta.
  • Nodal Tissue (Autoexcitable):
    1. Sino-atrial node (SAN): Upper right corner of the right atrium; generates 70–75 action potentials \(\mathrm{min^{-1}}\) (maximum rate), hence called the pacemaker.
    2. Atrio-ventricular node (AVN): Lower left corner of the right atrium near the AV septum.
    3. AV Bundle (Bundle of His) & Purkinje Fibres: Continues from AVN through the AV septum, divides into right and left bundle branches atop the inter-ventricular septum, and spreads as minute Purkinje fibres throughout ventricular musculature.

5. Cardiac Cycle, Electrocardiography & Double Circulation

5.1 Events of the Cardiac Cycle

  1. Joint Diastole: All four chambers are relaxed; tricuspid and bicuspid valves are open, allowing blood from pulmonary veins and vena cava to flow passively into ventricles; semilunar valves remain closed.
  2. Atrial Systole: SAN triggers simultaneous atrial contraction, increasing ventricular filling by about 30%.
  3. Ventricular Systole: Impulse travels via AVN → Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres. Ventricular contraction raises pressure, closing AV valves (producing the first heart sound, "lub") and forcing semilunar valves open so blood enters the pulmonary artery and aorta.
  4. Ventricular Diastole: Ventricles relax, pressure drops, and semilunar valves close (producing the second heart sound, "dub") to prevent backflow.
\[ \text{Cardiac Cycle Duration} = \frac{60\,\mathrm{s}}{\text{Heart Rate (72 beats/min)}} \approx 0.8\,\mathrm{s} \] \[ \text{Cardiac Output} = \text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate} \approx 70\,\mathrm{mL} \times 72\,\mathrm{min^{-1}} \approx 5000\,\mathrm{mL/min}\;(5\,\mathrm{L/min}) \]

5.2 Standard Electrocardiogram (ECG)

A standard ECG uses three electrical leads (one on each wrist and one on the left ankle) to record cardiac electrical activity:

  • P-wave: Electrical excitation (depolarisation) of the atria, leading to atrial contraction.
  • QRS complex: Depolarisation of the ventricles, initiating ventricular contraction (systole begins shortly after Q).
  • T-wave: Return of ventricles to normal state (repolarisation); the end of the T-wave marks the end of systole.

5.3 Double Circulation & Blood Vessels

Arteries and veins possess three concentric layers: inner tunica intima (squamous endothelium), middle tunica media (smooth muscle and elastic fibres; thinner in veins), and outer tunica externa (fibrous connective tissue with collagen).

  • Pulmonary Circulation: Right ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs (oxygenation) → Pulmonary veins → Left atrium.
  • Systemic Circulation: Left ventricle → Aorta → Arteries/Capillaries (body tissues) → Veins/Vena cava → Right atrium.
  • Special Pathways: The hepatic portal vein carries nutrient-rich blood from the intestine to the liver before systemic delivery; the coronary system exclusively supplies the cardiac musculature.

6. Regulation of Cardiac Activity & Circulatory Disorders

Because the human heart is myogenic (auto-regulated by nodal tissue), it beats intrinsically. However, a neural centre in the medulla oblongata moderates cardiac function via the autonomic nervous system (ANS):

  • Sympathetic Nerves: Increase heart rate, strength of ventricular contraction, and cardiac output.
  • Parasympathetic Nerves: Decrease heart rate, speed of conduction of action potential, and cardiac output.
  • Adrenal Medullary Hormones: Increase cardiac output.

Major Disorders

  • Hypertension (High Blood Pressure): Normal BP is 120/80 mm Hg (120 systolic, 80 diastolic). Repeated readings ≥ 140/90 mm Hg indicate hypertension, which damages the heart, brain, and kidneys.
  • Coronary Artery Disease (CAD / Atherosclerosis): Narrowing of coronary artery lumen due to deposits of calcium, fat, cholesterol, and fibrous tissue.
  • Angina (Angina Pectoris): Acute chest pain caused when insufficient \(\mathrm{O_2}\) reaches the heart muscle.
  • Heart Failure: Inability of the heart to pump blood effectively to meet body demands (often called congestive heart failure due to lung congestion). It is distinct from cardiac arrest (heart stops beating) and heart attack (sudden heart muscle damage due to inadequate blood supply).

7. Worked Examples

Calculating Cardiac Output and Cycle Duration

Problem (Hypothetical Data): Suppose an athlete during moderate training has a heart rate of \(90\,\mathrm{beats/min}\) and a stroke volume of \(85\,\mathrm{mL}\). Calculate (a) the duration of one cardiac cycle and (b) the cardiac output in litres per minute.

Solution:

(a) Cardiac cycle duration is the time taken for one heartbeat:

\[ T = \frac{60\,\mathrm{s}}{90} = \frac{2}{3}\,\mathrm{s} \approx 0.67\,\mathrm{s} \]

(b) Cardiac output is the product of stroke volume and heart rate:

\[ \text{Cardiac Output} = 85\,\mathrm{mL} \times 90\,\mathrm{min^{-1}} = 7650\,\mathrm{mL/min} = 7.65\,\mathrm{L/min} \]

Evaluating ABO and Rh Transfusion Compatibility

Problem (Assumed Scenario): A trauma patient has blood group B−ve. Which donor blood groups among O+ve, O−ve, B+ve, B−ve, and AB−ve can be safely transfused?

Solution:

  • ABO check: A group B recipient has anti-A antibodies in plasma, so donor RBCs must lack antigen A. Groups O and B lack antigen A; AB has antigen A and is incompatible.
  • Rh check: An Rh−ve recipient lacks the Rh antigen and will form anti-Rh antibodies if given Rh+ve blood. Thus, Rh+ve donors (O+ve, B+ve) must be avoided.
  • Conclusion: Only O−ve and B−ve are safe.

Interpreting ECG Waves and Heart Sounds

Problem (Assumed Clinical Trace): On a patient's ECG, 15 QRS complexes are counted in \(12\,\mathrm{s}\). Determine the heart rate and identify which electrical event immediately precedes the first heart sound ("lub").

Solution:

  • Each QRS complex represents one ventricular depolarisation (one heartbeat).
  • Heart rate \(= \frac{15}{12\,\mathrm{s}} \times 60\,\mathrm{s/min} = 75\,\mathrm{beats/min}\).
  • The QRS complex initiates ventricular systole, which raises ventricular pressure and closes the tricuspid and bicuspid valves—producing the first heart sound ("lub").

Suppose a person has a cardiac output of \(5.6\,\mathrm{L/min}\) and a heart rate of \(80\,\mathrm{beats/min}\). What is the stroke volume of each ventricle?

  • (B) \(70\,\mathrm{mL}\)
  • (C) \(75\,\mathrm{mL}\)

Answer: (B)

Explanation: \(\text{Cardiac Output} = 5.6\,\mathrm{L/min} = 5600\,\mathrm{mL/min}\). Using \(\text{Cardiac Output} = \text{Stroke Volume} \times \text{Heart Rate}\), we get \(\text{Stroke Volume} = \frac{5600}{80} = 70\,\mathrm{mL}\).

9. Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

  • Plasma vs. Serum: Serum lacks clotting factors (Serum = Plasma − Clotting factors); do not confuse it with lymph.
  • Reptile Exception: Reptiles generally have a 3-chambered heart, except crocodiles, which have a 4-chambered heart.
  • Atrial Systole Contribution: Atrial systole increases ventricular filling by ~30% (70% enters passively during joint diastole).
  • Heart Sounds: "Lub" = closure of AV valves (tricuspid + bicuspid) during ventricular systole; "Dub" = closure of semilunar valves during ventricular diastole.
  • Heart Failure vs. Heart Attack vs. Cardiac Arrest: Know the exact distinction for NEET UG MCQs.

10. Concise Final Recap

Blood transports gases, nutrients, and wastes via plasma (55%) and formed elements (45%), while lymph mediates tissue exchange, fat absorption (lacteals), and immunity. The myogenic four-chambered human heart drives complete double circulation (pulmonary and systemic) through a 0.8 s cardiac cycle paced by the SAN (72 beats/min; ~5 L/min cardiac output), modulated by the medulla oblongata via the ANS and monitored clinically by ECG waveforms (P, QRS, T).
