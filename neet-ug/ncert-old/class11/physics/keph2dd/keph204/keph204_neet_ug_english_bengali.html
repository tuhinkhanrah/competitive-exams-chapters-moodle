English

Learning Objectives and Overview

Thermodynamics investigates how thermal energy (heat) converts into mechanical work and how macroscopic properties of bulk matter—such as pressure, volume, and temperature—evolve during energy exchange. Unlike mechanics, which tracks the motion of a body as a whole, thermodynamics focuses on the internal macroscopic state of a system.

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Distinguish between thermodynamic equilibrium, state variables (extensive and intensive), and path functions.
  • Apply the Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics to define temperature and thermal equilibrium.
  • Use the First Law of Thermodynamics (\(\Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W\)) with proper sign conventions across isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric, and cyclic processes.
  • Relate molar specific heat capacities of ideal gases (\(C_p - C_v = R\)) and solids (\(C = 3R\)).
  • Evaluate the efficiency of a Carnot engine using the Second Law of Thermodynamics.

Macroscopic State, Equilibrium, and the Zeroth Law

A thermodynamic system is in thermodynamic equilibrium when its macroscopic variables—pressure (\(P\)), volume (\(V\)), temperature (\(T\)), mass, and chemical composition—remain constant over time. Whether two systems exchange heat depends on the boundary separating them:

  • Adiabatic Wall: An insulating boundary that blocks heat transfer between systems.
  • Diathermic Wall: A conducting boundary that permits heat flow until both systems reach thermal equilibrium.

The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If two systems \(A\) and \(B\) are each separately in thermal equilibrium with a third system \(C\), then \(A\) and \(B\) are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This law establishes temperature (\(T\)) as the unique state variable that takes the same value for all systems in mutual thermal equilibrium (\(T_A = T_B = T_C\)).

State Variables and Equation of State

Equilibrium states are described by state variables, which depend solely on the current state and not on how that state was reached. The relation connecting them is the equation of state; for \(\mu\) moles of an ideal gas, it is:

\[ PV = \mu R T \]

State variables are classified into two categories by imagining the equilibrium system split into two identical halves:

Variable Type Behavior on Halving System Examples
Extensive Variables Values become half (depend on system size/mass) Volume (\(V\)), Internal Energy (\(U\)), Mass (\(M\)), Number of moles (\(\mu\))
Intensive Variables Values remain unchanged (independent of size) Pressure (\(P\)), Temperature (\(T\)), Density (\(\rho\))

Heat, Internal Energy, Work, and the First Law

Internal Energy (\(U\)): The sum of molecular kinetic energies (translational, rotational, and vibrational) and intermolecular potential energies measured in the centre-of-mass rest frame of the system. Macroscopic kinetic energy of the whole system (e.g., a moving gas cylinder) is excluded from \(U\). Crucially, \(U\) is a state variable. For an ideal gas, intermolecular forces are negligible, so \(U\) depends only on absolute temperature \(T\).

Heat (\(\Delta Q\)) and Work (\(\Delta W\)): Neither heat nor work is a state variable; both are modes of energy transfer (energy in transit). Heat flows due to a temperature difference, whereas work is energy transfer via mechanical displacement (such as moving a piston).

\[ \Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W \] \[ \Delta W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\,\mathrm{d}V \quad (\text{for a quasi-static process}) \]

Standard Physics Sign Convention:

  • \(\Delta Q > 0\): Heat is absorbed by the system; \(\Delta Q < 0\): Heat is released by the system.
  • \(\Delta W > 0\): Work is done by the system (expansion); \(\Delta W < 0\): Work is done on the system (compression).
  • \(\Delta U = \Delta Q - \Delta W\) is path-independent, even though \(\Delta Q\) and \(\Delta W\) individually depend on the path taken between two states.

অভ্যন্তরীণ শক্তি (\(U\)): সংস্থার ভরকেন্দ্রের স্থির নির্দেশতন্ত্রে অণুগুলির এলোমেলো গতির গতিশক্তি (রৈখিক, ঘূর্ণন ও কম্পনজনিত) এবং আন্তঃআণবিক স্থিতিশক্তির সমষ্টিকে অভ্যন্তরীণ শক্তি বলে। সমগ্র সংস্থার সামগ্রিক গতিশক্তি \(U\)-এর অন্তর্ভুক্ত নয়। \(U\) একটি অবস্থার চলরাশি। আদর্শ গ্যাসের ক্ষেত্রে আন্তঃআণবিক বল নগণ্য হওয়ায় \(U\) কেবল পরম তাপমাত্রা \(T\)-এর ওপর নির্ভর করে।

তাপ (\(\Delta Q\)) এবং কার্য (\(\Delta W\)): তাপ বা কার্য কোনোটিই অবস্থার চলরাশি নয়; এগুলি হলো শক্তি স্থানান্তরের মাধ্যম। তাপমাত্রার পার্থক্যের কারণে তাপ প্রবাহিত হয়, আর পিস্টন সরানোর মতো যান্ত্রিক প্রক্রিয়ায় কার্য সম্পন্ন হয়।

\[ \Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W \] \[ \Delta W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P\,\mathrm{d}V \quad (\text{প্রায়-স্থিতিশীল প্রক্রিয়ার জন্য}) \]

পদার্থবিজ্ঞানের প্রচলিত চিহ্ন-প্রথা:

  • \(\Delta Q > 0\): সংস্থা তাপ গ্রহণ করলে; \(\Delta Q < 0\): সংস্থা তাপ বর্জন করলে।
  • \(\Delta W > 0\): সংস্থা দ্বারা কার্য হলে (প্রসারণ); \(\Delta W < 0\): সংস্থার ওপর কার্য হলে (সংকোচন)।
  • \(\Delta Q\) এবং \(\Delta W\) পৃথকভাবে পথ-নির্ভর হলেও এদের অন্তরফল \(\Delta U = \Delta Q - \Delta W\) সর্বদা পথ-নিরপেক্ষ।

Specific Heat Capacities of Solids, Liquids, and Gases

The heat capacity \(S = \Delta Q / \Delta T\) depends on the mass of the substance. Dividing by mass \(m\) gives the specific heat capacity \(s\), and dividing by moles \(\mu\) gives the molar specific heat capacity \(C\):

\[ s = \frac{1}{m}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\;(\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}), \qquad C = \frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\;(\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}) \]
  • Solids (Equipartition Prediction): Each atom in a solid vibrates in 3 dimensions with average energy \(3 k_B T\), giving \(U = 3 R T\) per mole. Since \(\Delta V\) is negligible for solids, \(C \approx \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T} = 3R \approx 24.9\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) at room temperature (carbon is a notable exception, and the relation breaks down at low temperatures).
  • Water and the Calorie: \(1\,\mathrm{cal} = 4.186\,\mathrm{J}\) is the heat needed to raise \(1\,\mathrm{g}\) of water from \(14.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) to \(15.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\). In SI units, \(s_{\text{water}} = 4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\).
  • Ideal Gases (Mayer's Relation): Because heat absorbed by a gas depends on the process, we define \(C_v\) (constant volume) and \(C_p\) (constant pressure). For \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of an ideal gas: \[ C_v = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T}, \qquad C_p = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T} + P\left(\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}\right)_p = C_v + R \implies C_p - C_v = R \] The adiabatic index is \(\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}\).

কঠিন, তরল এবং গ্যাসের আপেক্ষিক তাপ

তাপগ্রাহিতা \(S = \Delta Q / \Delta T\) পদার্থের ভরের ওপর নির্ভর করে। একে ভর \(m\) দিয়ে ভাগ করলে আপেক্ষিক তাপ \(s\) এবং মোল সংখ্যা \(\mu\) দিয়ে ভাগ করলে মোলার আপেক্ষিক তাপ \(C\) পাওয়া যায়:

\[ s = \frac{1}{m}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\;(\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}), \qquad C = \frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta T}\;(\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}) \]
  • কঠিন পদার্থ (শক্তির সমবিভাজন নীতি): কঠিন পদার্থের প্রতিটি পরমাণু ত্রিমাত্রিক কম্পনে \(3 k_B T\) গড় শক্তি ধারণ করে, ফলে এক মোলের শক্তি \(U = 3 R T\)। কঠিন পদার্থের \(\Delta V\) নগণ্য হওয়ায় সাধারণ তাপমাত্রায় \(C \approx \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T} = 3R \approx 24.9\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) হয় (কার্বন একটি ব্যতিক্রম এবং অতি নিম্ন তাপমাত্রায় এই সূত্র প্রযোজ্য নয়)।
  • জল এবং ক্যালোরি: \(1\,\mathrm{g}\) জলের তাপমাত্রা \(14.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) থেকে \(15.5\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\)-এ বৃদ্ধি করতে প্রয়োজনীয় তাপ হলো \(1\,\mathrm{cal} = 4.186\,\mathrm{J}\)। SI এককে জলের আপেক্ষিক তাপ \(s_{\text{water}} = 4186\,\mathrm{J\,kg^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)।
  • আদর্শ গ্যাস (মেয়ারের সম্পর্ক): গ্যাসে গৃহীত তাপ প্রক্রিয়ার ওপর নির্ভরশীল বলে \(C_v\) (স্থির আয়তন) এবং \(C_p\) (স্থির চাপ) সংজ্ঞায়িত করা হয়। \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) আদর্শ গ্যাসের জন্য: \[ C_v = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T}, \qquad C_p = \frac{\Delta U}{\Delta T} + P\left(\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta T}\right)_p = C_v + R \implies C_p - C_v = R \] রুদ্ধতাপীয় সূচক হলো \(\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}\)।

Quasi-Static Thermodynamic Processes and \(P\)-\(V\) Curves

A quasi-static process is an idealized, infinitely slow process during which the system remains in thermal and mechanical equilibrium with its surroundings at every instant (pressure and temperature differences are infinitesimal). Rapid processes—such as free expansion into a vacuum or an explosive chemical reaction—pass through non-equilibrium states and cannot be plotted as continuous curves on a \(P\)-\(V\)-\(T\) diagram.

Process Condition Work Done (\(W\)) First Law (\(\Delta Q = \Delta U + W\))
Isothermal \(T = \text{const}\)
(\(PV = \text{const}\))		 \(\mu R T \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right)\) \(\Delta U = 0 \implies \Delta Q = W\)
Adiabatic \(\Delta Q = 0\)
(\(PV^\gamma = \text{const}\))		 \(\frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{\mu R (T_1 - T_2)}{\gamma - 1}\) \(\Delta Q = 0 \implies W = -\Delta U\)

The Second Law, Reversibility, and the Carnot Engine

The First Law conserves energy, but it does not explain why spontaneous processes occur in only one direction (e.g., heat never flows spontaneously from a cold body to a hot body). The Second Law of Thermodynamics imposes fundamental limits:

  • Kelvin-Planck Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the absorption of heat from a reservoir and its complete conversion into work (a heat engine cannot have \(100\%\) efficiency, \(\eta < 1\)).
  • Clausius Statement: No process is possible whose sole result is the transfer of heat from a colder object to a hotter object (a refrigerator cannot have infinite coefficient of performance).

Reversible vs. Irreversible Processes: A process is reversible if both the system and surroundings can be restored to their exact initial states with no other change in the universe. This requires the process to be quasi-static and completely free of dissipative effects (friction, viscosity). All natural spontaneous processes are irreversible.

The Carnot Engine and Carnot's Theorem

Operating between a hot source at \(T_1\) and a cold sink at \(T_2\) (in kelvin), a reversible Carnot engine executes four steps: (1) isothermal expansion at \(T_1\) absorbing \(Q_1\), (2) adiabatic expansion from \(T_1\) to \(T_2\), (3) isothermal compression at \(T_2\) rejecting \(Q_2\), and (4) adiabatic compression from \(T_2\) back to \(T_1\).

\[ \eta = \frac{W}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{Q_2}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}, \qquad \frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \]

Carnot's Theorem: (a) No engine operating between two given temperatures \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) can have an efficiency greater than that of a Carnot engine; (b) the Carnot efficiency depends only on \(T_1\) and \(T_2\), and is independent of the working substance.

কার্নো ইঞ্জিন এবং কার্নোর উপপাদ্য

\(T_1\) তাপমাত্রার উৎস এবং \(T_2\) তাপমাত্রার গ্রাহকের মধ্যে কার্যরত একটি প্রত্যাবর্তী কার্নো ইঞ্জিন চারটি ধাপে চলে: (১) \(T_1\) তাপমাত্রায় সমোষ্ণ প্রসারণ (\(Q_1\) তাপ শোষণ), (২) \(T_1\) থেকে \(T_2\)-এ রুদ্ধতাপীয় প্রসারণ, (৩) \(T_2\) তাপমাত্রায় সমোষ্ণ সংকোচন (\(Q_2\) তাপ বর্জন), এবং (৪) \(T_2\) থেকে \(T_1\)-এ রুদ্ধতাপীয় সংকোচন।

\[ \eta = \frac{W}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{Q_2}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}, \qquad \frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{T_1}{T_2} \]

Example 1: Applying the First Law Across Different Paths

Problem (Hypothetical Assumption): A thermodynamic system moves from initial state \(A\) to final state \(B\) along Path 1, absorbing \(80\,\mathrm{J}\) of heat and performing \(35\,\mathrm{J}\) of work. If the system is taken from \(A\) to \(B\) along Path 2 while \(20\,\mathrm{J}\) of work is done on the system, how much heat is absorbed or released along Path 2?

Solution:

Along Path 1: \(\Delta Q_1 = +80\,\mathrm{J}\) and \(\Delta W_1 = +35\,\mathrm{J}\). By the First Law:

\[ \Delta U = \Delta Q_1 - \Delta W_1 = 80\,\mathrm{J} - 35\,\mathrm{J} = +45\,\mathrm{J} \]

Because internal energy \(U\) is a state function, \(\Delta U = +45\,\mathrm{J}\) for Path 2 as well. Along Path 2, work is done on the system, so \(\Delta W_2 = -20\,\mathrm{J}\). Therefore:

\[ \Delta Q_2 = \Delta U + \Delta W_2 = 45\,\mathrm{J} + (-20\,\mathrm{J}) = +25\,\mathrm{J} \]

Thus, the system absorbs \(25\,\mathrm{J}\) of heat along Path 2.

Example 2: Work and Heat in a Cyclic \(P\)-\(V\) Process

Problem (Hypothetical Assumption): An ideal gas undergoes a clockwise triangular cycle \(A \to B \to C \to A\) on a \(P\)-\(V\) diagram with vertices \(A(2.0\,\mathrm{m^3}, 400\,\mathrm{Pa})\), \(B(5.0\,\mathrm{m^3}, 400\,\mathrm{Pa})\), and \(C(5.0\,\mathrm{m^3}, 100\,\mathrm{Pa})\). Find the net work done by the gas and the net heat absorbed in one complete cycle.

Solution:

The net work done in a cyclic process equals the area enclosed by the loop. Since the cycle is clockwise (with \(P\) on the vertical axis and \(V\) on the horizontal axis), the net work is positive:

\[ W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height} = \frac{1}{2} \times (5.0 - 2.0)\,\mathrm{m^3} \times (400 - 100)\,\mathrm{Pa} = \frac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 300 = +450\,\mathrm{J} \]

For a complete cycle, \(\Delta U_{\text{cycle}} = 0\). By the First Law, \(Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}} = +450\,\mathrm{J}\) (heat is absorbed).

Example 3: Comparing Adiabatic and Isothermal Compression

Problem (Hypothetical Assumption): A monatomic ideal gas (\(\gamma = 5/3\)) initially at pressure \(P_0 = 1.0 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa}\) and volume \(V_0\) is compressed to \(V_0 / 8\). Compare the final pressure if the compression is (a) adiabatic and (b) isothermal.

Solution:

(a) For an adiabatic process, \(P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma\):

\[ P_{\text{adia}} = P_0 \left(\frac{V_0}{V_0 / 8}\right)^{5/3} = P_0 (8)^{5/3} = P_0 (2^3)^{5/3} = 32 P_0 = 3.2 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{Pa} \]

(b) For an isothermal process, \(P_1 V_1 = P_2 V_2\):

\[ P_{\text{iso}} = P_0 \left(\frac{V_0}{V_0 / 8}\right) = 8 P_0 = 8.0 \times 10^5\,\mathrm{Pa} \]

Thus, adiabatic compression yields a final pressure \(4\) times greater than isothermal compression.

Example 4: Calculating Carnot Engine Efficiency and Heat Rejected

Problem (Hypothetical Assumption): A Carnot engine operates between a hot reservoir at \(327\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\) and a cold reservoir at \(27\,^\circ\mathrm{C}\). If it absorbs \(1200\,\mathrm{J}\) of heat from the hot reservoir per cycle, calculate its efficiency and the heat rejected to the cold reservoir.

Convert temperatures to kelvin: \(T_1 = 327 + 273 = 600\,\mathrm{K}\) and \(T_2 = 27 + 273 = 300\,\mathrm{K}\).

\[ \eta = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1} = 1 - \frac{300}{600} = 0.50\;(50\%) \]

Work output per cycle: \(W = \eta Q_1 = 0.50 \times 1200\,\mathrm{J} = 600\,\mathrm{J}\). Heat rejected: \(Q_2 = Q_1 - W = 1200 - 600 = 600\,\mathrm{J}\).

Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist

  • Kelvin vs. Celsius in Carnot Efficiency: Always convert \(T_1\) and \(T_2\) to kelvin (\(\mathrm{K}\)) before substituting into \(\eta = 1 - T_2/T_1\). Using \(^\circ\mathrm{C}\) is a frequent exam mistake.
  • Sign of Work Done: Remember that in physics, \(\Delta W = +P\Delta V\) is positive during expansion (work done by the gas) and negative during compression (work done on the gas).
  • Confusing Heat with Internal Energy: A system possesses internal energy \(U\), never "heat". Heat is strictly energy in transit across a boundary.
  • Free Expansion Trap: Free expansion is adiabatic (\(\Delta Q = 0\)) AND does zero work (\(\Delta W = 0\)), but it is irreversible—do not apply \(PV^\gamma = \text{const}\) to intermediate states!

Concise Final Recap

Thermodynamics rests on three core pillars: the Zeroth Law defines temperature via thermal equilibrium; the First Law (\(\Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W\)) enforces energy conservation, linking path-dependent heat and work to the state function \(U\); and the Second Law sets the direction of natural processes and caps the maximum possible efficiency of any heat engine at the reversible Carnot limit, \(\eta = 1 - T_2/T_1\).

তাপগতিবিদ্যা তিনটি মূল স্তম্ভের ওপর প্রতিষ্ঠিত: শূন্যতম সূত্র তাপীয় সাম্যাবস্থার মাধ্যমে তাপমাত্রাকে সংজ্ঞায়িত করে; প্রথম সূত্র (\(\Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W\)) শক্তির সংরক্ষণ নীতি মেনে পথ-নির্ভর তাপ ও কার্যকে অবস্থার অপেক্ষক \(U\)-এর সাথে যুক্ত করে; এবং দ্বিতীয় সূত্র প্রাকৃতিক প্রক্রিয়ার দিকনির্দেশ নির্ধারণ করে ও যেকোনো তাপ ইঞ্জিনের সর্বোচ্চ কর্মদক্ষতাকে কার্নো ইঞ্জিনের সীমা \(\eta = 1 - T_2/T_1\)-এ আবদ্ধ রাখে।
