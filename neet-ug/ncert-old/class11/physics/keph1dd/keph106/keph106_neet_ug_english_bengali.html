Learning Objectives and Overview

Real objects—such as wheels, tops, beams, and molecules—have finite size and internal structure, so the point-particle model is often insufficient. This tutorial builds the framework to analyse extended systems and rigid bodies for NEET UG.

  • Distinguish between pure translational motion, fixed-axis rotational motion, and combined rolling motion.
  • Locate the centre of mass (CM) of discrete and continuous mass distributions, and apply Newton's second law to CM motion.
  • Use the vector (cross) product to define angular velocity, torque, and angular momentum.
  • Apply conditions of mechanical equilibrium, the principle of moments, and distinguish centre of gravity from centre of mass.
  • Calculate moments of inertia and radii of gyration for standard geometries, and solve rotational kinematics and dynamics problems using \(\tau = I\alpha\) and conservation of angular momentum.

1. Rigid Bodies and Types of Motion

An ideal rigid body has a definite, unchanging shape: the distance between every pair of its constituent particles remains strictly constant regardless of applied forces. Although real solids deform slightly, they are treated as rigid when deformations are negligible.

  • Pure Translational Motion: Every particle of the body has the exact same instantaneous velocity \(\mathbf{v}\) at any given instant. For example, a block sliding down an inclined plane without tipping moves in pure translation.
  • Rotational Motion about a Fixed Axis: When a rigid body is constrained along a straight line (the axis of rotation), every particle moves in a circle lying in a plane perpendicular to the axis, with its centre on the axis. Particles on the axis (\(r_{\perp} = 0\)) remain stationary. Examples include a ceiling fan or a potter's wheel.
  • Rotation with One Fixed Point (Precession): In a spinning top or an oscillating pedestal fan, only one point is fixed while the axis of rotation sweeps out a cone or oscillates.
  • Combined Translation and Rotation: An unpivoted rigid body can translate and rotate simultaneously—for example, a cylinder rolling down an incline. At any instant, different points on a rolling cylinder have different velocities; in rolling without slipping, the point of contact with the surface is momentarily at rest.

2. Centre of Mass (CM) and Its Motion

The centre of mass of a system of particles is the unique point that moves as if the entire mass of the system were concentrated at it and all external forces were applied directly to it.

Discrete System of \(n\) Particles: For particles of masses \(m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n\) at position vectors \(\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2, \dots, \mathbf{r}_n\) with total mass \(M = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i\):

\[ \mathbf{R} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \mathbf{r}_i}{M}, \qquad X = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{M}, \quad Y = \frac{\sum m_i y_i}{M}, \quad Z = \frac{\sum m_i z_i}{M} \]

Continuous Mass Distribution:

\[ \mathbf{R} = \frac{1}{M} \int \mathbf{r} \, \mathrm{d}m \]

Symmetry and Special Cases:

  • For two equal masses, the CM lies midway between them. For two unequal masses \(m_1\) and \(m_2\), the CM divides the line joining them in the inverse ratio of their masses: \(m_1 d_1 = m_2 d_2\).
  • For homogeneous bodies with reflection symmetry—such as a uniform thin rod, ring, disc, sphere, or cube—the CM coincides with the geometric centre. Note that the CM does not necessarily lie inside the material (for instance, a ring's CM is at its empty centre).
  • If the origin is placed at the CM, then \(\sum m_i \mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{0}\).

Dynamics and Conservation of Linear Momentum

Differentiating \(M\mathbf{R} = \sum m_i \mathbf{r}_i\) with respect to time gives the velocity \(\mathbf{V}\) and acceleration \(\mathbf{A}\) of the CM:

\[ \mathbf{P} = M\mathbf{V} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \mathbf{v}_i, \qquad M\mathbf{A} = \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{P}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}} \]

Internal forces between particles occur in equal and opposite action-reaction pairs (Newton's third law) and cancel out completely. Therefore, internal forces cannot alter the motion of the centre of mass. When \(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathbf{0}\), total linear momentum \(\mathbf{P}\) is conserved and the CM moves with constant velocity (or remains at rest). For example, if a projectile explodes mid-air, its fragments fly apart, but their CM continues along the exact same parabolic trajectory as before the explosion.

২. ভরকেন্দ্র (Centre of Mass) এবং এর গতি

কোনো কণাসমষ্টির ভরকেন্দ্র (CM) হলো এমন একটি বিন্দু, যা এমনভাবে গতিশীল হয় যেন সমগ্র সংস্থাটির মোট ভর ওই বিন্দুতে কেন্দ্রীভূত আছে এবং সমস্ত বাহ্যিক বল ওই বিন্দুতেই প্রযুক্ত হচ্ছে।

\(n\) সংখ্যক বিচ্ছিন্ন কণার ক্ষেত্রে: \(m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n\) ভরের কণাগুলোর অবস্থান ভেক্টর \(\mathbf{r}_1, \mathbf{r}_2, \dots, \mathbf{r}_n\) এবং মোট ভর \(M = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i\) হলে:

\[ \mathbf{R} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \mathbf{r}_i}{M}, \qquad X = \frac{\sum m_i x_i}{M}, \quad Y = \frac{\sum m_i y_i}{M}, \quad Z = \frac{\sum m_i z_i}{M} \]

অবিচ্ছিন্ন ভরবণ্টনের ক্ষেত্রে:

\[ \mathbf{R} = \frac{1}{M} \int \mathbf{r} \, \mathrm{d}m \]

প্রতিসাম্য ও বিশেষ ক্ষেত্র:

  • দুটি সমান ভরের কণার ভরকেন্দ্র তাদের সংযোগকারী রেখার মধ্যবিন্দুতে থাকে। দুটি অসমান ভর \(m_1\) ও \(m_2\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে ভরকেন্দ্র তাদের দূরত্বকে ভরের ব্যস্তানুপাতে বিভক্ত করে: \(m_1 d_1 = m_2 d_2\)।
  • প্রতিসম ও সুষম বস্তুর (যেমন সুষম দণ্ড, বৃত্তাকার রিং, চাকতি, গোলক বা ঘনক) ভরকেন্দ্র তাদের জ্যামিতিক কেন্দ্রে অবস্থিত। ভরকেন্দ্র সর্বদা বস্তুর উপাদানের ভেতরেই থাকবে এমন কোনো বাধ্যবাধকতা নেই (যেমন রিংয়ের ভরকেন্দ্র তার ফাঁকা কেন্দ্রে থাকে)।
  • ভরকেন্দ্রকে মূলবিন্দু হিসেবে ধরলে \(\sum m_i \mathbf{r}_i = \mathbf{0}\) হয়।

ভরকেন্দ্রের গতিবিজ্ঞান ও রৈখিক ভরবেগের সংরক্ষণ

\(M\mathbf{R} = \sum m_i \mathbf{r}_i\) সমীকরণকে সময়ের সাপেক্ষে অবকলন করে ভরকেন্দ্রের বেগ \(\mathbf{V}\) এবং ত্বরণ \(\mathbf{A}\) পাওয়া যায়:

\[ \mathbf{P} = M\mathbf{V} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i \mathbf{v}_i, \qquad M\mathbf{A} = \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{P}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}} \]

কণাগুলোর মধ্যকার অভ্যন্তরীণ বল নিউটনের তৃতীয় সূত্র অনুযায়ী সমান ও বিপরীতমুখী জোড়ায় ক্রিয়া করে বলে পরস্পরকে প্রশমিত করে। তাই অভ্যন্তরীণ বল ভরকেন্দ্রের গতির কোনো পরিবর্তন ঘটাতে পারে না। যদি \(\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathbf{0}\) হয়, তবে মোট রৈখিক ভরবেগ \(\mathbf{P}\) সংরক্ষিত থাকে এবং ভরকেন্দ্র সমবেগে চলে (বা স্থির থাকে)। যেমন, আকাশে কোনো প্রাসের বিস্ফোরণ ঘটলে টুকরোগুলো বিভিন্ন দিকে ছিটকে গেলেও তাদের ভরকেন্দ্র পূর্বের অধিবৃত্তাকার (parabolic) পথেই চলতে থাকে।

3. Vector Product and Rotational Kinematics

Two core rotational quantities—torque and angular momentum—are defined using the vector (cross) product. For two vectors \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) separated by angle \(\theta\) (\(0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\)):

\[ |\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}| = ab \sin\theta, \qquad \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a} \] \[ \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{i}} & \hat{\mathbf{j}} & \hat{\mathbf{k}} \\ a_x & a_y & a_z \\ b_x & b_y & b_z \end{vmatrix} \]

The direction of \(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\) is perpendicular to the plane of \(\mathbf{a}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\), given by the right-hand screw rule (curling right-hand fingers from \(\mathbf{a}\) to \(\mathbf{b}\) through the smaller angle points the thumb along \(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\)). Cyclic unit-vector products satisfy \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \times \hat{\mathbf{j}} = \hat{\mathbf{k}}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{j}} \times \hat{\mathbf{k}} = \hat{\mathbf{i}}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{k}} \times \hat{\mathbf{i}} = \hat{\mathbf{j}}\), and \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \times \hat{\mathbf{i}} = \mathbf{0}\).

Angular Velocity and Uniform Angular Acceleration

In fixed-axis rotation, every particle has the same instantaneous angular velocity \(\omega = \mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}t\). As a vector, \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) lies along the axis of rotation (given by the right-hand rule). If \(\mathbf{r}\) is the position vector of a particle from an origin on the axis and \(r_{\perp}\) is its perpendicular distance from the axis, its linear velocity is:

\[ \mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}, \qquad v = \omega r_{\perp} \]

Angular acceleration is \(\alpha = \mathrm{d}\omega/\mathrm{d}t\). For uniform angular acceleration \(\alpha\) about a fixed axis, the rotational kinematic equations mirror linear kinematics:

\[ \omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t, \qquad \theta = \theta_0 + \omega_0 t + \frac{1}{2}\alpha t^2, \qquad \omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha(\theta - \theta_0) \]

৩. ভেক্টর গুণফল এবং ঘূর্ণন গতির কাইনেমেটিক্স

ঘূর্ণন গতির দুটি প্রধান রাশি—টর্ক এবং কৌণিক ভরবেগ—ভেক্টর গুণফলের (cross product) মাধ্যমে সংজ্ঞায়িত হয়। দুটি ভেক্টর \(\mathbf{a}\) ও \(\mathbf{b}\)-এর মধ্যবর্তী কোণ \(\theta\) (\(0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\)) হলে:

\[ |\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}| = ab \sin\theta, \qquad \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = -\mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{a} \] \[ \mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b} = \begin{vmatrix} \hat{\mathbf{i}} & \hat{\mathbf{j}} & \hat{\mathbf{k}} \\ a_x & a_y & a_z \\ b_x & b_y & b_z \end{vmatrix} \]

\(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\)-এর দিক \(\mathbf{a}\) ও \(\mathbf{b}\)-এর তলের সাথে লম্ব এবং ডান-হাতি স্ক্রু নিয়ম (ডান হাতের আঙুলগুলোকে ক্ষুদ্রতর কোণ বরাবর \(\mathbf{a}\) থেকে \(\mathbf{b}\)-এর দিকে ঘোরালে বৃদ্ধাঙ্গুলি \(\mathbf{a} \times \mathbf{b}\)-এর দিক নির্দেশ করে) দ্বারা নির্ধারিত হয়। একক ভেক্টরের ক্ষেত্রে \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \times \hat{\mathbf{j}} = \hat{\mathbf{k}}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{j}} \times \hat{\mathbf{k}} = \hat{\mathbf{i}}\), \(\hat{\mathbf{k}} \times \hat{\mathbf{i}} = \hat{\mathbf{j}}\) এবং \(\hat{\mathbf{i}} \times \hat{\mathbf{i}} = \mathbf{0}\)।

কৌণিক বেগ ও সুষম কৌণিক ত্বরণ

স্থির অক্ষের সাপেক্ষে ঘূর্ণনে বস্তুর প্রতিটি কণার তাৎক্ষণিক কৌণিক বেগ \(\omega = \mathrm{d}\theta/\mathrm{d}t\) সমান হয়। ভেক্টর \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) ঘূর্ণন অক্ষ বরাবর ক্রিয়া করে। অক্ষের ওপর কোনো মূলবিন্দু থেকে কণার অবস্থান ভেক্টর \(\mathbf{r}\) এবং অক্ষ থেকে লম্ব দূরত্ব \(r_{\perp}\) হলে, কণাটির রৈখিক বেগ:

\[ \mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{r}, \qquad v = \omega r_{\perp} \]

4. Torque, Angular Momentum, and Equilibrium

Torque (Moment of Force): The rotational analogue of force is torque \(\boldsymbol{\tau}\). For a force \(\mathbf{F}\) acting at position \(\mathbf{r}\) relative to origin O:

\[ \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}, \qquad \tau = rF\sin\theta = r_{\perp}F = rF_{\perp} \]

Its SI unit is \(\mathrm{N\,m}\) and dimensions are \([\mathrm{ML}^2\mathrm{T}^{-2}]\). Although dimensionally identical to work, torque is a vector while work is a scalar. Torque vanishes if \(F = 0\), \(r = 0\), or the line of action of \(\mathbf{F}\) passes through the origin (\(\theta = 0^\circ\) or \(180^\circ\)).

Angular Momentum: The rotational analogue of linear momentum \(\mathbf{p} = m\mathbf{v}\) is angular momentum \(\mathbf{l}\) (unit: \(\mathrm{J\,s}\) or \(\mathrm{kg\,m^2\,s^{-1}}\), dimensions: \([\mathrm{ML}^2\mathrm{T}^{-1}]\)). For a single particle and for a system of particles:

\[ \mathbf{l} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p}, \qquad \mathbf{L} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{p}_i, \qquad \frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{L}}{\mathrm{d}t} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext}} \]

Here, internal torques cancel out provided internal forces act along the line joining each pair of particles. Consequently, when \(\boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{ext}} = \mathbf{0}\), the total angular momentum \(\mathbf{L}\) is conserved (\(\mathbf{L} = \text{constant}\)).

Mechanical Equilibrium, Couple, and Centre of Gravity

  • Conditions for Mechanical Equilibrium: A rigid body is in complete mechanical equilibrium when both net force and net torque vanish: \[ \sum \mathbf{F}_i = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{translational equilibrium}), \qquad \sum \boldsymbol{\tau}_i = \mathbf{0} \quad (\text{rotational equilibrium}) \] If translational equilibrium holds, the condition \(\sum \boldsymbol{\tau}_i = \mathbf{0}\) is independent of the choice of origin.
  • Couple: Two equal, opposite, parallel forces with different lines of action form a couple. For a couple, \(\sum \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\) (translational equilibrium), yet net torque is non-zero and independent of the reference point, producing pure rotation without translation.
  • Principle of Moments (Ideal Lever): In equilibrium under load \(F_1\) (load arm \(d_1\)) and effort \(F_2\) (effort arm \(d_2\)), \(F_1 d_1 = F_2 d_2\), giving Mechanical Advantage \(\mathrm{M.A.} = F_1 / F_2 = d_2 / d_1\).
  • Centre of Gravity (CG) vs. Centre of Mass: The CG is the point where total gravitational torque vanishes (\(\sum \mathbf{r}_i \times m_i\mathbf{g} = \mathbf{0}\)). In a uniform gravitational field, CG coincides with CM; for very large bodies where \(\mathbf{g}\) varies across the body, CG and CM differ.

5. Moment of Inertia and Rotational Dynamics

The rotational analogue of mass is the moment of inertia \(I\) about a chosen axis. Unlike mass, \(I\) is not an intrinsic constant of a body alone; it depends on total mass, geometry, mass distribution, and the position and orientation of the axis of rotation.

\[ I = \sum_{i=1}^{n} m_i r_i^2 = M k^2, \qquad K_{\mathrm{rot}} = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 \]

Here \(r_i\) is the perpendicular distance of the \(i\)-th particle from the axis, and \(k = \sqrt{I/M}\) is the radius of gyration (the effective distance from the axis where the entire mass could be concentrated to yield the same \(I\)). SI unit: \(\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\); dimensions: \([\mathrm{ML}^2]\).

Work, Power, and Newton's Second Law for Fixed-Axis Rotation

When a rigid body rotates about a fixed axis (say the \(z\)-axis), only the torque component \(\tau\) along the axis does work and causes angular acceleration:

\[ \mathrm{d}W = \tau \, \mathrm{d}\theta, \qquad P = \frac{\mathrm{d}W}{\mathrm{d}t} = \tau\omega, \qquad \tau = I\alpha \]

For a symmetric body rotating about its symmetry axis, \(\mathbf{L}_{\perp} = \mathbf{0}\) and total angular momentum lies along the axis: \(L_z = I\omega\). If external torque is zero (\(\tau_{\mathrm{ext}} = 0\)) while the mass distribution changes, angular momentum is conserved:

\[ I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2 \quad \text{(when } \tau_{\mathrm{ext}} = 0\text{)} \]

For example, a diver or skater pulling their arms inward reduces \(I\) and consequently spins faster (\(\omega\) increases). Note that kinetic energy \(K = L^2 / (2I)\) increases in this process because internal muscular work is done.

Example 3: Torque, Angular Acceleration, and Rotational Kinetic Energy

Problem (Assumed Values): A solid cylindrical drum of mass \(10\,\mathrm{kg}\) and radius \(0.4\,\mathrm{m}\) is mounted on a frictionless horizontal axle. A light cord wound around its rim is pulled with a constant tangential force of \(15\,\mathrm{N}\) starting from rest. Find (a) the angular acceleration of the drum, and (b) its rotational kinetic energy after \(4.0\,\mathrm{s}\).

Solution:

(a) Moment of inertia of the solid cylinder about its axis:

\[ I = \frac{1}{2}MR^2 = \frac{1}{2}(10)(0.4)^2 = 0.8\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2} \]

Applied torque \(\tau = FR = 15 \times 0.4 = 6.0\,\mathrm{N\,m}\). Therefore:

\[ \alpha = \frac{\tau}{I} = \frac{6.0}{0.8} = 7.5\,\mathrm{rad/s^2} \]

(b) Angular velocity after \(t = 4.0\,\mathrm{s}\) (with \(\omega_0 = 0\)):

\[ \omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t = 0 + (7.5)(4.0) = 30\,\mathrm{rad/s} \]

Rotational kinetic energy:

\[ K = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2 = \frac{1}{2}(0.8)(30)^2 = 360\,\mathrm{J} \]

Example 4: Conservation of Angular Momentum in a Rotating Turntable

Problem (Assumed Values): A horizontal circular turntable of moment of inertia \(4.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\) rotates freely at \(10\,\mathrm{rad/s}\) about a vertical frictionless axis. A small lump of clay of mass \(2.0\,\mathrm{kg}\) is dropped gently onto the turntable at a distance of \(0.5\,\mathrm{m}\) from the axis and sticks to it. Find the final angular speed and the loss in kinetic energy.

Solution: Since no external torque acts about the vertical axis, total angular momentum is conserved:

\[ I_i = 4.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}, \qquad I_f = I_i + mr^2 = 4.5 + 2.0(0.5)^2 = 5.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2} \] \[ I_i \omega_i = I_f \omega_f \implies 4.5 \times 10 = 5.0\,\omega_f \implies \omega_f = 9.0\,\mathrm{rad/s} \]

Initial and final kinetic energies:

\[ K_i = \frac{1}{2}(4.5)(10)^2 = 225\,\mathrm{J}, \qquad K_f = \frac{1}{2}(5.0)(9.0)^2 = 202.5\,\mathrm{J} \]

Loss in kinetic energy \(= K_i - K_f = 225 - 202.5 = 22.5\,\mathrm{J}\) (dissipated during the inelastic sticking impact).

Assertion (A): When a projectile explodes mid-air into several fragments due to internal chemical energy, the centre of mass of the fragments continues to follow the original parabolic trajectory until the fragments hit the ground.

Reason (R): Internal forces occur in equal and opposite pairs and cancel out, so the acceleration of the centre of mass depends only on the external gravitational force.

  • (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  • (C) A is true, but R is false.
  • (D) Both A and R are false.

Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Worked Solution: By \(M\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\), internal explosion forces cancel in action–reaction pairs and do not affect the motion of the centre of mass. Gravity remains the sole external force before and after the explosion, so the CM continues along the original parabola.

Practice Question 6 (Single-Correct MCQ)

An ice skater spinning with arms outstretched has a moment of inertia \(I_1 = 3.0\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\) and angular speed \(\omega_1 = 4.0\,\mathrm{rad/s}\). When she pulls her arms close to her body, her moment of inertia decreases to \(I_2 = 1.5\,\mathrm{kg\,m^2}\). Neglecting friction, what is the ratio of her final rotational kinetic energy to her initial rotational kinetic energy (\(K_2 : K_1\))?

  • (A) \(1 : 2\)
  • (B) \(1 : 1\)
  • (C) \(2 : 1\)
  • (D) \(4 : 1\)

Answer: (C) \(2 : 1\)

Worked Solution: With zero external torque, angular momentum \(L = I\omega\) is conserved. Since rotational kinetic energy is \(K = \frac{L^2}{2I}\), for constant \(L\) we have \(K \propto \frac{1}{I}\). Thus:

\[ \frac{K_2}{K_1} = \frac{I_1}{I_2} = \frac{3.0}{1.5} = \frac{2}{1} \]

Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist

  • Torque vs. Work: Both share dimensions \([\mathrm{ML}^2\mathrm{T}^{-2}]\), but torque is a vector (\(\mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{F}\), unit \(\mathrm{N\,m}\)) whereas work is a scalar (\(\mathbf{F} \cdot \mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\), unit \(\mathrm{J}\)). Never express torque in joules.
  • \(\mathbf{L}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) Parallelism: \(\mathbf{L} = I\boldsymbol{\omega}\) holds only when rotation is about a symmetry axis of a rigid body. In general, \(\mathbf{L}\) and \(\boldsymbol{\omega}\) need not be parallel.
  • Kinetic Energy in Angular Momentum Conservation: When \(I\) decreases at constant \(L\), \(\omega\) increases and kinetic energy \(K = L^2/(2I)\) increases—do not mistakenly assume \(K\) stays constant!
  • RPM to rad/s Conversion: Always convert angular speed from \(\mathrm{rpm}\) to \(\mathrm{rad/s}\) using \(\omega = \frac{2\pi \times \mathrm{rpm}}{60}\) before substituting into rotational formulas.

Concise Final Recap

The translational motion of any system of particles is governed entirely by its centre of mass via \(M\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\), while its rotational motion about a fixed axis is governed by \(\tau = I\alpha\), \(K = \frac{1}{2}I\omega^2\), and \(L_z = I\omega\). Mechanical equilibrium requires both \(\sum \mathbf{F} = \mathbf{0}\) and \(\sum \boldsymbol{\tau} = \mathbf{0}\). Mastering the direct correspondence between linear variables (\(x, v, a, m, F, p\)) and rotational variables (\(\theta, \omega, \alpha, I, \tau, L\)) streamlines nearly every NEET UG problem in this chapter.
