Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

English

Hydrogen is the simplest yet most versatile element in chemistry, consisting of a single proton and a single electron in its neutral atom. For NEET UG, questions from this topic frequently test periodic anomalies, isotope effects, industrial gas-shift equilibria, hydride classification, water-softening chemistry, and redox/stoichiometric calculations involving \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\).

Core Learning Targets

  • Justify the unique placement of hydrogen in the periodic table by comparing its properties with alkali metals and halogens.
  • Distinguish among protium, deuterium, and tritium in nuclear composition, physical constants, and reaction kinetics.
  • Master laboratory and commercial syntheses of dihydrogen, including syngas formation and the water-gas shift reaction.
  • Classify binary hydrides into ionic, covalent (electron-deficient, electron-precise, electron-rich), and metallic categories.
  • Relate the bent structure and hydrogen bonding of water and ice to their physical anomalies, amphoteric behaviour, and hydrate types.
  • Compare methods for removing temporary and permanent hardness of water, and understand the nuclear role of heavy water (\(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\)).
  • Analyse the non-planar structure, volume-strength stoichiometry, and dual redox behaviour of hydrogen peroxide (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\)).

1. Atomic Identity, Periodic Placement, and Isotopes

English

Why Hydrogen Occupies a Separate Position

With a ground-state electronic configuration of \(1s^1\), hydrogen shares traits with two contrasting groups yet fits neatly into neither:

  • Resemblance to Alkali Metals (Group 1): Like \(ns^1\) alkali metals, hydrogen can lose one electron to form a unipositive ion (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) and forms oxides (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), halides (\(\mathrm{HX}\)), and sulphides (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S}\)). However, hydrogen's ionization enthalpy (\(\Delta_i H = 1312\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) is far higher than that of lithium (\(520\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)), and it is a non-metal under ordinary conditions.
  • Resemblance to Halogens (Group 17): Like \(ns^2 np^5\) halogens, hydrogen is one electron short of the nearest noble gas configuration (helium, \(1s^2\)). It gains an electron to form the hydride ion (\(\mathrm{H}^-\)), forms a diatomic molecule (\(\mathrm{H}_2\)), and forms numerous covalent compounds. Still, its reactivity at room temperature is much lower than that of halogens.
  • Uniqueness of the Bare Proton (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)): Removing the sole electron from hydrogen leaves a bare nucleus of radius approximately \(1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{pm}\)—about five orders of magnitude smaller than typical atomic or ionic radii (\(50\text{–}200\,\mathrm{pm}\)). Because of this extreme charge density, free \(\mathrm{H}^+\) never exists independently in condensed phases; it always coordinates with other species (e.g., forming \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+\) in water).

Occurrence and Isotopes of Hydrogen

Hydrogen is the most abundant element in the universe (about \(70\%\) of total cosmic mass) and dominates stellar and gas-giant atmospheres. On Earth, free \(\mathrm{H}_2\) is scarce in the atmosphere (\(0.15\%\) by mass) because its low molecular mass allows it to escape Earth's gravity, but combined hydrogen accounts for \(15.4\%\) of the Earth's crust and oceans.

Hydrogen has three naturally occurring isotopes, all sharing the same atomic number (\(Z = 1\)) and electronic configuration (\(1s^1\)), but differing in neutron count:

हाइड्रोजन को आवर्त सारणी में अलग स्थान क्यों दिया जाता है?

मूल अवस्था में \(1s^1\) इलेक्ट्रॉनिक विन्यास के कारण हाइड्रोजन दो विपरीत वर्गों से समानता रखता है, फिर भी किसी एक वर्ग में पूर्णतः उपयुक्त नहीं बैठता:

  • क्षार धातुओं (वर्ग 1) से समानता: \(ns^1\) क्षार धातुओं की तरह हाइड्रोजन एक इलेक्ट्रॉन त्यागकर एकधनात्मक आयन (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) बना सकता है तथा ऑक्साइड (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), हैलाइड (\(\mathrm{HX}\)) और सल्फाइड (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S}\)) बनाता है। किंतु हाइड्रोजन की आयनन एन्थैल्पी (\(\Delta_i H = 1312\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) लीथियम (\(520\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) से बहुत अधिक है और सामान्य परिस्थितियों में यह अधातु है।
  • हैलोजनों (वर्ग 17) से समानता: \(ns^2 np^5\) हैलोजनों की तरह हाइड्रोजन को निकटतम उत्कृष्ट गैस विन्यास (हीलियम, \(1s^2\)) प्राप्त करने के लिए एक इलेक्ट्रॉन की कमी होती है। यह एक इलेक्ट्रॉन ग्रहण कर हाइड्राइड आयन (\(\mathrm{H}^-\)) बनाता है, द्विपरमाणुक अणु (\(\mathrm{H}_2\)) के रूप में रहता है और अनेक सहसंयोजक यौगिक बनाता है। फिर भी, कमरे के ताप पर इसकी क्रियाशीलता हैलोजनों की तुलना में बहुत कम होती है।
  • मुक्त प्रोटॉन (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) की विशिष्टता: हाइड्रोजन से एकमात्र इलेक्ट्रॉन निकलने पर लगभग \(1.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{pm}\) त्रिज्या वाला नाभिक बचता है, जो सामान्य परमाण्विक/आयनिक त्रिज्याओं (\(50\text{–}200\,\mathrm{pm}\)) की तुलना में अत्यंत सूक्ष्म है। अत्यधिक आवेश घनत्व के कारण मुक्त \(\mathrm{H}^+\) स्वतंत्र रूप से नहीं रह सकता और सदैव अन्य अणुओं से जुड़ा रहता है (जैसे जल में \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+\))।

2. Dihydrogen (\(\mathrm{H}_2\)): Preparation, Properties, and Uses

English

Laboratory and Commercial Production

  • Laboratory Methods:
    (a) Action of dilute mineral acid on granulated zinc: \(\mathrm{Zn(s)} + 2\mathrm{H^+(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zn^{2+}(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)}\)
    (b) Action of hot aqueous alkali on amphoteric zinc: \(\mathrm{Zn(s)} + 2\mathrm{NaOH(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Na_2ZnO_2(aq)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)}\) (sodium zincate)
  • Electrolysis Methods:
    (a) Electrolysis of acidified or alkaline water using platinum electrodes: \(2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2(g)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)}\)
    (b) Ultra-pure \(\mathrm{H}_2\) (\(>99.95\%\)) is produced by electrolysing warm aqueous \(\mathrm{Ba(OH)_2}\) between nickel electrodes.
    (c) As a chlor-alkali byproduct during brine (\(\mathrm{NaCl(aq)}\)) electrolysis: \(\mathrm{Cl}_2\) forms at the anode while \(\mathrm{H}_2\) and \(\mathrm{OH}^-\) form at the cathode (\(2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} + 2e^- \rightarrow \mathrm{H_2(g)} + 2\mathrm{OH^-(aq)}\)).
  • Hydrocarbon Reforming & Coal Gasification:
    Passing steam over hydrocarbons (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH}_4\)) or red-hot coke at \(1270\,\mathrm{K}\) (with \(\mathrm{Ni}\) catalyst for hydrocarbons) yields water gas or synthesis gas (syngas), a mixture of \(\mathrm{CO}\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2\): \[ \mathrm{CH_4(g)} + \mathrm{H_2O(g)} \xrightarrow[\mathrm{Ni}]{1270\,\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{CO(g)} + 3\mathrm{H_2(g)} \] \[ \mathrm{C(s)} + \mathrm{H_2O(g)} \xrightarrow{1270\,\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{CO(g)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)} \quad (\text{Coal gasification}) \]
  • Water-Gas Shift Reaction: To boost \(\mathrm{H}_2\) yield from syngas, \(\mathrm{CO}\) is reacted with steam at \(673\,\mathrm{K}\) over an iron chromate catalyst, and \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) is scrubbed out using sodium arsenite solution: \[ \mathrm{CO(g)} + \mathrm{H_2O(g)} \xrightarrow[\text{FeCrO}_4]{673\,\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{CO_2(g)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)} \]

Chemical Reactivity and Industrial Applications

Dihydrogen is a colourless, odourless, tasteless, combustible gas that is lighter than air and insoluble in water. Because the \(\mathrm{H–H}\) bond dissociation enthalpy (\(435.88\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) is the highest for any single bond between two atoms, \(\mathrm{H}_2\) is relatively inert at room temperature (only \(\sim 0.081\%\) dissociated at \(2000\,\mathrm{K}\), rising to \(95.5\%\) at \(5000\,\mathrm{K}\)). Atomic hydrogen is generated in an electric arc or under UV light and recombines on metal surfaces in an atomic hydrogen torch to reach \(\sim 4000\,\mathrm{K}\) for welding high-melting metals.

  • With Halogens: \(\mathrm{H_2(g)} + \mathrm{X_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{HX(g)}\). Reactivity decreases down the group: reacts explosively with \(\mathrm{F}_2\) even in the dark, but requires a catalyst with \(\mathrm{I}_2\).
  • With Dioxygen & Dinitrogen: Burns exothermically with \(\mathrm{O}_2\) (\(\Delta H^\circ = -285.9\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) to form water; combines with \(\mathrm{N}_2\) at \(673\,\mathrm{K}\), \(200\,\mathrm{atm}\), \(\mathrm{Fe}\) catalyst (Haber process, \(\Delta H^\circ = -92.6\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) to form \(\mathrm{NH}_3\).
  • Reducing Action: Reduces aqueous \(\mathrm{Pd}^{2+}\) to \(\mathrm{Pd(s)}\) and oxides of metals less reactive than iron (such as \(\mathrm{CuO}\)) to the free metal.
  • Organic Synthesis: Hydrogenates unsaturated vegetable oils over \(\mathrm{Ni}\) catalyst into edible fats (margarine/vanaspati), converts syngas into methanol over a cobalt catalyst (\(\mathrm{CO} + 2\mathrm{H}_2 \rightarrow \mathrm{CH_3OH}\)), and participates in hydroformylation of alkenes to aldehydes and alcohols.

  • वाटर-गैस शिफ्ट अभिक्रिया: सिनगैस से \(\mathrm{H}_2\) की मात्रा बढ़ाने के लिए \(673\,\mathrm{K}\) पर आयरन क्रोमेट उत्प्रेरक की उपस्थिति में \(\mathrm{CO}\) की भाप से अभिक्रिया कराई जाती है तथा \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) को सोडियम आर्सेनाइट विलयन द्वारा अवशोषित कर हटाया जाता है: \[ \mathrm{CO(g)} + \mathrm{H_2O(g)} \xrightarrow[\text{FeCrO}_4]{673\,\mathrm{K}} \mathrm{CO_2(g)} + \mathrm{H_2(g)} \]

3. Classification and Chemistry of Hydrides

English

Hydrogen combines with nearly all elements (except noble gases) to form binary hydrides (\(\mathrm{EH}_x\) or \(\mathrm{E}_m\mathrm{H}_n\)), divided into three principal classes based on bonding and stoichiometry:

Classification of Binary Hydrides
  • Ionic (Saline) Hydrides: Stoichiometric compounds formed by strongly electropositive \(s\)-block metals. They are crystalline, non-volatile solids that do not conduct electricity in the solid state, but their melts conduct and liberate \(\mathrm{H}_2\) at the anode (\(2\mathrm{H^-(\text{melt})} \rightarrow \mathrm{H_2(g)} + 2e^-\)), proving the presence of \(\mathrm{H}^-\). They react violently with water (\(\mathrm{NaH} + \mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \mathrm{NaOH} + \mathrm{H_2}\)), and \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) cannot extinguish such fires because hot \(\mathrm{NaH}\) reduces \(\mathrm{CO}_2\). Lighter hydrides (\(\mathrm{LiH}\), \(\mathrm{BeH}_2\), \(\mathrm{MgH}_2\)) show significant covalent character (\(\mathrm{BeH}_2\) and \(\mathrm{MgH}_2\) are polymeric). Unreactive \(\mathrm{LiH}\) is used to synthesize complex hydrides: \[ 8\mathrm{LiH} + \mathrm{Al_2Cl_6} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{LiAlH_4} + 6\mathrm{LiCl} \qquad;\qquad 2\mathrm{LiH} + \mathrm{B_2H_6} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{LiBH_4} \]
  • Covalent (Molecular) Hydrides: Volatile compounds of \(p\)-block elements classified by valence electron count:
    (i) Electron-deficient (Group 13): Fewer electrons than needed for a conventional Lewis octet (e.g., \(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\)); act as Lewis acids.
    (ii) Electron-precise (Group 14): Exact number of electrons for tetrahedral covalent bonds (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH}_4\)); neither Lewis acids nor bases.
    (iii) Electron-rich (Groups 15–17): Possess lone pairs (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\): 1, \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\): 2, \(\mathrm{HF}\): 3); act as Lewis bases. Strong intermolecular hydrogen bonding in \(\mathrm{NH}_3\), \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\), and \(\mathrm{HF}\) elevates their boiling points above those of heavier group members.
  • Metallic (Non-stoichiometric / Interstitial) Hydrides: Formed by \(d\)- and \(f\)-block metals (e.g., \(\mathrm{LaH}_{2.87}\), \(\mathrm{TiH}_{1.5\text{–}1.8}\), \(\mathrm{PdH}_{0.6\text{–}0.8}\)). Metals of Groups 7, 8, and 9 do not form hydrides (the hydride gap), and in Group 6 only \(\mathrm{CrH}\) is known. Except for \(\mathrm{Ni, Pd, Ce, Ac}\), hydride formation alters the parent metal lattice. Metals like \(\mathrm{Pd}\) and \(\mathrm{Pt}\) absorb large volumes of \(\mathrm{H}_2\), enabling hydrogen storage and catalytic hydrogenation.

उत्कृष्ट गैसों को छोड़कर लगभग सभी तत्वों के साथ हाइड्रोजन द्विअंगी हाइड्राइड (\(\mathrm{EH}_x\) या \(\mathrm{E}_m\mathrm{H}_n\)) बनाता है, जिन्हें आबंधन और रससमीकरणमिति के आधार पर तीन प्रमुख वर्गों में बाँटा जाता है:

द्विअंगी हाइड्राइडों का वर्गीकरण
  • आयनिक (लवण-सदृश) हाइड्राइड: प्रबल विद्युतधनी \(s\)-ब्लॉक धातुओं के रससमीकरणमितीय यौगिक। ये क्रिस्टलीय, अवाष्पशील ठोस हैं जो ठोस अवस्था में कुचालक होते हैं, किंतु गलित अवस्था में विद्युत चालन करते हैं और एनोड पर \(\mathrm{H}_2\) मुक्त करते हैं (\(2\mathrm{H^-(\text{गलित})} \rightarrow \mathrm{H_2(g)} + 2e^-\))। ये जल से तीव्र अभिक्रिया कर \(\mathrm{H}_2\) देते हैं; ऐसी आग को \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) से नहीं बुझाया जा सकता। हल्के हाइड्राइडों (\(\mathrm{LiH}\), \(\mathrm{BeH}_2\), \(\mathrm{MgH}_2\)) में सहसंयोजक लक्षण पाया जाता है (\(\mathrm{BeH}_2\) व \(\mathrm{MgH}_2\) बहुलक हैं)। \(\mathrm{LiH}\) का उपयोग \(\mathrm{LiAlH}_4\) और \(\mathrm{LiBH}_4\) के संश्लेषण में होता है।
  • सहसंयोजक (आण्विक) हाइड्राइड: \(p\)-ब्लॉक तत्वों के वाष्पशील यौगिक:
    (i) इलेक्ट्रॉन-न्यून (वर्ग 13): अष्टक से कम इलेक्ट्रॉन (जैसे \(\mathrm{B_2H_6}\)); लुईस अम्ल की तरह व्यवहार करते हैं।
    (ii) इलेक्ट्रॉन-परिशुद्ध (वर्ग 14): चतुष्फलकीय आबंधों के लिए सटीक इलेक्ट्रॉन (जैसे \(\mathrm{CH}_4\))।
    (iii) इलेक्ट्रॉन-धनी (वर्ग 15–17): एकाकी इलेक्ट्रॉन युग्म युक्त (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\): 1, \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\): 2, \(\mathrm{HF}\): 3); लुईस क्षार की तरह व्यवहार करते हैं। प्रबल हाइड्रोजन आबंधन के कारण \(\mathrm{NH}_3\), \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) और \(\mathrm{HF}\) के क्वथनांक अपने वर्ग के अन्य हाइड्राइडों से अधिक होते हैं।
  • धात्विक (अरससमीकरणमितीय / अंतराकाशी) हाइड्राइड: \(d\)- तथा \(f\)-ब्लॉक धातुओं द्वारा निर्मित (जैसे \(\mathrm{LaH}_{2.87}\), \(\mathrm{TiH}_{1.5\text{–}1.8}\), \(\mathrm{PdH}_{0.6\text{–}0.8}\))। वर्ग 7, 8 और 9 की धातुएँ हाइड्राइड नहीं बनातीं (हाइड्राइड अंतराल), और वर्ग 6 में केवल \(\mathrm{CrH}\) बनता है। \(\mathrm{Pd}\) और \(\mathrm{Pt}\) जैसी धातुएँ अत्यधिक \(\mathrm{H}_2\) अवशोषित कर सकती हैं, अतः इनका उपयोग हाइड्रोजन भंडारण और उत्प्रेरकी हाइड्रोजनीकरण में होता है।

4. Water (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), Ice, Heavy Water (\(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\)), and Water Softening

English

Molecular Geometry and the Open Structure of Ice

In the gas phase, water is a bent polar molecule with an \(\mathrm{H–O–H}\) bond angle of \(104.5^\circ\) and an \(\mathrm{O–H}\) bond length of \(95.7\,\mathrm{pm}\). In liquid water and ice, extensive intermolecular hydrogen bonding produces unusually high melting/boiling points, high heat capacity, high dielectric constant (\(78.39\)), and high enthalpy of vaporisation (\(40.66\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)).

At atmospheric pressure, ice crystallises in a hexagonal lattice where each oxygen atom is tetrahedrally surrounded by four other oxygen atoms at \(276\,\mathrm{pm}\) via hydrogen bonds. This creates an open, cage-like 3D network with wide interstitial cavities, making ice less dense than liquid water (water reaches maximum density at \(276.98\,\mathrm{K} \approx 4^\circ\mathrm{C}\)).

Key Chemical Reactions of Water

  • Amphoteric Nature & Auto-protolysis: Water acts as a Brønsted acid with \(\mathrm{NH}_3\) and a Brønsted base with \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{S}\), and undergoes self-ionization: \(2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H_3O^+(aq)} + \mathrm{OH^-(aq)}\).
  • Redox Behaviour: Reduced to \(\mathrm{H}_2\) by alkali metals (\(2\mathrm{Na} + 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{NaOH} + \mathrm{H}_2\)); oxidised to \(\mathrm{O}_2\) by \(\mathrm{F}_2\) (\(2\mathrm{F}_2 + 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightarrow 4\mathrm{H}^+ + 4\mathrm{F}^- + \mathrm{O}_2\)) and during photosynthesis.
  • Hydrolysis vs. Hydration: Hydrolyses covalent halides/oxides (\(\mathrm{P_4O_{10}} + 6\mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow 4\mathrm{H_3PO_4}\); \(\mathrm{SiCl}_4 + 2\mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \mathrm{SiO}_2 + 4\mathrm{HCl}\)) and nitrides (\(\mathrm{N}^{3-} + 3\mathrm{H_2O} \rightarrow \mathrm{NH}_3 + 3\mathrm{OH}^-\)). In hydrated salts, water binds as: (i) coordinated water (\([\mathrm{Cr(H_2O)_6}]^{3+}3\mathrm{Cl}^-\)), (ii) interstitial water (\(\mathrm{BaCl_2\cdot 2H_2O}\)), or (iii) hydrogen-bonded water (one outer-sphere \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) in \([\mathrm{Cu(H_2O)_4}]^{2+}\mathrm{SO}_4^{2-}\cdot\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)).

Heavy Water (\(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\))

Obtained by exhaustive electrolysis of water, \(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\) (bp \(374.4\,\mathrm{K}\), max density at \(284.2\,\mathrm{K}\)) slows down fast neutrons and is widely used as a moderator in nuclear reactors and in tracer/mechanism studies. 5. Hydrogen Peroxide (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\)) and Hydrogen Economy

English

Preparation and Non-Planar Structure

  • Laboratory & Electrolytic Routes:
    (a) Acidifying hydrated barium peroxide: \(\mathrm{BaO_2\cdot 8H_2O(s)} + \mathrm{H_2SO_4(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{BaSO_4(s)} + \mathrm{H_2O_2(aq)} + 8\mathrm{H_2O(l)}\).
    (b) Electrolytic oxidation of acidified sulphate at high current density gives peroxodisulphate (\(\mathrm{HO_3SOOSO_3H}\)), which hydrolyses to \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) (also used with \(\mathrm{K_2S_2O_8} + 2\mathrm{D_2O}\) to make \(\mathrm{D_2O_2}\)).
  • Industrial Method: Auto-oxidation of 2-ethylanthraquinol in air, followed by catalytic \(\mathrm{H}_2/\mathrm{Pd}\) regeneration, yields \(\sim 1\%\) \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\), concentrated by reduced-pressure distillation and freezing.
  • Open-Book Structure: \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) is non-planar. In the gas phase, the dihedral angle between the two \(\mathrm{O–O–H}\) planes is \(111.5^\circ\) (\(\mathrm{O–O} = 147.5\,\mathrm{pm}\), \(\mathrm{O–H} = 95.0\,\mathrm{pm}\), \(\angle\mathrm{H–O–O} = 94.8^\circ\)). In the solid phase at \(110\,\mathrm{K}\), hydrogen bonding compresses the dihedral angle to \(90.2^\circ\) (\(\mathrm{O–O} = 145.8\,\mathrm{pm}\), \(\mathrm{O–H} = 98.8\,\mathrm{pm}\), \(\angle\mathrm{H–O–O} = 101.9^\circ\)).

Volume Strength, Redox Reactions, Storage, and Hydrogen Economy

  • Volume Strength: An "\(x\,\mathrm{V}\)" \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) solution means \(1\,\mathrm{mL}\) (or \(1\,\mathrm{L}\)) of the solution evolves \(x\,\mathrm{mL}\) (or \(x\,\mathrm{L}\)) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\) gas at STP via \(2\mathrm{H_2O_2(l)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)}\). Using the STP molar volume of \(22.7\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\), a \(10\,\mathrm{V}\) solution corresponds to \(\sim 30\,\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}\) (\(3\%\,\mathrm{w/v}\)), and a \(100\,\mathrm{V}\) solution corresponds to \(\sim 30\%\) \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\).
  • Dual Redox Character: Oxygen is in the \(-1\) oxidation state, so \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) acts as both an oxidising and reducing agent in acidic and basic media:
    Oxidising (acidic): \(2\mathrm{Fe}^{2+} + 2\mathrm{H}^+ + \mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Fe}^{3+} + 2\mathrm{H_2O}\) ; \(\mathrm{PbS} + 4\mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow \mathrm{PbSO_4} + 4\mathrm{H_2O}\)
    Reducing (acidic): \(2\mathrm{MnO_4^-} + 6\mathrm{H}^+ + 5\mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Mn}^{2+} + 8\mathrm{H_2O} + 5\mathrm{O_2}\)
    Oxidising (basic): \(2\mathrm{Fe}^{2+} + \mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Fe}^{3+} + 2\mathrm{OH}^-\) ; \(\mathrm{Mn}^{2+} + \mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow \mathrm{Mn}^{4+} + 2\mathrm{OH}^-\)
    Reducing (basic): \(\mathrm{I}_2 + \mathrm{H_2O_2} + 2\mathrm{OH}^- \rightarrow 2\mathrm{I}^- + 2\mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{O_2}\) ; \(2\mathrm{MnO_4^-} + 3\mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{MnO_2} + 3\mathrm{O_2} + 2\mathrm{H_2O} + 2\mathrm{OH}^-\)
  • Storage & Uses: Light, alkali traces in glass, metal surfaces, and dust catalyse decomposition (\(2\mathrm{H_2O_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O} + \mathrm{O_2}\)), so it is stored in wax-lined glass or dark plastic bottles with urea as a stabiliser. Used as an antiseptic (perhydrol), textile/paper bleach, in sodium perborate detergents, and in green-chemistry effluent treatment.
  • Hydrogen Economy: Dihydrogen releases \(\sim 143\,\mathrm{kJ\,g^{-1}}\) on combustion—about three times the energy per gram of octane (\(47\,\mathrm{kJ\,g^{-1}}\))—and powers pollution-free fuel cells. Major challenges include heavy high-pressure cylinders and cryogenic liquefaction (\(20\,\mathrm{K}\)), prompting research into metal-alloy storage (\(\mathrm{NaNi}_5\), \(\mathrm{Ti–TiH}_2\), \(\mathrm{Mg–MgH}_2\)).

Example 1: Calculating Molarity and Percentage Strength from Volume Strength

Problem (Assumed Sample): A laboratory bottle is labelled as a \(22.7\,\mathrm{V}\) aqueous solution of hydrogen peroxide. Taking the molar volume of an ideal gas at STP as \(22.7\,\mathrm{L\,mol^{-1}}\) and the molar mass of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) as \(34\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), calculate (a) the molarity of the solution and (b) its strength in \(\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}\) and percentage (\(\%\,\mathrm{w/v}\)).

Solution:

A \(22.7\,\mathrm{V}\) solution means \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) of the \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) solution produces \(22.7\,\mathrm{L}\) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\) at STP.

\[ 2\mathrm{H_2O_2(aq)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O(l)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \]

From stoichiometry, \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) yield \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) (\(22.7\,\mathrm{L}\) at STP) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\). Since \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) of solution yields \(22.7\,\mathrm{L}\) (\(= 1\,\mathrm{mol}\)) of \(\mathrm{O}_2\), it must contain \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\).

(a) \(\text{Molarity} = \dfrac{2\,\mathrm{mol}}{1\,\mathrm{L}} = 2.0\,\mathrm{M}\).

(b) \(\text{Strength in g\,L}^{-1} = 2.0\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}} \times 34\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}} = 68\,\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}\).
Since \(1000\,\mathrm{mL}\) contains \(68\,\mathrm{g}\), \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) contains \(6.8\,\mathrm{g}\), so the percentage strength is \(6.8\%\,\mathrm{w/v}\).

Example 2: Identifying Hydride Categories and Aqueous Behaviour

Problem (Assumed Elements): Consider three elements with atomic numbers \(Z = 19\) (potassium), \(Z = 13\) (aluminium), and \(Z = 23\) (vanadium). Classify the binary hydrides they form and predict how the \(Z = 19\) hydride reacts with water.

  • \(Z = 23\) (Vanadium, \(d\)-block): Forms a metallic (non-stoichiometric) hydride such as \(\mathrm{VH}_{0.56}\), which conducts heat and electricity.

Example 3: Stoichiometry of Temporary Hardness Removal (Clark's Method)

Problem (Assumed Water Sample): A \(1000\,\mathrm{L}\) sample of groundwater contains \(1.46\,\mathrm{kg}\) of dissolved \(\mathrm{Mg(HCO_3)_2}\) (molar mass \(= 146\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) and no other hardness-causing salts. Calculate the minimum mass of slaked lime, \(\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2}\) (molar mass \(= 74\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)), required to soften this water by Clark's method.

Solution:

Moles of \(\mathrm{Mg(HCO_3)_2} = \dfrac{1460\,\mathrm{g}}{146\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}} = 10\,\mathrm{mol}\).

In Clark's method, \(\mathrm{Mg(HCO_3)_2}\) requires two equivalents of \(\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2}\) to precipitate \(\mathrm{Mg(OH)_2}\) and \(\mathrm{CaCO_3}\):

\[ \mathrm{Mg(HCO_3)_2} + 2\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{CaCO_3}\downarrow + \mathrm{Mg(OH)_2}\downarrow + 2\mathrm{H_2O} \]

Moles of \(\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2}\) required \(= 2 \times 10\,\mathrm{mol} = 20\,\mathrm{mol}\).

Mass of \(\mathrm{Ca(OH)_2} = 20\,\mathrm{mol} \times 74\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}} = 1480\,\mathrm{g} = 1.48\,\mathrm{kg}\).

  • (B) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.

Correct Answer: (A)

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

English

Frequent NEET UG Traps to Avoid

  • \(\mathrm{H}_2\) Evolution on Electrolysis of Saline Hydrides: In molten \(\mathrm{NaH}\), \(\mathrm{H}_2\) is liberated at the anode (oxidation of \(\mathrm{H}^-\)), whereas in aqueous electrolysis it is liberated at the cathode.
  • Boiling Temporary Hard Water: \(\mathrm{Ca(HCO_3)_2}\) precipitates as \(\mathrm{CaCO}_3\), but \(\mathrm{Mg(HCO_3)_2}\) precipitates as \(\mathrm{Mg(OH)_2}\) (not \(\mathrm{MgCO}_3\)) because \(\mathrm{Mg(OH)_2}\) has a lower solubility product.
  • \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) Dihedral Angle: Remember it is \(111.5^\circ\) in the gas phase and \(90.2^\circ\) in the solid phase; do not confuse \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\) as planar or non-polar—it is non-planar and polar.
  • \(\mathrm{MnO_4^-} + \mathrm{H_2O_2}\) Medium Dependence: Produces \(\mathrm{Mn}^{2+}\) in acidic medium, but \(\mathrm{MnO}_2\) in basic medium.

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Ionization enthalpy of \(\mathrm{H}\): \(1312\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\); \(\mathrm{H–H}\) bond enthalpy: \(435.88\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).
  • Radioactive isotope: Tritium (\(^3_1\mathrm{H}\)), \(\beta^-\)-emitter, \(t_{1/2} = 12.33\,\mathrm{years}\).
  • Hydride gap: Groups 7, 8, 9 (and Group 6 except \(\mathrm{CrH}\)).
  • \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) bond angle \(104.5^\circ\); ice \(\mathrm{O}\cdots\mathrm{O}\) distance \(276\,\mathrm{pm}\).
  • \(10\,\mathrm{V}\) \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2 \approx 30\,\mathrm{g\,L^{-1}}\) (\(3\%\)); \(100\,\mathrm{V} \approx 30\%\).

NEET UG में बचने योग्य सामान्य त्रुटियाँ

Concise Final Recap

English

Hydrogen's \(1s^1\) configuration gives it dual resemblance to alkali metals and halogens while justifying its independent periodic placement. Its high \(\mathrm{H–H}\) bond enthalpy (\(435.88\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) makes \(\mathrm{H}_2\) unreactive at room temperature yet industrially vital in Haber ammonia synthesis, oil hydrogenation, and clean fuel cells. Across its compounds—from ionic, covalent, and interstitial hydrides to tetrahedrally hydrogen-bonded ice, ion-exchanged soft water, nuclear-grade \(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\), and non-planar \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\)—structure and redox flexibility govern every exam-relevant property.

हिन्दी

हाइड्रोजन का \(1s^1\) विन्यास इसे क्षार धातुओं और हैलोजनों दोनों से समानता प्रदान करता है तथा आवर्त सारणी में इसके स्वतंत्र स्थान को उचित ठहराता है। उच्च \(\mathrm{H–H}\) आबंध एन्थैल्पी (\(435.88\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) इसे कमरे के ताप पर निष्क्रिय बनाती है, किंतु औद्योगिक रूप से यह अमोनिया संश्लेषण, तेल हाइड्रोजनीकरण और स्वच्छ ईंधन सेलों में अत्यंत महत्वपूर्ण है। इसके प्रमुख यौगिकों—आयनिक, सहसंयोजक व अंतराकाशी हाइड्राइड, चतुष्फलकीय बर्फ, आयन-विनिमित मृदु जल, नाभिकीय \(\mathrm{D}_2\mathrm{O}\), तथा असमतलीय \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}_2\)—के सभी परीक्षा-उपयोगी गुण उनकी संरचना और रेडॉक्स व्यवहार से नियंत्रित होते हैं।
