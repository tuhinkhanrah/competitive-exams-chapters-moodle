Learning Objectives & Exam Overview

English

Organic chemistry studies carbon-based covalent compounds—excluding simple inorganic carbonates, cyanides, and oxides—whose vast diversity arises from carbon's tetravalence and catenation (self-linking into chains and rings). Historically, Friedrich Wöhler's 1828 synthesis of urea (\(\mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}\)) from inorganic ammonium cyanate (\(\mathrm{NH_4CNO}\)) disproved the "vital force" theory, establishing that organic molecules obey universal chemical laws.

For NEET UG, this foundational topic carries high weightage because it underpins all reaction chapters. After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Determine carbon hybridisation (\(sp^3, sp^2, sp\)), molecular geometry, \(\sigma\)/\(\pi\) bond counts, and bond parameters.
  • Convert between complete, condensed, bond-line, and 3D wedge-dash representations.
  • Classify organic compounds and apply IUPAC nomenclature rules, including polyfunctional priority order.
  • Identify structural isomers and distinguish them from stereoisomers.
  • Predict reactive intermediate stability (carbocations, carbanions, free radicals) using inductive, resonance, electromeric, and hyperconjugation effects.
  • Select appropriate purification techniques and solve qualitative (Lassaigne's test) and quantitative (Liebig, Dumas, Kjeldahl, Carius) analysis problems.

1. Tetravalence of Carbon, Hybridisation & Structural Formulas

English

Hybridisation, \(s\)-Character, and Bond Properties

Carbon forms four covalent bonds using hybridised orbitals formed by mixing one \(2s\) and up to three \(2p\) orbitals. As the percentage of \(s\)-character increases, the hybrid orbital is held closer to the nucleus, making the carbon atom more electronegative and its bonds shorter and stronger.

Hybridisation % \(s\)-Character Geometry & Bond Angle Typical Example
\(sp^3\) (4 \(\sigma\) bonds) 25% Tetrahedral, \(109.5^\circ\) Methane (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\))
\(sp^2\) (3 \(\sigma\), 1 \(\pi\)) 33.3% Trigonal planar, \(120^\circ\) Ethene (\(\mathrm{C_2H_4}\))
\(sp\) (2 \(\sigma\), 2 \(\pi\)) 50% Linear, \(180^\circ\) Ethyne (\(\mathrm{C_2H_2}\))

Key Features of \(\pi\) Bonds: A \(\pi\) bond forms by sideways (lateral) overlap of parallel unhybridised \(p\) orbitals perpendicular to the molecular plane. Rotating one \(\mathrm{CH_2}\) group relative to the other in ethene breaks this overlap; hence, rotation around a \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bond is restricted. Because the \(\pi\)-electron cloud lies above and below the internuclear axis, \(\pi\) bonds are more exposed and act as the primary reactive centres toward electrophilic reagents.

Structural Representations

  • Complete Structural Formula: Shows every atom and every covalent bond as a dash (single `—`, double `=`, triple `≡`).
  • Condensed Formula: Omits most bond dashes and groups identical units using subscripts, e.g., \(\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_4CH_3}\) for hexane.
  • Bond-Line Formula: Draws carbon–carbon bonds as zig-zag lines; carbon and attached hydrogens are omitted. Line junctions and termini represent carbons (methyl groups at free ends unless another atom is written). Heteroatoms (\(\mathrm{O, N, Cl, Br}\)) and their attached hydrogens are explicitly shown.
  • 3D Wedge-Dash Formula: Normal lines lie in the paper plane, a solid wedge projects toward the viewer, and a dashed wedge projects away from the viewer.

1. कार्बन की चतुःसंयोजकता, संकरण एवं संरचनात्मक सूत्र

कार्बन एक \(2s\) और अधिकतम तीन \(2p\) कक्षकों के संकरण द्वारा चार सहसंयोजक बंध बनाता है। संकर कक्षक में \(s\)-लक्षण (s-character) बढ़ने पर इलेक्ट्रॉन नाभिक के अधिक निकट रहते हैं, जिससे कार्बन की विद्युतऋणात्मकता बढ़ती है तथा बंध छोटा और अधिक प्रबल होता है।

संकरण % \(s\)-लक्षण ज्यामिति एवं बंध कोण उदाहरण
\(sp^3\) (4 \(\sigma\) बंध) 25% चतुष्फलकीय, \(109.5^\circ\) मेथेन (\(\mathrm{CH_4}\))
\(sp^2\) (3 \(\sigma\), 1 \(\pi\)) 33.3% त्रिकोणीय समतलीय, \(120^\circ\) एथीन (\(\mathrm{C_2H_4}\))
\(sp\) (2 \(\sigma\), 2 \(\pi\)) 50% रेखीय, \(180^\circ\) एथाइन (\(\mathrm{C_2H_2}\))

\(\pi\) बंध के प्रमुख गुण: \(\pi\) बंध अणु के तल के लंबवत दो समानांतर असंकरित \(p\)-कक्षकों के पार्श्वीय (sideways) अतिव्यापन से बनता है। एक \(\mathrm{CH_2}\) समूह को दूसरे के सापेक्ष घुमाने पर यह अतिव्यापन टूट जाता है; अतः \(\mathrm{C=C}\) द्विबंध के चारों ओर घूर्णन प्रतिबंधित होता है। \(\pi\) इलेक्ट्रॉन अभ्र बंध तल के ऊपर और नीचे स्थित होने के कारण आसानी से उपलब्ध रहता है, इसलिए \(\pi\) बंध इलेक्ट्रॉनस्नेही अभिकर्मकों के प्रति सर्वाधिक अभिक्रियाशील केंद्र होते हैं।

संरचनात्मक निरूपण के प्रकार

  • पूर्ण संरचनात्मक सूत्र: प्रत्येक परमाणु व प्रत्येक सहसंयोजक बंध को रेखा (—, =, ≡) द्वारा दर्शाया जाता है।
  • संघनित सूत्र: बंध रेखाएँ हटाकर समान समूहों को पादांक (subscript) से लिखते हैं, जैसे हेक्सेन के लिए \(\mathrm{CH_3(CH_2)_4CH_3}\)।
  • बंध-रेखा सूत्र: कार्बन–कार्बन बंधों को टेढ़ी-मेढ़ी (zig-zag) रेखाओं से दर्शाते हैं; कोने और सिरे कार्बन परमाणु दर्शाते हैं। विषम परमाणु (\(\mathrm{O, N, Cl, Br}\)) और उनसे जुड़े हाइड्रोजन स्पष्ट लिखे जाते हैं।
  • त्रिविमीय वेज-डैश सूत्र: सामान्य रेखा कागज के तल में, ठोस वेज (solid wedge) दर्शक की ओर, तथा डैश वेज (dashed wedge) दर्शक से दूर जाते हुए बंध को दर्शाता है।

2. Classification & IUPAC Nomenclature

English

Structural Classification

  • Acyclic (Aliphatic): Straight or branched open-chain compounds (e.g., isobutane, acetic acid).
  • Alicyclic: Non-aromatic closed-ring compounds—either homocyclic (only carbon in the ring, e.g., cyclopropane, cyclohexene) or heterocyclic (ring contains \(\mathrm{O, N, S}\), e.g., tetrahydrofuran).
  • Aromatic: Special conjugated ring systems—benzenoid (benzene, aniline, naphthalene), non-benzenoid (tropone), and heterocyclic aromatic (furan, thiophene, pyridine).

A functional group is an atom or group of atoms bonded to a carbon chain that governs its characteristic chemical reactivity. A series of compounds sharing the same functional group where consecutive members differ by a \(-\mathrm{CH_2}-\) unit (14 u) forms a homologous series.

Essential IUPAC Rules

  1. Longest Chain & Lowest Locant: Select the longest continuous carbon chain containing the principal functional group (or maximum substituents in alkanes). Number from the end giving the principal functional group (or first point of branching) the lowest locant.
  2. Alphabetical Order & Prefixes: List substituents alphabetically. Multiplicative prefixes (di-, tri-, tetra-) and hyphenated prefixes (sec-, tert-) are ignored when alphabetising, whereas iso- and neo- are retained.
  3. Polyfunctional Priority Order (Decreasing):
    \(-\mathrm{COOH} > -\mathrm{SO_3H} > -\mathrm{COOR} > -\mathrm{COCl} > -\mathrm{CONH_2} > -\mathrm{CN} > -\mathrm{CHO} > \mathrm{>C=O} > -\mathrm{OH} > -\mathrm{NH_2} > \mathrm{>C=C<} > -\mathrm{C\equiv C-}\).
    Always-prefixed groups: \(-\mathrm{R}\), \(-\mathrm{C_6H_5}\) (phenyl), halogens (\(-\mathrm{F, -Cl, -Br, -I}\)), \(-\mathrm{NO_2}\), and \(-\mathrm{OR}\) (alkoxy).

\(-\mathrm{COOR}\) (Ester)alkoxycarbonyl--oate
\(-\mathrm{CONH_2}\) (Amide)carbamoyl--amide
\(-\mathrm{CN}\) (Nitrile)cyano--nitrile

\(\mathrm{>C=O}\) (Ketone)oxo--one

Substituted Benzenes: Disubstituted benzenes use 1,2- (ortho, o-), 1,3- (meta, m-), or 1,4- (para, p-) prefixes. For three or more substituents, apply the lowest locant rule; if a base name (like aniline, phenol, or anisole) is used, its functional group carbon is numbered C-1.

3. Isomerism: Structural vs. Stereoisomerism

English

Compounds sharing the same molecular formula but exhibiting different physical or chemical properties are isomers.

  • Chain Isomerism: Differ in the carbon skeleton (e.g., pentane, isopentane, neopentane for \(\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}\)).
  • Position Isomerism: Differ in the position of a substituent or functional group on the same skeleton (e.g., propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol).
  • Functional Group Isomerism: Possess different functional groups (e.g., propanal and propanone for \(\mathrm{C_3H_6O}\)).
  • Metamerism: Differ in the alkyl chains flanking a polyvalent functional group such as ether (\(-\mathrm{O}-\)) or ketone (e.g., methoxypropane and ethoxyethane for \(\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}\)).
  • Stereoisomerism: Identical bond connectivity but different spatial arrangement of atoms—subdivided into geometrical and optical isomerism.

3. समावयवता: संरचनात्मक बनाम त्रिविम समावयवता

समान आण्विक सूत्र लेकिन भिन्न भौतिक या रासायनिक गुणों वाले यौगिक समावयवी (isomers) कहलाते हैं।

  • शृंखला समावयवता: कार्बन कंकाल की लंबाई/शाखन में भिन्नता (जैसे \(\mathrm{C_5H_{12}}\) के लिए पेंटेन, आइसोपेंटेन, निओपेंटेन)।
  • स्थिति समावयवता: समान कार्बन कंकाल पर प्रतिस्थापी या क्रियात्मक समूह की स्थिति में भिन्नता (जैसे प्रोपेन-1-ऑल और प्रोपेन-2-ऑल)।
  • क्रियात्मक समूह समावयवता: भिन्न क्रियात्मक समूहों की उपस्थिति (जैसे \(\mathrm{C_3H_6O}\) के लिए प्रोपेनेल और प्रोपेनोन)।
  • मध्यावयवता (Metamerism): क्रियात्मक समूह (जैसे ईथर \(-\mathrm{O}-\) या कीटोन) के दोनों ओर भिन्न ऐल्किल शृंखलाओं के कारण (जैसे \(\mathrm{C_4H_{10}O}\) के लिए मेथॉक्सीप्रोपेन व एथॉक्सीएथेन)।
  • त्रिविम समावयवता: बंध क्रम समान परंतु अंतरिक्ष में परमाणुओं की त्रिविमीय व्यवस्था भिन्न—इसके दो प्रकार ज्यामितीय और प्रकाशिक समावयवता हैं।

4. Reaction Mechanisms, Intermediates & Electronic Effects

English

Bond Fission & Reactive Intermediates

  • Heterolytic Cleavage (Polar/Ionic): Both shared electrons move to one atom. Forms a carbocation (\(sp^2\), trigonal planar, electron sextet, vacant \(p\) orbital) or a carbanion (\(sp^3\), pyramidal/distorted tetrahedral, electron octet with lone pair).
  • Homolytic Cleavage (Non-polar/Free Radical): Each atom takes one electron (shown by half-headed fish-hook arrows), forming neutral free radicals with an unpaired electron.
Carbocation & Alkyl Free Radical Stability Order:
\[ 3^\circ \; (\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+}) \;>\; 2^\circ \; (\mathrm{(CH_3)_2CH^+}) \;>\; 1^\circ \; (\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^+}) \;>\; \mathrm{CH_3^+} \]

Nucleophiles (\(\mathrm{Nu:}\)) vs. Electrophiles (\(\mathrm{E^+}\)): Nucleophiles are electron-pair donors (e.g., \(\mathrm{HO^-}, \mathrm{CN^-}, \mathrm{HS^-}, \mathrm{NH_3}\)) that attack electron-deficient centres. Electrophiles are electron-pair acceptors (e.g., \(\mathrm{BF_3}, \mathrm{AlCl_3}, \mathrm{NO_2^+}, \mathrm{CH_3^+}\), carbonyl carbon) that attack electron-rich sites.

Permanent & Temporary Electronic Displacement Effects

Effect Nature & Range Key Mechanism & Groups
Inductive Effect (\(I\)) Permanent, \(\sigma\)-bond polarisation; vanishes after 3 bonds. \(-I\) (withdrawing): \(-\mathrm{NO_2}, -\mathrm{CN}, -\mathrm{COOH}, -\mathrm{X}\).
\(+I\) (donating): alkyl groups (\(-\mathrm{CH_3}, -\mathrm{C_2H_5}\)).
Resonance / Mesomeric (\(R/M\)) Permanent, \(\pi\)-electron delocalisation in conjugated systems. \(+R\): \(-\mathrm{OH}, -\mathrm{OR}, -\mathrm{NH_2}, -\mathrm{X}\) (donate lone pair).
\(-R\): \(-\mathrm{NO_2}, -\mathrm{CN}, -\mathrm{CHO}, -\mathrm{COOH}\) (withdraw \(\pi\) electrons).
Electromeric Effect (\(E\)) Temporary; occurs only in presence of attacking reagent. \(+E\): \(\pi\)-electrons shift toward atom where reagent attaches (e.g., \(\mathrm{H^+}\) on alkene).
\(-E\): \(\pi\)-electrons shift away from attaching atom (e.g., \(\mathrm{CN^-}\) on \(\mathrm{>C=O}\)).
Hyperconjugation Permanent "no-bond resonance"; delocalisation of \(\alpha\text{-}\mathrm{C-H}\) \(\sigma\)-electrons into adjacent vacant \(p\) or \(\pi^*\) orbital. More \(\alpha\)-hydrogens = greater stability of carbocations, free radicals, and alkenes.

Four Core Reaction Types: (1) Substitution (one group replaces another), (2) Addition (reagent adds across a multiple bond), (3) Elimination (loss of two groups to form a multiple bond), and (4) Rearrangement (migration of an atom/group within the molecule).

Qualitative Analysis (Lassaigne's Test)

Covalent \(\mathrm{N, S, X}\) are converted to ionic \(\mathrm{NaCN, Na_2S, NaX}\) by fusing with metallic sodium and extracting with distilled water (sodium fusion extract, SFE):

  • Nitrogen: SFE + \(\mathrm{FeSO_4}\) + conc. \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) → Prussian blue precipitate of \(\mathrm{Fe_4[Fe(CN)_6]_3}\).
  • Sulphur: (a) SFE + acetic acid + lead acetate → black \(\mathrm{PbS}\); (b) SFE + sodium nitroprusside → violet \(\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_5NOS]^{4-}}\).
  • N and S together: Forms \(\mathrm{NaSCN}\), giving a blood-red colour \(\mathrm{[Fe(SCN)]^{2+}}\) with \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}\) (no Prussian blue unless excess \(\mathrm{Na}\) decomposes \(\mathrm{NaSCN}\) into \(\mathrm{NaCN + Na_2S}\)).
  • Halogens: Boil SFE with conc. \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) (to expel \(\mathrm{HCN}\) and \(\mathrm{H_2S}\) interference), then add \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\): \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) (white, soluble in \(\mathrm{NH_4OH}\)), \(\mathrm{AgBr}\) (pale yellow, sparingly soluble), \(\mathrm{AgI}\) (yellow, insoluble).
  • Phosphorus: Fuse with \(\mathrm{Na_2O_2}\), boil with \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\), add ammonium molybdate → canary yellow precipitate of \(\mathrm{(NH_4)_3PO_4 \cdot 12MoO_3}\).

  • Halogens (Carius Method): \(\%\mathrm{X} = \dfrac{\text{Atomic mass of X} \times m_{\mathrm{AgX}} \times 100}{\text{Molar mass of AgX} \times m}\).

Example 1: Hybridisation, Bond Counting, and IUPAC Naming

Problem: Consider the molecule \(\mathrm{HO-CH_2-CH=CH-C\equiv N}\). Determine (a) the hybridisation state of each carbon atom from left to right, (b) the total number of \(\sigma\) and \(\pi\) bonds, and (c) its IUPAC name.

Solution:

  • (a) Hybridisation: Writing the carbon skeleton from left to right: \(\mathrm{C_4H_2(OH)-C_3H=C_2H-C_1\equiv N}\). \(\mathrm{C_4}\) has 4 single bonds → \(sp^3\); \(\mathrm{C_3}\) has 1 double + 2 single bonds → \(sp^2\); \(\mathrm{C_2}\) has 1 double + 2 single bonds → \(sp^2\); \(\mathrm{C_1}\) has 1 triple + 1 single bond → \(sp\).
  • (b) Bond Count: Every single bond is 1 \(\sigma\); a double bond is \(1\sigma + 1\pi\); a triple bond is \(1\sigma + 2\pi\).
    Single bonds: \(\mathrm{O-H}\) (1), \(\mathrm{C-O}\) (1), \(\mathrm{C_4-H}\) (2), \(\mathrm{C_4-C_3}\) (1), \(\mathrm{C_3-H}\) (1), \(\mathrm{C_2-H}\) (1), \(\mathrm{C_2-C_1}\) (1) = 8 \(\sigma\).
    Plus \(\mathrm{C_3=C_2}\) (\(1\sigma + 1\pi\)) and \(\mathrm{C_1\equiv N}\) (\(1\sigma + 2\pi\)).
    Total = 10 \(\sigma\) bonds and 3 \(\pi\) bonds.
  • (c) IUPAC Name: Between \(-\mathrm{CN}\) and \(-\mathrm{OH}\), \(-\mathrm{CN}\) has higher priority (principal functional group, suffix -nitrile). Numbering starts at the nitrile carbon (\(\mathrm{C_1}\)). The \(-\mathrm{OH}\) at \(\mathrm{C_4}\) is prefixed as 4-hydroxy, and the double bond is at \(\mathrm{C_2}\). Thus, the name is 4-Hydroxybut-2-enenitrile.

Example 2: Comparing Carbocation Stability via Hyperconjugation

Problem: Arrange the following carbocations in decreasing order of stability: (I) \(\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_2CH_3}\), (II) \(\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+}\), (III) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^+}\).

Solution: Carbocation stability increases with the number of \(\alpha\)-hydrogens available for hyperconjugation and \(+I\) alkyl groups.

  • In (II) \(\mathrm{(CH_3)_3C^+}\) (\(3^\circ\)): three methyl groups provide 9 \(\alpha\)-H atoms.
  • In (I) \(\mathrm{CH_3-\overset{+}{C}H-CH_2CH_3}\) (\(2^\circ\)): one methyl (\(-\mathrm{CH_3}\)) and one methylene (\(-\mathrm{CH_2}-\)) provide \(3 + 2 = \mathbf{5\;\alpha\text{-H}}\) atoms.
  • In (III) \(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2^+}\) (\(1^\circ\)): one methyl group provides 3 \(\alpha\)-H atoms.

Therefore, the decreasing stability order is: (II) > (I) > (III).

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

English
  • Alphabetising Substituents: Never count di-, tri-, sec-, or tert- when alphabetising; always count iso- and neo-.
  • Halogen Test in Lassaigne's Extract: Forgetting to boil with conc. \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) leaves \(\mathrm{NaCN}\) or \(\mathrm{Na_2S}\) intact, which precipitate with \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) and give false positives.
  • Acetic Acid vs. \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) in Lead Acetate Test: Always acidify with acetic acid, because \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) precipitates \(\mathrm{PbSO_4}\) (white) with lead acetate.
  • Kjeldahl's Scope: Remember to exclude nitro, azo, and heterocyclic ring-N compounds; Dumas method works for all nitrogenous compounds.
  • \(\mathrm{CCl_4}\) with \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\): \(\mathrm{CCl_4}\) is covalent and does not ionise to give \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\), so it gives no \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) precipitate without sodium fusion.

सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावृत्ति जांच-सूची
