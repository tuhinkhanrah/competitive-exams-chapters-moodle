1. Learning Objectives and Chapter Scope / শিখন উদ্দেশ্য ও অধ্যায়ের পরিধি

English

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds composed exclusively of carbon and hydrogen atoms. They serve as the primary backbone of fuels (LPG, CNG, petrol, diesel) and industrial feedstocks for polymers, dyes, and pharmaceuticals. For NEET UG, mastering this topic requires a clear command over nomenclature, isomerism, conformational stability, reaction mechanisms, and aromaticity.

  • Classify hydrocarbons into alkanes, cycloalkanes, alkenes, alkynes, and aromatic hydrocarbons (arenes).
  • Apply IUPAC rules and identify structural, conformational (ethane), and geometrical (cis-trans) isomers.
  • Predict major products of alkane reactions (Wurtz reaction, decarboxylation, Kolbe's electrolysis, free-radical halogenation).
  • Determine regioselectivity and stereoselectivity in alkene and alkyne reactions (Markovnikov rule, peroxide effect, Lindlar reduction, ozonolysis, hydration).
  • Evaluate aromaticity using Hückel's \((4n + 2)\,\pi\) rule and predict electrophilic substitution products using substituent directive effects.

3. Alkanes: Structure, Conformations, Preparation, and Reactions / অ্যালকেন: গঠন, কনফর্মেশন, প্রস্তুতি ও বিক্রিয়া

English

3.1 Isomerism, Carbon Classification, and Physical Trends

Alkanes were historically called paraffins (Latin: parum affinis, meaning little affinity) due to their low reactivity toward acids and bases under normal conditions. Alkanes with four or more carbons exhibit chain isomerism (e.g., butane has 2 isomers, pentane has 3, hexane has 5). Carbon atoms are classified by the number of attached carbons: primary (\(1^\circ\)), secondary (\(2^\circ\)), tertiary (\(3^\circ\)), and quaternary (\(4^\circ\)).

  • Physical State at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\): \(\mathrm{C_1–C_4}\) are gases, \(\mathrm{C_5–C_{17}}\) are liquids, and \(\mathrm{C_{18}}\) and above are solids.
  • Boiling Point Trend: Boiling point increases with molecular mass due to stronger van der Waals forces. Among isomers, branching decreases boiling point because the molecule becomes more spherical, reducing surface contact area (e.g., n-pentane \(309.1\,\mathrm{K}\) > isopentane \(300.9\,\mathrm{K}\) > neopentane \(282.5\,\mathrm{K}\)).

3.2 Conformations of Ethane

Because the \(\mathrm{C–C}\) \(\sigma\)-bond is cylindrically symmetrical, rotation around it is nearly free, producing infinite spatial arrangements called conformers or rotamers. Two extreme conformations of ethane are staggered (dihedral angle \(60^\circ\), C–H bonds maximally separated, lowest torsional strain, most stable) and eclipsed (dihedral angle \(0^\circ\), C–H bonds aligned, highest electron-cloud repulsion, least stable). The energy barrier between them is only \(12.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\), so conformers interconvert rapidly at room temperature and cannot be isolated separately.

3.3 Key Methods of Preparation and Reactions of Alkanes

  • Catalytic Hydrogenation: Alkenes or alkynes react with \(\mathrm{H}_2\) over \(\mathrm{Pt}\), \(\mathrm{Pd}\) (room temperature), or \(\mathrm{Ni}\) (elevated temperature and pressure) to yield alkanes.
  • Reduction of Alkyl Halides: \(\mathrm{R–X}\) (except fluorides) with \(\mathrm{Zn/H^+}\) yields \(\mathrm{R–H}\).
  • Wurtz Reaction: Treatment of alkyl halides with \(\mathrm{Na}\) in dry ether produces symmetrical alkanes with an even number of carbons: \[2\mathrm{R–Br} + 2\mathrm{Na} \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} \mathrm{R–R} + 2\mathrm{NaBr}\] Using two different alkyl halides (\(\mathrm{R–X}\) and \(\mathrm{R'–X}\)) yields a mixture of three alkanes (\(\mathrm{R–R}\), \(\mathrm{R'–R'}\), \(\mathrm{R–R'}\)) that are difficult to separate.
  • Soda-Lime Decarboxylation: Heating sodium carboxylate with \(\mathrm{NaOH + CaO}\) removes \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) as \(\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{CO}_3\), yielding an alkane with one carbon less than the parent acid salt.
  • Kolbe's Electrolytic Method: Electrolysis of aqueous \(\mathrm{RCOONa}\) or \(\mathrm{RCOOK}\) forms symmetrical alkane \(\mathrm{R–R}\) and \(\mathrm{CO}_2\) at the anode, and \(\mathrm{H}_2\) at the cathode. Methane cannot be prepared by this method.
  • Free-Radical Halogenation: Occurs in UV light or heat (\(573–773\,\mathrm{K}\)) via initiation, propagation, and termination steps. Reactivity order of halogens: \(\mathrm{F}_2 > \mathrm{Cl}_2 > \mathrm{Br}_2 > \mathrm{I}_2\). Ease of H-abstraction: \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ\). Iodination is reversible and requires an oxidising agent like \(\mathrm{HIO}_3\) or \(\mathrm{HNO}_3\) to consume \(\mathrm{HI}\).
  • Other Reactions:
    • Controlled Oxidation: \(\mathrm{CH}_4 + \mathrm{O}_2\) gives \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{OH}\) (\(\mathrm{Cu}\), \(523\,\mathrm{K}\), \(100\,\mathrm{atm}\)) or \(\mathrm{HCHO}\) (\(\mathrm{Mo}_2\mathrm{O}_3\), \(\Delta\)). Alkanes with a \(3^\circ\) H-atom are oxidised by \(\mathrm{KMnO}_4\) to \(3^\circ\) alcohols.
    • Isomerisation: n-Alkanes with anhydrous \(\mathrm{AlCl}_3 / \mathrm{HCl}\) rearrange to branched alkanes.
    • Aromatization (Reforming): n-Hexane heated at \(773\,\mathrm{K}\), \(10–20\,\mathrm{atm}\) over \(\mathrm{Cr}_2\mathrm{O}_3\), \(\mathrm{V}_2\mathrm{O}_5\), or \(\mathrm{Mo}_2\mathrm{O}_3\) on alumina yields benzene.
    • Pyrolysis (Cracking): Thermal decomposition of higher alkanes into lower alkanes and alkenes via free radicals.

4. Alkenes: Geometrical Isomerism, Synthesis, and Addition Mechanisms / অ্যালকিন: জ্যামিতিক সমাবয়বতা, সংশ্লেষণ ও যুত বিক্রিয়া

English

4.1 Structure and Geometrical (cis-trans) Isomerism

The \(\mathrm{C=C}\) double bond consists of one strong \(\sigma\)-bond (\(sp^2–sp^2\) head-on overlap) and one weaker \(\pi\)-bond (sideways \(2p–2p\) overlap). The loosely held \(\pi\)-electron cloud makes alkenes susceptible to electrophilic addition reactions.

Restricted rotation around the \(\mathrm{C=C}\) bond gives rise to geometrical isomerism whenever each doubly bonded carbon carries two different atoms or groups (e.g., \(\mathrm{XYC=CXY}\), \(\mathrm{XYC=CXZ}\)).

  • In the cis isomer, identical groups lie on the same side of the double bond; in the trans isomer, they lie on opposite sides.
  • cis-But-2-ene has a net dipole moment (\(\mu = 0.33\,\mathrm{D}\)) and higher boiling point (\(277\,\mathrm{K}\)), whereas trans-but-2-ene has \(\mu \approx 0\,\mathrm{D}\) (bond dipoles cancel) and lower boiling point (\(274\,\mathrm{K}\)), though trans isomers generally pack better in solids and have higher melting points.

4.2 Preparation of Alkenes

  1. Partial Reduction of Alkynes:
    • Using Lindlar's catalyst (\(\mathrm{Pd/C}\) partially poisoned with sulphur compounds or quinoline) gives cis-alkene.
    • Using \(\mathrm{Na}\) in liquid \(\mathrm{NH}_3\) gives trans-alkene.
  2. Dehydrohalogenation (\(\beta\)-elimination): Heating alkyl halides with alcoholic \(\mathrm{KOH}\) eliminates \(\mathrm{HX}\). Rate order: \(\mathrm{R–I} > \mathrm{R–Br} > \mathrm{R–Cl}\) and \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ\) alkyl groups.
  3. Dehalogenation of Vicinal Dihalides: Treating vicinal dihalides with \(\mathrm{Zn}\) dust yields alkenes and \(\mathrm{ZnX}_2\).
  4. Acidic Dehydration of Alcohols: Heating alcohols with conc. \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\) eliminates \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) via \(\beta\)-elimination.

4.3 Key Reactions of Alkenes

  • Test for Unsaturation (Bromine Water/CCl4): Addition of \(\mathrm{Br}_2\) in \(\mathrm{CCl}_4\) forms colourless vicinal dibromides, discharging the reddish-orange colour.
  • Markovnikov Rule (Electrophilic Addition of \(\mathrm{HX}\)): Reactivity order is \(\mathrm{HI} > \mathrm{HBr} > \mathrm{HCl}\). In unsymmetrical alkenes, \(\mathrm{H}^+\) adds to the carbon with more hydrogens so that the more stable carbocation (\(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ\)) forms; the halide ion then attacks this carbocation.
  • Anti-Markovnikov / Peroxide / Kharasch Effect: In the presence of organic peroxides (e.g., benzoyl peroxide), only \(\mathrm{HBr}\) (not \(\mathrm{HCl}\) or \(\mathrm{HI}\)) adds opposite to Markovnikov's rule via a free-radical chain mechanism, forming the more stable carbon radical intermediate.
  • Acid-Catalysed Hydration: Addition of \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) in presence of conc. \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\) follows Markovnikov's rule to yield alcohols.
  • Oxidation:
    • Baeyer's Reagent (cold, dilute, aqueous \(\mathrm{KMnO}_4\) at \(273\,\mathrm{K}\)): produces vicinal glycols (1,2-diols) and decolorises purple \(\mathrm{KMnO}_4\) (unsaturation test).
    • Acidic \(\mathrm{KMnO}_4\) or \(\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{Cr}_2\mathrm{O}_7\): cleaves the double bond to form ketones and/or carboxylic acids.
  • Reductive Ozonolysis (\(\mathrm{O}_3\) followed by \(\mathrm{Zn/H_2O}\)): Cleaves the \(\mathrm{C=C}\) bond cleanly into two carbonyl compounds (aldehydes/ketones), pinpointing the double bond position.

5. Alkynes: Bonding, Acidic Character, and Key Conversions / অ্যালকাইন: বন্ধন, আম্লিক ধর্ম ও প্রধান রূপান্তর

English

5.1 Structure and Acidic Nature of Terminal Alkynes

Alkynes contain a \(\mathrm{C\equiv C}\) triple bond composed of one \(\sigma\)-bond (\(sp–sp\)) and two perpendicular \(\pi\)-bonds. The \(sp\) hybridised carbon possesses 50% \(s\)-character, making it significantly more electronegative than \(sp^2\) (33.3% \(s\)) or \(sp^3\) (25% \(s\)) carbons. Consequently, hydrogen atoms attached directly to a triply bonded carbon (terminal alkynes) are acidic and react with strong bases like \(\mathrm{Na}\) metal or \(\mathrm{NaNH}_2\) to release \(\mathrm{H}_2\) or \(\mathrm{NH}_3\).

Acidic Strength Order: \(\mathrm{HC\equiv CH} > \mathrm{CH_3–C\equiv CH} \gg \mathrm{H_2C=CH_2} > \mathrm{CH_3–CH_3}\)

Internal alkynes such as but-2-yne (\(\mathrm{CH_3–C\equiv C–CH_3}\)) lack a terminal \(\equiv \mathrm{C–H}\) proton and do not react with \(\mathrm{Na}\) or \(\mathrm{NaNH}_2\).

5.2 Preparation and Reactions of Alkynes

  • Industrial Preparation of Ethyne: \(\mathrm{CaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} \mathrm{CaO} + \mathrm{CO}_2\); \(\mathrm{CaO} + 3\mathrm{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaC}_2 + \mathrm{CO}\); \(\mathrm{CaC}_2 + 2\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightarrow \mathrm{Ca(OH)}_2 + \mathrm{C_2H_2}\).
  • From Vicinal Dihalides: Two-step elimination using alcoholic \(\mathrm{KOH}\) followed by \(\mathrm{NaNH}_2\) (stronger base required to remove the second, vinylic halide).
  • Electrophilic Addition (\(\mathrm{X}_2\), \(\mathrm{HX}\)): Adds two equivalents of reagent via a vinylic cation intermediate, following Markovnikov's rule for unsymmetrical alkynes (e.g., propyne + \(2\mathrm{HBr} \rightarrow\) 2,2-dibromopropane, a gem-dihalide).
  • Hydration (\(\mathrm{HgSO}_4 / \text{dil. }\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\) at \(333\,\mathrm{K}\)): Forms an unstable enol that tautomerises (isomerises) to a carbonyl compound. Ethyne yields ethanal; propyne yields propanone.
  • Cyclic Polymerisation: Passing ethyne through a red-hot iron tube at \(873\,\mathrm{K}\) trimerises three molecules into benzene—a classic aliphatic-to-aromatic synthetic bridge.

English

6.1 Resonance in Benzene and Hückel's Rule

Benzene (\(\mathrm{C}_6\mathrm{H}_6\)) is a resonance hybrid of two Kekulé structures. X-ray diffraction confirms it is a planar regular hexagon with all six \(\mathrm{C–C}\) bond lengths equal to \(139\,\mathrm{pm}\) (intermediate between single \(154\,\mathrm{pm}\) and double \(133\,\mathrm{pm}\)). Delocalisation of six \(\pi\)-electrons over all six carbons gives benzene exceptional stability, explaining why it prefers electrophilic substitution (retaining aromaticity) over addition.

Hückel's Criteria for Aromaticity:
  1. The ring must be planar.
  2. There must be complete delocalisation of \(\pi\)-electrons around the ring (every ring atom conjugated).
  3. The ring must contain \((4n + 2)\,\pi\) electrons, where \(n = 0, 1, 2, \dots\) (i.e., \(2, 6, 10, 14, \dots\) \(\pi\)-electrons).
Examples: Benzene (\(6\pi\)), cyclopentadienyl anion (\(6\pi\)), cycloheptatrienyl (tropylium) cation (\(6\pi\)), naphthalene (\(10\pi\)), anthracene (\(14\pi\)).

6.2 Preparation and Electrophilic Substitution (\(S_E\)) Mechanism

Besides cyclic polymerisation of ethyne, benzene is prepared in the lab by decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda lime (\(\mathrm{NaOH/CaO}\)) or by reduction of phenol with heated \(\mathrm{Zn}\) dust.

Electrophilic aromatic substitution proceeds in three steps: (1) generation of electrophile \(\mathrm{E}^+\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{NO}_2^+\) from \(\mathrm{HNO}_3 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{SO}_4\), \(\mathrm{Cl}^+\) from \(\mathrm{Cl}_2 + \text{anhyd. }\mathrm{AlCl}_3\)), (2) attack of \(\mathrm{E}^+\) to form a resonance-stabilised \(\sigma\)-complex (arenium ion) containing one \(sp^3\) carbon, and (3) loss of \(\mathrm{H}^+\) from that \(sp^3\) carbon to restore aromaticity.

Example 1: Predicting Markovnikov and Peroxide-Mediated Addition

Problem (Assumed Scenario): Suppose 2-methylbut-2-ene reacts with \(\mathrm{HBr}\) under two separate conditions: (a) in the dark without peroxide, and (b) in the presence of benzoyl peroxide. Identify the major product in each case.

8. Common Pitfalls and Final Revision Checklist / সাধারণ ভুল ও চূড়ান্ত পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

Watch Out for These NEET UG Traps:
  • Peroxide Effect Scope: Anti-Markovnikov addition with peroxide works only for \(\mathrm{HBr}\). \(\mathrm{HCl}\) and \(\mathrm{HI}\) still follow Markovnikov's rule even in the presence of peroxides.
  • Halogen Orientation vs. Reactivity: Halogens (\(-\mathrm{Cl}, -\mathrm{Br}\)) on a benzene ring are ortho/para-directing (via \(+R\)), yet they deactivate the ring (via \(-I > +R\)).
  • Friedel–Crafts Carbocation Rearrangement: Treating benzene with 1-chloropropane and anhydrous \(\mathrm{AlCl}_3\) gives isopropylbenzene (cumene) as the major product—not n-propylbenzene—because the \(1^\circ\) propyl carbocation rearranges to the more stable \(2^\circ\) isopropyl carbocation.

Concise Final Recap

  • Alkanes undergo free-radical substitution; reactivity \(3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ\) H. Staggered ethane is more stable than eclipsed by \(12.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).
  • Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition; Baeyer's reagent and \(\mathrm{Br}_2/\mathrm{CCl}_4\) test for unsaturation; ozonolysis locates \(\mathrm{C=C}\).
  • Terminal alkynes are acidic; hydration gives ethanal from ethyne and ketones from higher alkynes.
  • Aromaticity requires planarity, full conjugation, and \((4n+2)\,\pi\) electrons; \(S_E\) reactions proceed via the arenium ion (\(\sigma\)-complex).
