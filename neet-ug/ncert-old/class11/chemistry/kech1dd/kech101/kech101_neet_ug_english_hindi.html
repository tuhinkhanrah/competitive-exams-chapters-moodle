1. Learning Objectives and Chapter Overview / अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं अध्याय अवलोकन

English

Chemistry is the scientific discipline that investigates the composition, structure, properties, and transformations of matter. Every macroscopic change—from rusting of iron to metabolic reactions in living cells—is governed by interactions among atoms and molecules. For NEET UG, this foundational chapter is critical because it establishes quantitative chemical reasoning: unit conversions, significant figures, the mole concept, stoichiometry, limiting reagents, and solution concentration terms that recur across physical and inorganic chemistry.

Learning Objectives

  • Classify matter by physical state (solid, liquid, gas) and chemical composition (pure substances vs. mixtures).
  • Apply SI base units, metric prefixes, temperature conversions, scientific notation, significant figures, and dimensional analysis.
  • State and apply the five fundamental laws of chemical combination and Dalton's atomic theory.
  • Calculate average atomic mass, molecular mass, formula mass, and molar mass using the \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) scale and Avogadro constant.
  • Determine percentage composition, empirical formula, and molecular formula from analytical data.
  • Solve stoichiometric problems involving limiting reagents and concentration units (mass %, mole fraction, molarity, and molality).

2. Nature and Classification of Matter / द्रव्य की प्रकृति एवं वर्गीकरण

English

Matter is anything that possesses mass and occupies space. At ordinary conditions, matter exists in three interconvertible physical states governed by the balance between intermolecular forces and thermal energy:

  • Solid: Constituent particles are packed tightly in an ordered lattice with minimal freedom of motion. Solids possess both a definite shape and a definite volume.
  • Liquid: Particles remain close together but can slide past one another. Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container (indefinite shape).
  • Gas: Particles are widely separated and move rapidly in random directions. Gases have neither definite shape nor definite volume, expanding to fill the entire container.
Tutorial Diagram: Microscopic Particle Packing in the Three States of Matter

At the macroscopic level, matter is divided into mixtures and pure substances:

  • Mixtures: Contain two or more pure substances (components) in any variable ratio. Their components can be separated by physical methods (filtration, distillation, crystallisation, hand-picking).
    • Homogeneous mixtures have uniform composition throughout (e.g., aqueous sugar solution, clean air).
    • Heterogeneous mixtures have non-uniform composition with visible boundaries (e.g., salt and sand, oil and water).
  • Pure Substances: Have fixed composition and cannot be separated by physical methods.
    • Elements consist of only one type of atom, existing as individual atoms (e.g., \(\mathrm{Na}\), \(\mathrm{Cu}\)) or homonuclear molecules (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2}\), \(\mathrm{O_2}\)).
    • Compounds form when atoms of two or more different elements combine in a fixed, definite ratio (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\)). A compound's properties differ completely from those of its constituent elements, and its components can only be separated by chemical methods.

3. Measurement, SI Units, and Uncertainty / मापन, SI मात्रक एवं अनिश्चितता

English

Physical properties (colour, melting point, density) are observed without changing a substance's chemical identity, whereas chemical properties (combustibility, acidity) require a chemical change. Quantitative measurements require a standardized system: the International System of Units (SI), built on seven base quantities.

Key Derived Quantities and Conversions

  • Mass vs. Weight: Mass is the constant amount of matter in an object; weight is the gravitational force acting on it and varies with gravity.
  • Volume and Density: \(1\,\mathrm{L} = 1000\,\mathrm{mL} = 1000\,\mathrm{cm^3} = 1\,\mathrm{dm^3} = 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{m^3}\). Density is mass per unit volume: \(\text{Density} = \frac{\text{Mass}}{\text{Volume}}\) (SI unit \(\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}\); laboratory unit \(\mathrm{g\,cm^{-3}}\)).
  • Temperature Scales: Negative temperatures are possible in Celsius (°C) and Fahrenheit (°F), but Kelvin (K) has no negative values.
    \[{}^{\circ}\mathrm{F} = \frac{9}{5}({}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}) + 32 \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathrm{K} = {}^{\circ}\mathrm{C} + 273.15\]
  • Common SI Prefixes: femto (\(\mathrm{f} = 10^{-15}\)), pico (\(\mathrm{p} = 10^{-12}\)), nano (\(\mathrm{n} = 10^{-9}\)), micro (\(\mathrm{µ} = 10^{-6}\)), milli (\(\mathrm{m} = 10^{-3}\)), centi (\(\mathrm{c} = 10^{-2}\)), deci (\(\mathrm{d} = 10^{-1}\)), kilo (\(\mathrm{k} = 10^{3}\)), mega (\(\mathrm{M} = 10^{6}\)), giga (\(\mathrm{G} = 10^{9}\)).

Scientific Notation, Significant Figures, Precision, and Accuracy

Extremely large or small values are written in scientific notation as \(N \times 10^n\), where \(1.000\dots \le N < 10\). For addition/subtraction, exponents must first be matched; for multiplication/division, exponents are added or subtracted.

Precision is the closeness of repeated measurements to each other, whereas accuracy is the agreement of a measurement with the true value. Uncertainty is expressed through significant figures (all certain digits plus one estimated digit):

  1. All non-zero digits are significant (e.g., \(285\) has 3).
  2. Leading zeros before the first non-zero digit are not significant (e.g., \(0.0052\) has 2).
  3. Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (e.g., \(2.005\) has 4).
  4. Trailing zeros to the right of a decimal point are significant (e.g., \(0.200\) has 3; \(500.0\) has 4), whereas trailing zeros in a whole number without a decimal point are ambiguous/not significant unless written in scientific notation.
  5. Exact counted numbers (e.g., 20 atoms) have infinite significant figures.

Calculation Rules: In addition/subtraction, keep as many decimal places as the term with the fewest decimal places. In multiplication/division, keep as many significant figures as the factor with the fewest significant figures. Dimensional analysis (unit factor method) converts units by multiplying by ratios equal to 1 (such as \(\frac{2.54\,\mathrm{cm}}{1\,\mathrm{in}}\)) so unwanted units cancel.

परिशुद्धता (Precision) एक ही राशि के विभिन्न मापों की परस्पर निकटता है, जबकि यथार्थता (Accuracy) किसी माप की वास्तविक मान से निकटता दर्शाती है। मापन की अनिश्चितता को सार्थक अंकों (Significant Figures) द्वारा व्यक्त किया जाता है:

4. Laws of Chemical Combination and Dalton's Atomic Theory / रासायनिक संयोजन के नियम एवं डाल्टन का परमाणु सिद्धांत

English
Law Core Statement Illustrative Example
Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier) Matter can neither be created nor destroyed in any physical or chemical change; total mass of reactants equals total mass of products. \(12\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{C}\) + \(32\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) \(\rightarrow 44\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{CO_2}\).
Law of Definite Proportions (Proust) A given chemical compound always contains the same elements combined in the exact same proportion by mass, regardless of source. Pure \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) from any source always has \(\mathrm{C}:\mathrm{O} = 12:32 = 3:8\) by mass.
Law of Multiple Proportions (Dalton) When two elements combine to form more than one compound, the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other bear a simple whole-number ratio. In \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) and \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}\), \(2\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{H}\) combines with \(16\,\mathrm{g}\) and \(32\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{O}\) respectively (\(1:2\)).
Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes At constant temperature and pressure, gases combine or are produced in simple whole-number ratios by volume. \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{H_2(g)}\) + \(50\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{O_2(g)}\) \(\rightarrow 100\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{H_2O(g)}\) (\(2:1:2\)).
Avogadro's Law Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. \(2\,\mathrm{L}\) \(\mathrm{H_2}\) has twice as many molecules as \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) at identical \(T, P\).

Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808): (1) Matter consists of indivisible atoms; (2) all atoms of a given element have identical properties and mass, while atoms of different elements differ in mass; (3) compounds form when atoms of different elements combine in fixed whole-number ratios; (4) chemical reactions rearrange atoms without creating or destroying them. Limitation: Dalton's theory could explain mass laws but could not explain Gay-Lussac's law of gaseous volumes or why atoms combine.

निश्चित अनुपात का नियम (प्राउस्ट) किसी यौगिक में तत्व सदैव द्रव्यमान के एक निश्चित अनुपात में विद्यमान होते हैं, चाहे वह किसी भी स्रोत से प्राप्त किया गया हो। किसी भी स्रोत से प्राप्त शुद्ध \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) में \(\mathrm{C}:\mathrm{O} = 3:8\) (द्रव्यमान से) होता है।
गुणित अनुपात का नियम (डाल्टन) यदि दो तत्व संयोजित होकर एक से अधिक यौगिक बनाते हैं, तो एक तत्व के साथ दूसरे तत्व के संयुक्त होने वाले द्रव्यमान छोटे पूर्णांकों के अनुपात में होते हैं। \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) और \(\mathrm{H_2O_2}\) में \(2\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{H}\) क्रमशः \(16\,\mathrm{g}\) व \(32\,\mathrm{g}\) \(\mathrm{O}\) से संयोग करता है (\(1:2\))।
गे-लुसाक का गैसीय आयतन का नियम समान ताप और दाब पर गैसें आयतन के सरल अनुपात में संयोग करती हैं या उत्पन्न होती हैं। \(100\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{H_2(g)}\) + \(50\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{O_2(g)}\) \(\rightarrow 100\,\mathrm{mL}\) \(\mathrm{H_2O(g)}\) (\(2:1:2\))।
आवोगाद्रो का नियम समान ताप और दाब पर सभी गैसों के समान आयतन में अणुओं की संख्या समान होती है। समान \(T, P\) पर \(2\,\mathrm{L}\) \(\mathrm{H_2}\) में \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) की तुलना में दोगुने अणु होते हैं।

डाल्टन का परमाणु सिद्धांत (1808): (1) द्रव्य अविभाज्य परमाणुओं से बना है; (2) एक ही तत्व के सभी परमाणुओं के गुण व द्रव्यमान समान होते हैं, जबकि भिन्न तत्वों के परमाणुओं के द्रव्यमान भिन्न होते हैं; (3) भिन्न तत्वों के परमाणु निश्चित अनुपात में संयोग कर यौगिक बनाते हैं; (4) रासायनिक अभिक्रियाओं में परमाणु केवल पुनर्व्यवस्थित होते हैं। सीमा: यह गैसीय आयतनों के नियम तथा परमाणुओं के संयोग के कारण की व्याख्या नहीं कर सका।

5. Atomic Mass, Molecular Mass, and the Mole Concept / परमाणु द्रव्यमान, आण्विक द्रव्यमान एवं मोल संकल्पना

English
  • Unified Atomic Mass Unit (\(\mathrm{u}\) or \(\mathrm{amu}\)): Defined as exactly \(\frac{1}{12}\) of the mass of one \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) atom: \(1\,\mathrm{u} = 1.66056 \times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{g}\).
  • Average Atomic Mass: Elements with isotopes have a weighted average atomic mass:
    \[\text{Average Atomic Mass} = \sum (\text{Fractional Abundance}_i \times \text{Isotopic Mass}_i)\]
  • Molecular Mass & Formula Mass: Molecular mass is the sum of atomic masses of all atoms in a molecule (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH_4} = 12.011 + 4(1.008) = 16.043\,\mathrm{u}\)). For ionic solids that form extended 3D lattices without discrete molecules (e.g., \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)), we use formula mass (\(23.0 + 35.5 = 58.5\,\mathrm{u}\)).
  • The Mole (\(\mathrm{mol}\)) & Avogadro Constant (\(N_A\)): One mole is the amount of substance containing exactly \(6.02214076 \times 10^{23}\) (commonly \(6.022 \times 10^{23}\)) elementary entities (atoms, molecules, ions, or electrons).
  • Molar Mass (\(M\)): The mass of \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of a substance in grams (\(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)). Numerically, molar mass in \(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\) equals the atomic, molecular, or formula mass in \(\mathrm{u}\).

  • मोलर द्रव्यमान (\(M\)): किसी पदार्थ के 1 मोल का ग्राम में द्रव्यमान (\(\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\))। इसका संख्यात्मक मान \(\mathrm{u}\) में व्यक्त परमाणु/आण्विक/सूत्र द्रव्यमान के बराबर होता है।

6. Percentage Composition, Empirical Formula, and Molecular Formula / प्रतिशत संघटन, मूलानुपाती सूत्र एवं आण्विक सूत्र

English
\[\text{Mass \% of an element} = \frac{\text{Mass of that element in 1 mol of compound}}{\text{Molar mass of compound}} \times 100\]

An empirical formula shows the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound, whereas a molecular formula shows the exact number of atoms of each element in a molecule.

Stepwise Method: (1) Assume \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) of compound so percentages become masses in grams; (2) divide each mass by its atomic mass to obtain moles; (3) divide all mole values by the smallest mole value (multiplying by a small integer if needed) to get the simplest whole-number ratio \(\rightarrow\) empirical formula; (4) compute \(n = \frac{\text{Molar mass}}{\text{Empirical formula mass}}\), then \(\text{Molecular formula} = (\text{Empirical formula})_n\).

7. Stoichiometry, Limiting Reagent, and Solution Concentration / रससमीकरणमिति, सीमांत अभिकर्मक एवं विलयन की सांद्रता

English

Stoichiometry deals with quantitative relationships between reactants and products in a balanced chemical equation. Never alter subscripts when balancing; adjust only stoichiometric coefficients.

Limiting Reagent: When reactants are mixed in non-stoichiometric proportions, the reactant that is completely consumed first limits the amount of product formed. Always identify the limiting reagent by comparing \(\frac{\text{Available moles}}{\text{Stoichiometric coefficient}}\) for each reactant—the smallest ratio identifies the limiting reagent.

Example 1: Calculating Average Atomic Mass from Isotopic Data

Problem (Assumed Data): A hypothetical element \(X\) has two naturally occurring isotopes: \(^{63}X\) (isotopic mass \(= 62.93\,\mathrm{u}\), abundance \(= 70.0\%\)) and \(^{65}X\) (isotopic mass \(= 64.93\,\mathrm{u}\), abundance \(= 30.0\%\)). Calculate the average atomic mass of \(X\).

Solution:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Average Atomic Mass} &= (0.700 \times 62.93\,\mathrm{u}) + (0.300 \times 64.93\,\mathrm{u}) \\ &= 44.051\,\mathrm{u} + 19.479\,\mathrm{u} = 63.53\,\mathrm{u} \end{aligned} \]

Example 2: Determining Empirical and Molecular Formulas

Problem (Assumed Data): An organic compound contains \(40.0\%\) carbon, \(6.67\%\) hydrogen, and \(53.33\%\) oxygen by mass. If its molar mass is \(180\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), determine its empirical and molecular formulas.

Solution: Assuming a \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) sample:

  • Moles of \(\mathrm{C} = \frac{40.0}{12.0} = 3.33\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Moles of \(\mathrm{H} = \frac{6.67}{1.0} = 6.67\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Moles of \(\mathrm{O} = \frac{53.33}{16.0} = 3.33\,\mathrm{mol}\)

\(n = \frac{180}{30} = 6 \implies \text{Molecular formula} = (\mathrm{CH_2O})_6 = \mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}\).

Example 3: Identifying the Limiting Reagent and Product Yield

Problem (Assumed Data): In the reaction \(2\mathrm{SO_2(g)} + \mathrm{O_2(g)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{SO_3(g)}\), \(32.0\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{SO_2}\) is mixed with \(12.8\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{O_2}\). Identify the limiting reagent and calculate the mass of \(\mathrm{SO_3}\) formed. (Molar masses: \(\mathrm{SO_2} = 64.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), \(\mathrm{O_2} = 32.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), \(\mathrm{SO_3} = 80.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)).

Solution:

  • Moles of \(\mathrm{SO_2} = \frac{32.0}{64.0} = 0.50\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Moles of \(\mathrm{O_2} = \frac{12.8}{32.0} = 0.40\,\mathrm{mol}\)
  • Ratio check: \(\frac{0.50}{2} = 0.25\) for \(\mathrm{SO_2}\), and \(\frac{0.40}{1} = 0.40\) for \(\mathrm{O_2}\). Since \(0.25 < 0.40\), \(\mathrm{SO_2}\) is the limiting reagent.
  • From stoichiometry, \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\,\mathrm{SO_2}\) produces \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\,\mathrm{SO_3}\), so \(0.50\,\mathrm{mol}\,\mathrm{SO_2}\) produces \(0.50\,\mathrm{mol}\,\mathrm{SO_3}\).
  • Mass of \(\mathrm{SO_3} = 0.50\,\mathrm{mol} \times 80.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}} = 40.0\,\mathrm{g}\).

Example 4: Interconverting Molarity and Molality Using Density

Problem (Assumed Data): A \(2.0\,\mathrm{M}\) aqueous solution of urea (\(\mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}\), molar mass \(= 60.0\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) has a density of \(1.12\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}\). Calculate its molality.

Solution: Consider \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) (\(1000\,\mathrm{mL}\)) of solution:

  • Moles of urea \(= 2.0\,\mathrm{mol}\); Mass of urea \(= 2.0 \times 60.0 = 120.0\,\mathrm{g}\).

  • (D) (A) असत्य है, किंतु (R) सत्य है।

उत्तर: (B)

हल: अभिकथन (A) परिशुद्धता और यथार्थता की सही परिभाषा देता है। कारण (R) भी सत्य है (त्रुटिपूर्ण उपकरण से बार-बार लिए गए माप परस्पर निकट किंतु वास्तविक मान से दूर हो सकते हैं), परंतु यह (A) में दी गई परिभाषा की व्याख्या नहीं करता।

10. Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावृत्ति जाँच-सूची

English

Common Pitfalls to Avoid in NEET UG

  • Confusing Molarity and Molality: Molarity uses litres of solution (temperature-dependent), whereas molality uses kilograms of solvent (temperature-independent).
  • Assuming the Smaller Mass is the Limiting Reagent: Always compare \(\frac{\text{moles}}{\text{stoichiometric coefficient}}\), never raw masses in grams.
  • Misinterpreting Trailing Zeros: \(0.0200\) has 3 significant figures (leading zeros do not count; trailing zeros after the decimal do).
  • Forgetting Atomic vs. Molecular Entities: \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) contains \(N_A\) molecules but \(2N_A\) oxygen atoms.

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Can I convert °C to K and °F without error?
  • Do I know all seven SI base units and common prefixes (\(\mathrm{p}, \mathrm{n}, \mathrm{µ}, \mathrm{m}, \mathrm{k}\))?
  • Can I derive an empirical formula and scale it to a molecular formula?
  • Can I convert molarity to molality when density is given?
हिन्दी (Hindi)

NEET UG में बचने योग्य सामान्य त्रुटियाँ

  • मोलरता और मोललता में भ्रम: मोलरता में विलयन का आयतन (लीटर में) प्रयुक्त होता है (ताप पर निर्भर), जबकि मोललता में विलायक का द्रव्यमान (kg में) प्रयुक्त होता है (ताप से स्वतंत्र)।
  • कम द्रव्यमान को सीमांत अभिकर्मक मान लेना: सदैव \(\frac{\text{मोल}}{\text{गुणांक}}\) के अनुपात की तुलना करें, ग्राम में द्रव्यमान की नहीं।
  • शून्य के सार्थक अंक: \(0.0200\) में 3 सार्थक अंक हैं (प्रारंभिक शून्य नहीं गिने जाते, दशमलव के बाद अंत के शून्य गिने जाते हैं)।
  • परमाणु बनाम अणु: \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) में \(N_A\) अणु होते हैं, परंतु \(2N_A\) ऑक्सीजन परमाणु होते हैं।

त्वरित पुनरावृत्ति जाँच-सूची

  • क्या मैं °C को K और °F में त्रुटिरहित बदल सकता हूँ?
  • क्या मुझे सभी सात SI मूल मात्रक और प्रमुख उपसर्ग (\(\mathrm{p}, \mathrm{n}, \mathrm{µ}, \mathrm{m}, \mathrm{k}\)) याद हैं?
  • क्या मैं मूलानुपाती और आण्विक सूत्र की गणना कर सकता हूँ?
  • क्या मैं घनत्व दिए होने पर मोलरता को मोललता में बदल सकता हूँ?

11. Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Chemistry studies matter—classified into pure substances (elements and compounds) and mixtures (homogeneous and heterogeneous). Quantitative work relies on SI base units, scientific notation, significant figures, and dimensional analysis. Chemical reactions obey the five laws of combination and are quantified using the mole (\(N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}\,\mathrm{mol^{-1}}\)), molar mass, empirical/molecular formulas, limiting reagents, and temperature-dependent (\(M\)) or temperature-independent (\(m, x_A, \mathrm{w/w\%}\)) concentration terms.
