1. Learning Objectives and Core Intuition

English

Under ordinary conditions, noble gases exist as free monatomic species, whereas almost all other elements combine to form molecules or extended ionic lattices. A chemical bond is the net attractive force holding atoms or ions together in a stable chemical species. Bonding occurs because the resulting arrangement has lower potential energy than the separated atoms.

After completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Apply the Kössel–Lewis electronic approach, draw Lewis structures, calculate formal charges, and identify exceptions to the octet rule.
  • Explain ionic bond formation in terms of ionization enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, lattice enthalpy, and Fajan's rules for polarization.
  • Compare bond length, bond angle, bond enthalpy, bond order, resonance stabilization, and molecular dipole moments.
  • Predict molecular geometries and bond-angle distortions using VSEPR theory and orbital hybridisation (\(sp\), \(sp^2\), \(sp^3\), \(sp^3d\), \(sp^3d^2\), \(dsp^2\)).
  • Construct molecular orbital (MO) configurations for homonuclear diatomic species (\(B_2\) to \(\mathrm{Ne}_2\) and their ions) to determine bond order, stability, and magnetic character.
  • Distinguish between intermolecular and intramolecular hydrogen bonding and their physical consequences.

2. Kössel–Lewis Approach, Lewis Structures, and Formal Charge

English

Kössel and Lewis (1916) explained chemical combination using the stability of the noble-gas valence configuration (\(ns^2np^6\), or \(1s^2\) duplet for helium). According to the octet rule, atoms achieve stability by transferring electrons (forming ionic/electrovalent bonds) or sharing electron pairs (forming covalent bonds) until each valence shell holds eight electrons.

  • Lewis Symbols: Dots around an elemental symbol represent valence electrons. Group valence is generally equal to the number of dots (for 1–4 valence electrons) or \(8 - (\text{number of valence electrons})\).
  • Steps to Draw Lewis Structures:
    1. Sum the valence electrons of all atoms; add 1 electron per unit negative charge (anions) or subtract 1 electron per unit positive charge (cations).
    2. Place the least electronegative atom (never H or F) at the central position and connect terminal atoms with single bonds.
    3. Complete octets of terminal atoms first (duplet for H), then place remaining electrons on the central atom. If the central atom lacks an octet, convert lone pairs on terminal atoms into double or triple bonds.
Formal Charge Formula: \[\text{F.C.} = V - L - \frac{1}{2}B\] where \(V\) = valence electrons in the free atom, \(L\) = non-bonding (lone pair) electrons on the atom, and \(B\) = total shared (bonding) electrons around the atom. Among competing Lewis structures, the one with minimal formal charges and negative formal charge on the more electronegative atom is the lowest-energy contributor.

Limitations of the Octet Rule

  • Incomplete Octet (< 8 electrons): Central atoms with fewer than 4 valence electrons, e.g., \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\) (4 e⁻), \(\mathrm{BF_3}\) (6 e⁻), \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\) (6 e⁻).
  • Odd-Electron Molecules: Molecules with an odd total valence electron count, e.g., \(\mathrm{NO}\) (11 valence e⁻) and \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) (17 valence e⁻).
  • Expanded Octet (> 8 electrons): Period 3 and heavier elements can utilize \(d\) orbitals, e.g., \(\mathrm{PF_5}\) (10 e⁻), \(\mathrm{SF_6}\) (12 e⁻), \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) (12 e⁻ around S).
  • Other Drawbacks: Noble gases such as Xe and Kr form compounds (\(\mathrm{XeF_2}\), \(\mathrm{XeOF_2}\), \(\mathrm{KrF_2}\)). The octet rule also fails to predict molecular shapes or relative bond energies.

২. কোসেল–লুইস তত্ত্ব, লুইস গঠন এবং ফরমাল চার্জ

কোসেল ও লুইস (১৯১৬) নিষ্ক্রিয় গ্যাসের সুস্থিত যোজ্যতা কক্ষের ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাসের (\(ns^2np^6\), অথবা হিলিয়ামের ক্ষেত্রে \(1s^2\) দ্বৈত) ভিত্তিতে রাসায়নিক সংযোগ ব্যাখ্যা করেন। অষ্টক সূত্র (Octet Rule) অনুসারে, পরমাণুগুলি ইলেকট্রন আদান-প্রদান (আয়নীয় বন্ধন) অথবা ইলেকট্রন জোড় শেয়ার করার (সমযোজী বন্ধন) মাধ্যমে যোজ্যতা কক্ষে ৮টি ইলেকট্রন পূর্ণ করে স্থায়িত্ব লাভ করে।

  • লুইস প্রতীক: মৌলের চিহ্নের চারপাশে বিন্দু দিয়ে যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন দেখানো হয়। মৌলের শ্রেণি-যোজ্যতা সাধারণত বিন্দু সংখ্যার সমান অথবা \(8 - (\text{যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা})\) হয়।
  • লুইস গঠন আঁকার ধাপসমূহ:
    1. সব পরমাণুর মোট যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন যোগ করুন; অ্যানায়নের প্রতিটি ঋণাত্মক আধানের জন্য ১টি ইলেকট্রন যোগ এবং ক্যাটায়নের প্রতিটি ধনাত্মক আধানের জন্য ১টি ইলেকট্রন বিয়োগ করুন।
    2. সবচেয়ে কম তড়িৎঋণাত্মক পরমাণুটিকে (কখনোই H বা F নয়) কেন্দ্রে বসিয়ে প্রান্তীয় পরমাণুগুলির সঙ্গে একক বন্ধন দ্বারা যুক্ত করুন।
    3. প্রথমে প্রান্তীয় পরমাণুগুলির অষ্টক (H-এর দ্বৈত) পূর্ণ করুন, তারপর অবশিষ্ট ইলেকট্রন কেন্দ্রীয় পরমাণুতে রাখুন। কেন্দ্রীয় পরমাণুতে অষ্টক পূর্ণ না হলে প্রান্তীয় পরমাণুর নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড়কে দ্বি-বন্ধন বা ত্রি-বন্ধনে পরিণত করুন।
ফরমাল চার্জের সূত্র (Formal Charge): \[\text{F.C.} = V - L - \frac{1}{2}B\] এখানে \(V\) = মুক্ত পরমাণুর যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন, \(L\) = নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় (অবন্ধন) ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা, এবং \(B\) = মোট বন্ধন (শেয়ার করা) ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা। একাধিক লুইস গঠনের মধ্যে যে গঠনে ফরমাল চার্জ ন্যূনতম এবং ঋণাত্মক চার্জ অধিক তড়িৎঋণাত্মক পরমাণুতে থাকে, সেটিই সবচেয়ে স্থায়ী।

  • অসম্পূর্ণ অষ্টক (< ৮টি ইলেকট্রন): ৪টির কম যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রনযুক্ত কেন্দ্রীয় পরমাণু, যেমন \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\) (৪টি e⁻), \(\mathrm{BF_3}\) (৬টি e⁻), \(\mathrm{AlCl_3}\) (৬টি e⁻)।
  • অযুগ্ম-ইলেকট্রন অণু: যেসব অণুতে মোট যোজ্যতা ইলেকট্রন সংখ্যা বিজোড়, যেমন \(\mathrm{NO}\) (১১টি যোজ্যতা e⁻) এবং \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) (১৭টি যোজ্যতা e⁻)।
  • প্রসারিত অষ্টক (> ৮টি ইলেকট্রন): তৃতীয় পর্যায় ও পরবর্তী মৌলগুলি \(d\)-কক্ষক ব্যবহার করতে পারে, যেমন \(\mathrm{PF_5}\) (১০টি e⁻), \(\mathrm{SF_6}\) (১২টি e⁻), \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) (S-এর চারপাশে ১২টি e⁻)।
  • অন্যান্য ত্রুটি: Xe ও Kr-এর মতো নিষ্ক্রিয় গ্যাস যৌগ গঠন করে (\(\mathrm{XeF_2}\), \(\mathrm{XeOF_2}\), \(\mathrm{KrF_2}\))। এছাড়া অষ্টক সূত্র অণুর জ্যামিতিক আকৃতি বা বন্ধন শক্তি ব্যাখ্যা করতে পারে না।

3. Ionic Bonding, Lattice Enthalpy, and Fajan's Rules

English

An ionic (electrovalent) bond forms through electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions produced by electron transfer. Favourable conditions include: (i) low ionization enthalpy (\(\Delta_i H\)) of the metal, (ii) high negative electron gain enthalpy (\(\Delta_{eg} H\)) of the non-metal, and (iii) high lattice enthalpy.

Lattice Enthalpy is the energy required to completely separate one mole of a solid ionic crystal into its gaseous constituent ions. Even when \(\Delta_i H + \Delta_{eg} H > 0\) (e.g., for \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), \(+495.8 - 348.7 = +147.1\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)), the large exothermic enthalpy of lattice formation (\(-788\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)) makes the overall crystal formation strongly favourable.

Fajan's Rules (Covalent Character in Ionic Bonds)

No bond is 100% ionic. A small, highly charged cation distorts (polarizes) the electron cloud of a large anion, pulling electron density between the nuclei and imparting covalent character. Covalent character increases with:

  • Smaller cation size and larger anion size (higher polarizing power and higher polarizability).
  • Higher magnitude of charge on the cation and/or anion.
  • Pseudo-noble-gas configuration \((n-1)d^{10}ns^0\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{Cu^+}\), \(\mathrm{Ag^+}\), \(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}\)), which shields nuclear charge poorly and polarizes anions more strongly than a noble-gas \(ns^2np^6\) cation of comparable size and charge.

৩. আয়নীয় বন্ধন, ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি এবং ফাজানের নিয়ম

ইলেকট্রন স্থানান্তরের ফলে সৃষ্ট বিপরীত আধানযুক্ত ক্যাটায়ন ও অ্যানায়নের মধ্যে স্থিরতড়িৎ আকর্ষণ বলের মাধ্যমে আয়নীয় বা তড়িৎযোজী বন্ধন গঠিত হয়। এর অনুকূল শর্তগুলি হলো: (i) ধাতুর নিম্ন আয়নীয়ন এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_i H\)), (ii) অধাতুর উচ্চ ঋণাত্মক ইলেকট্রন গ্রহণ এনথ্যালপি (\(\Delta_{eg} H\)), এবং (iii) উচ্চ ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি।

ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি: এক মোল কঠিন আয়নীয় কেলাসকে সম্পূর্ণরূপে পৃথক করে গ্যাসীয় আয়নে পরিণত করতে যে শক্তির প্রয়োজন হয়। \(\Delta_i H + \Delta_{eg} H > 0\) হলেও (যেমন \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে \(+495.8 - 348.7 = +147.1\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)), কেলাস গঠনের সময় নির্গত বিপুল ল্যাটিস শক্তি (\(-788\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)) সামগ্রিক প্রক্রিয়াটিকে স্থায়ী করে।

ফাজানের নিয়ম (আয়নীয় বন্ধনে সমযোজী চরিত্র)

কোনো বন্ধনই ১০০% আয়নীয় নয়। ক্ষুদ্র ও উচ্চ আধানযুক্ত ক্যাটায়ন বৃহৎ অ্যানায়নের ইলেকট্রন মেঘকে বিকৃত (ধ্রুবায়িত) করে, ফলে দুই নিউক্লিয়াসের মাঝে ইলেকট্রন ঘনত্ব বাড়ে এবং সমযোজী চরিত্র সৃষ্টি হয়। সমযোজী চরিত্র বৃদ্ধি পায় যখন:

  • ক্যাটায়নের আকার ছোট এবং অ্যানায়নের আকার বড় হয়।
  • ক্যাটায়ন বা অ্যানায়নের আধানের মান বেশি হয়।
  • ক্যাটায়নের ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস \((n-1)d^{10}ns^0\) প্রকৃতির (ছদ্ম-নিষ্ক্রিয় গ্যাস বিন্যাস, যেমন \(\mathrm{Cu^+}\), \(\mathrm{Ag^+}\), \(\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}\)) হয়, কারণ \(d\)-ইলেকট্রনের আবরণী ক্ষমতা কম হওয়ায় একই আকার ও আধানের \(ns^2np^6\) ক্যাটায়নের চেয়ে এদের ধ্রুবায়ন ক্ষমতা বেশি হয়।

English
Parameter Definition & Key Distinction NEET UG Trend
Bond Length Equilibrium internuclear distance between two bonded atoms. For a homonuclear single bond, covalent radius \(r_{\text{cov}} = d/2\); van der Waals radius \(r_{\text{vdw}}\) is half the distance between non-bonded atoms in adjacent molecules. \(r_{\text{vdw}} > r_{\text{cov}}\). Bond length decreases as bond order increases: \(\text{C–C}\ (154\ \text{pm}) > \text{C=C}\ (133\ \text{pm}) > \text{C≡C}\ (120\ \text{pm})\).
Bond Enthalpy Energy required to break 1 mole of a specific gaseous bond. In polyatomic molecules (e.g., \(\mathrm{H_2O}\)), successive O–H bond dissociation energies differ (\(502\) and \(427\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)), so mean bond enthalpy (\(464.5\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)) is used. Higher bond order \(\Rightarrow\) higher bond enthalpy and stronger bond.
Bond Order Number of covalent bonds between two atoms in a Lewis structure (or \(\frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)\) in MO theory). Isoelectronic species have identical bond orders (e.g., \(\mathrm{N_2}\), \(\mathrm{CO}\), \(\mathrm{NO^+}\) all have B.O. = 3).

Resonance

When a single Lewis structure cannot match experimental bond lengths (for instance, in \(\mathrm{O_3}\), both O–O bonds are equal at \(128\ \mathrm{pm}\), intermediate between single \(148\ \mathrm{pm}\) and double \(121\ \mathrm{pm}\) bonds), the molecule is represented as a resonance hybrid of two or more canonical forms having identical nuclear positions and similar energies.

Crucial Misconceptions to Avoid: Canonical structures are hypothetical and do not exist independently. The molecule does not oscillate or stay in equilibrium between canonical forms (unlike keto–enol tautomerism). The resonance hybrid is a single real structure with lower energy (resonance stabilization) than any individual canonical form.

Bond Polarity and Dipole Moment

A polar covalent bond arises when shared electrons shift toward the more electronegative atom. The dipole moment (\(\mu\)) is a vector quantity defined as:

\[\mu = Q \times r \qquad \text{with } 1\ \mathrm{D} = 3.33564 \times 10^{-30}\ \mathrm{C\ m}\]

In chemistry, the dipole arrow (\(\longmapsto\) or crossed arrow) points from the positive end (cross) toward the negative end (head). For polyatomic molecules, the net dipole moment is the vector sum of bond dipoles and lone-pair orbital dipoles:

  • Zero dipole moment due to symmetry: \(\mathrm{BeF_2}\) (linear, \(180^\circ\)), \(\mathrm{BF_3}\) (trigonal planar, \(120^\circ\)), \(\mathrm{CH_4}\) and \(\mathrm{CCl_4}\) (regular tetrahedral), \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) (linear).
  • \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) vs. \(\mathrm{NF_3}\): Both are trigonal pyramidal. In \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (\(\mu = 1.47\ \mathrm{D}\)), the orbital dipole of the lone pair reinforces the resultant of the three N–H bond dipoles. In \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) (\(\mu = 0.23\ \mathrm{D}\)), the lone-pair dipole opposes the resultant of the three N–F bond dipoles, sharply reducing the net dipole moment.

5. VSEPR Theory and Molecular Geometry

English

Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion (VSEPR) theory predicts molecular shapes from the repulsion among valence-shell electron pairs around the central atom. Electron pairs arrange themselves as far apart as possible to minimize repulsion. Multiple bonds (double or triple) are treated as a single electron domain ("super pair").

Repulsion Hierarchy: \[\text{Lone pair (lp)–Lone pair (lp)} > \text{Lone pair (lp)–Bond pair (bp)} > \text{Bond pair (bp)–Bond pair (bp)}\]

Because lone pairs are localized on the central atom rather than shared between two nuclei, they occupy more angular space and compress adjacent bond angles (e.g., tetrahedral \(109.5^\circ\) compresses to \(107^\circ\) in \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) with 1 lp, and to \(104.5^\circ\) in \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) with 2 lp).

6. Valence Bond Theory and Orbital Hybridisation

English

Valence Bond (VB) Theory explains covalent bond formation through the partial interpenetration (overlap) of half-filled atomic orbitals containing electrons with opposite spins. As two H atoms approach, attractive forces (nucleus–electron) outweigh repulsive forces until the system reaches a potential energy minimum at the equilibrium bond length (\(74\ \mathrm{pm}\), bond enthalpy \(435.8\ \mathrm{kJ\ mol^{-1}}\)).

  • Directional & Phase Criterion: Only positive (in-phase) overlap produces a stable bond; out-of-phase (negative) or zero overlap (orthogonal orbitals such as \(s\) and \(p_x\) along the \(z\)-axis) cannot form a bond.
  • Sigma (\(\sigma\)) Bond: Formed by head-on (axial) overlap along the internuclear axis (\(s\text{–}s\), \(s\text{–}p\), or \(p\text{–}p\)). Greater overlap makes \(\sigma\) bonds stronger than \(\pi\) bonds and allows free rotation.
  • Pi (\(\pi\)) Bond: Formed by sidewise (lateral) overlap of parallel \(p\) orbitals perpendicular to the internuclear axis. A \(\pi\) bond forms only alongside a \(\sigma\) bond in multiple bonds and restricts rotation.

Hybridisation of Atomic Orbitals

Hybridisation is the intermixing of valence orbitals of slightly different energies on the same atom to produce an equal number of equivalent hybrid orbitals oriented for minimum repulsion. Both half-filled and completely filled valence orbitals can hybridise, and prior electron promotion is not mandatory (e.g., \(\mathrm{NH_3}\), \(\mathrm{H_2O}\)).

7. Molecular Orbital (MO) Theory

English

Developed by Hund and Mulliken, Molecular Orbital (MO) Theory treats electrons as occupying polycentric molecular orbitals formed by the Linear Combination of Atomic Orbitals (LCAO): \(\psi_{\text{MO}} = \psi_A \pm \psi_B\).

  • Bonding MO (\(\sigma, \pi\)): Constructive interference (\(\psi_A + \psi_B\)) concentrates electron density between nuclei, lowering energy below that of the parent atomic orbitals.
  • Antibonding MO (\(\sigma^*, \pi^*\)): Destructive interference (\(\psi_A - \psi_B\)) creates a nodal plane between nuclei, raising energy above that of the parent atomic orbitals.
  • Conditions for LCAO: Combining orbitals must have comparable energy, identical symmetry about the internuclear (\(z\)) axis, and significant overlap.

Critical Energy Ordering (2s–2p Mixing)

  • Up to 14 electrons (\(\mathrm{Li_2, Be_2, B_2, C_2, N_2}\)): Due to \(2s\text{–}2p\) mixing, \(\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y\) lies below \(\sigma 2p_z\): \[\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z\]
  • More than 14 electrons (\(\mathrm{O_2, F_2, Ne_2}\)): \(\sigma 2p_z\) lies below \(\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y\): \[\sigma 1s < \sigma^* 1s < \sigma 2s < \sigma^* 2s < \sigma 2p_z < (\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y) < (\pi^* 2p_x = \pi^* 2p_y) < \sigma^* 2p_z\]
Bond Order and Stability: \[\text{Bond Order} = \frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)\] where \(N_b\) = bonding electrons and \(N_a\) = antibonding electrons. A species is stable if B.O. > 0 and unstable/non-existent if B.O. \(\le 0\) (e.g., \(\mathrm{He_2}\), \(\mathrm{Be_2}\), \(\mathrm{Ne_2}\)). Higher bond order means higher bond dissociation enthalpy and shorter bond length.

Key Exam Highlights: (1) \(\mathrm{O_2}\) has 2 unpaired electrons in \(\pi^* 2p_x^1 \pi^* 2p_y^1\), explaining its paramagnetism (B.O. = 2). (2) \(\mathrm{B_2}\) is also paramagnetic (2 unpaired electrons in \(\pi 2p_x^1 \pi 2p_y^1\), B.O. = 1). (3) \(\mathrm{C_2}\) (B.O. = 2, diamagnetic) has a double bond composed of two \(\pi\) bonds (\(\pi 2p_x^2 \pi 2p_y^2\)) rather than one \(\sigma\) and one \(\pi\).

৭. আণবিক কক্ষক তত্ত্ব (MOT)

পারমাণবিক কক্ষকের রৈখিক সমবায় (LCAO: \(\psi_{\text{MO}} = \psi_A \pm \psi_B\)) দ্বারা বন্ধন (\(\sigma, \pi\)) ও প্রতিবন্ধন (\(\sigma^*, \pi^*\)) আণবিক কক্ষক গঠিত হয়।

  • ১৪ বা তার কম ইলেকট্রন (\(\mathrm{B_2, C_2, N_2}\)): \(\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y\) কক্ষকের শক্তি \(\sigma 2p_z\)-এর নিচে থাকে।
  • ১৪-এর বেশি ইলেকট্রন (\(\mathrm{O_2, F_2, Ne_2}\)): \(\sigma 2p_z\) কক্ষকের শক্তি \(\pi 2p_x = \pi 2p_y\)-এর নিচে থাকে।
বন্ধন ক্রম (Bond Order): \[\text{Bond Order} = \frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)\] \(\text{B.O.} \le 0\) হলে অণু অস্থায়ী (যেমন \(\mathrm{He_2, Be_2, Ne_2}\))। \(\mathrm{O_2}\)-তে \(\pi^*2p\)-তে ২টি অযুগ্ম ইলেকট্রন থাকায় এটি পরাচুম্বকীয় (paramagnetic)। \(\mathrm{C_2}\)-এর দ্বি-বন্ধন দুটি \(\pi\)-বন্ধন দ্বারা গঠিত এবং এটি তিরশ্চুম্বকীয় (diamagnetic)।

8. Hydrogen Bonding

English

A hydrogen bond is the attractive electrostatic interaction between a covalently bonded hydrogen atom (attached to a strongly electronegative atom F, O, or N) and another electronegative atom (F, O, or N) carrying a lone pair. Its strength is weaker than a covalent bond but stronger than van der Waals forces, and is maximum in the solid state and minimum in the gas phase.

  • Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding: Occurs between two separate molecules of the same or different compounds (e.g., \(\mathrm{HF}\), \(\mathrm{H_2O}\), \(\mathrm{NH_3}\), alcohols). It raises boiling point, viscosity, and water solubility.
  • Intramolecular Hydrogen Bonding: Occurs within the same molecule when H lies between two electronegative atoms forming a stable ring (e.g., o-nitrophenol). It lowers boiling point and increases steam volatility compared to its para isomer.

Calculating Formal Charge and Selecting the Most Stable Lewis Structure of \(\mathrm{SCN^-}\)

Problem: The thiocyanate ion, \(\mathrm{SCN^-}\), has three possible resonance structures with C as the central atom: (I) \([:\ddot{\mathrm{S}}=\mathrm{C}=\ddot{\mathrm{N}}:]^-\), (II) \([:\stackrel{\cdot\cdot}{\mathrm{S}}-\mathrm{C}\equiv\mathrm{N}:]^-\), and (III) \([:\mathrm{S}\equiv\mathrm{C}-\ddot{\mathrm{N}}:]^-\). Determine the formal charges on S, C, and N in each structure and identify the major contributor. (Total valence electrons assumed from standard group numbers: S = 6, C = 4, N = 5, plus 1 for negative charge = 16 e⁻.)

Solution: Using \(\text{F.C.} = V - L - \frac{1}{2}B\):

  • Structure I (\(\mathrm{S=C=N}\)): \(\text{F.C.(S)} = 6 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = 0\); \(\text{F.C.(C)} = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0\); \(\text{F.C.(N)} = 5 - 4 - \frac{1}{2}(4) = -1\).
  • Structure II (\(\mathrm{S-C\equiv N}\)): \(\text{F.C.(S)} = 6 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -1\); \(\text{F.C.(C)} = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0\); \(\text{F.C.(N)} = 5 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = 0\).
  • Structure III (\(\mathrm{S\equiv C-N}\)): \(\text{F.C.(S)} = 6 - 2 - \frac{1}{2}(6) = +1\); \(\text{F.C.(C)} = 4 - 0 - \frac{1}{2}(8) = 0\); \(\text{F.C.(N)} = 5 - 6 - \frac{1}{2}(2) = -2\).

Conclusion: Structure III is disfavoured because it has large formal charges (\(+1, -2\)) and puts a positive charge on S. Between I and II, both have minimal charges (\(0, 0, -1\)), but Structure I places the \(-1\) formal charge on the more electronegative nitrogen atom (\(\chi_{\mathrm{N}} > \chi_{\mathrm{S}}\)), making Structure I the most stable contributor.

Predicting Hybridisation and Geometry of \(\mathrm{XeOF_4}\)

Problem: Determine the hybridisation of the central xenon atom, number of lone pairs, and molecular shape of \(\mathrm{XeOF_4}\).

Solution:

  1. Valence electrons of Xe = 8. Four F atoms form 4 single \(\sigma\) bonds (4 electrons used), and one O atom forms 1 \(\sigma\) + 1 \(\pi\) double bond (2 electrons of Xe used).

Applying Fajan's Rules to Compare Covalent Character

Problem: Arrange the chlorides \(\mathrm{LiCl}\), \(\mathrm{BeCl_2}\), and \(\mathrm{BCl_3}\) in increasing order of covalent character, and explain why \(\mathrm{CuCl}\) has higher covalent character than \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) despite nearly similar ionic radii (hypothetical comparison assuming \(r_{\mathrm{Cu^+}} \approx r_{\mathrm{Na^+}}\)).

Solution:

  1. Across \(\mathrm{Li^+}\), \(\mathrm{Be^{2+}}\), and \(\mathrm{B^{3+}}\), the cation charge increases (\(+1 \to +2 \to +3\)) while cation size decreases, increasing polarizing power on \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\). Therefore, covalent character increases as: \[\mathrm{LiCl} < \mathrm{BeCl_2} < \mathrm{BCl_3}\]
  2. \(\mathrm{Cu^+}\) has a \(3d^{10}\) pseudo-noble-gas outer configuration, whereas \(\mathrm{Na^+}\) has a \(2s^2 2p^6\) noble-gas configuration. Because \(d\) electrons shield nuclear charge poorly, \(\mathrm{Cu^+}\) exerts a stronger effective nuclear attraction on the \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) electron cloud, giving \(\mathrm{CuCl}\) greater covalent character than \(\mathrm{NaCl}\).

Practice Question 1 (Single-Correct MCQ): Which of the following molecules has a non-zero dipole moment?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{BeF_2}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{BF_3}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{NF_3}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{CCl_4}\)

Answer: (C) \(\mathrm{NF_3}\)

Worked Solution: \(\mathrm{BeF_2}\) (linear), \(\mathrm{BF_3}\) (trigonal planar), and \(\mathrm{CCl_4}\) (regular tetrahedral) are completely symmetrical, so their individual bond dipoles cancel out to zero (\(\mu = 0\)). \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) has a trigonal pyramidal geometry with 1 lone pair on N; the resultant of the three N–F bond dipoles is not cancelled by the opposite lone-pair dipole, leaving a net dipole moment (\(\mu = 0.23\ \mathrm{D}\)).

Practice Question 2 (Single-Correct MCQ): According to Molecular Orbital Theory, which of the following species is diamagnetic and has a double bond consisting of two \(\pi\) bonds only?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{B_2}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{C_2}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{O_2}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{N_2}\)

Answer: (B) \(\mathrm{C_2}\)

Worked Solution: \(\mathrm{C_2}\) has 12 electrons with configuration \(KK(\sigma 2s)^2(\sigma^* 2s)^2(\pi 2p_x^2 = \pi 2p_y^2)\). All electrons are paired (diamagnetic), bond order is \(\frac{1}{2}(8 - 4) = 2\), and all four bonding valence electrons occupy \(\pi 2p\) orbitals, meaning the double bond consists of two \(\pi\) bonds.

Practice Question 3 (Single-Correct MCQ): What is the hybridisation of the central atom and the molecular shape of \(\mathrm{ClF_3}\)?

  • (A) \(sp^3\), trigonal pyramidal
  • (B) \(sp^3d\), T-shaped
  • (C) \(sp^3d\), trigonal planar
  • (D) \(sp^2\), bent

Answer: (B) \(sp^3d\), T-shaped

Worked Solution: Chlorine has 7 valence electrons; 3 form \(\sigma\) bonds with F atoms, leaving 4 electrons (2 lone pairs). Steric number = \(3 + 2 = 5 \Rightarrow sp^3d\) hybridisation. Both lone pairs occupy equatorial positions of the trigonal bipyramid to minimize repulsion, yielding a T-shaped geometry.

Practice Question 4 (Statement-Based MCQ): Read the following two statements carefully:

Statement I: In \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\), the axial P–Cl bonds are longer and weaker than the equatorial P–Cl bonds.
Statement II: Axial bond pairs in \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\) suffer greater repulsive interaction from the equatorial bond pairs than equatorial bond pairs suffer from each other.

  • (A) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
  • (B) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
  • (C) Statement I is correct but Statement II is incorrect.
  • (D) Statement I is incorrect but Statement II is correct.

Answer: (A) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.

Worked Solution: Each axial P–Cl bond in trigonal bipyramidal \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\) experiences three \(90^\circ\) repulsions from equatorial bond pairs, whereas each equatorial bond experiences only two \(90^\circ\) repulsions (from the two axial bonds) and two \(120^\circ\) repulsions. Greater repulsion elongates and weakens the axial bonds.

Practice Question 5 (Assertion–Reason MCQ):
Assertion (A): The H–O–H bond angle in \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) (\(104.5^\circ\)) is smaller than the H–N–H bond angle in \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (\(107^\circ\)).
Reason (R): \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) has two lone pairs on the central atom, whereas \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) has only one lone pair, and \(\text{lp–lp}\) repulsion is greater than \(\text{lp–bp}\) repulsion.

  • (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.
  • (C) A is true, but R is false.
  • (D) A is false, but R is true.

Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Worked Solution: Both \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) and \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) have \(sp^3\) hybridisation (ideal tetrahedral angle \(109.5^\circ\)). One lone pair in \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) compresses the angle to \(107^\circ\), while two lone pairs in \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) exert stronger \(\text{lp–lp}\) and \(\text{lp–bp}\) repulsions, compressing the H–O–H angle further to \(104.5^\circ\).

Practice Question 7 (Single-Correct MCQ): Taking the \(x\)-axis as the internuclear axis, which combination of atomic orbitals will not form a \(\sigma\) bond?

  • (A) \(1s\) and \(1s\)
  • (B) \(1s\) and \(2p_x\)
  • (C) \(2p_y\) and \(2p_y\)
  • (D) \(2p_x\) and \(2p_x\)

Answer: (C) \(2p_y\) and \(2p_y\)

Worked Solution: With the \(x\)-axis as the internuclear axis, \(2p_y\) orbitals are perpendicular to the internuclear axis and overlap sidewise (laterally) to form a \(\pi\) bond, not a \(\sigma\) bond. All other combinations overlap axially along the \(x\)-axis to form \(\sigma\) bonds.

Practice Question 8 (Single-Correct MCQ): How many \(\sigma\) and \(\pi\) bonds are present in propene (\(\mathrm{CH_3-CH=CH_2}\)), and what are the hybridisations of its three carbon atoms from left to right?

  • (A) \(8\,\sigma,\ 1\,\pi\);\ \(sp^3, sp^2, sp^2\)
  • (B) \(7\,\sigma,\ 2\,\pi\);\ \(sp^3, sp^2, sp\)
  • (C) \(8\,\sigma,\ 1\,\pi\);\ \(sp^2, sp^2, sp^3\)
  • (D) \(6\,\sigma,\ 1\,\pi\);\ \(sp^3, sp^2, sp^2\)

Answer: (A) \(8\,\sigma,\ 1\,\pi\);\ \(sp^3, sp^2, sp^2\)

The \(\mathrm{-CH_3}\) carbon has 4 \(\sigma\) bonds (\(sp^3\)), while both \(\mathrm{=CH-}\) and \(\mathrm{=CH_2}\) carbons have 3 \(\sigma\) bonds and 1 \(\pi\) bond (\(sp^2\)).

11. Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist

English
  • MO Energy Inversion: Never use the \(\mathrm{O_2/F_2}\) MO order (\(\sigma 2p_z < \pi 2p\)) for \(\mathrm{B_2}\), \(\mathrm{C_2}\), or \(\mathrm{N_2}\); doing so wrongly predicts \(\mathrm{B_2}\) as diamagnetic.
  • Lone-Pair Placement in \(sp^3d\): Always place lone pairs in equatorial positions first (\(\mathrm{SF_4}\), \(\mathrm{ClF_3}\), \(\mathrm{XeF_2}\)), never axial.
  • Resonance vs. Tautomerism: Canonical forms are imaginary contributors to one hybrid; they do not interconvert over time.
  • \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) vs. \(\mathrm{NF_3}\) Dipole Moment: Higher electronegativity of F does not mean higher molecular dipole moment for \(\mathrm{NF_3}\); vector direction of the lone-pair orbital dipole reverses the trend.
  • Formal Charge vs. Oxidation State: Formal charge assumes equal sharing of bonding electrons and does not represent actual ionic charge.

  • \(sp^3d\) সংকরায়ণে নিঃসঙ্গ জোড়: \(\mathrm{SF_4}\), \(\mathrm{ClF_3}\) বা \(\mathrm{XeF_2}\)-এ নিঃসঙ্গ জোড় সর্বদা নিরক্ষীয় অবস্থানে বসান।
  • রেজোন্যান্স বনাম টটোমারিজম: ক্যানোনিকাল গঠনগুলি বাস্তবে পৃথকভাবে অস্তিত্বশীল নয়।
  • \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) ও \(\mathrm{NF_3}\): F-এর তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতা বেশি হলেও নিঃসঙ্গ জোড়ের বিপরীতমুখী ভেক্টরের জন্য \(\mathrm{NF_3}\)-এর দ্বিমেরু ভ্রামক \(\mathrm{NH_3}\)-এর চেয়ে কম।

12. Concise Final Recap

English

Chemical bonding lowers system energy through electron transfer (ionic crystals stabilized by lattice enthalpy) or electron sharing (covalent bonds). Real bonds show partial character governed by electronegativity differences and Fajan's polarization rules. Molecular shapes are dictated by VSEPR repulsion (\(\text{lp–lp} > \text{lp–bp} > \text{bp–bp}\)) and orbital hybridisation, while Molecular Orbital Theory accurately accounts for bond order, stability, and magnetic properties via \(\frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)\). Finally, hydrogen bonding (intermolecular and intramolecular) strongly governs physical properties such as boiling point and volatility.

১২. সংক্ষিপ্ত সারসংক্ষেপ

ইলেকট্রন স্থানান্তর (ল্যাটিস এনথ্যালপি দ্বারা স্থায়ী আয়নীয় কেলাস) অথবা ইলেকট্রন শেয়ারিংয়ের (সমযোজী বন্ধন) মাধ্যমে রাসায়নিক বন্ধন গঠিত হয়ে ব্যবস্থার শক্তি হ্রাস করে। তড়িৎঋণাত্মকতার পার্থক্য ও ফাজানের নিয়ম বন্ধনের মেরুতা ও সমযোজী চরিত্র নিয়ন্ত্রণ করে। VSEPR তত্ত্ব ও সংকরায়ণ অণুর আকৃতি ব্যাখ্যা করে, আর আণবিক কক্ষক তত্ত্ব \(\frac{1}{2}(N_b - N_a)\) সূত্রের মাধ্যমে বন্ধন ক্রম, স্থায়িত্ব ও চৌম্বক ধর্ম নির্ণয় করে। হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধন পদার্থের স্ফুটনাঙ্ক ও উদ্বায়িতার মতো ভৌত ধর্মে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ প্রভাব ফেলে।
