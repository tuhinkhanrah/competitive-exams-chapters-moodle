1. Sub-Atomic Particles and Early Atomic Models

Early nineteenth-century Daltonian theory treated the atom as an indivisible sphere. However, gas-discharge experiments at low pressure and high voltage revealed that atoms are divisible and contain negatively charged electrons (cathode rays), positively charged protons (smallest positive ion from hydrogen in canal rays), and electrically neutral neutrons (discovered by James Chadwick by bombarding beryllium with α-particles).

J.J. Thomson determined the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron as \(\frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}\) using perpendicular electric and magnetic fields. Later, R.A. Millikan's oil-drop experiment established that electric charge is quantized (\(q = n e\)) with elementary magnitude \(e = 1.602176 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\), yielding \(m_e = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\).

Thomson vs. Rutherford Nuclear Model

Thomson envisioned an atom (radius \(\approx 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)) as a uniform sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded inside. This failed to explain Ernest Rutherford's α-particle scattering experiment on thin gold foil (\(\sim 100\,\mathrm{nm}\) thick): while most α-particles passed straight through, a small fraction deflected by small angles, and roughly 1 in 20,000 rebounded by nearly \(180^\circ\).

Tutorial Diagram 1: Rutherford's α-particle scattering showing empty atomic space and a dense positive nucleus.

Rutherford concluded that almost all mass and positive charge reside in a tiny central nucleus (radius \(\approx 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\), whereas atomic radius \(\approx 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)). The number of protons defines the atomic number (\(Z\)), and the sum of protons and neutrons (nucleons) defines the mass number (\(A\)):

\[ A = Z + n \qquad \text{and for a neutral atom: } Z = p^+ = e^- \]
  • Isotopes: Atoms of the same element with identical \(Z\) but different \(A\) (e.g., \({}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}\), \({}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{D}\), \({}^{3}_{1}\mathrm{T}\); \({}^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\), \({}^{37}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\)). They exhibit identical chemical behaviour because chemical properties depend on electron count.
  • Isobars: Atoms of different elements with the same \(A\) but different \(Z\) (e.g., \({}^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}\) and \({}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}\)).
  • Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model: According to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charged electron in a circular orbit must continuously emit radiation, lose energy, and spiral into the nucleus within \(\sim 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}\). Thus, Rutherford's model could not explain atomic stability or discrete line spectra.

2. Wave-Particle Duality of Radiation and Atomic Spectra

Electromagnetic radiation consists of mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields propagating through vacuum at the speed of light \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Wavelength (\(\lambda\)), frequency (\(\nu\)), and wavenumber (\(\bar{\nu}\)) are related by:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \qquad \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \]

Planck's Quantum Theory and Photoelectric Effect

Wave theory explains interference and diffraction, but fails to explain black-body radiation and the photoelectric effect. In 1900, Max Planck proposed that energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called quanta (photons for light), where the energy of one quantum is:

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad \left(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\right) \]

When light of frequency \(\nu\) strikes a clean metal surface (such as K, Rb, or Cs), electrons are ejected instantaneously only if \(\nu \ge \nu_0\) (threshold frequency). Increasing light intensity increases the number of ejected electrons, whereas increasing frequency increases their maximum kinetic energy. Einstein's photoelectric equation expresses energy conservation:

\[ h\nu = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} \]

Here, \(W_0 = h\nu_0\) is the work function of the metal.

Line Spectrum of Atomic Hydrogen

Unlike white light (continuous spectrum), excited gaseous hydrogen emits discrete bright lines. Johannes Rydberg unified all hydrogen spectral series using the wavenumber equation:

\[ \bar{\nu} = R_H \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right) \qquad \left(R_H = 109677\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}} = 1.09677 \times 10^7\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\right) \]

২. বিকিরণের দ্বৈত প্রকৃতি এবং পারমাণবিক বর্ণালী

তড়িৎচুম্বকীয় বিকিরণে পরস্পর লম্বভাবে স্পন্দিত তড়িৎ ও চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্র থাকে, যা শূন্যস্থানে আলোর বেগে (\(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)) অগ্রসর হয়। তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্য (\(\lambda\)), কম্পাঙ্ক (\(\nu\)) এবং তরঙ্গ-সংখ্যা (\(\bar{\nu}\))-এর সম্পর্ক হলো:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \qquad \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \]

প্ল্যাঙ্কের কোয়ান্টাম তত্ত্ব ও আলোক-তড়িৎ ক্রিয়া

তরঙ্গ তত্ত্ব ব্যতিচার ও অপবর্তন ব্যাখ্যা করতে পারলেও কৃষ্ণবস্তুর বিকিরণ এবং আলোক-তড়িৎ ক্রিয়া ব্যাখ্যা করতে ব্যর্থ হয়। ১৯০০ সালে ম্যাক্স প্ল্যাঙ্ক প্রস্তাব করেন যে শক্তি নিরবচ্ছিন্নভাবে নয়, বরং বিচ্ছিন্ন প্যাকেট বা কোয়ান্টা (আলোর ক্ষেত্রে ফোটন) আকারে শোষিত বা বিকিরিত হয়। প্রতিটি কোয়ান্টার শক্তি:

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad \left(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\right) \]

কোনো ধাতব পৃষ্ঠে (যেমন K, Rb, Cs) \(\nu\) কম্পাঙ্কের আলো আপতিত হলে কেবল \(\nu \ge \nu_0\) (সূচনা কম্পাঙ্ক) হলেই তাৎক্ষণিকভাবে ইলেকট্রন নির্গত হয়। আলোর তীব্রতা বাড়ালে নির্গত ইলেকট্রনের সংখ্যা বাড়ে, আর কম্পাঙ্ক বাড়ালে ইলেকট্রনের সর্বোচ্চ গতিশক্তি বাড়ে। আইনস্টাইনের আলোক-তড়িৎ সমীকরণ:

\[ h\nu = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} \]

এখানে \(W_0 = h\nu_0\) হলো ধাতুটির কার্য-অপেক্ষক।

3. Bohr's Model for Hydrogen and Hydrogen-Like Species

Niels Bohr (1913) explained atomic stability and the hydrogen line spectrum by proposing that an electron revolves only in discrete stationary orbits without radiating energy. Transitions occur only when an electron jumps between stationary states \(n_i\) and \(n_f\), emitting or absorbing a photon of energy \(\Delta E = h\nu\). Furthermore, the electron's angular momentum is quantized:

\[ m_e v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi} \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots) \]

For any single-electron species (H, \(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\), \(\mathrm{Be}^{3+}\)) with nuclear charge \(Z\), the radius and energy of the \(n\)-th stationary state are:

\[ r_n = 52.9 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{pm} = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å} \] \[ E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{J\,atom^{-1}} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV\,atom^{-1}} \]

The negative sign signifies that a bound electron has lower energy than a free electron at infinite separation (\(E_\infty = 0\)). For a transition between \(n_i\) and \(n_f\) in a hydrogen-like ion:

\[ \Delta E = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_i^2} - \frac{1}{n_f^2}\right)\,\mathrm{J} \]
Tutorial Diagram 2: Electronic transitions in the hydrogen atom producing the Lyman, Balmer, and Paschen series.
NEET UG Shortcut — Maximum Spectral Lines: When an electron drops from level \(n\) to the ground state (\(n=1\)) in a large sample of hydrogen atoms, the maximum number of emission lines is \(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\). If it drops from \(n_2\) to \(n_1\), the count is \(\frac{(n_2 - n_1)(n_2 - n_1 + 1)}{2}\).

Limitations of Bohr's Model: It cannot explain multi-electron spectra (even He), fine-structure doublets, splitting of spectral lines in magnetic fields (Zeeman effect) or electric fields (Stark effect), or chemical bonding. Moreover, fixed circular orbits violate wave-particle duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

4. Quantum Mechanical Model, Quantum Numbers, and Orbitals

Louis de Broglie (1924) proposed that moving matter possesses wave character with wavelength \(\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{p}\). Werner Heisenberg (1927) proved that it is impossible to determine simultaneously the exact position and exact momentum of a microscopic particle:

\[ \Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \quad \implies \quad \Delta x \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} \]

This rules out definite Bohr trajectories and replaces them with probability distributions derived from Erwin Schrödinger's wave equation \(\hat{H}\psi = E\psi\). Here, the wave function \(\psi\) itself has no physical meaning, whereas \(|\psi|^2\) gives the probability density of finding an electron at a point. An atomic orbital is a one-electron wave function \(\psi\) characterized by three quantum numbers \((n, l, m_l)\), while the electron's intrinsic spin is specified by \(m_s\).

Quantum Number Symbol Permitted Values Physical Meaning
Principal \(n\) \(1, 2, 3, \dots\) Shell, orbital size, and energy (solely for H)
Azimuthal \(l\) \(0, 1, \dots, (n-1)\) Subshell (\(s, p, d, f\)) and 3D shape; orbital angular momentum \(=\sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi}\)
Magnetic \(m_l\) \(-l, \dots, 0, \dots, +l\) Spatial orientation; \((2l+1)\) orbitals per subshell
Electron Spin \(m_s\) \(+\frac{1}{2}\) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\) Spin orientation (\(\uparrow\) or \(\downarrow\))

Shapes of Orbitals and Nodal Surfaces

Boundary surface diagrams enclose ~90% electron probability (never 100%, because \(|\psi|^2\) approaches zero only at infinity).

  • \(s\)-orbitals (\(l=0\)): Spherically symmetric; size increases as \(1s < 2s < 3s\).
  • \(p\)-orbitals (\(l=1\)): Dumbbell-shaped with two lobes separated by a nodal plane passing through the nucleus (\(p_x, p_y, p_z\)).
  • \(d\)-orbitals (\(l=2\)): Five degenerate orbitals (\(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}, d_{x^2-y^2}\) are cloverleaf-shaped; \(d_{z^2}\) has a dumbbell with a central torus).
\[ \text{Radial Nodes} = n - l - 1 \qquad \text{Angular Nodes (Nodal Planes)} = l \qquad \text{Total Nodes} = n - 1 \]

5. Orbital Energies and Electronic Configuration Rules

In hydrogen, orbital energy depends solely on \(n\) (\(2s = 2p\), \(3s = 3p = 3d\)). In multi-electron atoms, inner-shell electrons shield outer electrons from the full nuclear charge, creating an effective nuclear charge (\(Z_{\text{eff}}\)). Because penetration decreases in the order \(s > p > d > f\), orbital energies depend on both \(n\) and \(l\):

  • \((n+l)\) Rule: Orbitals with a lower \((n+l)\) value have lower energy. If two orbitals share the same \((n+l)\), the one with lower \(n\) has lower energy (e.g., \(4s\) has \(n+l = 4+0 = 4\), lower than \(3d\) with \(n+l = 3+2 = 5\)).
  • Aufbau Principle: Ground-state electrons fill orbitals in increasing order of energy: \(1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p\dots\)
  • Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can share the exact same set of four quantum numbers; hence an orbital holds at most 2 electrons with opposite spins, and a shell holds at most \(2n^2\) electrons.
  • Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Pairing in degenerate orbitals (\(p, d, f\)) does not occur until every orbital in that subshell is singly occupied with parallel spins.
Anomalous Configurations (\(\mathrm{Cr}\) and \(\mathrm{Cu}\)): Chromium (\(Z=24\)) is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1\) (not \(3d^4 4s^2\)) and Copper (\(Z=29\)) is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10} 4s^1\) (not \(3d^9 4s^2\)). Half-filled (\(d^5\)) and completely filled (\(d^{10}\)) subshells gain extra stability from symmetrical electron distribution and maximum exchange energy.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculating Photoelectric Kinetic Energy and Threshold Wavelength

Problem: Suppose a hypothetical metal surface has a work function \(W_0 = 2.48\,\mathrm{eV}\) (assumed value). If monochromatic radiation of wavelength \(\lambda = 400\,\mathrm{nm}\) strikes this surface, find (a) the threshold wavelength \(\lambda_0\) and (b) the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected photoelectrons in \(\mathrm{eV}\). (Use \(hc \approx 1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}\)).

Solution:

(a) Using \(W_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0}\):

\[ \lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{W_0} = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{2.48\,\mathrm{eV}} = 500\,\mathrm{nm} \]

(b) Energy of the incident photon:

\[ E = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{400\,\mathrm{nm}} = 3.10\,\mathrm{eV} \]

By Einstein's photoelectric equation, \(\mathrm{KE}_{\max} = E - W_0 = 3.10\,\mathrm{eV} - 2.48\,\mathrm{eV} = 0.62\,\mathrm{eV}\).

Example 2: Applying Bohr's Model to Hydrogen-Like Ions

Problem: Calculate the ratio of the radius of the 3rd Bohr orbit of \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z=3\)) to the radius of the 2nd Bohr orbit of \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z=2\)), and find the energy of an electron in the 2nd orbit of \(\mathrm{He}^+\) in \(\mathrm{eV}\).

Solution:

Since \(r_n = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å}\):

\[ \frac{r_3(\mathrm{Li}^{2+})}{r_2(\mathrm{He}^+)} = \frac{3^2 / 3}{2^2 / 2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \]

For \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z=2, n=2\)):

\[ E_2(\mathrm{He}^+) = -13.6 \times \frac{2^2}{2^2}\,\mathrm{eV} = -13.6\,\mathrm{eV} \]

Example 3: Determining de Broglie Wavelength and Position Uncertainty

Problem: Assume a particle of mass \(m = 1.0 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{kg}\) moves with speed \(v = 3.313 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. If the uncertainty in its velocity is \(0.1\%\), what is the minimum uncertainty in its position?

Solution:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}}{(1.0 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{kg})(3.313 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})} = 2.0 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{Å} \]

Velocity uncertainty \(\Delta v = 0.001 \times 3.313 \times 10^6 = 3.313 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Using Heisenberg's relation:

\[ \Delta x_{\min} = \frac{h}{4\pi m \Delta v} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-30} \times 3.313 \times 10^3} \approx 1.59 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m} \]

Example 4: Identifying Valid Quantum Numbers and Counting Nodes

Problem: Consider a \(4d\) subshell. State the values of \(n\) and \(l\), list the possible \(m_l\) values, and calculate the number of radial nodes, angular nodes, and total nodes.

Solution:

For \(4d\): \(n = 4\), \(l = 2\). Possible \(m_l\) values are \(-2, -1, 0, +1, +2\).

  • Radial nodes \(= n - l - 1 = 4 - 2 - 1 = 1\)
  • Angular nodes \(= l = 2\)
  • Total nodes \(= n - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3\)

1. Sub-Atomic Particles and Early Atomic Models

Early nineteenth-century Daltonian theory treated the atom as an indivisible sphere. However, gas-discharge experiments at low pressure and high voltage revealed that atoms are divisible and contain negatively charged electrons (cathode rays), positively charged protons (the lightest positive ions obtained from hydrogen in canal rays), and electrically neutral neutrons (discovered by James Chadwick by bombarding beryllium with α-particles).

J.J. Thomson determined the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron as \(\frac{e}{m_e} = 1.758820 \times 10^{11}\,\mathrm{C\,kg^{-1}}\) using perpendicular electric and magnetic fields. Later, R.A. Millikan's oil-drop experiment established that electric charge is quantized (\(q = n e\)) with elementary magnitude \(e = 1.602176 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\), yielding \(m_e = 9.1094 \times 10^{-31}\,\mathrm{kg}\).

Thomson vs. Rutherford Nuclear Model

Thomson envisioned an atom (radius \(\approx 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)) as a uniform sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded inside. This failed to explain Ernest Rutherford's α-particle scattering experiment on thin gold foil (\(\sim 100\,\mathrm{nm}\) thick): while most α-particles passed straight through, a small fraction deflected by small angles, and roughly 1 in 20,000 rebounded by nearly \(180^\circ\).

Tutorial Diagram 1: Rutherford's α-particle scattering showing empty atomic space and a dense positive nucleus.

Rutherford concluded that almost all mass and positive charge reside in a tiny central nucleus (radius \(\approx 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\), whereas atomic radius \(\approx 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)). The number of protons defines the atomic number (\(Z\)), and the sum of protons and neutrons (nucleons) defines the mass number (\(A\)):

\[ A = Z + n \qquad \text{and for a neutral atom: } Z = p^+ = e^- \]
  • Isotopes: Atoms of the same element with identical \(Z\) but different \(A\) (e.g., \({}^{1}_{1}\mathrm{H}\), \({}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{D}\), \({}^{3}_{1}\mathrm{T}\); \({}^{35}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\), \({}^{37}_{17}\mathrm{Cl}\)). They exhibit identical chemical behaviour because chemical properties depend on electron count.
  • Isobars: Atoms of different elements with the same \(A\) but different \(Z\) (e.g., \({}^{14}_{6}\mathrm{C}\) and \({}^{14}_{7}\mathrm{N}\)).
  • Drawbacks of Rutherford's Model: According to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charged electron in a circular orbit must continuously emit radiation, lose energy, and spiral into the nucleus within \(\sim 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{s}\). Thus, Rutherford's model could not explain atomic stability or discrete line spectra.

2. Wave-Particle Duality of Radiation and Atomic Spectra

Electromagnetic radiation consists of mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields propagating through vacuum at the speed of light \(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Wavelength (\(\lambda\)), frequency (\(\nu\)), and wavenumber (\(\bar{\nu}\)) are related by:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \qquad \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \]

Planck's Quantum Theory and Photoelectric Effect

Wave theory explains interference and diffraction, but fails to explain black-body radiation and the photoelectric effect. In 1900, Max Planck proposed that energy is emitted or absorbed in discrete packets called quanta (photons for light), where the energy of one quantum is:

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad \left(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\right) \]

When light of frequency \(\nu\) strikes a clean metal surface (such as K, Rb, or Cs), electrons are ejected instantaneously only if \(\nu \ge \nu_0\) (threshold frequency). Increasing light intensity increases the number of ejected electrons, whereas increasing frequency increases their maximum kinetic energy. Einstein's photoelectric equation expresses energy conservation:

\[ h\nu = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} \]

Here, \(W_0 = h\nu_0\) is the work function of the metal.

Line Spectrum of Atomic Hydrogen

Unlike white light (continuous spectrum), excited gaseous hydrogen emits discrete bright lines. Johannes Rydberg unified all hydrogen spectral series using the wavenumber equation:

\[ \bar{\nu} = R_H \left(\frac{1}{n_1^2} - \frac{1}{n_2^2}\right) \qquad \left(R_H = 109677\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}} = 1.09677 \times 10^7\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\right) \]

২. বিকিরণের দ্বৈত প্রকৃতি এবং পারমাণবিক বর্ণালী

তড়িৎচুম্বকীয় বিকিরণে পরস্পর লম্বভাবে স্পন্দিত তড়িৎ ও চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্র থাকে, যা শূন্যস্থানে আলোর বেগে (\(c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)) অগ্রসর হয়। তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্য (\(\lambda\)), কম্পাঙ্ক (\(\nu\)) এবং তরঙ্গ-সংখ্যা (\(\bar{\nu}\))-এর সম্পর্ক হলো:

\[ c = \nu \lambda \qquad \bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c} \]

প্ল্যাঙ্কের কোয়ান্টাম তত্ত্ব ও আলোক-তড়িৎ ক্রিয়া

তরঙ্গ তত্ত্ব ব্যতিচার ও অপবর্তন ব্যাখ্যা করতে পারলেও কৃষ্ণবস্তুর বিকিরণ এবং আলোক-তড়িৎ ক্রিয়া ব্যাখ্যা করতে ব্যর্থ হয়। ১৯০০ সালে ম্যাক্স প্ল্যাঙ্ক প্রস্তাব করেন যে শক্তি নিরবচ্ছিন্নভাবে নয়, বরং বিচ্ছিন্ন প্যাকেট বা কোয়ান্টা (আলোর ক্ষেত্রে ফোটন) আকারে শোষিত বা বিকিরিত হয়। প্রতিটি কোয়ান্টার শক্তি:

\[ E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} \qquad \left(h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}\right) \]

কোনো ধাতব পৃষ্ঠে (যেমন K, Rb, Cs) \(\nu\) কম্পাঙ্কের আলো আপতিত হলে কেবল \(\nu \ge \nu_0\) (সূচনা কম্পাঙ্ক) হলেই তাৎক্ষণিকভাবে ইলেকট্রন নির্গত হয়। আলোর তীব্রতা বাড়ালে নির্গত ইলেকট্রনের সংখ্যা বাড়ে, আর কম্পাঙ্ক বাড়ালে ইলেকট্রনের সর্বোচ্চ গতিশক্তি বাড়ে। আইনস্টাইনের আলোক-তড়িৎ সমীকরণ:

\[ h\nu = h\nu_0 + \frac{1}{2}m_e v^2 = W_0 + \mathrm{KE}_{\max} \]

3. Bohr's Model for Hydrogen and Hydrogen-Like Species

Niels Bohr (1913) explained atomic stability and the hydrogen line spectrum by proposing that an electron revolves only in discrete stationary orbits without radiating energy. Transitions occur only when an electron jumps between stationary states \(n_i\) and \(n_f\), emitting or absorbing a photon of energy \(\Delta E = h\nu\). Furthermore, the electron's angular momentum is quantized:

\[ m_e v r = n \frac{h}{2\pi} \qquad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots) \]

For any single-electron species (H, \(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\), \(\mathrm{Be}^{3+}\)) with nuclear charge \(Z\), the radius and energy of the \(n\)-th stationary state are:

\[ r_n = 52.9 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{pm} = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å} \] \[ E_n = -2.18 \times 10^{-18} \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{J\,atom^{-1}} = -13.6 \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV\,atom^{-1}} \]

The negative sign signifies that a bound electron has lower energy than a free electron at infinite separation (\(E_\infty = 0\)). For a transition between \(n_i\) and \(n_f\) in a hydrogen-like ion:

\[ \Delta E = 2.18 \times 10^{-18} Z^2 \left(\frac{1}{n_i^2} - \frac{1}{n_f^2}\right)\,\mathrm{J} \]
Tutorial Diagram 2: Electronic transitions in the hydrogen atom producing the Lyman, Balmer, and Paschen series.
NEET UG Shortcut — Maximum Spectral Lines: When an electron drops from level \(n\) to the ground state (\(n=1\)) in a large sample of hydrogen atoms, the maximum number of emission lines is \(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\). If it drops from \(n_2\) to \(n_1\), the count is \(\frac{(n_2 - n_1)(n_2 - n_1 + 1)}{2}\).

Limitations of Bohr's Model: It cannot explain multi-electron spectra (even He), fine-structure doublets, splitting of spectral lines in magnetic fields (Zeeman effect) or electric fields (Stark effect), or chemical bonding. Moreover, fixed circular orbits violate wave-particle duality and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle.

4. Quantum Mechanical Model, Quantum Numbers, and Orbitals

Louis de Broglie (1924) proposed that moving matter possesses wave character with wavelength \(\lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{h}{p}\). Werner Heisenberg (1927) proved that it is impossible to determine simultaneously the exact position and exact momentum of a microscopic particle:

\[ \Delta x \cdot \Delta p_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi} \quad \implies \quad \Delta x \cdot \Delta v_x \ge \frac{h}{4\pi m} \]

This rules out definite Bohr trajectories and replaces them with probability distributions derived from Erwin Schrödinger's wave equation \(\hat{H}\psi = E\psi\). Here, the wave function \(\psi\) itself has no physical meaning, whereas \(|\psi|^2\) gives the probability density of finding an electron at a point. An atomic orbital is a one-electron wave function \(\psi\) characterized by three quantum numbers \((n, l, m_l)\), while the electron's intrinsic spin is specified by \(m_s\).

Quantum Number Symbol Permitted Values Physical Meaning
Principal \(n\) \(1, 2, 3, \dots\) Shell, orbital size, and energy (solely for H)
Azimuthal \(l\) \(0, 1, \dots, (n-1)\) Subshell (\(s, p, d, f\)) and 3D shape; orbital angular momentum \(=\sqrt{l(l+1)}\frac{h}{2\pi}\)
Magnetic \(m_l\) \(-l, \dots, 0, \dots, +l\) Spatial orientation; \((2l+1)\) orbitals per subshell
Electron Spin \(m_s\) \(+\frac{1}{2}\) or \(-\frac{1}{2}\) Spin orientation (\(\uparrow\) or \(\downarrow\))

Shapes of Orbitals and Nodal Surfaces

Boundary surface diagrams enclose ~90% electron probability (never 100%, because \(|\psi|^2\) approaches zero only at infinity).

  • \(s\)-orbitals (\(l=0\)): Spherically symmetric; size increases as \(1s < 2s < 3s\).
  • \(p\)-orbitals (\(l=1\)): Dumbbell-shaped with two lobes separated by a nodal plane passing through the nucleus (\(p_x, p_y, p_z\)).
  • \(d\)-orbitals (\(l=2\)): Five degenerate orbitals (\(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}, d_{x^2-y^2}\) are cloverleaf-shaped; \(d_{z^2}\) has a dumbbell with a central torus).
\[ \text{Radial Nodes} = n - l - 1 \qquad \text{Angular Nodes (Nodal Planes)} = l \qquad \text{Total Nodes} = n - 1 \]

5. Orbital Energies and Electronic Configuration Rules

In hydrogen, orbital energy depends solely on \(n\) (\(2s = 2p\), \(3s = 3p = 3d\)). In multi-electron atoms, inner-shell electrons shield outer electrons from the full nuclear charge, creating an effective nuclear charge (\(Z_{\text{eff}}\)). Because penetration decreases in the order \(s > p > d > f\), orbital energies depend on both \(n\) and \(l\):

  • \((n+l)\) Rule: Orbitals with a lower \((n+l)\) value have lower energy. If two orbitals share the same \((n+l)\), the one with lower \(n\) has lower energy (e.g., \(4s\) has \(n+l = 4+0 = 4\), lower than \(3d\) with \(n+l = 3+2 = 5\)).
  • Aufbau Principle: Ground-state electrons fill orbitals in increasing order of energy: \(1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p\dots\)
  • Pauli Exclusion Principle: No two electrons in an atom can share the exact same set of four quantum numbers; hence an orbital holds at most 2 electrons with opposite spins, and a shell holds at most \(2n^2\) electrons.
  • Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity: Pairing in degenerate orbitals (\(p, d, f\)) does not occur until every orbital in that subshell is singly occupied with parallel spins.
Anomalous Configurations (\(\mathrm{Cr}\) and \(\mathrm{Cu}\)): Chromium (\(Z=24\)) is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^5 4s^1\) (not \(3d^4 4s^2\)) and Copper (\(Z=29\)) is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^{10} 4s^1\) (not \(3d^9 4s^2\)). Half-filled (\(d^5\)) and completely filled (\(d^{10}\)) subshells gain extra stability from symmetrical electron distribution and maximum exchange energy.

হাইড্রোজেন পরমাণুতে অরবিটালের শক্তি কেবল \(n\)-এর ওপর নির্ভর করে (\(2s = 2p\), \(3s = 3p = 3d\))। কিন্তু বহু-ইলেকট্রন পরমাণুতে অভ্যন্তরীণ ইলেকট্রনগুলো বাইরের ইলেকট্রনকে নিউক্লিয়াসের পূর্ণ আকর্ষণ থেকে আড়াল (আবরণী প্রভাব) করে, ফলে বাইরের ইলেকট্রন কার্যকরী নিউক্লীয় আধান (\(Z_{\text{eff}}\)) অনুভব করে। ভেদন ক্ষমতা \(s > p > d > f\) ক্রমে কমায়, অরবিটালের শক্তি \(n\) ও \(l\) উভয়ের ওপর নির্ভর করে:

  • \((n+l)\) নীতি: যে অরবিটালের \((n+l)\) মান কম, তার শক্তি কম। যদি দুটি অরবিটালের \((n+l)\) মান সমান হয়, তবে কম \(n\)-মান বিশিষ্ট অরবিটালের শক্তি কম (যেমন: \(4s\)-এর \(n+l = 4\), যা \(3d\)-এর \(n+l = 5\)-এর চেয়ে কম)।
  • আউফবাউ নীতি: ভূমি স্তরে ইলেকট্রন ক্রমবর্ধমান শক্তির ক্রমে অরবিটালে প্রবেশ করে: \(1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, 5s, 4d, 5p, 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p\dots\)

Worked Examples

Calculating Photoelectric Kinetic Energy and Threshold Wavelength

Problem: Suppose a hypothetical metal surface has a work function \(W_0 = 2.48\,\mathrm{eV}\) (assumed value). If monochromatic radiation of wavelength \(\lambda = 400\,\mathrm{nm}\) strikes this surface, find (a) the threshold wavelength \(\lambda_0\) and (b) the maximum kinetic energy of the ejected photoelectrons in \(\mathrm{eV}\). (Use \(hc \approx 1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}\)).

Solution:

(a) Using \(W_0 = \frac{hc}{\lambda_0}\):

\[ \lambda_0 = \frac{hc}{W_0} = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{2.48\,\mathrm{eV}} = 500\,\mathrm{nm} \]

(b) Energy of the incident photon:

\[ E = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\,nm}}{400\,\mathrm{nm}} = 3.10\,\mathrm{eV} \]

By Einstein's photoelectric equation, \(\mathrm{KE}_{\max} = E - W_0 = 3.10\,\mathrm{eV} - 2.48\,\mathrm{eV} = 0.62\,\mathrm{eV}\).

Applying Bohr's Model to Hydrogen-Like Ions

Problem: Calculate the ratio of the radius of the 3rd Bohr orbit of \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z=3\)) to the radius of the 2nd Bohr orbit of \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z=2\)), and find the energy of an electron in the 2nd orbit of \(\mathrm{He}^+\) in \(\mathrm{eV}\).

Solution:

Since \(r_n = 0.529 \frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å}\):

\[ \frac{r_3(\mathrm{Li}^{2+})}{r_2(\mathrm{He}^+)} = \frac{3^2 / 3}{2^2 / 2} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \]

For \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z=2, n=2\)):

\[ E_2(\mathrm{He}^+) = -13.6 \times \frac{2^2}{2^2}\,\mathrm{eV} = -13.6\,\mathrm{eV} \]

Determining de Broglie Wavelength and Position Uncertainty

Problem: Assume a particle of mass \(m = 1.0 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{kg}\) moves with speed \(v = 3.313 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. If the uncertainty in its velocity is \(0.1\%\), what is the minimum uncertainty in its position?

Solution:

\[ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\,s}}{(1.0 \times 10^{-30}\,\mathrm{kg})(3.313 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}})} = 2.0 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m} = 2.0\,\mathrm{Å} \]

Velocity uncertainty \(\Delta v = 0.001 \times 3.313 \times 10^6 = 3.313 \times 10^3\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Using Heisenberg's relation:

\[ \Delta x_{\min} = \frac{h}{4\pi m \Delta v} = \frac{6.626 \times 10^{-34}}{4 \times 3.1416 \times 1.0 \times 10^{-30} \times 3.313 \times 10^3} \approx 1.59 \times 10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m} \]

Identifying Valid Quantum Numbers and Counting Nodes

Problem: Consider a \(4d\) subshell. State the values of \(n\) and \(l\), list the possible \(m_l\) values, and calculate the number of radial nodes, angular nodes, and total nodes.

Solution:

For \(4d\): \(n = 4\), \(l = 2\). Possible \(m_l\) values are \(-2, -1, 0, +1, +2\).

  • Radial nodes \(= n - l - 1 = 4 - 2 - 1 = 1\)
  • Angular nodes \(= l = 2\)
  • Total nodes \(= n - 1 = 4 - 1 = 3\)

\[ \frac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{m_\alpha q_\alpha}{m_p q_p}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 m_p \times 2 q_p}{m_p \times q_p}} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \]

একটি প্রোটন এবং একটি α-কণাকে স্থির অবস্থা থেকে একই বিভব পার্থক্য \(V\)-এর মধ্য দিয়ে ত্বরিত করা হলো। এদের ডি ব্রগলি তরঙ্গদৈর্ঘ্যের অনুপাত (\(\lambda_p : \lambda_\alpha\)) কত?

  • (A) \(2 : 1\)
  • (B) \(2\sqrt{2} : 1\)
  • (C) \(4 : 1\)
  • (D) \(1 : 2\sqrt{2}\)

উত্তর: (B) \(2\sqrt{2} : 1\)

সমাধান: অর্জিত গতিশক্তি \(\mathrm{KE} = qV\), তাই ভরবেগ \(p = \sqrt{2mqV}\) এবং \(\lambda = \frac{h}{\sqrt{2mqV}}\)। যেহেতু \(m_\alpha = 4 m_p\) এবং \(q_\alpha = 2 q_p\):

\[ \frac{\lambda_p}{\lambda_\alpha} = \sqrt{\frac{m_\alpha q_\alpha}{m_p q_p}} = \sqrt{\frac{4 m_p \times 2 q_p}{m_p \times q_p}} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \]
Practice Question 6

Which of the following sets of quantum numbers \((n, l, m_l, m_s)\) is not permissible for an electron in an atom?

  • (A) \((3, 2, -2, +\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (B) \((4, 0, 0, -\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (C) \((2, 2, 0, +\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (D) \((3, 1, -1, -\frac{1}{2})\)

Answer: (C) \((2, 2, 0, +\frac{1}{2})\)

Worked Solution: For a given principal quantum number \(n\), the azimuthal quantum number \(l\) can take values from \(0\) to \((n-1)\) only. When \(n = 2\), the maximum allowed value of \(l\) is \(1\) (not \(2\)). Thus, a \(2d\) orbital does not exist.

অনুশীলনী প্রশ্ন ৬

কোনো পরমাণুতে একটি ইলেকট্রনের জন্য নিচের কোন কোয়ান্টাম সংখ্যার সেটটি \((n, l, m_l, m_s)\) সম্ভব নয়?

  • (A) \((3, 2, -2, +\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (B) \((4, 0, 0, -\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (C) \((2, 2, 0, +\frac{1}{2})\)
  • (D) \((3, 1, -1, -\frac{1}{2})\)

উত্তর: (C) \((2, 2, 0, +\frac{1}{2})\)

সমাধান: নির্দিষ্ট \(n\)-এর জন্য \(l\)-এর মান \(0\) থেকে \((n-1)\) পর্যন্ত হতে পারে। তাই \(n = 2\) হলে \(l\)-এর সর্বোচ্চ মান হতে পারে \(1\) (\(2\) নয়)। অর্থাৎ \(2d\) অরবিটালের কোনো অস্তিত্ব নেই।

Practice Question 7

The number of radial nodes and angular nodes present in a \(3p\) orbital are, respectively:

  • (A) 2 and 1
  • (B) 1 and 1
  • (C) 1 and 2
  • (D) 0 and 2

Answer: (B) 1 and 1

Worked Solution: For a \(3p\) orbital, \(n = 3\) and \(l = 1\). Radial nodes \(= n - l - 1 = 3 - 1 - 1 = 1\). Angular nodes \(= l = 1\).

সমাধান: \(3p\) অরবিটালের জন্য \(n = 3\) এবং \(l = 1\)। রেডিয়াল নোড \(= n - l - 1 = 3 - 1 - 1 = 1\) এবং কৌণিক নোড \(= l = 1\)।

Practice Question 8

How many unpaired electrons are present in the ground state of \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\) ion (atomic number of \(\mathrm{Fe} = 26\))?

  • (A) 2
  • (B) 3
  • (C) 4
  • (D) 5

Answer: (C) 4

Worked Solution: The ground-state electronic configuration of \(\mathrm{Fe}\) (\(Z=26\)) is \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6 4s^2\). To form \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\), two electrons are removed from the outermost \(4s\) subshell, giving \([\mathrm{Ar}]\,3d^6\). According to Hund's rule, the five \(3d\) orbitals first receive one electron each, and the sixth electron pairs up in one orbital, leaving 4 unpaired electrons.

Common Pitfalls and NEET UG Revision Checklist

  • Ionization vs. Electron Removal: Always remove electrons from the highest principal quantum number (\(n\)) shell first (e.g., \(4s\) before \(3d\) in transition metals).
  • Bohr Energy Sign: Remember \(E_n\) is negative; as \(n\) increases, \(E_n\) becomes less negative (increases).
  • Degeneracy in Hydrogen vs. Multi-Electron Atoms: In H and \(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(2s = 2p\) and \(3s = 3p = 3d\). In multi-electron atoms, \(2s < 2p\) and \(3s < 3p < 3d\).
  • Node Formulas: Do not confuse radial nodes (\(n - l - 1\)) with total nodes (\(n - 1\)).
