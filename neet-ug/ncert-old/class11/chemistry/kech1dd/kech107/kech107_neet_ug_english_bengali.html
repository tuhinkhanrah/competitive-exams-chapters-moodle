1. Dynamic Nature of Physical and Chemical Equilibria

Equilibrium is attained only in a closed system at a constant temperature. Although macroscopic properties (pressure, concentration, colour intensity) become constant, microscopic forward and reverse processes continue at equal rates—making equilibrium dynamic, not static.

Key Types of Physical Equilibria

Physical Equilibrium Example Constant Equilibrium Parameter (at fixed \(T\))
Solid ⇌ Liquid \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})\) Normal melting/freezing point (\(273\,\mathrm{K}\) at \(1.013\,\mathrm{bar}\))
Liquid ⇌ Vapour \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{g})\) Equilibrium vapour pressure \(p_{\mathrm{H_2O}}\)
Solid ⇌ Vapour \(\mathrm{I}_2(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{I}_2(\mathrm{g})\) Sublimation vapour pressure
Solid ⇌ Solution \(\text{Sugar}(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \text{Sugar}(\mathrm{aq})\) Saturation solubility (constant concentration)
Gas ⇌ Solution \(\mathrm{CO}_2(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CO}_2(\mathrm{aq})\) \([\mathrm{CO}_2(\mathrm{aq})] \propto p_{\mathrm{CO}_2}\) (Henry's law)

Chemical Equilibrium: For a reversible reaction \(a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \rightleftharpoons c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}\), starting with reactants causes reactant concentrations to fall and product concentrations to rise until the forward rate equals the reverse rate (\(r_f = r_b\)). The same equilibrium state can be approached from either pure reactants or pure products.

Attainment of dynamic chemical equilibrium: concentrations become constant once forward and reverse rates equalize.

2. Law of Chemical Equilibrium, \(K_c\), \(K_p\), \(Q\), and Gibbs Energy

For a balanced reversible reaction \(a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \rightleftharpoons c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}\) at a given temperature, the equilibrium constant in terms of molar concentrations (\(K_c\)) is:

\[K_c = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c [\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a [\mathrm{B}]^b}\]

Rules for Manipulating Equilibrium Constants

Modification to Balanced Equation New Equilibrium Constant
Reverse the equation: \(c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D} \rightleftharpoons a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B}\) \(K' = \frac{1}{K}\)
Multiply all coefficients by \(n\): \(na\mathrm{A} + nb\mathrm{B} \rightleftharpoons nc\mathrm{C} + nd\mathrm{D}\) \(K'' = K^n\)
Add two reactions with constants \(K_1\) and \(K_2\) \(K_{\text{net}} = K_1 \times K_2\)

Gaseous Equilibria (\(K_p\)) and Heterogeneous Equilibria

For gaseous reactions, partial pressures (in \(\mathrm{bar}\)) give \(K_p\). Using the ideal gas relation \(p = [\mathrm{gas}]RT\) (with \(R = 0.0831\,\mathrm{bar\,L\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\)):

\[K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n_g}\]

Here \(\Delta n_g = (\text{moles of gaseous products}) - (\text{moles of gaseous reactants})\). In heterogeneous equilibria (more than one phase), the molar concentrations of pure solids and pure liquids are constant and are omitted from \(K_c\) and \(K_p\) expressions (for example, in \(\mathrm{CaCO}_3(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{CaO}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{CO}_2(\mathrm{g})\), \(K_p = p_{\mathrm{CO}_2}\) and \(K_c = [\mathrm{CO}_2]\)), provided those pure phases are physically present.

Predicting Extent (\(K\)) and Direction (\(Q\))

  • Extent of reaction: \(K_c > 10^3\) means products strongly predominate (nearly complete); \(K_c < 10^{-3}\) means reactants strongly predominate (reaction barely proceeds); \(10^{-3} \le K_c \le 10^3\) means comparable amounts of reactants and products coexist.
  • Reaction quotient (\(Q_c\)): Defined identically to \(K_c\) but using concentrations at any arbitrary instant \(t\). Comparing \(Q_c\) with \(K_c\) predicts the direction of net change.

Predicting the direction of net reaction by comparing \(Q_c\) and \(K_c\).

Thermodynamic Relation with Gibbs Energy

\[\Delta G = \Delta G^\circ + RT \ln Q \qquad \text{At equilibrium } (\Delta G = 0,\; Q = K): \quad \Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K\]

Thus, \(\Delta G^\circ < 0 \implies K > 1\) (products favoured under standard conditions), \(\Delta G^\circ > 0 \implies K < 1\) (reactants favoured), and \(\Delta G^\circ = 0 \implies K = 1\).

  • বিক্রিয়া ভাগফল (\(Q_c\)): যেকোনো মুহূর্ত \(t\)-তে গাঢ়ত্ব বসিয়ে \(K_c\)-এর মতোই \(Q_c\) নির্ণয় করা হয়। \(Q_c\) এবং \(K_c\)-এর তুলনা করে নিট বিক্রিয়ার দিক জানা যায়।

\(Q_c\) এবং \(K_c\)-এর মান তুলনা করে নিট বিক্রিয়ার দিক নির্ণয়।

\[\Delta G = \Delta G^\circ + RT \ln Q \qquad \text{সাম্যাবস্থায় } (\Delta G = 0,\; Q = K): \quad \Delta G^\circ = -RT \ln K = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K\]

3. Le Chatelier's Principle and Factors Affecting Equilibrium

Le Chatelier's Principle: If a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, pressure, or temperature, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that tends to counteract or reduce the imposed change.

Perturbation Direction of Shift Effect on \(K\)
Add reactant or remove product Forward (towards products) No change
Increase pressure (decrease volume) Towards side with fewer moles of gas (no shift if \(\Delta n_g = 0\)) No change
Add inert gas at constant volume No shift (partial pressures of reacting gases stay unchanged) No change
Increase temperature Endothermic direction (\(\Delta H > 0\)); decreases yield for exothermic (\(\Delta H < 0\)) Changes (\(K\) increases if \(\Delta H > 0\); decreases if \(\Delta H < 0\))
Add a catalyst No shift (lowers forward and reverse activation energies equally) No change (equilibrium is reached faster)

4. Ionic Equilibrium: Acids, Bases, Water Ionization, and \(\mathrm{pH}\)

Strong electrolytes (e.g., \(\mathrm{HCl}\), \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) ionize almost completely in water, whereas weak electrolytes (e.g., \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}\), \(\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{OH}\)) ionize partially and establish an ionic equilibrium between unionized molecules and ions.

Three Acid–Base Concepts

Theory Acid Definition Base Definition Example
Arrhenius (aqueous only) Produces \(\mathrm{H}^+(\mathrm{aq})\) / \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+\) Produces \(\mathrm{OH}^-(\mathrm{aq})\) \(\mathrm{HCl}\), \(\mathrm{NaOH}\)
Brönsted–Lowry Proton (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) donor Proton (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)) acceptor \(\mathrm{NH}_3 + \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{NH}_4^+ + \mathrm{OH}^-\)
Lewis Electron-pair acceptor Electron-pair donor \(\mathrm{BF}_3 + :\mathrm{NH}_3 \rightarrow \mathrm{F}_3\mathrm{B}:\mathrm{NH}_3\)

Conjugate Acid–Base Pairs: Differ by a single proton (\(\mathrm{H}^+\)). A strong acid has a very weak conjugate base (e.g., \(\mathrm{HCl}\) → \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\)), and a weak acid has a strong conjugate base. Water is amphoteric: it forms \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+\) as its conjugate acid and \(\mathrm{OH}^-\) as its conjugate base.

Ionic Product of Water and the \(\mathrm{pH}\) Scale

\[K_w = [\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+][\mathrm{OH}^-] = 1.0 \times 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{M}^2 \;\text{at } 298\,\mathrm{K} \qquad \mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = \mathrm{p}K_w = 14\]

By definition, \(\mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+]\) and \(\mathrm{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{OH}^-]\). At \(298\,\mathrm{K}\): acidic solutions have \(\mathrm{pH} < 7\), neutral solutions have \(\mathrm{pH} = 7\), and basic solutions have \(\mathrm{pH} > 7\). Note that \(K_w\) increases with temperature, so neutral \(\mathrm{pH}\) drops slightly below 7 above \(298\,\mathrm{K}\).

Weak Acids (\(K_a\)), Weak Bases (\(K_b\)), and Conjugate Relation

For a weak monobasic acid \(\mathrm{HA}\) of initial concentration \(c\) and degree of ionization \(\alpha\):

\[K_a = \frac{c\alpha^2}{1 - \alpha} \approx c\alpha^2 \;\;(\text{if } \alpha \ll 1), \qquad [\mathrm{H}^+] = c\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_a c}\]

Similarly, for a weak base \(\mathrm{BOH}\), \(K_b = \frac{c\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}\) and \([\mathrm{OH}^-] \approx \sqrt{K_b c}\). For any conjugate acid–base pair at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\):

\[K_a \times K_b = K_w = 10^{-14} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \mathrm{p}K_a + \mathrm{p}K_b = \mathrm{p}K_w = 14\]

For polyprotic acids (e.g., \(\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{PO}_4\)), \(K_{a_1} \gg K_{a_2} \gg K_{a_3}\) because removing a proton from an increasingly negatively charged anion is electrostatically harder. Periodic acid strength trends: down a group, decreasing \(\mathrm{H–A}\) bond strength dominates (\(\mathrm{HF} \ll \mathrm{HCl} \ll \mathrm{HBr} \ll \mathrm{HI}\)); across a period, increasing electronegativity/polarity dominates (\(\mathrm{CH}_4 < \mathrm{NH}_3 < \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} < \mathrm{HF}\)).

সংজ্ঞানুসারে, \(\mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{H}_3\mathrm{O}^+]\) এবং \(\mathrm{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\mathrm{OH}^-]\)। \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় আম্লিক দ্রবণের \(\mathrm{pH} < 7\), প্রশম দ্রবণের \(\mathrm{pH} = 7\), এবং ক্ষারীয় দ্রবণের \(\mathrm{pH} > 7\)। তাপমাত্রা বাড়লে \(K_w\) বাড়ে, ফলে প্রশম জলের \(\mathrm{pH}\) ৭-এর সামান্য নিচে নেমে যায়।

মৃদু অ্যাসিড (\(K_a\)), মৃদু ক্ষার (\(K_b\)) এবং অনুবন্ধী সম্পর্ক

\(c\) প্রাথমিক গাঢ়ত্ব এবং \(\alpha\) বিয়োজন মাত্রার একটি মৃদু একক্ষারীয় অ্যাসিড \(\mathrm{HA}\)-এর জন্য:

\[K_a = \frac{c\alpha^2}{1 - \alpha} \approx c\alpha^2 \;\;(\text{যদি } \alpha \ll 1), \qquad [\mathrm{H}^+] = c\alpha \approx \sqrt{K_a c}\]

অনুরূপভাবে মৃদু ক্ষার \(\mathrm{BOH}\)-এর জন্য \(K_b = \frac{c\alpha^2}{1-\alpha}\) এবং \([\mathrm{OH}^-] \approx \sqrt{K_b c}\)। \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় যেকোনো অনুবন্ধী অ্যাসিড–ক্ষার যুগলের জন্য:

\[K_a \times K_b = K_w = 10^{-14} \qquad \text{এবং} \qquad \mathrm{p}K_a + \mathrm{p}K_b = \mathrm{p}K_w = 14\]

5. Common Ion Effect, Salt Hydrolysis, Buffers, and Solubility Product (\(K_{sp}\))

Common Ion Effect: Suppressing the ionization of a weak electrolyte (or solubility of a sparingly soluble salt) by adding a strong electrolyte that shares a common ion, in accordance with Le Chatelier's principle (e.g., adding \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}\) to \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}\) reduces \([\mathrm{H}^+]\)).

Salt Hydrolysis Summary (at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\))

Salt Type Example Nature of Solution \(\mathrm{pH}\) Formula
Strong Acid + Strong Base \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), \(\mathrm{KBr}\) Neutral (no hydrolysis) \(\mathrm{pH} = 7\)
Weak Acid + Strong Base \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}\), \(\mathrm{NaNO}_2\) Basic (anionic hydrolysis) \(\mathrm{pH} = 7 + \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{p}K_a + \log_{10} c)\)
Strong Acid + Weak Base \(\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\), \(\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{NO}_3\) Acidic (cationic hydrolysis) \(\mathrm{pH} = 7 - \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{p}K_b + \log_{10} c)\)
Weak Acid + Weak Base \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONH}_4\) Depends on \(\mathrm{p}K_a - \mathrm{p}K_b\) \(\mathrm{pH} = 7 + \frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{p}K_a - \mathrm{p}K_b)\)

Buffer Solutions (Henderson–Hasselbalch Equations)

A buffer solution resists changes in \(\mathrm{pH}\) upon dilution or addition of small amounts of acid or base:

\[\text{Acidic Buffer (weak acid + its salt): } \mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log_{10}\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]}\] \[\text{Basic Buffer (weak base + its salt): } \mathrm{pOH} = \mathrm{p}K_b + \log_{10}\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Base}]}, \quad \mathrm{pH} = 14 - \mathrm{pOH}\]

Solubility Product Constant (\(K_{sp}\))

For a sparingly soluble salt \(\mathrm{M}_x\mathrm{X}_y(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons x\mathrm{M}^{p+}(\mathrm{aq}) + y\mathrm{X}^{q-}(\mathrm{aq})\) with molar solubility \(S\) in pure water:

\[K_{sp} = [\mathrm{M}^{p+}]^x [\mathrm{X}^{q-}]^y = (xS)^x (yS)^y = x^x y^y S^{x+y}\]
  • Precipitation criterion: Compare ionic product \(Q_{sp}\) with \(K_{sp}\). Precipitation occurs if \(Q_{sp} > K_{sp}\); solution is unsaturated if \(Q_{sp} < K_{sp}\); saturated equilibrium exists if \(Q_{sp} = K_{sp}\).
  • Effect of \(\mathrm{pH}\): Salts of weak acids (e.g., phosphates, carbonates, benzoates) become more soluble at lower \(\mathrm{pH}\) because \(\mathrm{H}^+\) protonates the anion, shifting dissolution forward.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Relating \(K_p\) and \(K_c\) for Gaseous Dissociation

Problem: Assume that at \(400\,\mathrm{K}\), the equilibrium constant \(K_c\) for the dissociation \(\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{O}_4(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{NO}_2(\mathrm{g})\) is \(2.50\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\) (hypothetical value). Calculate \(K_p\) at \(400\,\mathrm{K}\) using \(R = 0.0831\,\mathrm{bar\,L\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\).

Solution:

\[ \Delta n_g = n_{\text{products(g)}} - n_{\text{reactants(g)}} = 2 - 1 = 1 \] \[ K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n_g} = 2.50 \times (0.0831 \times 400)^1 = 2.50 \times 33.24 = 83.1\,\mathrm{bar} \]

Example 2: Connecting Standard Gibbs Energy Change (\(\Delta G^\circ\)) to \(K\)

Problem: A hypothetical reversible reaction at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) has \(\Delta G^\circ = -11.41\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) (assumed). Using \(2.303 RT \approx 5705.8\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), estimate the equilibrium constant \(K\).

Solution:

\[ \Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K \implies \log_{10} K = \frac{-\Delta G^\circ}{2.303 RT} = \frac{11410\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}{5705.8\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}} \approx 2.00 \] \[ K = 10^{2.00} = 100 \]

Example 3: Calculating Degree of Ionization and \(\mathrm{pH}\) of a Weak Acid

Problem: Consider a hypothetical \(0.05\,\mathrm{M}\) aqueous solution of a weak monobasic acid \(\mathrm{HA}\) having \(K_a = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) (assumed). Find its degree of ionization \(\alpha\) and \(\mathrm{pH}\).

Solution: Assuming \(\alpha \ll 1\):

\[ \alpha \approx \sqrt{\frac{K_a}{c}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.0 \times 10^{-5}}{0.05}} = \sqrt{4.0 \times 10^{-4}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-2} \;\;(2\%) \]

Since \(\alpha = 0.02 < 0.05\), the approximation is valid. Thus:

\[ [\mathrm{H}^+] = c\alpha = 0.05 \times 0.02 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{M} \implies \mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}(1.0 \times 10^{-3}) = 3.00 \]

Example 4: Designing an Acidic Buffer Solution

Problem: Equal volumes of hypothetical \(0.20\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}\) (\(\mathrm{p}K_a = 4.76\)) and \(0.40\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}\) are mixed at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\). Calculate the \(\mathrm{pH}\) of the resulting buffer (take \(\log_{10} 2 = 0.30\)).

Solution: Mixing equal volumes halves each concentration: \([\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) and \([\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}] = 0.20\,\mathrm{M}\).

\[ \mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log_{10}\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} = 4.76 + \log_{10}\left(\frac{0.20}{0.10}\right) = 4.76 + 0.30 = 5.06 \]

Example 5: Molar Solubility and Common Ion Suppression via \(K_{sp}\)

Problem: Suppose a sparingly soluble hydroxide \(\mathrm{M(OH)}_2\) has \(K_{sp} = 4.0 \times 10^{-12}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) (assumed). Calculate its molar solubility in (a) pure water and (b) \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{NaOH}\).

Solution:

(a) In pure water, \(\mathrm{M(OH)}_2(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{M}^{2+} + 2\mathrm{OH}^-\), so \([\mathrm{M}^{2+}] = S\) and \([\mathrm{OH}^-] = 2S\):

\[ K_{sp} = S(2S)^2 = 4S^3 = 4.0 \times 10^{-12} \implies S^3 = 1.0 \times 10^{-12} \implies S = 1.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}} \]

(b) In \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), \([\mathrm{OH}^-] = 0.10 + 2S' \approx 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\):

\[ K_{sp} = S'(0.10)^2 = 4.0 \times 10^{-12} \implies S' = \frac{4.0 \times 10^{-12}}{1.0 \times 10^{-2}} = 4.0 \times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}} \]

উদাহরণ ১: গ্যাসীয় বিয়োজনে \(K_p\) এবং \(K_c\)-এর সম্পর্ক নির্ণয়

সমস্যা: ধরি, \(400\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় \(\mathrm{N}_2\mathrm{O}_4(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{NO}_2(\mathrm{g})\) বিক্রিয়ার সাম্যধ্রুবক \(K_c = 2.50\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\) (কাল্পনিক মান)। \(R = 0.0831\,\mathrm{bar\,L\,mol^{-1}\,K^{-1}}\) ধরে \(K_p\) নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

\[ \Delta n_g = n_{\text{products(g)}} - n_{\text{reactants(g)}} = 2 - 1 = 1 \] \[ K_p = K_c (RT)^{\Delta n_g} = 2.50 \times (0.0831 \times 400)^1 = 2.50 \times 33.24 = 83.1\,\mathrm{bar} \]

উদাহরণ ২: প্রমাণ গিবস মুক্ত শক্তি (\(\Delta G^\circ\)) থেকে \(K\) নির্ণয়

সমস্যা: \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় একটি কাল্পনিক উভমুখী বিক্রিয়ার \(\Delta G^\circ = -11.41\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) (ধরে নেওয়া মান)। \(298\,\mathrm{K}\)-তে \(2.303 RT \approx 5705.8\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}\) ধরে সাম্যধ্রুবক \(K\) নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

\[ \Delta G^\circ = -2.303 RT \log_{10} K \implies \log_{10} K = \frac{-\Delta G^\circ}{2.303 RT} = \frac{11410\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}}{5705.8\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}}} \approx 2.00 \] \[ K = 10^{2.00} = 100 \]

উদাহরণ ৩: মৃদু অ্যাসিডের বিয়োজন মাত্রা ও \(\mathrm{pH}\) নির্ণয়

সমস্যা: \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় একটি কাল্পনিক \(0.05\,\mathrm{M}\) মৃদু একক্ষারীয় অ্যাসিড \(\mathrm{HA}\)-এর \(K_a = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}\) (ধরে নেওয়া মান)। এর বিয়োজন মাত্রা \(\alpha\) এবং \(\mathrm{pH}\) নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান: \(\alpha \ll 1\) ধরে:

\[ \alpha \approx \sqrt{\frac{K_a}{c}} = \sqrt{\frac{2.0 \times 10^{-5}}{0.05}} = \sqrt{4.0 \times 10^{-4}} = 2.0 \times 10^{-2} \;\;(2\%) \]

যেহেতু \(\alpha = 0.02 < 0.05\), আসন্নীকরণটি বৈধ। অতএব:

\[ [\mathrm{H}^+] = c\alpha = 0.05 \times 0.02 = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{M} \implies \mathrm{pH} = -\log_{10}(1.0 \times 10^{-3}) = 3.00 \]

উদাহরণ ৪: আম্লিক বাফার দ্রবণের \(\mathrm{pH}\) নির্ণয়

সমস্যা: \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় কাল্পনিক \(0.20\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}\) (\(\mathrm{p}K_a = 4.76\)) এবং \(0.40\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}\) সমআয়তনে মেশানো হলো। উৎপন্ন বাফার দ্রবণের \(\mathrm{pH}\) নির্ণয় করো (\(\log_{10} 2 = 0.30\))।

সমাধান: সমআয়তনে মেশালে উভয়ের গাঢ়ত্ব অর্ধেক হয়: \([\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COOH}] = 0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) এবং \([\mathrm{CH}_3\mathrm{COONa}] = 0.20\,\mathrm{M}\)।

\[ \mathrm{pH} = \mathrm{p}K_a + \log_{10}\frac{[\text{Salt}]}{[\text{Acid}]} = 4.76 + \log_{10}\left(\frac{0.20}{0.10}\right) = 4.76 + 0.30 = 5.06 \]

উদাহরণ ৫: \(K_{sp}\) থেকে মোলার দ্রাব্যতা ও সম-আয়ন প্রভাব

সমস্যা: ধরি, \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) তাপমাত্রায় স্বল্পদ্রাব্য হাইড্রোক্সাইড \(\mathrm{M(OH)}_2\)-এর \(K_{sp} = 4.0 \times 10^{-12}\)। (ক) বিশুদ্ধ জলে এবং (খ) \(0.10\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) দ্রবণে এর মোলার দ্রাব্যতা নির্ণয় করো।

সমাধান:

(ক) বিশুদ্ধ জলে, \(\mathrm{M(OH)}_2(\mathrm{s}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{M}^{2+} + 2\mathrm{OH}^-\), তাই \([\mathrm{M}^{2+}] = S\) এবং \([\mathrm{OH}^-] = 2S\):

Practice Question 1

For the gaseous equilibrium \(\mathrm{A}(\mathrm{g}) + 2\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons 2\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{g})\) at a fixed temperature, the equilibrium constant is \(K_1 = 25\). What is the equilibrium constant \(K_2\) for the reaction \(\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{A}(\mathrm{g}) + \mathrm{B}(\mathrm{g})\) at the same temperature?

  • (A) \(0.04\)
  • (B) \(0.20\)
  • (C) \(5.0\)
  • (D) \(12.5\)

Answer: (B) \(0.20\)

Worked Solution: Reversing the original reaction gives \(2\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{g}) \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{A}(\mathrm{g}) + 2\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{g})\) with constant \(K' = 1/K_1 = 1/25\).   • (B) \(7.00\)

Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist

  • Pure solids and liquids: Never include \([\mathrm{solid}]\) or \([\mathrm{liquid}]\) in \(K_c\), \(K_p\), or \(\Delta n_g\) calculations.
  • Inert gas addition: Adding an inert gas at constant volume causes no shift; only at constant pressure (increasing volume) does it shift toward more gas moles.
  • Very dilute acids/bases: Do not report \(\mathrm{pH} = 8\) for \(10^{-8}\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{HCl}\); always include water's \(10^{-7}\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{H}^+\) contribution.
  • Comparing \(K_{sp}\) values: A larger \(K_{sp}\) means higher solubility only when comparing salts of the same stoichiometry (e.g., both \(1:1\)); otherwise, compute \(S\) explicitly.

Concise Final Recap

Equilibrium is a dynamic balance where forward and reverse rates match. \(K_c\) and \(K_p = K_c(RT)^{\Delta n_g}\) depend solely on temperature, \(\Delta G^\circ = -RT\ln K\) links equilibrium to spontaneity, and Le Chatelier's principle predicts shifts under external stress. In aqueous solutions, \(\mathrm{pH} + \mathrm{pOH} = 14\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), \(K_a \times K_b = K_w\) governs conjugate pairs, buffers obey the Henderson–Hasselbalch equations, and precipitation occurs whenever \(Q_{sp} > K_{sp}\).
