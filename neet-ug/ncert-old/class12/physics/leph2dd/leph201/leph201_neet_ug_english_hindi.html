Learning Objectives & Chapter Overview

English

Light is electromagnetic radiation in the visible wavelength range of approximately \(400\,\mathrm{nm}\) to \(750\,\mathrm{nm}\), travelling in vacuum at \(c = 2.99792458 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) (commonly taken as \(3 \times 10^8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\)). When light interacts with objects much larger than its wavelength, wave diffraction effects are negligible, and light can be modelled as travelling along straight-line paths called rays.

By completing this tutorial, you will be able to:

  • Apply the Cartesian sign convention accurately to spherical mirrors, single refracting surfaces, and thin lenses.
  • Use the mirror equation, magnification relations, and Snell's law to locate and characterize real and virtual images.
  • Calculate critical angles and analyse Total Internal Reflection (TIR) in prisms and optical fibres.
  • Derive and apply the Lens Maker's formula, lens power, and combinations of thin lenses in contact.
  • Determine prism deviation, minimum deviation, and refractive index using the prism relation.
  • Calculate magnifying power and tube length for simple microscopes, compound microscopes, and astronomical telescopes.

Reflection at Spherical Mirrors & Sign Convention

English

At any smooth reflecting surface (plane or curved), the laws of reflection hold at the point of incidence: (1) the angle of incidence \(i\) equals the angle of reflection \(r\), and (2) the incident ray, reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane. For a spherical mirror, the normal at any point passes through the centre of curvature \(C\). The geometric centre of the mirror is the pole \(P\), and the line joining \(P\) and \(C\) is the principal axis.

Cartesian Sign Convention

  • All distances are measured from the pole \(P\) (for mirrors) or optical centre \(O\) (for lenses).
  • Distances measured along the direction of incident light are taken as positive; distances measured opposite to the direction of incident light are negative.
  • Heights measured upwards perpendicular to the principal axis are positive; heights measured downwards are negative.

Focal Length, Mirror Equation & Magnification

For paraxial rays (rays close to the principal axis making small angles with it), the focal length \(f\) is half the radius of curvature \(R\):

\[f = \frac{R}{2}, \qquad \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}, \qquad m = \frac{h'}{h} = -\frac{v}{u} = \frac{f}{f - u} = \frac{f - v}{f}\]

Here \(u\) is the object distance, \(v\) is the image distance, \(f\) is negative for a concave mirror and positive for a convex mirror. A negative magnification (\(m < 0\)) indicates an inverted (real for a real object) image, while \(m > 0\) indicates an erect (virtual for a real object) image.

NEET UG Conceptual Insight: Partial Covering of a Mirror or Lens
If the lower half of a concave mirror (or convex lens) is covered with an opaque black paper, the complete image of the object is still formed because rays from every point of the object reflect/refract from the remaining uncovered half. However, since the reflecting/refracting area is halved, the brightness (intensity) of the image reduces to half.

Refraction, Apparent Depth & Total Internal Reflection

English

When an obliquely incident ray crosses the boundary between two transparent media, its direction changes due to a change in speed of light. According to Snell's Law:

\[n_1 \sin i = n_2 \sin r \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = n_{21} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}\]

Here \(n_{21}\) is the refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1, and \(n_{12} = 1/n_{21}\). Remember that optical density (determined by refractive index \(n = c/v\)) is distinct from mass density: for example, turpentine has a lower mass density than water, yet its optical density (\(n \approx 1.47\)) is higher than that of water (\(n = 1.33\)).

Parallel Slab, Lateral Shift & Apparent Depth

  • Parallel-sided glass slab: The emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray (zero net angular deviation), but undergoes a lateral shift.
  • Apparent depth (near-normal viewing): An object at real depth \(h_{\text{real}}\) in a medium of refractive index \(n_1\) viewed from a medium of refractive index \(n_2\) appears at depth \(h_{\text{app}} = h_{\text{real}} \cdot \frac{n_2}{n_1}\). For viewing from air (\(n_2 = 1\)) into a medium of index \(n\): \[h_{\text{app}} = \frac{h_{\text{real}}}{n}, \qquad \text{Normal shift } (\Delta t) = t \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)\]

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

When light travels from an optically denser medium (\(n_1\)) to an optically rarer medium (\(n_2 < n_1\)), the refracted ray bends away from the normal. As the angle of incidence \(i\) increases, the angle of refraction \(r\) reaches \(90^\circ\) at a specific angle called the critical angle \(i_c\):

\[\sin i_c = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = n_{21} \quad (n_1 > n_2)\]

For \(i > i_c\), no refraction occurs and the entire incident light is reflected back into the denser medium—this is Total Internal Reflection (TIR). Unlike ordinary mirror reflection, TIR involves zero transmission loss.

Tutorial Diagram 2: Refraction, critical angle (r = 90°), and Total Internal Reflection (i > i_c).

Key Applications of TIR:

  • Totally reflecting prisms: Right-angled isosceles prisms (\(45^\circ\text{–}90^\circ\text{–}45^\circ\)) made of crown or flint glass (\(i_c < 45^\circ\)) bend light by \(90^\circ\) or \(180^\circ\) with virtually \(100\%\) efficiency.
  • Optical fibres: Consist of a high-index glass/quartz core surrounded by a lower-index cladding (\(n_{\text{core}} > n_{\text{cladding}}\)), guiding light signals over long distances via repeated TIR even along curved paths.
  • Diamond: High refractive index (\(n \approx 2.42\)) gives a very small critical angle (\(i_c \approx 24.4^\circ\)), causing light to undergo multiple internal reflections and sparkle brilliantly.

Refraction at Spherical Surfaces & Thin Lenses

English

For paraxial rays refracting from a medium of refractive index \(n_1\) into a medium of refractive index \(n_2\) across a spherical surface of radius of curvature \(R\):

\[\frac{n_2}{v} - \frac{n_1}{u} = \frac{n_2 - n_1}{R}\]

Lens Maker's Formula, Thin Lens Equation & Power

Applying this relation successively to the two surfaces of a thin lens (material index \(n_2\), surrounding medium index \(n_1\), radii of curvature \(R_1\) and \(R_2\)) gives the Lens Maker's Formula and the Thin Lens Formula:

\[\frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right), \qquad \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}, \qquad m = \frac{h'}{h} = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{f}{f + u} = \frac{f - v}{f}\]

The power \(P\) of a lens is \(P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (in m)}}\) (or \(P = \frac{n_1}{f}\) if surrounding medium is not air, though in standard problems in air \(P = 1/f\)), measured in dioptre (D), where \(1\,\mathrm{D} = 1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\). Power is positive for a converging lens and negative for a diverging lens.

Combination of Thin Lenses in Contact

When thin lenses of focal lengths \(f_1, f_2, f_3, \dots\) are placed coaxially in contact:

\[\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} + \frac{1}{f_3} + \dots, \qquad P_{\text{eq}} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots, \qquad m_{\text{total}} = m_1 \cdot m_2 \cdot m_3 \dots\]

If two thin lenses of focal lengths \(f_1\) and \(f_2\) are separated by a distance \(d\) in air, the equivalent focal length is given by \(\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} - \frac{d}{f_1 f_2}\).

पतले लेंस (पदार्थ का अपवर्तनांक \(n_2\), बाहरी माध्यम \(n_1\), वक्रता त्रिज्याएँ \(R_1\) व \(R_2\)) के दोनों पृष्ठों पर यह सूत्र लगाने पर लेंस निर्माता सूत्र तथा पतले लेंस का सूत्र प्राप्त होते हैं:

\[\frac{1}{f} = \left(\frac{n_2}{n_1} - 1\right)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right), \qquad \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}, \qquad m = \frac{h'}{h} = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{f}{f + u} = \frac{f - v}{f}\]

लेंस की क्षमता \(P\), \(P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (मीटर में)}}\) होती है, जिसका SI मात्रक डायोप्टर (D) है (\(1\,\mathrm{D} = 1\,\mathrm{m^{-1}}\))। अभिसारी (उत्तल) लेंस की क्षमता धनात्मक और अपसारी (अवतल) लेंस की क्षमता ऋणात्मक होती है।

संपर्क में रखे पतले लेंसों का संयोजन

जब \(f_1, f_2, f_3, \dots\) फोकस दूरी के पतले लेंस समाक्षीय रूप से संपर्क में रखे जाते हैं:

\[\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} + \frac{1}{f_3} + \dots, \qquad P_{\text{eq}} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots, \qquad m_{\text{total}} = m_1 \cdot m_2 \cdot m_3 \dots\]

यदि \(f_1\) व \(f_2\) फोकस दूरी के दो पतले लेंस वायु में \(d\) दूरी पर रखे हों, तो तुल्य फोकस दूरी \(\frac{1}{f_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2} - \frac{d}{f_1 f_2}\) होती है।

Refraction Through a Triangular Prism

English

Consider a triangular glass prism of refracting angle \(A\) and refractive index \(n_{21} = n_2/n_1\). A ray incident at angle \(i\) refracts at angle \(r_1\) on the first face, strikes the second face at angle \(r_2\), and emerges at angle \(e\).

\[r_1 + r_2 = A, \qquad \delta = i + e - A\]

Because \(i\) and \(e\) appear symmetrically in \(\delta = i + e - A\), any deviation \(\delta > D_m\) corresponds to two angles of incidence (\(i_1\) and \(i_2\) such that \(i_2 = e_1\)). At the angle of minimum deviation \(D_m\), the ray passes symmetrically through the prism:

\[i = e, \quad r_1 = r_2 = \frac{A}{2}, \qquad i = \frac{A + D_m}{2} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad n_{21} = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)}\]

For a thin prism (small refracting angle \(A\), typically \(\le 10^\circ\)), \(\sin \theta \approx \theta\) gives \(D_m = (n_{21} - 1)A\), which is independent of the angle of incidence (for small \(i\)).

Example 1: Applying the Mirror Equation and Magnification

Problem (Assumed values): A small object of height \(2.0\,\mathrm{cm}\) is placed at a distance of \(24\,\mathrm{cm}\) in front of a concave mirror of radius of curvature \(32\,\mathrm{cm}\). Find the position, nature, and height of the image.

Solution:

Using the Cartesian sign convention, for a concave mirror:

\(R = -32\,\mathrm{cm} \implies f = \frac{R}{2} = -16\,\mathrm{cm}\), and \(u = -24\,\mathrm{cm}\), \(h = +2.0\,\mathrm{cm}\).

Applying the mirror equation \(\frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{f}\):

\[ \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{-16} - \frac{1}{-24} = -\frac{1}{16} + \frac{1}{24} = \frac{-3 + 2}{48} = -\frac{1}{48} \implies v = -48\,\mathrm{cm} \]

Linear magnification: \(m = -\frac{v}{u} = -\frac{(-48)}{(-24)} = -2\).

Image height: \(h' = m \cdot h = (-2)(2.0\,\mathrm{cm}) = -4.0\,\mathrm{cm}\).

Result: The image is formed \(48\,\mathrm{cm}\) in front of the mirror, and is real, inverted, and magnified with height \(4.0\,\mathrm{cm}\).

Example 2: Total Internal Reflection and Area of Emergence

Problem (Assumed values): A point light source is placed at the bottom of a tank filled with a transparent liquid of refractive index \(n = \sqrt{2}\) up to a depth of \(h = 1.0\,\mathrm{m}\). Find the area of the liquid surface through which light can emerge into air (\(n_{\text{air}} = 1\)).

Solution:

Light emerges only within a cone whose half-angle equals the critical angle \(i_c\):

\[ \sin i_c = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies i_c = 45^\circ \]

The radius \(r\) of the circular patch on the surface is:

\[ r = h \tan i_c = (1.0\,\mathrm{m}) \tan 45^\circ = 1.0\,\mathrm{m} \]

Therefore, the area of emergence is:

\[ A = \pi r^2 = \pi (1.0)^2 = \pi\,\mathrm{m^2} \approx 3.14\,\mathrm{m^2} \]

Example 4: Minimum Deviation in a Prism

Problem (Assumed values): An equilateral prism (\(A = 60^\circ\)) is made of glass of refractive index \(n = \sqrt{3}\). Calculate the angle of minimum deviation \(D_m\) and the angle of incidence at minimum deviation.

Solution:

From the prism formula:

\[ n = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{A + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)} \implies \sqrt{3} = \frac{\sin\left(\frac{60^\circ + D_m}{2}\right)}{\sin 30^\circ} \]

Since \(\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}\):

\[ \sin\left(\frac{60^\circ + D_m}{2}\right) = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \sin 60^\circ \implies \frac{60^\circ + D_m}{2} = 60^\circ \implies D_m = 60^\circ \]

Angle of incidence at minimum deviation: \(i = \frac{A + D_m}{2} = \frac{60^\circ + 60^\circ}{2} = 60^\circ\).

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

English
  • Sign Convention Mix-ups: Never substitute signs into an algebraic formula twice. Always assign signs once to known numerical values (\(u, v, f, R\)), leaving the unknown variable without an assumed sign.
  • Optical vs. Mass Density: Higher mass density does not guarantee a higher refractive index. Always compare \(n\) values directly.
  • TIR Direction Trap: Total Internal Reflection occurs only when light travels from a denser medium to a rarer medium (\(n_{\text{incident}} > n_{\text{refracted}}\)) and \(i > i_c\).
  • Prism Maximum Angle for No Emergence: No ray can emerge from the second face of a prism of any angle of incidence if \(A > 2 i_c\).
  • Microscope vs. Telescope Lenses: In a microscope, both \(f_o\) and \(f_e\) are small (with \(f_e > f_o\)). In a telescope, \(f_o\) is very large (and aperture is large) while \(f_e\) is small.
हिन्दी (Hindi)
  • चिह्न परिपाटी में त्रुटि: किसी सूत्र में चिह्न दो बार न लगाएँ। केवल ज्ञात राशियों (\(u, v, f, R\)) के संख्यात्मक मानों में एक बार चिह्न रखें, तथा अज्ञात राशि को बिना चिह्न माने हल करें।
  • प्रकाशिक बनाम द्रव्यमान घनत्व: अधिक द्रव्यमान घनत्व का अर्थ सदैव अधिक अपवर्तनांक नहीं होता। हमेशा \(n\) के मानों की तुलना करें।
  • TIR की दिशा: पूर्ण आंतरिक परावर्तन केवल तभी होता है जब प्रकाश सघन माध्यम से विरल माध्यम में जाए (\(n_{\text{आपतित}} > n_{\text{अपवर्तित}}\)) और \(i > i_c\) हो।
  • प्रिज्म से निर्गमन न होने की शर्त: यदि \(A > 2 i_c\) हो, तो किसी भी आपतन कोण के लिए प्रिज्म के दूसरे पृष्ठ से कोई किरण बाहर नहीं निकलेगी।
  • सूक्ष्मदर्शी बनाम दूरदर्शी के लेंस: सूक्ष्मदर्शी में \(f_o\) व \(f_e\) दोनों छोटे होते हैं (\(f_e > f_o\)), जबकि दूरदर्शी में \(f_o\) बहुत बड़ा (और द्वारक बड़ा) तथा \(f_e\) छोटा होता है।
