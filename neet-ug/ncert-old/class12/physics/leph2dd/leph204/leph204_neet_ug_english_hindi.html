2. Evolution of Atomic Models: From Thomson to Rutherford / परमाण्वीय मॉडलों का विकास: थॉमसन से रदरफोर्ड तक

English

Thomson's Plum Pudding Model

After J. J. Thomson demonstrated in 1897 that all atoms contain identical negatively charged electrons while remaining electrically neutral overall, he proposed in 1898 that an atom is a sphere of uniformly distributed positive charge in which electrons are embedded like seeds in a watermelon. In this picture, mass and positive charge are spread nearly uniformly throughout the atomic volume (radius \(\approx 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\)).

Why it failed: Although electrons could sit in electrostatic equilibrium at the centre or specific positions, the configuration is electrostatically unstable against small perturbations, and it cannot explain why alpha particles suffer large-angle deflections or why atoms emit sharp discrete spectral lines.

Geiger–Marsden Alpha-Particle Scattering and Rutherford's Nuclear Model

At Rutherford's suggestion, Hans Geiger and Ernst Marsden directed a narrow collimated beam of \(5.5\,\mathrm{MeV}\) \(\alpha\)-particles (from a \(^{214}_{83}\mathrm{Bi}\) source, collimated through lead bricks) onto an ultra-thin gold foil (thickness \(\approx 2.1 \times 10^{-7}\,\mathrm{m}\)) inside an evacuated chamber. Scattered \(\alpha\)-particles struck a rotatable zinc sulphide (\(\mathrm{ZnS}\)) screen, producing tiny light flashes (scintillations) counted through a microscope as a function of scattering angle \(\theta\).

  • Most \(\alpha\)-particles passed straight through with little or no deflection, showing that most of the atom is empty space.
  • Only about \(0.14\%\) scattered through angles greater than \(1^\circ\).
  • About \(1\) in \(8000\) deflected by more than \(90^\circ\) (some nearly \(180^\circ\) straight back).

Rutherford concluded that the entire positive charge (\(+Ze\)) and almost all the mass of the atom are concentrated in a tiny central core called the nucleus (size \(\approx 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\) to \(10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}\)), whereas the atom's radius is around \(10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\) (about \(10^4\) to \(10^5\) times larger than the nucleus). Light atomic electrons exert negligible force on the much heavier \(\alpha\)-particle (mass \(= 4\,\mathrm{u}\), charge \(= +2e\)), and the gold nucleus (\(Z = 79\), roughly \(50\) times heavier than an \(\alpha\)-particle) remains practically stationary during a single scattering event.

English

Impact Parameter, Scattering Angle, and Distance of Closest Approach

Under single-nucleus Coulomb repulsion, the electrostatic force magnitude between an \(\alpha\)-particle (charge \(2e\)) and a stationary nucleus (charge \(Ze\)) at separation \(r\) is:

\[F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(2e)(Ze)}{r^2}\]

The trajectory depends on the impact parameter \(b\), defined as the perpendicular distance of the incoming \(\alpha\)-particle's initial velocity vector from the centre of the nucleus:

  • Large \(b\): Weak Coulomb repulsion, resulting in a very small deflection (\(\theta \approx 0\)).
  • Small \(b\): Close approach to the nucleus, experiencing intense repulsion and large scattering angle \(\theta\).
  • Head-on collision (\(b = 0\)): The \(\alpha\)-particle moves directly toward the nucleus, comes momentarily to rest at the distance of closest approach \(d\), and rebounds straight back (\(\theta = 180^\circ\)).

By conservation of mechanical energy for a head-on encounter with initial kinetic energy \(K\):

\[K = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{(2e)(Ze)}{d} \implies d = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 K}\]

Because the \(\alpha\)-particle turns around before touching the nuclear surface, \(d\) sets an upper limit on the nuclear radius (for \(7.7\,\mathrm{MeV}\) \(\alpha\)-particles on gold, \(d \approx 30\,\mathrm{fm}\), whereas the actual gold nuclear radius is about \(6\,\mathrm{fm}\), where \(1\,\mathrm{fm} = 10^{-15}\,\mathrm{m}\)).

Classical Electron Orbits and Why Rutherford's Model Failed

In a classical hydrogen atom, the electrostatic attraction between the proton and the orbiting electron supplies the centripetal force:

\[\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{e^2}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \implies r = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 mv^2}\]

The electron's kinetic energy \(K\), electrostatic potential energy \(U\), and total mechanical energy \(E\) are:

\[K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}, \qquad U = -\frac{e^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r}, \qquad E = K + U = -\frac{e^2}{8\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\]

Notice the fundamental bound-orbit relation: \(E = -K = \frac{U}{2}\). The negative sign of \(E\) confirms the electron is bound to the nucleus.

Two Fatal Classical Contradictions:

  1. Atomic Instability: An orbiting electron experiences continuous centripetal acceleration. According to classical electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charge continuously radiates electromagnetic waves, losing energy and spiralling into the nucleus in a tiny fraction of a second.
  2. Continuous vs. Line Spectrum: As the electron spirals inward, its orbital frequency increases continuously, so classical theory predicts a continuous spectrum of emitted frequencies—directly contradicting the sharp discrete line spectra observed experimentally.

3. Atomic Spectra and Bohr's Quantum Model / परमाण्वीय स्पेक्ट्रम और बोर का क्वांटम मॉडल

English

Emission and Absorption Line Spectra

Dense solids, liquids, and compressed gases emit a continuous spectrum due to strong interatomic interactions. In contrast, when a rarefied atomic gas (where atoms are far apart) is excited electrically or thermally, it emits radiation containing only specific discrete wavelengths—an emission line spectrum (bright lines on a dark background). Each element has a unique set of wavelengths acting as an atomic fingerprint.

Conversely, when white light passes through a cool rarefied gas, the transmitted spectrum shows dark lines at the exact wavelengths that the gas emits when excited; this is an absorption spectrum.

In atomic hydrogen, empirical spectral lines group into series: the Lyman series in the ultraviolet (\(91\,\mathrm{nm}\) to \(122\,\mathrm{nm}\)), the Balmer series in the visible/near-UV (\(365\,\mathrm{nm}\) to \(656\,\mathrm{nm}\)), and the Paschen series in the infrared (\(820\,\mathrm{nm}\) to \(1875\,\mathrm{nm}\)).

Bohr's Three Postulates (1913)

To resolve the stability and spectral puzzles for hydrogenic (single-electron) atoms, Niels Bohr combined classical mechanics with early quantum ideas:

  1. Stationary Orbits: An electron can revolve in certain discrete stable orbits without radiating electromagnetic energy. Each allowed orbit has a fixed total energy.
  2. Quantisation of Angular Momentum: Only those circular orbits are permitted for which the electron's orbital angular momentum \(L\) is an integral multiple of \(\frac{h}{2\pi}\): \[L = mvr = n\frac{h}{2\pi}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots\] where \(h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\) is Planck's constant and \(n\) is the principal quantum number.
  3. Frequency Condition (Quantum Transitions): Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps between two stationary states of energies \(E_i\) and \(E_f\). For emission (\(E_i > E_f\)), the emitted photon has energy: \[h\nu = E_i - E_f\] For absorption, a photon of the exact same energy raises the electron from a lower state to a higher state.

उत्सर्जन और अवशोषण रेखा स्पेक्ट्रम

सघन ठोस, द्रव और संपीड़ित गैसें अंतर-परमाण्वीय अन्योन्यक्रियाओं के कारण सतत स्पेक्ट्रम उत्सर्जित करती हैं। इसके विपरीत, जब किसी विरल परमाण्वीय गैस (जहाँ परमाणु एक-दूसरे से काफी दूर होते हैं) को विद्युत या ऊष्मीय रूप से उत्तेजित किया जाता है, तो वह केवल कुछ विशिष्ट विविक्त तरंगदैर्ध्य वाला विकिरण उत्सर्जित करती है—इसे उत्सर्जन रेखा स्पेक्ट्रम (काली पृष्ठभूमि पर चमकीली रेखाएँ) कहते हैं। प्रत्येक तत्व की तरंगदैर्ध्य का समुच्चय अद्वितीय होता है और उसकी पहचान के अंगुली-चिह्न (fingerprint) जैसा कार्य करता है।

इसके विपरीत, जब श्वेत प्रकाश को किसी ठंडी विरल गैस से गुजारा जाता है, तो पारगमित स्पेक्ट्रम में ठीक उन्हीं तरंगदैर्ध्य पर काली रेखाएँ दिखाई देती हैं जिन्हें वह गैस उत्तेजित होने पर उत्सर्जित करती है; इसे अवशोषण स्पेक्ट्रम कहते हैं।

परमाण्वीय हाइड्रोजन में स्पेक्ट्रमी रेखाएँ विभिन्न श्रेणियों में पाई जाती हैं: पराबैंगनी क्षेत्र में लाइमन श्रेणी (\(91\,\mathrm{nm}\) से \(122\,\mathrm{nm}\)), दृश्य/निकट-पराबैंगनी क्षेत्र में बामर श्रेणी (\(365\,\mathrm{nm}\) से \(656\,\mathrm{nm}\)), और अवरक्त क्षेत्र में पाशन श्रेणी (\(820\,\mathrm{nm}\) से \(1875\,\mathrm{nm}\))।

बोर के तीन अभिगृहीत (1913)

हाइड्रोजन-सदृश (एकल-इलेक्ट्रॉन) परमाणुओं के स्थायित्व और स्पेक्ट्रमी पहेली को सुलझाने के लिए नील्स बोर ने चिरसम्मत यांत्रिकी को प्रारंभिक क्वांटम विचारों के साथ जोड़ा:

  1. स्थायी कक्षाएँ: इलेक्ट्रॉन नाभिक के चारों ओर केवल कुछ निश्चित स्थायी कक्षाओं में बिना कोई विद्युतचुंबकीय ऊर्जा उत्सर्जित किए परिक्रमा कर सकता है। प्रत्येक अनुमत कक्षा की एक निश्चित कुल ऊर्जा होती है।
  2. कोणीय संवेग का क्वांटमीकरण: केवल वे ही वृत्तीय कक्षाएँ अनुमत होती हैं जिनमें इलेक्ट्रॉन का कक्षीय कोणीय संवेग \(L\), \(\frac{h}{2\pi}\) का पूर्णांक गुणज होता है: \[L = mvr = n\frac{h}{2\pi}, \qquad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots\] जहाँ \(h \approx 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\,\mathrm{J\cdot s}\) प्लांक नियतांक है और \(n\) मुख्य क्वांटम संख्या है।
  3. आवृत्ति प्रतिबंध (क्वांटम संक्रमण): विकिरण का उत्सर्जन या अवशोषण केवल तब होता है जब इलेक्ट्रॉन \(E_i\) और \(E_f\) ऊर्जा वाली दो स्थायी अवस्थाओं के बीच संक्रमण करता है। उत्सर्जन (\(E_i > E_f\)) के लिए, उत्सर्जित फोटॉन की ऊर्जा होती है: \[h\nu = E_i - E_f\] अवशोषण में, ठीक इसी ऊर्जा का फोटॉन इलेक्ट्रॉन को निम्न अवस्था से उच्च अवस्था में पहुँचा देता है।

\[r_n = \frac{n^2 h^2 \varepsilon_0}{\pi m Z e^2} = \left(0.529\,\mathrm{Å}\right)\frac{n^2}{Z} \propto \frac{n^2}{Z}\] \[v_n = \frac{Z e^2}{2 \varepsilon_0 n h} \approx \left(2.18 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{m/s}\right)\frac{Z}{n} \propto \frac{Z}{n}\] \[E_n = -\frac{m Z^2 e^4}{8 \varepsilon_0^2 n^2 h^2} = -13.6\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV} \propto \frac{Z^2}{n^2}\]

4. de Broglie's Wave Explanation and Limitations of Bohr's Model / डी ब्रॉग्ली की तरंग व्याख्या और बोर के मॉडल की सीमाएँ

English

Standing Matter Waves on Circular Orbits

In 1923, Louis de Broglie explained why angular momentum is quantised. Treating the orbiting electron as a matter wave of wavelength \(\lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{h}{mv_n}\), only those orbits persist in which the electron wave forms a closed standing wave without destructive interference. For constructive resonance around a circular orbit of radius \(r_n\), the circumference must equal an integer multiple of \(\lambda\):

\[2\pi r_n = n\lambda = n\frac{h}{mv_n} \implies mv_n r_n = n\frac{h}{2\pi}\]

Thus, each stationary state \(n\) corresponds to exactly \(n\) de Broglie wavelengths fitting along the orbital circumference.

Tutorial Diagram 3: Closed standing de Broglie electron wave for n = 4 around the nucleus.

Limitations of Bohr's Model

  • Restricted to Hydrogenic Atoms: It applies only to single-electron systems (\(\mathrm{H}\), \(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\)). It fails even for neutral helium because it ignores electron–electron electrostatic repulsion, which is comparable in strength to the electron–nucleus attraction.
  • Cannot Explain Relative Intensities: While Bohr's model predicts spectral line frequencies accurately for hydrogenic atoms, it cannot explain why certain transitions produce bright (intense) lines while others are faint.
  • Semiclassical Nature: Well-defined circular trajectories violate Heisenberg's uncertainty principle; modern quantum mechanics replaces orbits with three-dimensional probability distributions characterised by four quantum numbers \((n, l, m, s)\).

बोर के मॉडल की सीमाएँ

  • केवल हाइड्रोजन-सदृश परमाणुओं तक सीमित: यह केवल एकल-इलेक्ट्रॉन निकायों (\(\mathrm{H}\), \(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\)) पर लागू होता है। यह उदासीन हीलियम के लिए भी विफल रहता है क्योंकि यह इलेक्ट्रॉन–इलेक्ट्रॉन स्थिरवैद्युत प्रतिकर्षण की उपेक्षा करता है, जो इलेक्ट्रॉन–नाभिक आकर्षण के समान ही प्रबल होता है।
  • सापेक्ष तीव्रता की व्याख्या में असमर्थ: यद्यपि बोर का मॉडल हाइड्रोजन-सदृश परमाणुओं की स्पेक्ट्रमी रेखाओं की आवृत्तियों की सटीक भविष्यवाणी करता है, यह यह नहीं समझा सकता कि कुछ संक्रमण चमकीली (तीव्र) रेखाएँ और कुछ धुँधली रेखाएँ क्यों उत्पन्न करते हैं।
  • अर्ध-चिरसम्मत स्वरूप: सुस्पष्ट वृत्तीय पथ हाइजेनबर्ग के अनिश्चितता सिद्धांत का उल्लंघन करते हैं; आधुनिक क्वांटम यांत्रिकी कक्षाओं के स्थान पर चार क्वांटम संख्याओं \((n, l, m, s)\) द्वारा निरूपित त्रि-आयामी प्रायिकता वितरण का उपयोग करती है।

Example 1: Calculating the Distance of Closest Approach

Problem: Suppose in a hypothetical scattering setup, an \(\alpha\)-particle with an assumed kinetic energy of \(6.0\,\mathrm{MeV}\) makes a head-on approach toward a stationary silver nucleus (\(Z = 47\)). Estimate the distance of closest approach. (Use \(\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9.0 \times 10^9\,\mathrm{N\cdot m^2/C^2}\) and \(e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{C}\)).

Solution:

At the turning point, all initial kinetic energy \(K\) converts into electrostatic potential energy:

\[K = 6.0\,\mathrm{MeV} = 6.0 \times 10^6 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\,\mathrm{J} = 9.6 \times 10^{-13}\,\mathrm{J}\] \[d = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 K} = \frac{2 \times 47 \times (9.0 \times 10^9) \times (1.6 \times 10^{-19})^2}{9.6 \times 10^{-13}}\] \[d = \frac{94 \times 9.0 \times 10^9 \times 2.56 \times 10^{-38}}{9.6 \times 10^{-13}} = \frac{2.16576 \times 10^{-26}}{9.6 \times 10^{-13}} \approx 2.26 \times 10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m} = 22.6\,\mathrm{fm}\]

Example 2: Kinetic, Potential, and Total Energy in an Excited State

Problem: Consider a singly ionised helium ion (\(\mathrm{He}^+\), \(Z = 2\)) with its electron in the \(n = 2\) stationary state. Find the total energy, kinetic energy, and potential energy of the electron in \(\mathrm{eV}\).

Solution:

Using Bohr's energy formula \(E_n = -13.6\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV}\) with \(Z = 2\) and \(n = 2\):

\[E_2 = -13.6 \times \frac{2^2}{2^2} = -13.6\,\mathrm{eV}\]

For a Coulomb bound orbit, \(K = -E\) and \(U = 2E\):

\[K = -(-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}) = +13.6\,\mathrm{eV}, \qquad U = 2(-13.6\,\mathrm{eV}) = -27.2\,\mathrm{eV}\]

Problem: A hydrogen atom transitions from the \(n = 3\) state to the \(n = 2\) state. Calculate the energy (in \(\mathrm{eV}\)) and wavelength (in \(\mathrm{nm}\)) of the emitted photon. (Take \(hc \approx 1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}\)).

The energies of the initial and final states are \(E_3 = -\frac{13.6}{9} \approx -1.51\,\mathrm{eV}\) and \(E_2 = -\frac{13.6}{4} = -3.40\,\mathrm{eV}\).

\[\Delta E = E_3 - E_2 = -1.51 - (-3.40) = 1.89\,\mathrm{eV}\] \[\lambda = \frac{hc}{\Delta E} = \frac{1240\,\mathrm{eV\cdot nm}}{1.89\,\mathrm{eV}} \approx 656\,\mathrm{nm}\]

This wavelength lies in the red region of the visible Balmer series.

Example 4: Applying de Broglie's Condition to Quantised Orbits

Problem: What is the ratio of the de Broglie wavelength of an electron in the \(n = 3\) orbit of a hydrogen atom to its de Broglie wavelength in the \(n = 1\) orbit?

Solution:

Since \(2\pi r_n = n\lambda_n\) and \(r_n = a_0 n^2\), we have:

\[\lambda_n = \frac{2\pi r_n}{n} = \frac{2\pi a_0 n^2}{n} = (2\pi a_0) n \propto n\]

(Equivalently, \(\lambda_n = \frac{h}{mv_n} \propto \frac{1}{v_n} \propto n\)). Therefore, the ratio is:

\[\frac{\lambda_3}{\lambda_1} = \frac{3}{1} = 3 : 1\]

6. Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ और पुनरावृत्ति जाँच-सूची

English

Common Pitfalls to Avoid in NEET UG

  • Confusing "first excited state" with \(n = 1\): \(n = 1\) is the ground state; the first excited state is \(n = 2\), and the second excited state is \(n = 3\).
  • Sign errors in \(K\), \(U\), and \(E\): Always verify \(K > 0\), \(U < 0\), and \(E < 0\) for bound states, with \(E = -K = \frac{U}{2}\).
  • Forgetting \(Z\) in hydrogenic ions: For \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z = 2\)) or \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z = 3\)), remember \(r_n \propto \frac{n^2}{Z}\), \(v_n \propto \frac{Z}{n}\), and \(E_n \propto -\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\).
  • Using Bohr's model for neutral \(\mathrm{He}\) or \(\mathrm{Li}\): Only single-electron species obey Bohr's formulas.
  • Assuming \(d\) equals nuclear radius: Distance of closest approach \(d\) only gives an upper bound on nuclear size when the \(\alpha\)-particle does not penetrate the nucleus.

NEET UG में बचने योग्य सामान्य त्रुटियाँ

  • "प्रथम उत्तेजित अवस्था" को \(n = 1\) समझने की भूल: \(n = 1\) मूल अवस्था है; प्रथम उत्तेजित अवस्था \(n = 2\) तथा द्वितीय उत्तेजित अवस्था \(n = 3\) होती है।
  • \(K\), \(U\) और \(E\) में चिह्न की त्रुटि: बद्ध अवस्थाओं के लिए सदैव जाँचें कि \(K > 0\), \(U < 0\) और \(E < 0\) हो, तथा \(E = -K = \frac{U}{2}\) संतुष्ट हो।
  • हाइड्रोजन-सदृश आयनों में \(Z\) को भूल जाना: \(\mathrm{He}^+\) (\(Z = 2\)) या \(\mathrm{Li}^{2+}\) (\(Z = 3\)) के लिए याद रखें कि \(r_n \propto \frac{n^2}{Z}\), \(v_n \propto \frac{Z}{n}\) तथा \(E_n \propto -\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\)।
  • उदासीन \(\mathrm{He}\) या \(\mathrm{Li}\) पर बोर मॉडल लागू करना: केवल एकल-इलेक्ट्रॉन प्रजातियाँ ही बोर के सूत्रों का पालन करती हैं।
  • \(d\) को नाभिकीय त्रिज्या के बराबर मान लेना: जब \(\alpha\)-कण नाभिक में प्रवेश नहीं करता, तब निकटतम पहुँच की दूरी \(d\) केवल नाभिकीय आकार की एक ऊपरी सीमा देती है।

7. Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम पुनरावृत्ति

English
  • Rutherford Scattering: Most of the atom is empty space (\(r_{\text{atom}} \sim 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\), \(r_{\text{nucleus}} \sim 10^{-15}\text{–}10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}\)); smaller impact parameter \(b\) causes larger deflection \(\theta\); head-on \(d = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 K}\).
  • Bohr Quantisation: \(L = mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}\) (explained by de Broglie standing waves \(2\pi r_n = n\lambda\)); \(r_n = 0.529\,\frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å}\); \(v_n = 2.18 \times 10^6\,\frac{Z}{n}\,\mathrm{m/s}\); \(E_n = -13.6\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV}\).
  • Transitions: \(h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = E_i - E_f\); valid strictly for single-electron hydrogenic atoms.
हिन्दी
  • रदरफोर्ड प्रकीर्णन: परमाणु का अधिकांश भाग रिक्त है (\(r_{\text{atom}} \sim 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m}\), \(r_{\text{nucleus}} \sim 10^{-15}\text{–}10^{-14}\,\mathrm{m}\)); छोटा संघट्ट प्राचल \(b\) बड़ा विक्षेपण \(\theta\) उत्पन्न करता है; सम्मुख टक्कर के लिए \(d = \frac{2Ze^2}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 K}\)।
  • बोर क्वांटमीकरण: \(L = mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}\) (डी ब्रॉग्ली अप्रगामी तरंग \(2\pi r_n = n\lambda\) द्वारा स्पष्ट); \(r_n = 0.529\,\frac{n^2}{Z}\,\mathrm{Å}\); \(v_n = 2.18 \times 10^6\,\frac{Z}{n}\,\mathrm{m/s}\); \(E_n = -13.6\,\frac{Z^2}{n^2}\,\mathrm{eV}\)।
  • संक्रमण: \(h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = E_i - E_f\); यह केवल एकल-इलेक्ट्रॉन हाइड्रोजन-सदृश परमाणुओं के लिए ही मान्य है।
