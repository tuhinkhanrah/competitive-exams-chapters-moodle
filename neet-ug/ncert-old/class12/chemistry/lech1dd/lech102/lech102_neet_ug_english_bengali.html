A solution is a single-phase, homogeneous mixture of two or more chemically non-reacting substances whose composition can be varied within limits. 2. Solubility of Solids and Gases: Henry's Law

Solubility is the maximum quantity of solute that dissolves in a fixed amount of solvent at a given temperature, establishing a dynamic equilibrium between dissolution and crystallisation:

\[ \text{Solute} + \text{Solvent} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution} \]

Solid in Liquid

  • Nature of Interactions ("Like Dissolves Like"): Polar or ionic solids (e.g., \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), sugar) dissolve in polar solvents like water, whereas non-polar solids (e.g., naphthalene, anthracene) dissolve in non-polar solvents like benzene.
  • Temperature Effect (Le Chatelier's Principle): If dissolution is endothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H > 0\)), solubility rises with temperature; if exothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H < 0\)), solubility decreases as temperature rises.
  • Pressure Effect: Negligible, because solids and liquids are virtually incompressible.

Gas in Liquid and Henry's Law

Unlike solids, gas solubility in liquids is strongly sensitive to both pressure and temperature. Because gas dissolution resembles condensation, it is generally exothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H < 0\)), meaning gas solubility decreases with increasing temperature (aquatic life thrives better in cold water).

Henry's Law: At constant temperature, the partial pressure \(p\) of a gas in the vapour phase above a liquid is directly proportional to its mole fraction \(x\) in the solution:

\[p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x\]

Here \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) is the Henry's law constant, which depends on the nature of the gas and solvent and increases with temperature. Consequently, at a fixed partial pressure, a higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) corresponds to lower solubility.

Tutorial Diagram 1: Increasing external gas pressure raises dissolved gas concentration; the slope of partial pressure versus mole fraction gives Henry's constant \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\).

High-Yield NEET UG Applications of Henry's Law

  • Carbonated Beverages: Bottles are sealed under high \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) pressure to maximise dissolved gas.
  • Scuba Diving & Bends: High underwater pressure forces more \(\mathrm{N_2}\) into blood. Rapid ascent lowers pressure, releasing \(\mathrm{N_2}\) bubbles that block capillaries (bends). Diving tanks use air diluted with helium (\(11.7\%\ \mathrm{He}\), \(56.2\%\ \mathrm{N_2}\), \(32.1\%\ \mathrm{O_2}\)) to reduce nitrogen toxicity and bends.
  • High-Altitude Anoxia: Lower partial pressure of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) at high altitudes reduces blood oxygen concentration, causing weakness and impaired thinking (anoxia).

  • আন্তঃআণবিক আকর্ষণ: ধ্রুবীয় দ্রাব (যেমন \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), চিনি) ধ্রুবীয় দ্রাবকে (জলে) দ্রবীভূত হয় এবং অধ্রুবীয় দ্রাব (যেমন ন্যাপথালিন) অধ্রুবীয় দ্রাবকে (বেঞ্জিনে) দ্রবীভূত হয়।

এখানে \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) হলো হেনরির ধ্রুবক, যা গ্যাসের প্রকৃতি ও উষ্ণতার ওপর নির্ভর করে। উষ্ণতা বাড়লে \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) বাড়ে। নির্দিষ্ট চাপে \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর মান যত বেশি হয়, গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতা তত কম হয়।

3. Vapour Pressure, Raoult's Law, and Real Solutions

Raoult's Law states that for a solution of volatile liquids, the partial vapour pressure of each component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the liquid phase at constant temperature:

\[p_1 = p_1^0 x_1, \qquad p_2 = p_2^0 x_2\] \[p_{\mathrm{total}} = p_1 + p_2 = p_1^0 + (p_2^0 - p_1^0)x_2\]

By Dalton's law, the mole fraction \(y_i\) of any component in the equilibrium vapour phase is given by \(p_i = y_i p_{\mathrm{total}}\). Notice that the vapour phase is always richer in the more volatile component (the one with higher \(p^0\)).

Raoult's Law as a Special Case of Henry's Law: Comparing \(p_i = p_i^0 x_i\) with \(p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x\) shows that Raoult's law is a special case of Henry's law where \(K_{\mathrm{H}} = p_i^0\).

4. Colligative Properties and Molar Mass Determination

Adding a non-volatile solute to a volatile solvent reduces the fraction of the liquid surface occupied by solvent molecules, lowering the solvent's escaping tendency and vapour pressure. Properties that depend strictly on the number of solute particles relative to total particles—and not on their chemical identity—are called colligative properties.

Colligative Property Governing Equation (Non-Electrolyte) Key Constants & Molar Mass Relation
Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure (RLVP) \(\dfrac{p_1^0 - p_1}{p_1^0} = x_2 = \dfrac{n_2}{n_1 + n_2}\) For dilute solutions (\(n_2 \ll n_1\)): \(\dfrac{\Delta p_1}{p_1^0} \approx \dfrac{w_2 M_1}{M_2 w_1}\)
Elevation of Boiling Point (\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\)) \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = T_{\mathrm{b}} - T_{\mathrm{b}}^0 = K_{\mathrm{b}} m\) \(K_{\mathrm{b}} = \dfrac{R M_1 (T_{\mathrm{b}}^0)^2}{1000\,\Delta_{\mathrm{vap}}H}\); \(M_2 = \dfrac{1000\,K_{\mathrm{b}}\,w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\,w_1}\)
Depression of Freezing Point (\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\)) \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = T_{\mathrm{f}}^0 - T_{\mathrm{f}} = K_{\mathrm{f}} m\) \(K_{\mathrm{f}} = \dfrac{R M_1 (T_{\mathrm{f}}^0)^2}{1000\,\Delta_{\mathrm{fus}}H}\); \(M_2 = \dfrac{1000\,K_{\mathrm{f}}\,w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\,w_1}\)

Osmosis, Tonicity, and Reverse Osmosis

  • Osmosis: Net spontaneous flow of solvent molecules through a semipermeable membrane (SPM) from pure solvent (or dilute solution) into a concentrated solution.
  • Isotonic, Hypertonic & Hypotonic Solutions: Solutions with equal osmotic pressure at a given temperature are isotonic (e.g., \(0.9\%\ \text{w/V}\) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) is isotonic with human red blood cells). A solution with higher osmotic pressure (\(> 0.9\%\ \text{w/V}\) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) is hypertonic (cells shrink/crenate), whereas one with lower osmotic pressure (\(< 0.9\%\ \text{w/V}\) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) is hypotonic (cells swell).
  • Why Osmotic Pressure Excels for Macromolecules: Measurements are performed near room temperature (preventing thermal degradation of proteins), molarity is used, and \(\Pi\) has a large, accurately measurable magnitude even for very dilute solutions.
  • Reverse Osmosis: Applying external pressure greater than \(\Pi\) on the solution side forces pure solvent out through a cellulose acetate SPM—widely used in seawater desalination.

5. Abnormal Molar Masses and the van't Hoff Factor

When a solute dissociates into ions (e.g., \(\mathrm{KCl}\) in water) or associates into larger aggregates (e.g., acetic acid or benzoic acid dimerising via hydrogen bonding in benzene), the actual number of dissolved particles differs from the nominal moles added. Consequently, the experimentally determined molar mass differs from the true molar mass (an abnormal molar mass).

\[i = \frac{\text{Normal molar mass}}{\text{Abnormal molar mass}} = \frac{\text{Observed colligative property}}{\text{Calculated colligative property}} = \frac{\text{Total moles of particles after dissociation/association}}{\text{Moles of particles before dissociation/association}}\]
  • Dissociation (\(i > 1\)): If \(1\ \mathrm{mol}\) of electrolyte \(\mathrm{A}_x\mathrm{B}_y\) dissociates into \(n = x + y\) ions with degree of dissociation \(\alpha\): \[i = 1 + (n - 1)\alpha \implies \alpha = \frac{i - 1}{n - 1}\]
  • Association (\(i < 1\)): If \(n\) solute molecules associate to form 1 aggregate (\(\mathrm{A}_n\)) with degree of association \(\beta\): \[i = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)\beta \implies \beta = \frac{1 - i}{1 - \frac{1}{n}}\]
  • Modified Colligative Equations: \[\frac{p_1^0 - p_1}{p_1^0} = i\frac{n_2}{n_1}, \quad \Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = i K_{\mathrm{b}} m, \quad \Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = i K_{\mathrm{f}} m, \quad \Pi = i C R T\]

Converting Mass Percentage and Density to Molality and Molarity

Problem (Assumed Values): A hypothetical aqueous glucose (\(\mathrm{C_6H_{12}O_6}\), molar mass \(= 180\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}\)) solution is \(18\%\) by mass (w/w) and has a density of \(1.08\ \mathrm{g\ mL^{-1}}\) at \(298\ \mathrm{K}\). Calculate (a) its molality and (b) its molarity.

Solution:

  1. Assume \(100\ \mathrm{g}\) of solution. Then mass of glucose \(w_2 = 18\ \mathrm{g}\) and mass of water \(w_1 = 100 - 18 = 82\ \mathrm{g} = 0.082\ \mathrm{kg}\).
  2. Moles of glucose \(n_2 = \dfrac{18\ \mathrm{g}}{180\ \mathrm{g\ mol^{-1}}} = 0.10\ \mathrm{mol}\).
  3. Molality (\(m\)): \(m = \dfrac{0.10\ \mathrm{mol}}{0.082\ \mathrm{kg}} \approx 1.22\ \mathrm{mol\ kg^{-1}}\).
  4. Volume of solution: \(V = \dfrac{100\ \mathrm{g}}{1.08\ \mathrm{g\ mL^{-1}}} = 92.59\ \mathrm{mL} = 0.09259\ \mathrm{L}\).
  5. Molarity (\(M\)): \(M = \dfrac{0.10\ \mathrm{mol}}{0.09259\ \mathrm{L}} = 1.08\ \mathrm{mol\ L^{-1}}\).

Computing Degree of Association Using the van't Hoff Factor

Solution:

  1. Normal molality: \(m = \dfrac{1.22 / 122}{0.050} = 0.20\ \mathrm{mol\ kg^{-1}}\).
  2. Calculated \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f,calc}} = K_{\mathrm{f}} m = 5.12 \times 0.20 = 1.024\ \mathrm{K}\).
  3. van't Hoff factor: \(i = \dfrac{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f,obs}}}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f,calc}}} = \dfrac{0.563}{1.024} \approx 0.55\).
  4. For dimerisation (\(n = 2\)), \(i = 1 - \dfrac{\beta}{2} \implies \dfrac{\beta}{2} = 1 - 0.55 = 0.45 \implies \beta = 0.90\), or 90% association.

The vapour pressure of pure water at \(298\ \mathrm{K}\) is \(24.0\ \mathrm{mmHg}\). When \(0.20\ \mathrm{mol}\) of a non-volatile, non-electrolyte solute is dissolved in \(9.80\ \mathrm{mol}\) of water, what is the vapour pressure of the resulting solution?

  • (A) \(23.52\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (B) \(23.04\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (C) \(22.80\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (D) \(23.76\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
Answer: (A)

Explanation: Mole fraction of solvent \(x_1 = \dfrac{9.80}{9.80 + 0.20} = 0.98\). By Raoult's law, \(p_1 = x_1 p_1^0 = 0.98 \times 24.0 = 23.52\ \mathrm{mmHg}\).

\(298\ \mathrm{K}\) উষ্ণতায় বিশুদ্ধ জলের বাষ্পচাপ \(24.0\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)। \(9.80\ \mathrm{mol}\) জলে \(0.20\ \mathrm{mol}\) একটি অনুদ্বায়ী অতড়িৎ-বিশ্লেষ্য দ্রাব দ্রবীভূত করলে উৎপন্ন দ্রবণের বাষ্পচাপ কত হবে?

  • (ক) \(23.52\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (খ) \(23.04\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (গ) \(22.80\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
  • (ঘ) \(23.76\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)
উত্তর: (ক)

ব্যাখ্যা: দ্রাবকের মোল ভগ্নাংশ \(x_1 = \dfrac{9.80}{9.80 + 0.20} = 0.98\)। রাউল্টের সূত্রানুসারে \(p_1 = 0.98 \times 24.0 = 23.52\ \mathrm{mmHg}\)।

Practice Question 6

Assertion (A): A \(0.1\ \mathrm{m}\) aqueous solution of \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) has a lower freezing point than a \(0.1\ \mathrm{m}\) aqueous solution of glucose.
Reason (R): \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) dissociates in water to yield a greater number of solute particles per formula unit, increasing the freezing point depression.

  • (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
  • (B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
  • (C) A is true, but R is false
  • (D) A is false, but R is true
Answer: (A)

Explanation: Since \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = i K_{\mathrm{f}} m\), \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) (\(i \approx 2\)) produces nearly twice the freezing point depression of glucose (\(i = 1\)) at the same molality, resulting in a lower freezing point \(T_{\mathrm{f}} = T_{\mathrm{f}}^0 - \Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\).

অনুশীলন প্রশ্ন ৬

বিবৃতি (A): সমান \(0.1\ \mathrm{m}\) গাঢ়ত্বের গ্লুকোজ দ্রবণের তুলনায় \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)-এর জলীয় দ্রবণের হিমাঙ্ক কম হয়।
কারণ (R): \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) জলে আয়নিত হয়ে বেশি সংখ্যক দ্রাব কণা উৎপন্ন করে, ফলে হিমাঙ্ক অবনমন বৃদ্ধি পায়।

  • (ক) A ও R উভয়ই সত্য এবং R হলো A-এর সঠিক ব্যাখ্যা
  • (খ) A ও R উভয়ই সত্য, কিন্তু R হলো A-এর সঠিক ব্যাখ্যা নয়
  • (গ) A সত্য, কিন্তু R মিথ্যা
  • (ঘ) A মিথ্যা, কিন্তু R সত্য
উত্তর: (ক)

ব্যাখ্যা: \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = i K_{\mathrm{f}} m\) অনুযায়ী \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)-এর জন্য \(i \approx 2\) এবং গ্লুকোজের জন্য \(i = 1\)। তাই \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) দ্রবণে হিমাঙ্ক অবনমন বেশি হয় এবং হিমাঙ্ক কম হয়।

Practice Question 7

When human red blood cells are placed in a \(1.5\%\) (w/V) aqueous \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) solution, what happens to the cells?

  • (A) They swell and burst because the external solution is hypotonic
  • (B) They shrink because the external solution is hypertonic
  • (C) They remain unchanged because the external solution is isotonic
  • (D) They undergo reverse osmosis and gain water
Answer: (B)

Explanation: Normal saline (\(0.9\%\ \text{w/V}\) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\)) is isotonic with blood cell fluid. A \(1.5\%\) (w/V) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) solution is hypertonic, so water flows out of the cells by osmosis, causing them to shrink.

Practice Question 8

A \(0.05\ \mathrm{m}\) aqueous solution of a weak monobasic acid \(\mathrm{HA}\) freezes at \(-0.1116^\circ\mathrm{C}\). Given \(K_{\mathrm{f}}\) for water \(= 1.86\ \mathrm{K\ kg\ mol^{-1}}\), what is the percentage dissociation of \(\mathrm{HA}\)?

  • (A) \(10\%\)
  • (B) \(15\%\)
  • (C) \(20\%\)
  • (D) \(25\%\)
Answer: (C)

Explanation: Observed \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 0.1116\ \mathrm{K}\). Calculated \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 1.86 \times 0.05 = 0.093\ \mathrm{K}\). Thus \(i = \dfrac{0.1116}{0.093} = 1.20\). For \(\mathrm{HA} \rightleftharpoons \mathrm{H}^+ + \mathrm{A}^-\), \(i = 1 + \alpha \implies \alpha = 1.20 - 1 = 0.20 = 20\%\).

  • (ক) \(10\%\)
  • (খ) \(15\%\)
  • (গ) \(20\%\)
  • (ঘ) \(25\%\)
উত্তর: (গ)

ব্যাখ্যা: পরীক্ষালব্ধ \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 0.1116\ \mathrm{K}\)। তাত্ত্বিক \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 1.86 \times 0.05 = 0.093\ \mathrm{K}\)। সুতরাং \(i = \dfrac{0.1116}{0.093} = 1.20\)। \(\mathrm{HA}\)-এর ক্ষেত্রে \(i = 1 + \alpha \implies \alpha = 0.20 = 20\%\)।

English

Common Pitfalls, Revision Checklist, and Final Recap

Common NEET UG Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Forgetting \(i\) for Electrolytes: Always multiply \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\), \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\), \(\Pi\), and RLVP by the van't Hoff factor \(i\) whenever the solute is an acid, base, or salt.
  • Unit Mismatch in \(\Pi = CRT\): If \(\Pi\) is in \(\mathrm{bar}\) and \(V\) in \(\mathrm{L}\), use \(R = 0.083\ \mathrm{L\ bar\ K^{-1}\ mol^{-1}}\); if \(\Pi\) is in \(\mathrm{atm}\), use \(R = 0.0821\ \mathrm{L\ atm\ K^{-1}\ mol^{-1}}\); if in \(\mathrm{Pa}\) and \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\), use \(R = 8.314\ \mathrm{J\ K^{-1}\ mol^{-1}}\).
  • Higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) Misconception: Higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) means lower gas solubility, not higher!

Quick Revision Checklist

  • Molality, mole fraction, and % w/w are temperature-independent; molarity is temperature-dependent.
  • Henry's law: \(p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x\); gas solubility decreases with rising temperature.
  • Raoult's law: \(p_1 = p_1^0 x_1\); vapour phase is enriched in the more volatile component.
  • Positive deviation \(\rightarrow \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H > 0,\ \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V > 0\), minimum-boiling azeotrope; Negative deviation \(\rightarrow \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H < 0,\ \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V < 0\), maximum-boiling azeotrope.
  • Colligative properties depend only on particle count; use \(i = 1 + (n-1)\alpha\) for dissociation and \(i = 1 - (1 - 1/n)\beta\) for association.

Final Recap: Solutions link microscopic intermolecular forces to macroscopic measurable properties—vapour pressure, boiling/freezing shifts, and osmotic pressure—all unified by mole fraction, molality, molarity, and the van't Hoff factor.

NEET UG-তে যে ভুলগুলি এড়িয়ে চলবে:
  • ভ্যান্ট হফ গুণক \(i\) ভুলে যাওয়া: অ্যাসিড, ক্ষার বা লবণের ক্ষেত্রে \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\), \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\), \(\Pi\) এবং বাষ্পচাপের আপেক্ষিক অবনমনে অবশ্যই \(i\) গুণ করতে হবে।
  • \(\Pi = CRT\)-তে \(R\)-এর একক: চাপ \(\mathrm{bar}\)-এ হলে \(R = 0.083\), \(\mathrm{atm}\)-এ হলে \(R = 0.0821\), এবং \(\mathrm{Pa}\) ও \(\mathrm{m^3}\) হলে \(R = 8.314\ \mathrm{J\ K^{-1}\ mol^{-1}}\) ব্যবহার করবে।
  • \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\)-এর ভুল ধারণা: \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) বেশি হলে গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতা কম হয়, বেশি নয়।

দ্রুত পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

  • মোলালিটি, মোল ভগ্নাংশ ও ভর শতাংশ উষ্ণতা-নিরপেক্ষ; মোলারিটি উষ্ণতা-নির্ভরশীল।
  • হেনরির সূত্র: \(p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x\); উষ্ণতা বাড়লে গ্যাসের দ্রাব্যতা কমে।
  • রাউল্টের সূত্র: \(p_1 = p_1^0 x_1\); বাষ্প দশায় অধিক উদ্বায়ী উপাদান বেশি থাকে।
  • ধনাত্মক বিচ্যুতি \(\rightarrow \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H > 0,\ \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V > 0\), নিম্ন-স্ফুটনাঙ্ক অ্যাজিওট্রপ; ঋণাত্মক বিচ্যুতি \(\rightarrow \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H < 0,\ \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V < 0\), উচ্চ-স্ফুটনাঙ্ক অ্যাজিওট্রপ।
  • সংখ্যাগত ধর্ম কণার সংখ্যার ওপর নির্ভরশীল; বিয়োজনে \(i = 1 + (n-1)\alpha\) এবং সংযোজনে \(i = 1 - (1 - 1/n)\beta\)।
