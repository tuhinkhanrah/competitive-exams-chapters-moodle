Learning Objectives and Chapter Overview | अधिगम उद्देश्य एवं अध्याय की रूपरेखा

English

Almost every physiological and chemical reaction occurs in a liquid medium. Understanding how solutes alter the thermodynamic behaviour of solvents—such as lowering vapour pressure, elevating boiling point, depressing freezing point, and generating osmotic pressure—is essential for solving NEET UG numerical and conceptual problems.

  • Classify binary solutions by the physical states of solute and solvent.
  • Convert seamlessly between concentration units (mass %, ppm, mole fraction, molarity, and molality) and identify which depend on temperature.
  • Apply Henry's law to gas-liquid equilibria and Raoult's law to volatile and non-volatile liquid mixtures.
  • Distinguish ideal solutions from positive and negative deviations, including minimum- and maximum-boiling azeotropes.
  • Determine molar masses using the four colligative properties and account for dissociation or association via the van't Hoff factor \(i\).

1. Classification of Binary Solutions & Concentration Units | द्विअंगी विलयनों का वर्गीकरण एवं सांद्रता इकाइयाँ

English

A solution is a single-phase, homogeneous mixture of two or more substances whose composition can be varied within limits. In a binary solution (two components), the component present in the larger proportion—or the one whose physical state matches the final solution—is the solvent, while the other component is the solute.

2. Solubility of Solids and Gases in Liquids | द्रवों में ठोसों एवं गैसों की विलेयता

English

Solubility is the maximum quantity of solute that dissolves in a fixed amount of solvent at a given temperature and pressure, forming a saturated solution in dynamic equilibrium: \(\text{Solute} + \text{Solvent} \rightleftharpoons \text{Solution}\).

  • Solids in Liquids ("Like Dissolves Like"): Polar or ionic solids dissolve in polar solvents (e.g., \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) in water) due to strong ion-dipole or dipole-dipole interactions, whereas nonpolar solids dissolve in nonpolar solvents (e.g., naphthalene in benzene). By Le Chatelier's principle, if dissolution is endothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H > 0\)), solubility rises with temperature; if exothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H < 0\)), solubility drops with rising temperature. Pressure has negligible effect because solids and liquids are nearly incompressible.
  • Gases in Liquids & Henry's Law: Gas dissolution resembles condensation and is almost always exothermic (\(\Delta_{\mathrm{sol}}H < 0\)). Hence, gas solubility decreases as temperature increases (aquatic organisms thrive better in cold water).
Henry's Law: At constant temperature, the partial pressure \(p\) of a gas in the vapour phase above a liquid is directly proportional to its mole fraction \(x\) in the solution: \[ p = K_{\mathrm{H}}\,x \] Here \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) is the Henry's law constant (units of pressure, e.g., \(\mathrm{bar}\) or \(\mathrm{kbar}\)). At a given pressure, a higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) implies lower gas solubility. Since \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) increases with temperature, gas solubility decreases upon heating.

Key Applications: (1) Carbonated beverages are bottled under high \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) pressure. (2) Scuba divers breathe helium-diluted air (\(11.7\%\ \mathrm{He}\), \(56.2\%\ \mathrm{N_2}\), \(32.1\%\ \mathrm{O_2}\)) to prevent bends (painful \(\mathrm{N_2}\) bubbles forming in blood during rapid ascent). (3) At high altitudes, low partial pressure of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) reduces blood oxygen, causing anoxia.

3. Vapour Pressure, Raoult's Law & Non-Ideal Deviations | वाष्प दाब, राउल्ट का नियम एवं अनादर्श विचलन

English

Raoult's Law states that for any solution containing volatile components, the partial vapour pressure of each volatile component is directly proportional to its mole fraction in the liquid phase:

\[ p_1 = p_1^0\,x_1, \qquad p_2 = p_2^0\,x_2 \] \[ p_{\mathrm{total}} = p_1 + p_2 = p_1^0 + (p_2^0 - p_1^0)x_2 \] Vapour-Phase Composition (Dalton's Law): If \(y_1\) and \(y_2\) are mole fractions in the vapour phase: \[ p_i = y_i\,p_{\mathrm{total}} \implies y_1 = \frac{p_1^0\,x_1}{p_{\mathrm{total}}} \] Note that the vapour phase is always richer in the more volatile component (the one with higher \(p^0\)). Also, Raoult's law is a special case of Henry's law where \(K_{\mathrm{H}} = p_i^0\).

Ideal vs. Non-Ideal Solutions

  • Ideal Solutions: Obey Raoult's law across all compositions. Intermolecular forces satisfy \(F_{A-B} \approx F_{A-A} \approx F_{B-B}\). Consequently, \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H = 0\) and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V = 0\). Examples: n-hexane + n-heptane; benzene + toluene; bromoethane + chloroethane.
  • Positive Deviation: \(F_{A-B} < F_{A-A},\,F_{B-B}\). Molecules escape more easily, raising vapour pressure above Raoult's prediction. Here \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H > 0\) (endothermic) and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V > 0\). Examples: ethanol + acetone; acetone + \(\mathrm{CS_2}\); ethanol + water. Large positive deviations form minimum-boiling azeotropes (e.g., \(95\%\) ethanol + \(5\%\) water by volume).
  • Negative Deviation: \(F_{A-B} > F_{A-A},\,F_{B-B}\) (often due to new hydrogen bonding). Escaping tendency drops, lowering vapour pressure. Here \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H < 0\) (exothermic) and \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V < 0\). Examples: chloroform + acetone; phenol + aniline; \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) + water. Large negative deviations form maximum-boiling azeotropes (e.g., \(68\%\) \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) + \(32\%\) water by mass, b.p. \(393.5\,\mathrm{K}\)).

राउल्ट का नियम: वाष्पशील घटकों वाले किसी भी विलयन में, प्रत्येक वाष्पशील घटक का आंशिक वाष्प दाब विलयन में उसके मोल अंश के समानुपाती होता है:

\[ p_1 = p_1^0\,x_1, \qquad p_2 = p_2^0\,x_2 \] \[ p_{\mathrm{total}} = p_1 + p_2 = p_1^0 + (p_2^0 - p_1^0)x_2 \] वाष्प प्रावस्था का संघटन (डॉल्टन का नियम): यदि वाष्प प्रावस्था में मोल अंश \(y_1\) और \(y_2\) हैं, तो: \[ p_i = y_i\,p_{\mathrm{total}} \implies y_1 = \frac{p_1^0\,x_1}{p_{\mathrm{total}}} \] ध्यान दें कि वाष्प प्रावस्था सदैव अधिक वाष्पशील घटक (जिसका \(p^0\) अधिक हो) में समृद्ध होती है। राउल्ट का नियम हेनरी के नियम की एक विशेष स्थिति है जहाँ \(K_{\mathrm{H}} = p_i^0\) होता है।

आदर्श बनाम अनादर्श विलयन

  • आदर्श विलयन: सभी सांद्रताओं पर राउल्ट के नियम का पालन करते हैं। इनमें अंतराआण्विक बल \(F_{A-B} \approx F_{A-A} \approx F_{B-B}\) होते हैं। अतः \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H = 0\) तथा \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V = 0\) होता है। उदाहरण: n-हेक्सेन + n-हेप्टेन; बेंजीन + टॉलूईन; ब्रोमोएथेन + क्लोरोएथेन।
  • धनात्मक विचलन: \(F_{A-B} < F_{A-A},\,F_{B-B}\)। अणु आसानी से पलायन करते हैं, जिससे कुल वाष्प दाब अपेक्षित मान से अधिक होता है। इसमें \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H > 0\) (ऊष्माशोषी) और \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V > 0\) होता है। उदाहरण: एथेनॉल + ऐसीटोन; ऐसीटोन + \(\mathrm{CS_2}\)। अत्यधिक धनात्मक विचलन न्यूनतम क्वथनांकी स्थिरक्वाथी मिश्रण (Azeotrope) बनाते हैं (जैसे आयतन से \(95\%\) एथेनॉल + \(5\%\) जल)।
  • ऋणात्मक विचलन: \(F_{A-B} > F_{A-A},\,F_{B-B}\) (प्रायः नए हाइड्रोजन बंध बनने के कारण)। पलायन प्रवृत्ति घटती है और वाष्प दाब कम हो जाता है। इसमें \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H < 0\) (ऊष्माक्षेपी) और \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V < 0\) होता है। उदाहरण: क्लोरोफॉर्म + ऐसीटोन; फीनॉल + ऐनिलीन; \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) + जल। अत्यधिक ऋणात्मक विचलन अधिकतम क्वथनांकी स्थिरक्वाथी मिश्रण बनाते हैं (जैसे द्रव्यमान से \(68\%\) \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) + \(32\%\) जल, क्वथनांक \(393.5\,\mathrm{K}\))।

4. Colligative Properties & Molar Mass Determination | अणुसंख्य गुणधर्म एवं मोलर द्रव्यमान निर्धारण

English

Colligative properties depend strictly on the number ratio of solute particles to solvent particles in a dilute solution, and not on the chemical identity of the solute. Adding a non-volatile solute lowers the solvent's vapour pressure because solute particles occupy a fraction of the surface, reducing the escaping tendency of solvent molecules.

Colligative Property Governing Equation (Non-electrolyte) Molar Mass (\(M_2\)) Formula
1. Relative Lowering of Vapour Pressure (RLVP) \(\frac{p_1^0 - p_1}{p_1^0} = x_2 \approx \frac{n_2}{n_1}\) (dilute) \(M_2 = \frac{w_2\,M_1}{w_1} \cdot \frac{p_1^0}{p_1^0 - p_1}\)
2. Elevation of Boiling Point (\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\)) \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = T_{\mathrm{b}} - T_{\mathrm{b}}^0 = K_{\mathrm{b}}\,m\) \(M_2 = \frac{1000\,K_{\mathrm{b}}\,w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{b}}\,w_1}\)
3. Depression of Freezing Point (\(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\)) \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = T_{\mathrm{f}}^0 - T_{\mathrm{f}} = K_{\mathrm{f}}\,m\) \(M_2 = \frac{1000\,K_{\mathrm{f}}\,w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}\,w_1}\)
4. Osmotic Pressure (\(\Pi\)) \(\Pi = C R T = \frac{n_2}{V} R T\) \(M_2 = \frac{w_2 R T}{\Pi V}\)
Thermodynamic Relations for Ebullioscopic (\(K_{\mathrm{b}}\)) & Cryoscopic (\(K_{\mathrm{f}}\)) Constants: \[ K_{\mathrm{b}} = \frac{R\,M_1\,(T_{\mathrm{b}}^0)^2}{1000\,\Delta_{\mathrm{vap}}H}, \qquad K_{\mathrm{f}} = \frac{R\,M_1\,(T_{\mathrm{f}}^0)^2}{1000\,\Delta_{\mathrm{fus}}H} \] For water: \(K_{\mathrm{b}} = 0.52\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\), \(K_{\mathrm{f}} = 1.86\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\).

Osmosis, Tonicity & Reverse Osmosis: Solvent flows spontaneously across a semipermeable membrane (SPM) from a dilute solution (lower \(\Pi\)) to a concentrated solution (higher \(\Pi\)). The excess pressure applied to the solution side to just halt this flow is the osmotic pressure \(\Pi\). Osmotic pressure is preferred for determining molar masses of polymers and proteins because it is measured at room temperature (preventing biomolecule denaturation) and has a large, easily measurable magnitude even for very dilute solutions.

  • Isotonic: Equal \(\Pi\) (e.g., \(0.9\%\,w/V\) \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) is isotonic with human red blood cells).
  • Hypertonic: Higher \(\Pi\) than cell fluid (cells shrink/plasmolyse).
  • Hypotonic: Lower \(\Pi\) than cell fluid (cells swell/burst).
  • Reverse Osmosis (RO): Applying external pressure \(P > \Pi\) on seawater forces pure water out through a cellulose acetate SPM for desalination.

5. Abnormal Molar Masses and the van't Hoff Factor | असामान्य मोलर द्रव्यमान एवं वांट हॉफ गुणांक

English

Electrolytes dissociate into ions in polar solvents (increasing particle count), whereas solutes like carboxylic acids associate into dimers via hydrogen bonding in low-dielectric-constant solvents such as benzene (decreasing particle count). Since colligative properties track particle count, the experimentally determined molar mass differs from the theoretical formula mass—termed an abnormal molar mass.

van't Hoff Factor (\(i\)): \[ i = \frac{\text{Normal (theoretical) molar mass}}{\text{Abnormal (observed) molar mass}} = \frac{\text{Observed colligative property}}{\text{Calculated colligative property}} = \frac{\text{Total moles of particles after change}}{\text{Initial moles before change}} \]
  • Dissociation (\(A_xB_y \to xA^{y+} + yB^{x-}\), giving \(n = x + y\) ions; degree \(\alpha\)): \(\; i = 1 + (n - 1)\alpha \implies i > 1\).
  • Association (\(nA \rightleftharpoons A_n\); degree \(\alpha\)): \(\; i = 1 - \alpha\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right) \implies i < 1\).
Modified Colligative Equations: \[ \frac{p_1^0 - p_1}{p_1^0} = i\,\frac{n_2}{n_1}, \qquad \Delta T_{\mathrm{b}} = i\,K_{\mathrm{b}}\,m, \qquad \Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = i\,K_{\mathrm{f}}\,m, \qquad \Pi = i\,C R T \]

Example 1: Interconverting Concentration Units (Assumed Solution)

Problem: Assume a laboratory prepares a \(15\%\ (w/w)\) aqueous solution of urea (\(\mathrm{NH_2CONH_2}\), molar mass \(60\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) with a measured density of \(1.04\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}\). Calculate its (a) molality, (b) molarity, and (c) mole fraction of urea.

Solution:

Take \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) of solution as the basis: mass of urea \(w_2 = 15\,\mathrm{g}\), mass of water \(w_1 = 85\,\mathrm{g} = 0.085\,\mathrm{kg}\).

Moles of urea: \(n_2 = \frac{15}{60} = 0.25\,\mathrm{mol}\). Moles of water: \(n_1 = \frac{85}{18} \approx 4.722\,\mathrm{mol}\).

  • (a) Molality (\(m\)): \(m = \frac{0.25\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.085\,\mathrm{kg}} \approx 2.94\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}\).
  • (b) Molarity (\(M\)): Volume of \(100\,\mathrm{g}\) solution \(V = \frac{100\,\mathrm{g}}{1.04\,\mathrm{g\,mL^{-1}}} = 96.15\,\mathrm{mL} = 0.09615\,\mathrm{L}\). Thus, \(M = \frac{0.25\,\mathrm{mol}}{0.09615\,\mathrm{L}} \approx 2.60\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\).
  • (c) Mole fraction (\(x_2\)): \(x_2 = \frac{0.25}{0.25 + 4.722} = \frac{0.25}{4.972} \approx 0.0503\).

Example 2: Applying Henry's Law to Gas Solubility

Problem: At \(293\,\mathrm{K}\), Henry's law constant for oxygen gas in water is \(K_{\mathrm{H}} = 34.86\,\mathrm{kbar}\). Suppose oxygen is maintained at an assumed partial pressure of \(0.40\,\mathrm{bar}\) above \(1\,\mathrm{L}\) of pure water (\(55.5\,\mathrm{mol}\)). How many millimoles of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) dissolve?

Solution:

Using \(p = K_{\mathrm{H}}\,x\):

\[ x_{\mathrm{O_2}} = \frac{0.40\,\mathrm{bar}}{34860\,\mathrm{bar}} \approx 1.147 \times 10^{-5} \]

Since \(n_{\mathrm{O_2}} \ll 55.5\), we approximate \(x_{\mathrm{O_2}} \approx \frac{n_{\mathrm{O_2}}}{55.5}\):

\[ n_{\mathrm{O_2}} = 1.147 \times 10^{-5} \times 55.5\,\mathrm{mol} = 6.37 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol} = 0.637\,\mathrm{mmol} \]

Example 3: Calculating Total Vapour Pressure and Vapour Composition

Problem: At \(300\,\mathrm{K}\), pure benzene and pure toluene have vapour pressures of \(50.7\,\mathrm{mmHg}\) and \(32.1\,\mathrm{mmHg}\), respectively. Assume an ideal equimolar mixture is formed by mixing \(1.0\,\mathrm{mol}\) of benzene with \(1.0\,\mathrm{mol}\) of toluene. Find the total vapour pressure and the mole fraction of benzene in the vapour phase.

Solution:

Liquid mole fractions: \(x_{\mathrm{B}} = 0.50\), \(x_{\mathrm{T}} = 0.50\).

Partial pressures: \(p_{\mathrm{B}} = 50.7 \times 0.50 = 25.35\,\mathrm{mmHg}\); \(p_{\mathrm{T}} = 32.1 \times 0.50 = 16.05\,\mathrm{mmHg}\).

Total vapour pressure: \(p_{\mathrm{total}} = 25.35 + 16.05 = 41.40\,\mathrm{mmHg}\).

Mole fraction of benzene in vapour: \(y_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{p_{\mathrm{B}}}{p_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{25.35}{41.40} \approx 0.612\).

हल:

द्रव प्रावस्था में मोल अंश: \(x_{\mathrm{B}} = 0.50\), \(x_{\mathrm{T}} = 0.50\)।

आंशिक दाब: \(p_{\mathrm{B}} = 50.7 \times 0.50 = 25.35\,\mathrm{mmHg}\); \(p_{\mathrm{T}} = 32.1 \times 0.50 = 16.05\,\mathrm{mmHg}\)।

कुल वाष्प दाब: \(p_{\mathrm{total}} = 25.35 + 16.05 = 41.40\,\mathrm{mmHg}\)।

वाष्प प्रावस्था में बेंजीन का मोल अंश: \(y_{\mathrm{B}} = \frac{p_{\mathrm{B}}}{p_{\mathrm{total}}} = \frac{25.35}{41.40} \approx 0.612\)।

Example 4: Determining Molar Mass via Freezing Point Depression

Problem: Suppose \(1.50\,\mathrm{g}\) of a non-electrolyte organic compound is dissolved in \(60.0\,\mathrm{g}\) of benzene (\(K_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\)), causing the freezing point to drop by \(0.64\,\mathrm{K}\). Calculate the molar mass of the solute.

Solution:

\[ M_2 = \frac{1000 \times K_{\mathrm{f}} \times w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} \times w_1} = \frac{1000 \times 5.12 \times 1.50}{0.64 \times 60.0} = \frac{7680}{38.4} = 200\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}} \]

उदाहरण 4: हिमांक अवनमन द्वारा मोलर द्रव्यमान का निर्धारण

प्रश्न: मान लीजिए \(1.50\,\mathrm{g}\) एक अन-अपघट्य कार्बनिक यौगिक को \(60.0\,\mathrm{g}\) बेंजीन (\(K_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\)) में घोलने पर हिमांक में \(0.64\,\mathrm{K}\) का अवनमन होता है। विलेय का मोलर द्रव्यमान ज्ञात कीजिए।

हल:

\[ M_2 = \frac{1000 \times K_{\mathrm{f}} \times w_2}{\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} \times w_1} = \frac{1000 \times 5.12 \times 1.50}{0.64 \times 60.0} = \frac{7680}{38.4} = 200\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}} \]

Example 5: Computing Degree of Association Using van't Hoff Factor

Problem: Assume \(1.22\,\mathrm{g}\) of benzoic acid (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}\), normal molar mass \(122\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) is dissolved in \(50\,\mathrm{g}\) of benzene (\(K_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\)). The observed freezing point depression is \(0.5632\,\mathrm{K}\). Determine the percentage of benzoic acid that dimerizes.

Solution:

Calculated molality assuming no association: \(m = \frac{1.22 / 122}{0.050} = 0.20\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}\).

Calculated \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12 \times 0.20 = 1.024\,\mathrm{K}\).

van't Hoff factor: \(i = \frac{\text{Observed }\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}}{\text{Calculated }\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}} = \frac{0.5632}{1.024} = 0.55\).

For dimerization (\(n = 2\)): \(i = 1 - \frac{\alpha}{2} \implies \frac{\alpha}{2} = 1 - 0.55 = 0.45 \implies \alpha = 0.90\ (90\%)\).

उदाहरण 5: वांट हॉफ गुणांक द्वारा संगुणन की मात्रा की गणना

प्रश्न: मान लीजिए \(1.22\,\mathrm{g}\) बेंजोइक अम्ल (\(\mathrm{C_6H_5COOH}\), सामान्य मोलर द्रव्यमान \(122\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) को \(50\,\mathrm{g}\) बेंजीन (\(K_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12\,\mathrm{K\,kg\,mol^{-1}}\)) में घोला जाता है। प्रेक्षित हिमांक अवनमन \(0.5632\,\mathrm{K}\) है। अम्ल के द्विलकीकरण (संगुणन) का प्रतिशत ज्ञात कीजिए।

हल:

बिना संगुणन के मोललता: \(m = \frac{1.22 / 122}{0.050} = 0.20\,\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}\)।

परिकलित \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = 5.12 \times 0.20 = 1.024\,\mathrm{K}\)।

वांट हॉफ गुणांक: \(i = \frac{\text{प्रेक्षित }\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}}{\text{परिकलित }\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}}} = \frac{0.5632}{1.024} = 0.55\)।

द्विलकीकरण (\(n = 2\)) के लिए: \(i = 1 - \frac{\alpha}{2} \implies \frac{\alpha}{2} = 1 - 0.55 = 0.45 \implies \alpha = 0.90\ (90\%)\)।

  • Forgetting the van't Hoff Factor (\(i\)): Never apply \(\Delta T_{\mathrm{f}} = K_{\mathrm{f}} m\) without checking whether the solute is an electrolyte (\(\mathrm{NaCl}\), \(\mathrm{K_2SO_4}\)) or an associating species (\(\mathrm{CH_3COOH}\) in benzene).
  • Unit Consistency in \(\Pi = CRT\): If \(\Pi\) is in \(\mathrm{atm}\), use \(R = 0.0821\,\mathrm{L\,atm\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\); if \(\Pi\) is in \(\mathrm{bar}\), use \(R = 0.083\,\mathrm{L\,bar\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\); if \(\Pi\) is in \(\mathrm{Pa}\) and \(V\) in \(\mathrm{m^3}\), use \(R = 8.314\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\).

Concise Final Recap | संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Key Takeaways: (1) Mass %, ppm, mole fraction, and molality are temperature-independent. (2) Henry's law (\(p = K_{\mathrm{H}} x\)) governs gas solubility; higher \(K_{\mathrm{H}}\) or higher \(T\) means lower solubility. (3) Ideal solutions obey Raoult's law with \(\Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}H = 0, \Delta_{\mathrm{mix}}V = 0\); positive deviations give minimum-boiling azeotropes, while negative deviations give maximum-boiling azeotropes. (4) All four colligative properties scale linearly with the van't Hoff factor \(i\), which exceeds \(1\) for dissociation and is less than \(1\) for association.
