1. Learning Objectives & Exam Overview

Electrochemistry connects chemical reactions with electrical energy. Spontaneous redox reactions can generate electricity in galvanic cells, whereas external electrical energy can drive non-spontaneous chemical transformations in electrolytic cells. For NEET UG, this topic carries high weightage across both numerical problem-solving (Nernst equation, conductance, and Faraday's laws) and conceptual questions (electrochemical series, electrolysis products, batteries, and corrosion).

  • Distinguish between galvanic and electrolytic cells, including how an opposing external potential reverses cell operation.
  • Determine cell EMF using standard electrode potentials and the Nernst equation at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\).
  • Relate standard cell potential (\(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}\)) to standard Gibbs energy (\(\Delta_r G^\ominus\)) and equilibrium constant (\(K_c\)).
  • Calculate resistivity (\(\rho\)), conductivity (\(\kappa\)), cell constant (\(G^*\)), and molar conductivity (\(\Lambda_m\)), and explain their concentration dependence.
  • Apply Kohlrausch's law of independent migration of ions to find \(\Lambda_m^\circ\), degree of dissociation (\(\alpha\)), and \(K_a\) of weak electrolytes.
  • Predict products of electrolysis and perform stoichiometric calculations using Faraday's laws.
  • Understand the chemistry of primary batteries, secondary batteries, hydrogen–oxygen fuel cells, and electrochemical corrosion.

2. Electrochemical Cells: Galvanic vs. Electrolytic Operation

An electrochemical cell consists of two metallic electrodes immersed in electrolytic solutions. Depending on whether the net redox reaction is spontaneous or driven by an external source, the cell functions in two distinct modes:

  • Galvanic (Voltaic) Cell: Converts chemical energy of a spontaneous redox reaction (\(\Delta_r G < 0\)) into electrical work. In a Daniell cell, zinc acts as the anode (oxidation, negative electrode) and copper acts as the cathode (reduction, positive electrode). Electrons flow through the external wire from Zn to Cu, while conventional current flows from Cu to Zn.
  • Salt Bridge: An inverted U-tube containing an inert electrolyte (such as \(\mathrm{KCl}\) or \(\mathrm{KNO_3}\)) in agar-agar gel. It completes the internal circuit and maintains electrical neutrality in both half-cells by allowing ion migration without mixing the two solutions.
  • Electrolytic Cell: Uses an external DC voltage to force a non-spontaneous redox reaction (\(\Delta_r G > 0\)).

2. वैद्युत रासायनिक सेल: गैल्वैनी बनाम विद्युत-अपघटनी कार्यप्रणाली

  • गैल्वैनी (वोल्टीय) सेल: यह एक स्वतःप्रवर्तित रेडॉक्स अभिक्रिया (\(\Delta_r G < 0\)) की रासायनिक ऊर्जा को विद्युत कार्य में परिवर्तित करता है। डैनियल सेल में जिंक ऐनोड (ऑक्सीकरण, ऋणात्मक इलेक्ट्रोड) और कॉपर कैथोड (अपचयन, धनात्मक इलेक्ट्रोड) के रूप में कार्य करता है। बाह्य तार में इलेक्ट्रॉन Zn से Cu की ओर प्रवाहित होते हैं, जबकि पारंपरिक विद्युत धारा Cu से Zn की ओर बहती है।
  • लवण सेतु (Salt Bridge): यह अगर-अगर (agar-agar) जेल में अक्रिय विद्युत-अपघट्य (जैसे \(\mathrm{KCl}\) या \(\mathrm{KNO_3}\)) से भरी एक उल्टी U-आकार की नली होती है। यह आंतरिक परिपथ को पूर्ण करती है और दोनों विलयनों को आपस में मिलाए बिना आयनों के अभिगमन द्वारा दोनों अर्ध-सेलों में विद्युत उदासीनता बनाए रखती है।
  • विद्युत-अपघटनी सेल: यह एक अस्वतःप्रवर्तित रेडॉक्स अभिक्रिया (\(\Delta_r G > 0\)) को संपन्न कराने के लिए बाह्य DC वोल्टता का उपयोग करता है।

3. Standard Electrode Potential, SHE & Electrochemical Series

An individual half-cell potential cannot be measured in isolation; only the potential difference between two half-cells can be determined. By IUPAC convention, the Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is chosen as the universal reference electrode and assigned a potential of exactly \(0.00\,\mathrm{V}\) at all temperatures.

  • SHE Construction: A platinum foil coated with finely divided platinum black is dipped in \(1\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{H^+(aq)}\) solution, with pure \(\mathrm{H_2(g)}\) bubbled over it at \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) pressure. Representation: \(\mathrm{Pt(s)} \mid \mathrm{H_2(g,\,1\,bar)} \mid \mathrm{H^+(aq,\,1\,M)}\).
  • Standard Electrode Potential (\(E^\ominus\)): By IUPAC convention, standard electrode potential refers to the standard reduction potential measured at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), \(1\,\mathrm{M}\) ion concentration, and \(1\,\mathrm{bar}\) gas pressure.

A metal with a lower (more negative) \(E^\ominus\) can displace a metal with a higher (more positive) \(E^\ominus\) from its aqueous salt solution. For instance, \(\mathrm{Zn}\) (\(E^\ominus = -0.76\,\mathrm{V}\)) displaces \(\mathrm{Cu}\) (\(E^\ominus = +0.34\,\mathrm{V}\)) from \(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\) solution; hence, \(\mathrm{CuSO_4}\) cannot be stored in a zinc vessel.

4. Nernst Equation, Gibbs Energy & Equilibrium Constant

When species are not at unit concentration (\(1\,\mathrm{M}\)), electrode and cell potentials depend on concentration and temperature according to the Nernst equation. For a general cell reaction \(a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \xrightarrow{n e^-} c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}\):

\[E_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} - \frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q, \quad \text{where } Q = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c[\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a[\mathrm{B}]^b}\] \[\text{At } T = 298\,\mathrm{K}:\quad E_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} - \frac{0.059}{n}\log_{10} Q\]

Note that pure solids and pure liquids have unit activity (\(=1\)) and are omitted from \(Q\). For a hydrogen electrode in contact with a solution of given \(\mathrm{pH}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) (\(p_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 1\,\mathrm{bar}\)), \(E_{\mathrm{H^+/H_2}} = -0.059\,\mathrm{pH}\).

Thermodynamic Relationships

  • Gibbs Energy and Cell Potential: The maximum electrical work obtainable from a reversible galvanic cell equals the decrease in Gibbs energy:
    \[\Delta_r G = -nFE_{\text{cell}} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Delta_r G^\ominus = -nFE^\ominus_{\text{cell}}\]
    Here \(F = 96487\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}} \approx 96500\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}\). For a spontaneous reaction, \(E_{\text{cell}} > 0\) and \(\Delta_r G < 0\).
  • Equilibrium Constant (\(K_c\)): When the cell reaches equilibrium, \(E_{\text{cell}} = 0\) and \(Q = K_c\):
    \[E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = \frac{2.303RT}{nF}\log_{10} K_c \xrightarrow{298\,\mathrm{K}} E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = \frac{0.059}{n}\log_{10} K_c\]
NEET UG Crucial Distinction: Intensive vs. Extensive

\(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}\) is an intensive property (independent of stoichiometric scaling; if you double the reaction, \(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}\) stays the same). In contrast, \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\) is an extensive property (proportional to \(n\); doubling the reaction doubles \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\)). Because \(E^\ominus\) is intensive, never add \(E^\ominus\) values directly when combining two half-reactions to form a third half-reaction; always add \(\Delta G^\ominus = -nFE^\ominus\) and divide by the new \(nF\).

4. नेर्न्स्ट समीकरण, गिब्स ऊर्जा एवं साम्यावस्था स्थिरांक

जब अभिक्रिया में भाग लेने वाली स्पीशीज़ की सांद्रता इकाई (\(1\,\mathrm{M}\)) न हो, तो इलेक्ट्रोड एवं सेल विभव नेर्न्स्ट समीकरण के अनुसार सांद्रता और ताप पर निर्भर करते हैं। एक सामान्य सेल अभिक्रिया \(a\mathrm{A} + b\mathrm{B} \xrightarrow{n e^-} c\mathrm{C} + d\mathrm{D}\) के लिए:

\[E_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} - \frac{RT}{nF}\ln Q, \quad \text{जहाँ } Q = \frac{[\mathrm{C}]^c[\mathrm{D}]^d}{[\mathrm{A}]^a[\mathrm{B}]^b}\] \[T = 298\,\mathrm{K}\text{ पर}:\quad E_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} - \frac{0.059}{n}\log_{10} Q\]

ध्यान दें कि शुद्ध ठोस और शुद्ध द्रव की सक्रियता इकाई (\(=1\)) ली जाती है और इन्हें \(Q\) में शामिल नहीं किया जाता। \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) पर (\(p_{\mathrm{H_2}} = 1\,\mathrm{bar}\)) किसी निश्चित \(\mathrm{pH}\) वाले विलयन के संपर्क में स्थित हाइड्रोजन इलेक्ट्रोड के लिए, \(E_{\mathrm{H^+/H_2}} = -0.059\,\mathrm{pH}\) होता है।

5. Conductance of Electrolytic Solutions & Kohlrausch's Law

Unlike electronic (metallic) conductance—which occurs via electron movement and decreases as temperature rises—ionic (electrolytic) conductance occurs via ion movement in solution and increases with temperature because inter-ionic attractions weaken and solvent viscosity decreases.

Conductance (\(G\)) \(G = \frac{1}{R} = \kappa\frac{A}{l}\) \(\mathrm{S}\) (siemens \(= \Omega^{-1}\))

To measure the resistance of an ionic solution on a Wheatstone bridge, an alternating current (AC) oscillator is used instead of DC to prevent electrolysis from altering the solution's composition.

Effect of Dilution on \(\kappa\) and \(\Lambda_m\)

  • Conductivity (\(\kappa\)) always decreases on dilution for both strong and weak electrolytes, because the number of current-carrying ions per unit volume decreases.
  • Molar conductivity (\(\Lambda_m\)) always increases on dilution because \(\Lambda_m = \kappa V\), and the increase in total volume \(V\) containing \(1\,\mathrm{mol}\) of electrolyte more than compensates for the decrease in \(\kappa\).
  • Strong Electrolytes: \(\Lambda_m\) increases slowly and linearly with \(\sqrt{c}\) according to the Debye–Hückel–Onsager equation: \(\Lambda_m = \Lambda_m^\circ - A c^{1/2}\). Extrapolation to \(c^{1/2} = 0\) gives the limiting molar conductivity \(\Lambda_m^\circ\).
  • Weak Electrolytes: \(\Lambda_m\) rises steeply at low concentrations due to a sharp increase in the degree of dissociation (\(\alpha\)). Therefore, \(\Lambda_m^\circ\) cannot be obtained by graphical extrapolation.

Kohlrausch's Law of Independent Migration of Ions

At infinite dilution, each ion makes a definite contribution to the limiting molar conductivity of an electrolyte regardless of the co-ion:

6. Electrolysis, Faraday's Laws & Products of Electrolysis

  • Faraday's First Law: The mass (\(w\)) of a substance deposited or liberated at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electricity (\(Q = It\)) passed:
    \[w = ZIt = \frac{E_{\text{eq}}}{F} \times It = \frac{M_{\text{atomic}}}{nF} \times It\]
  • Faraday's Second Law: When the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes connected in series, the masses deposited are proportional to their chemical equivalent weights: \(\frac{w_1}{w_2} = \frac{E_{\text{eq},1}}{E_{\text{eq},2}}\).

Predicting Products of Electrolysis (Inert Pt vs. Active Electrodes)

When competing ions are present, the species with the higher \(E^\ominus_{\text{reduction}}\) is preferentially reduced at the cathode, while the species with the lower \(E^\ominus_{\text{reduction}}\) (higher oxidation potential) is preferentially oxidised at the anode—subject to kinetic overpotential.

जंग लगना वायु और नमी (जिसमें घुली हुई \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) से \(\mathrm{H^+}\) प्राप्त होते हैं) की उपस्थिति में होता है:

  • ऐनोड: \(2\mathrm{Fe(s)} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Fe^{2+}(aq)} + 4\mathrm{e^-}\) (\(E^\ominus_{\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe}} = -0.44\,\mathrm{V}\))
  • कैथोड: \(\mathrm{O_2(g)} + 4\mathrm{H^+(aq)} + 4\mathrm{e^-} \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H_2O(l)}\) (\(E^\ominus = +1.23\,\mathrm{V}\))
  • \(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) वायुमंडलीय \(\mathrm{O_2}\) द्वारा आगे ऑक्सीकृत होकर जलयोजित फेरिक ऑक्साइड (जंग, \(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O}\)) बनाता है।
  • बचाव: अवरोधक लेपन (पेंट, बिसफीनॉल) अथवा अधिक विद्युत-धनात्मक (निम्न \(E^\ominus\)) धातु जैसे \(\mathrm{Zn}\) (यशदलेपन/गैल्वनीकरण) या \(\mathrm{Mg}\) का उत्सर्ग इलेक्ट्रोड के रूप में उपयोग।

Worked Examples

Example 1: Calculating Cell EMF Using the Nernst Equation

Problem (Assumed Scenario): Consider a hypothetical galvanic cell at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\): \(\mathrm{Zn(s)} \mid \mathrm{Zn^{2+}}(0.1\,\mathrm{M}) \parallel \mathrm{Ag^+}(0.01\,\mathrm{M}) \mid \mathrm{Ag(s)}\). Given \(E^\ominus_{\mathrm{Zn^{2+}/Zn}} = -0.76\,\mathrm{V}\) and \(E^\ominus_{\mathrm{Ag^+/Ag}} = +0.80\,\mathrm{V}\), calculate \(E_{\text{cell}}\).

Solution:

1. Overall reaction: \(\mathrm{Zn(s)} + 2\mathrm{Ag^+(aq)} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zn^{2+}(aq)} + 2\mathrm{Ag(s)}\), so \(n = 2\).

2. Standard cell potential: \(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cathode}} - E^\ominus_{\text{anode}} = 0.80 - (-0.76) = 1.56\,\mathrm{V}\).

3. Reaction quotient: \(Q = \frac{[\mathrm{Zn^{2+}}]}{[\mathrm{Ag^+}]^2} = \frac{0.1}{(0.01)^2} = \frac{0.1}{10^{-4}} = 10^3\).

4. Applying Nernst equation at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\): \[E_{\text{cell}} = 1.56 - \frac{0.059}{2}\log_{10}(10^3) = 1.56 - (0.0295 \times 3) = 1.56 - 0.0885 \approx 1.47\,\mathrm{V}\]

Example 2: Standard Gibbs Energy and Equilibrium Constant

Problem (Assumed Scenario): For the cell \(\mathrm{Fe(s)} \mid \mathrm{Fe^{2+}}(1\,\mathrm{M}) \parallel \mathrm{Cu^{2+}}(1\,\mathrm{M}) \mid \mathrm{Cu(s)}\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), given \(E^\ominus_{\mathrm{Fe^{2+}/Fe}} = -0.44\,\mathrm{V}\) and \(E^\ominus_{\mathrm{Cu^{2+}/Cu}} = +0.34\,\mathrm{V}\), calculate \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\) and \(\log_{10} K_c\) (use \(F = 96500\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}\)).

Solution:

1. \(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = 0.34 - (-0.44) = 0.78\,\mathrm{V}\), with \(n = 2\).

2. \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -nFE^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = -2 \times 96500 \times 0.78 = -150540\,\mathrm{J\,mol^{-1}} = -150.54\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\).

3. \(\log_{10} K_c = \frac{n E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}}{0.059} = \frac{2 \times 0.78}{0.059} = \frac{1.56}{0.059} \approx 26.44\).

Example 3: Cell Constant, Molar Conductivity, and Kohlrausch's Law

Problem (Assumed Values): A conductivity cell filled with \(0.05\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{KCl}\) (\(\kappa = 0.65\,\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\)) shows a resistance of \(200\,\Omega\). When filled with \(0.01\,\mathrm{M}\) propanoic acid, the resistance is \(2600\,\Omega\). Given \(\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{HCl}) = 426.0\), \(\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COONa}) = 86.0\), and \(\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{NaCl}) = 126.0\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\), find \(\alpha\) and \(K_a\) of propanoic acid.

Solution:

1. Cell constant: \(G^* = \kappa \times R = 0.65\,\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}} \times 200\,\Omega = 130\,\mathrm{m^{-1}} = 1.30\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\).

2. Conductivity of propanoic acid: \(\kappa = \frac{G^*}{R} = \frac{1.30}{2600} = 5.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}\).

3. Molar conductivity: \(\Lambda_m = \frac{5.0 \times 10^{-4} \times 1000}{0.01} = 50\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\).

4. By Kohlrausch's law: \(\Lambda_m^\circ(\mathrm{CH_3CH_2COOH}) = 426.0 + 86.0 - 126.0 = 386.0\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\).

5. \(\alpha = \frac{50}{386.0} \approx 0.1295\), and \(K_a = \frac{c\alpha^2}{1-\alpha} = \frac{0.01 \times (0.1295)^2}{1 - 0.1295} \approx 1.93 \times 10^{-4}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\).

Example 4: Applying Faraday's First Law of Electrolysis

Problem (Assumed Values): A steady current of \(2.0\,\mathrm{A}\) is passed through molten \(\mathrm{Al_2O_3}\) (with cryolite) for \(4825\,\mathrm{s}\). Calculate the mass of aluminium deposited at the cathode (atomic mass of \(\mathrm{Al} = 27\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\), \(F = 96500\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}\)).

Solution:

1. Charge passed: \(Q = I \times t = 2.0 \times 4825 = 9650\,\mathrm{C} = \frac{9650}{96500}\,\mathrm{F} = 0.1\,\mathrm{F}\).

2. Cathode reaction: \(\mathrm{Al^{3+}} + 3\mathrm{e^-} \rightarrow \mathrm{Al(s)}\) (\(n = 3\)).

3. Mass deposited: \(w = \frac{Q}{F} \times \frac{M_{\text{Al}}}{n} = 0.1 \times \frac{27}{3} = 0.90\,\mathrm{g}\).

If the stoichiometric coefficients of the entire equation are doubled, what are the new values of \(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}}\) and \(\Delta_r G^\ominus\)? (Take \(F = 96500\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}\))

Practice Question 3 (Single-Correct MCQ)

The standard reduction potentials of four metals \(P, Q, R,\) and \(S\) are \(-2.90\,\mathrm{V},\ +0.80\,\mathrm{V},\ -0.76\,\mathrm{V},\) and \(-0.25\,\mathrm{V}\) respectively. Arrange these metals in the increasing order of their reducing power.

  • (A) \(Q < S < R < P\)
  • (B) \(P < R < S < Q\)
  • (C) \(Q < R < S < P\)
  • (D) \(S < Q < R < P\)

Correct Answer: (A) \(Q < S < R < P\)

Worked Solution: A lower (more negative) standard reduction potential indicates a greater tendency to undergo oxidation and therefore stronger reducing power. Ordering from highest to lowest \(E^\ominus\) gives increasing reducing power: \(Q (+0.80\,\mathrm{V}) < S (-0.25\,\mathrm{V}) < R (-0.76\,\mathrm{V}) < P (-2.90\,\mathrm{V})\).

Practice Question 4 (Single-Correct MCQ)

The resistance of a \(0.02\,\mathrm{M}\) aqueous solution of an electrolyte in a conductivity cell having cell constant \(0.80\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}\) is \(400\,\Omega\) at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\). What is its molar conductivity?

  • (A) \(100\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (B) \(50\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (C) \(200\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (D) \(10\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)

Correct Answer: (A) \(100\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)

Worked Solution: Conductivity \(\kappa = \frac{G^*}{R} = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}}{400\,\Omega} = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}\).   • (A) \(100\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (B) \(50\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (C) \(200\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)
  • (D) \(10\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)

सही उत्तर: (A) \(100\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)

हल: चालकता \(\kappa = \frac{G^*}{R} = \frac{0.80\,\mathrm{cm^{-1}}}{400\,\Omega} = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}\)। मोलर चालकता \(\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{M} = \frac{2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 1000}{0.02} = \frac{2.0}{0.02} = 100\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\)।

Practice Question 5 (Single-Correct MCQ)

At \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), the limiting molar conductivities (\(\Lambda_m^\circ\)) of \(\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\), \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), and \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) are \(130.0\), \(248.0\), and \(126.0\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\) respectively. If the molar conductivity of \(0.01\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{NH_4OH}\) is \(12.6\,\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\), what is its percentage dissociation?

  • (A) \(2.5\%\)
  • (B) \(5.0\%\)
  • (C) \(10.0\%\)
  • (D) \(0.5\%\)

Correct Answer: (B) \(5.0\%\)

Degree of dissociation \(\alpha = \frac{\Lambda_m}{\Lambda_m^\circ} = \frac{12.6}{252.0} = 0.05\), which corresponds to \(5.0\%\).

Practice Question 6 (Single-Correct MCQ)

Two electrolytic cells containing molten \(\mathrm{MgCl_2}\) and aqueous \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) are connected in series. If \(5.4\,\mathrm{g}\) of \(\mathrm{Ag}\) (atomic mass \(= 108\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) is deposited at the cathode of the second cell, what mass of \(\mathrm{Mg}\) (atomic mass \(= 24\,\mathrm{g\,mol^{-1}}\)) is deposited at the cathode of the first cell?

  • (A) \(1.20\,\mathrm{g}\)
  • (B) \(0.60\,\mathrm{g}\)
  • (C) \(2.40\,\mathrm{g}\)
  • (D) \(0.30\,\mathrm{g}\)

Correct Answer: (B) \(0.60\,\mathrm{g}\)

Worked Solution: By Faraday's second law, \(\frac{w_{\mathrm{Mg}}}{w_{\mathrm{Ag}}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{Mg}}}{E_{\mathrm{Ag}}}\). Equivalent weight of \(\mathrm{Ag} = \frac{108}{1} = 108\), and equivalent weight of \(\mathrm{Mg} = \frac{24}{2} = 12\). Thus, \(w_{\mathrm{Mg}} = 5.4 \times \frac{12}{108} = 0.05 \times 12 = 0.60\,\mathrm{g}\).

  • (D) \(0.30\,\mathrm{g}\)

सही उत्तर: (B) \(0.60\,\mathrm{g}\)

Practice Question 7 (Assertion–Reason MCQ)

Assertion (A): During the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous \(\mathrm{NaCl}\) solution using inert platinum electrodes, \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\) gas is liberated at the anode instead of \(\mathrm{O_2}\) gas.
Reason (R): The oxidation of water to \(\mathrm{O_2}\) is kinetically slow and requires an extra potential known as overpotential.

  • (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.
  • (B) Both A and R are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.
  • (C) A is true, but R is false.
  • (D) A is false, but R is true.

Correct Answer: (A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Worked Solution: Although \(E^\ominus\) for water oxidation (\(1.23\,\mathrm{V}\)) is lower than that for \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) oxidation (\(1.36\,\mathrm{V}\)), oxygen evolution is kinetically sluggish and demands extra overpotential, making \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\) evolution the preferred anodic process.

8. Common Pitfalls, Revision Checklist & Final Recap

Common Pitfalls to Avoid in NEET UG

  • Sign & Electrode Confusion: In a galvanic cell, the anode is negative and the cathode is positive; in an electrolytic cell, the anode is connected to the positive terminal and the cathode to the negative terminal. However, oxidation ALWAYS occurs at the anode and reduction ALWAYS occurs at the cathode in both cell types.
  • Unit Mismatch in \(\Lambda_m\): When \(\kappa\) is in \(\mathrm{S\,cm^{-1}}\) and \(M\) is in \(\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\), use \(\Lambda_m = \frac{\kappa \times 1000}{M}\) to get \(\mathrm{S\,cm^2\,mol^{-1}}\). When \(\kappa\) is in \(\mathrm{S\,m^{-1}}\), convert molarity to \(\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}\) (\(1\,\mathrm{M} = 1000\,\mathrm{mol\,m^{-3}}\)).
  • Using \(0.059/n\) at Non-298 K Temperatures: The factor \(0.059\,\mathrm{V}\) applies strictly at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\). At any other temperature, use \(\frac{2.303RT}{nF}\).

Quick Revision Checklist

  • \(E^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = E^\ominus_{\text{cathode}} - E^\ominus_{\text{anode}}\); \(\Delta_r G^\ominus = -nFE^\ominus_{\text{cell}} = -2.303RT\log_{10} K_c\).
  • On dilution: \(\kappa\) decreases, \(\Lambda_m\) increases.
  • \(1\,\mathrm{F} \approx 96500\,\mathrm{C\,mol^{-1}}\) deposits \(1\,\text{gram-equivalent}\) of any substance.
  • Mercury cell gives constant \(1.35\,\mathrm{V}\); Lead storage battery uses \(38\%\) \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\); Fuel cell efficiency \(\approx 70\%\).

8. सामान्य त्रुटियाँ, पुनरावृत्ति जाँच-सूची एवं संक्षिप्त सारांश

  • चिह्न एवं इलेक्ट्रोड भ्रम: गैल्वैनी सेल में ऐनोड ऋणात्मक और कैथोड धनात्मक होता है; विद्युत-अपघटनी सेल में ऐनोड धनात्मक टर्मिनल से और कैथोड ऋणात्मक टर्मिनल से जुड़ा होता है। किंतु दोनों सेलों में ऑक्सीकरण सदैव ऐनोड पर और अपचयन सदैव कैथोड पर होता है।
