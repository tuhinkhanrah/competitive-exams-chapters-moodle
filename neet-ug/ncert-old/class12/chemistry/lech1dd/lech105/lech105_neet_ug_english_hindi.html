Surface chemistry investigates physical and chemical processes occurring at the boundary (interface) separating two bulk phases, such as solid–gas, solid–liquid, or liquid–gas. Because gases mix completely in all proportions, no gas–gas interface exists. Ultra-clean metal surfaces for precision surface studies require ultra-high vacuum (\(10^{-8}\) to \(10^{-9}\,\mathrm{Pa}\)) to prevent immediate coverage by atmospheric \(\mathrm{O_2}\) and \(\mathrm{N_2}\).

  • Compare physisorption and chemisorption, and apply the Freundlich adsorption isotherm to gases and solutions.
  • Explain catalytic mechanisms, promoters, poisons, selectivity, zeolite shape-selectivity, and enzyme kinetics.
  • Classify colloids by physical state, phase interaction, and particle nature, and explain micellization, purification, optical/kinetic/electrical properties, the Hardy–Schulze rule, and emulsions.

1. Interfacial Phenomena & Adsorption / अंतरापृष्ठीय परिघटनाएँ एवं अधिशोषण

English

Core Concepts: Adsorption vs. Absorption

Adsorption is the accumulation of molecular species at the surface of a solid or liquid rather than in its bulk. The substance accumulating at the surface is the adsorbate, while the material providing the surface is the adsorbent (e.g., activated charcoal, silica gel, alumina gel, clay, finely divided metals). Removing an adsorbed species from a surface is called desorption.

  • Adsorption (surface phenomenon): Silica gel adsorbs water vapour on its surface; animal charcoal decolourises raw sugar or methylene blue solutions by adsorbing dye molecules; powdered charcoal reduces gas pressure in a closed vessel containing \(\mathrm{O_2}\), \(\mathrm{H_2}\), \(\mathrm{CO}\), \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\), \(\mathrm{NH_3}\), or \(\mathrm{SO_2}\).
  • Absorption (bulk phenomenon): The substance distributes uniformly throughout the interior bulk. For instance, anhydrous \(\mathrm{CaCl_2}\) absorbs water vapour into its bulk, whereas silica gel adsorbs it at the surface. When a chalk stick is dipped in ink, coloured ink molecules adsorb on the outer surface while the solvent penetrates inward by absorption.
  • Sorption: When adsorption and absorption occur simultaneously, the combined process is termed sorption.

Thermodynamic Mechanism of Adsorption

Inside the bulk of a solid, inter-particle forces are balanced in all directions. At the surface, particles lack neighbours on one side and therefore possess unbalanced (residual) attractive forces. Attracting adsorbate molecules reduces these residual forces and lowers surface energy, releasing heat. Consequently, adsorption is always exothermic (\(\Delta H < 0\)).

Once gas molecules are held on a surface, their translational freedom decreases, so entropy decreases (\(\Delta S < 0\)). For spontaneity at constant temperature and pressure, Gibbs free energy change must be negative:

\[ \Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0 \]

Since \(-T\Delta S\) is positive, \(\Delta H\) must be sufficiently negative to make \(\Delta G < 0\). As adsorption progresses, \(\Delta H\) becomes less negative until \(\Delta H = T\Delta S\), giving \(\Delta G = 0\) at adsorption equilibrium.

मूल अवधारणाएँ: अधिशोषण बनाम अवशोषण

  • अधिशोषण (पृष्ठीय परिघटना): सिलिका जेल जलवाष्प को सतह पर अधिशोषित करता है; जंतु चारकोल मेथिलीन ब्लू या कच्ची शर्करा के विलयन से रंगीन अशुद्धियों को अधिशोषित कर विलयन को रंगहीन बनाता है; बंद पात्र में चूर्णित चारकोल \(\mathrm{O_2}\), \(\mathrm{H_2}\), \(\mathrm{CO}\), \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\), \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) या \(\mathrm{SO_2}\) गैसों का दाब कम कर देता है।
  • अवशोषण (स्थूल परिघटना): इसमें पदार्थ ठोस के संपूर्ण आंतरिक भाग में एकसमान रूप से वितरित हो जाता है। उदाहरणार्थ, निर्जल \(\mathrm{CaCl_2}\) जलवाष्प को अवशोषित करता है, जबकि सिलिका जेल उसे अधिशोषित करता है। स्याही में चॉक डुबोने पर रंग के कण सतह पर अधिशोषित होते हैं, जबकि विलायक भीतर अवशोषित हो जाता है।
  • शोषण (Sorption): जब अधिशोषण और अवशोषण दोनों साथ-साथ होते हैं, तो उसे शोषण कहते हैं।

अधिशोषण की ऊष्मागतिकी क्रियाविधि

ठोस के भीतरी भाग में कणों पर लगने वाले बल सभी दिशाओं से संतुलित होते हैं, परंतु सतह के कणों पर असंतुलित या अवशिष्ट आकर्षण बल होते हैं। अधिशोष्य के अणुओं के जुड़ने से पृष्ठीय ऊर्जा घटती है और ऊष्मा मुक्त होती है, अतः अधिशोषण सदैव ऊष्माक्षेपी (\(\Delta H < 0\)) होता है।

सतह पर बंधने से गैस अणुओं की स्वतंत्रता घटती है, जिससे एन्ट्रॉपी घटती है (\(\Delta S < 0\))। स्थिर ताप व दाब पर स्वतःप्रवर्तिता के लिए गिब्स मुक्त ऊर्जा परिवर्तन ऋणात्मक होना आवश्यक है:

\[ \Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S < 0 \]

चूँकि \(-T\Delta S\) धनात्मक होता है, अतः \(\Delta G < 0\) रखने के लिए \(\Delta H\) का पर्याप्त ऋणात्मक होना आवश्यक है। अधिशोषण बढ़ने के साथ \(\Delta H\) कम ऋणात्मक होता जाता है और अंततः \(\Delta H = T\Delta S\) होने पर \(\Delta G = 0\) हो जाता है—यही अधिशोषण साम्य है।

Physisorption vs. Chemisorption

Depending on the nature of surface forces, gas adsorption on solids is classified into physical adsorption (physisorption) and chemical adsorption (chemisorption). At low temperature, a gas such as \(\mathrm{H_2}\) on nickel may first undergo physisorption and then dissociate into atoms to form chemisorbed hydrides as temperature rises.

Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm & Solution-Phase Adsorption

An adsorption isotherm plots the extent of adsorption \(x/m\) (mass \(x\) of gas adsorbed per unit mass \(m\) of adsorbent) against pressure \(p\) at constant temperature. Freundlich (1909) proposed an empirical relation valid over a limited pressure range:

\[ \frac{x}{m} = k\,p^{1/n} \quad (n > 1, \text{ so } 0 < \frac{1}{n} < 1) \] \[ \log \left(\frac{x}{m}\right) = \log k + \frac{1}{n} \log p \]
  • Plotting \(\log(x/m)\) vs. \(\log p\) yields a straight line with slope \(= 1/n\) (typically \(0.1\text{–}0.5\)) and \(y\)-intercept \(= \log k\).
  • When \(1/n = 0\), \(x/m = k\) (independent of pressure at high \(p\) saturation); when \(1/n = 1\), \(x/m = k p\) (directly proportional to \(p\) at low \(p\)). Because experimental curves reach saturation at very high pressure, Freundlich's equation fails at high pressure.
  • Adsorption from solutions: Replacing \(p\) with equilibrium solute concentration \(C\) gives \(\frac{x}{m} = k\,C^{1/n}\), or \(\log\left(\frac{x}{m}\right) = \log k + \frac{1}{n}\log C\). Extent of adsorption increases with surface area and solute concentration, and decreases with rising temperature.
  • Key applications: High-vacuum generation (charcoal), gas masks in coal mines, humidity control (silica/alumina gels), decolourising solutions (animal charcoal), heterogeneous catalysis, noble gas separation on coconut charcoal, froth floatation of sulphide ores, adsorption indicators (eosin/fluorescein on silver halides), and chromatography.

\[ \frac{x}{m} = k\,p^{1/n} \quad (n > 1,\; 0 < \frac{1}{n} < 1) \] \[ \log \left(\frac{x}{m}\right) = \log k + \frac{1}{n} \log p \]

2. Catalysis: Interfaces, Zeolites & Enzymes / उत्प्रेरण: अंतरापृष्ठ, ज़िओलाइट एवं एंजाइम

English

A catalyst alters the rate of a chemical reaction while remaining chemically and quantitatively unchanged at the end (e.g., \(\mathrm{MnO_2}\) lowers the decomposition temperature of \(\mathrm{KClO_3}\) from \(653\text{–}873\,\mathrm{K}\) to \(473\text{–}633\,\mathrm{K}\)). Promoters enhance catalyst activity (e.g., \(\mathrm{Mo}\) promotes \(\mathrm{Fe}\) in Haber's ammonia synthesis), whereas poisons suppress activity (e.g., \(\mathrm{CO}\) poisons the iron catalyst in Haber's process).

  • Homogeneous catalysis: Reactants and catalyst share the same phase — e.g., \(\mathrm{NO(g)}\) in lead-chamber oxidation of \(\mathrm{SO_2(g)}\); \(\mathrm{H^+(aq)}\) from \(\mathrm{HCl}\) in methyl acetate hydrolysis; \(\mathrm{H^+(aq)}\) from \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) in sucrose inversion.
  • Heterogeneous catalysis: Catalyst and reactants occupy different phases — e.g., \(\mathrm{Pt(s)}\) for \(\mathrm{SO_2}\) oxidation or \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) oxidation (Ostwald's process); finely divided \(\mathrm{Fe(s)}\) in Haber's process; \(\mathrm{Ni(s)}\) in hydrogenation of vegetable oils to vegetable ghee.

Modern Adsorption Theory of Heterogeneous Catalysis

Combining intermediate compound formation with surface adsorption, catalysis on a solid surface proceeds in five sequential steps: (1) diffusion of reactants to the catalyst surface, (2) adsorption of reactants onto free surface valencies, (3) surface reaction via an adsorbed intermediate, (4) desorption of products freeing active sites, and (5) diffusion of products away from the surface. This explains why a small mass of catalyst drives large-scale reactions without being consumed, though it does not independently explain promoters and poisons.

Activity, Selectivity & Shape-Selective Catalysis

  • Activity: Depends on optimal chemisorption strength—strong enough to activate reactants, yet not so strong that molecules become immobilised. For hydrogenation, activity increases across Groups 5–11, peaking in Groups 7–9.
  • Selectivity: Ability of a catalyst to direct identical reactants toward distinct products:
    \(\mathrm{CO(g) + 3H_2(g) \xrightarrow{Ni} CH_4(g) + H_2O(g)}\)
    \(\mathrm{CO(g) + 2H_2(g) \xrightarrow{Cu/ZnO\text{-}Cr_2O_3} CH_3OH(g)}\)
    \(\mathrm{CO(g) + H_2(g) \xrightarrow{Cu} HCHO(g)}\)
  • Shape-selective catalysis (Zeolites): Zeolites are microporous aluminosilicates with honeycomb-like 3D \(\mathrm{Al\text{–}O\text{–}Si}\) frameworks whose pore dimensions control which reactant/product shapes can enter or exit. In petrochemicals, ZSM-5 dehydrates alcohols directly into gasoline (petrol).

3. The Colloidal State: Classification, Preparation & Purification / कोलॉइडी अवस्था: वर्गीकरण, विरचन एवं शोधन

English

A colloid is a heterogeneous system containing a dispersed phase distributed as fine particles of diameter \(1\text{–}1000\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(10^{-9}\text{–}10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\)) in a continuous dispersion medium. Dividing a bulk solid to colloidal size produces an enormous surface area per unit mass.

Sols in water are aquasols (hydrosols); in alcohol, alcosols.

(B) Based on Phase Interaction:

  • Lyophilic sols (liquid-loving, reversible): Directly formed by mixing gum, gelatin, starch, or rubber with a suitable liquid. They are strongly solvated, stable, not easily coagulated, and can be reconstituted after evaporation.
  • Lyophobic sols (liquid-hating, irreversible): Metals and metal sulphides require special preparation methods. They are unstable, readily coagulated by small amounts of electrolytes, heating, or shaking, and require stabilising agents.

(C) Based on Dispersed Particle Type:

  • Multimolecular colloids: Aggregates of many small atoms/molecules (e.g., gold sol, \(\mathrm{S_8}\) sulphur sol).
  • Macromolecular colloids: Single giant molecules in the colloidal range (e.g., starch, cellulose, proteins, enzymes, polythene, nylon, polystyrene).
  • Associated colloids (Micelles): Substances behaving as normal strong electrolytes at low concentration, but aggregating into micelles above the Kraft temperature (\(T_k\)) and critical micelle concentration (CMC). For soaps (e.g., sodium stearate \(\mathrm{C_{17}H_{35}COO^-Na^+}\)), \(\text{CMC} \approx 10^{-4}\text{–}10^{-3}\,\mathrm{mol\,L^{-1}}\). Each micelle contains \(\ge 100\) ions with hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails pointing inward toward an oil/grease droplet and hydrophilic \(-\mathrm{COO^-}\) (or \(-\mathrm{SO_4^-}\) in detergents) heads facing water, enabling emulsification and cleansing.

Preparation & Purification of Colloids

  • Chemical methods: Double decomposition (\(\mathrm{As_2O_3 + 3H_2S \rightarrow As_2S_3(sol) + 3H_2O}\)); Oxidation (\(\mathrm{SO_2 + 2H_2S \rightarrow 3S(sol) + 2H_2O}\)); Reduction (\(\mathrm{2AuCl_3 + 3HCHO + 3H_2O \rightarrow 2Au(sol) + 3HCOOH + 6HCl}\)); Hydrolysis (\(\mathrm{FeCl_3 + 3H_2O \rightarrow Fe(OH)_3(sol) + 3HCl}\)).
  • Bredig's Arc method: An electric arc struck between metal electrodes (\(\mathrm{Au}\), \(\mathrm{Ag}\), \(\mathrm{Pt}\)) immersed in an ice-cooled dispersion medium vapourises the metal, which condenses into colloidal particles (dispersion + condensation).
  • Peptization: Converting a freshly prepared precipitate into a colloidal sol by shaking with dispersion medium and a small amount of electrolyte (peptizing agent).
  • Purification: Excess electrolytes destabilise sols and must be reduced to trace levels via Dialysis (diffusion of ions/crystalloids out through animal/parchment/cellophane membrane), Electro-dialysis (accelerated by an electric field), or Ultrafiltration (filter paper impregnated with \(4\%\) collodion—nitrocellulose in alcohol/ether—hardened with formaldehyde).

4. Properties of Colloids, Coagulation & Emulsions / कोलॉइडों के गुण, स्कंदन एवं पायस

English
  • Colligative properties: Because colloidal particles are large aggregates, the number of particles per unit volume is small; hence osmotic pressure, \(\Delta T_b\), \(\Delta T_f\), and \(\Delta p\) are much smaller than in true solutions of the same mass concentration.
  • Tyndall effect: Scattering of light by colloidal particles, producing an illuminated Tyndall cone viewed at right angles. Observed only when (i) particle diameter is not much smaller than the light wavelength, and (ii) the refractive indices of dispersed phase and medium differ greatly. Used in Zsigmondy's ultramicroscope (which detects scattered light points, not actual particle size/shape).
  • Brownian movement: Continuous zig-zag motion caused by unbalanced bombardment by dispersion-medium molecules; faster for smaller particles and lower viscosity; prevents settling under gravity.
  • Charge on colloidal particles: All particles in a given sol carry the same sign of charge:
    • Positively charged sols: Hydrated metal oxides (\(\mathrm{Al_2O_3\cdot xH_2O}\), \(\mathrm{CrO_3\cdot xH_2O}\), \(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O}\)), basic dyes (methylene blue), haemoglobin, \(\mathrm{TiO_2}\).
    • Negatively charged sols: Metals (\(\mathrm{Cu}\), \(\mathrm{Ag}\), \(\mathrm{Au}\)), metallic sulphides (\(\mathrm{As_2S_3}\), \(\mathrm{Sb_2S_3}\), \(\mathrm{CdS}\)), acid dyes (eosin, congo red), starch, gum, gelatin, clay, charcoal.
  • Preferential ion adsorption & Helmholtz Electrical Double Layer: Adding dilute \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) to excess \(\mathrm{KI}\) gives negatively charged \(\mathrm{AgI/I^-}\) (counter-ion \(\mathrm{K^+}\)), whereas adding \(\mathrm{KI}\) to excess \(\mathrm{AgNO_3}\) gives positively charged \(\mathrm{AgI/Ag^+}\) (counter-ion \(\mathrm{NO_3^-}\) or \(\mathrm{I^-}\)). Similarly, \(\mathrm{FeCl_3}\) in excess hot water forms \(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O/Fe^{3+}}\) (+ve), while in \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) it forms \(\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O/OH^-}\) (−ve). The firmly held fixed layer and mobile diffused layer form the Helmholtz electrical double layer, creating an electrokinetic or zeta potential.
  • Electrophoresis & Electroosmosis: Movement of charged sol particles toward cathode (+ve sol) or anode (−ve sol) under an electric field is electrophoresis. If particle movement is prevented, the dispersion medium moves instead (electroosmosis).

Coagulation, Hardy–Schulze Rule & Emulsions

  • Coagulation (precipitation): Settling of lyophobic sol particles by neutralising their charge via (i) electrophoresis, (ii) mixing two oppositely charged sols (mutual coagulation, e.g., \(+\text{ve }\mathrm{Fe_2O_3\cdot xH_2O}\) and \(-\text{ve }\mathrm{As_2S_3}\)), (iii) boiling, (iv) persistent dialysis, or (v) adding excess electrolytes.
  • Hardy–Schulze Rule: Greater the valence of the oppositely charged coagulating (flocculating) ion, greater is its coagulating power:
    For negative sols: \(\mathrm{Al^{3+} > Ba^{2+} > Na^+}\)
    For positive sols: \(\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_6]^{4-} > PO_4^{3-} > SO_4^{2-} > Cl^-}\)
  • Coagulating value: Minimum electrolyte concentration in \(\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}\) required to precipitate a sol in \(2\,\mathrm{h}\). Smaller coagulating value \(\implies\) higher coagulating power.
  • Protective colloids: Lyophilic sols form a solvated protective sheath around lyophobic particles, shielding them from electrolytes.
  • Emulsions (liquid–liquid colloids): Oil-in-water (O/W) (milk, vanishing cream; emulsifiers: proteins, gums, soaps) and Water-in-oil (W/O) (butter, cream; emulsifiers: heavy-metal fatty-acid salts, long-chain alcohols, lampblack). Emulsions can be diluted with the dispersion medium and broken (demulsified) by heating, freezing, or centrifuging.
  • Daily-life & industrial colloids: Blue sky (scattering), clouds/artificial rain (oppositely charged sand/sol), delta formation (sea electrolytes coagulating river clay), Cottrell smoke precipitator (\(\ge 30{,}000\,\mathrm{V}\)), alum in water purification and styptic blood clotting, leather tanning (tannin/chromium salts), AgBr-gelatin photographic plates, and latex rubber coagulation.

Applying the Freundlich Adsorption Isotherm

English

Problem (Assumed Laboratory Data): In a hypothetical experiment at \(298\,\mathrm{K}\), \(2.0\,\mathrm{g}\) of activated charcoal adsorbs \(0.40\,\mathrm{g}\) of a gas at \(p = 1.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) and \(0.80\,\mathrm{g}\) of the same gas at \(p = 4.0\,\mathrm{bar}\). Assuming the Freundlich isotherm holds, determine the constants \(k\) and \(1/n\), and calculate \(x/m\) at \(p = 9.0\,\mathrm{bar}\).

Solution:

Using \(\dfrac{x}{m} = k\,p^{1/n}\):

At \(p = 1.0\,\mathrm{bar}\): \(\dfrac{x}{m} = \dfrac{0.40}{2.0} = 0.20\). Thus, \(0.20 = k\,(1.0)^{1/n} \implies k = 0.20\).

At \(p = 4.0\,\mathrm{bar}\): \(\dfrac{x}{m} = \dfrac{0.80}{2.0} = 0.40\). Substituting \(k = 0.20\): \[ 0.40 = 0.20 \times (4.0)^{1/n} \implies 2.0 = 4.0^{1/n} = 2^{2/n} \implies \frac{2}{n} = 1 \implies \frac{1}{n} = 0.5 \]

At \(p = 9.0\,\mathrm{bar}\): \(\dfrac{x}{m} = 0.20 \times (9.0)^{0.5} = 0.20 \times 3.0 = 0.60\).

हिन्दी

प्रश्न (काल्पनिक प्रयोगशाला आँकड़े): \(298\,\mathrm{K}\) पर एक काल्पनिक प्रयोग में, \(2.0\,\mathrm{g}\) सक्रियत चारकोल \(p = 1.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) दाब पर \(0.40\,\mathrm{g}\) गैस तथा \(p = 4.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) दाब पर \(0.80\,\mathrm{g}\) गैस अधिशोषित करता है। फ्रॉयंडलिक समतापी को मान्य मानते हुए स्थिरांक \(k\) व \(1/n\) ज्ञात कीजिए तथा \(p = 9.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) पर \(x/m\) की गणना कीजिए।

हल:

\(\dfrac{x}{m} = k\,p^{1/n}\) का उपयोग करने पर:

\(p = 1.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) पर: \(\dfrac{x}{m} = \dfrac{0.40}{2.0} = 0.20\), अतः \(0.20 = k\,(1.0)^{1/n} \implies k = 0.20\)।

\(p = 4.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) पर: \(\dfrac{x}{m} = \dfrac{0.80}{2.0} = 0.40\), अतः \(0.40 = 0.20 \times (4.0)^{1/n} \implies 2.0 = 2^{2/n} \implies \dfrac{1}{n} = 0.5\)।

\(p = 9.0\,\mathrm{bar}\) पर: \(\dfrac{x}{m} = 0.20 \times (9.0)^{0.5} = 0.20 \times 3.0 = 0.60\)।

Evaluating Thermodynamic Spontaneity of Adsorption

English

Problem (Assumed Values): For a hypothetical physisorption process, \(\Delta H = -30\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) and \(\Delta S = -75\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\). Calculate \(\Delta G\) at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) and find the temperature above which adsorption becomes non-spontaneous (assuming \(\Delta H\) and \(\Delta S\) are temperature-independent).

Solution:

Convert entropy change to \(\mathrm{kJ}\): \(\Delta S = -0.075\,\mathrm{kJ\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\).

At \(T = 300\,\mathrm{K}\): \[ \Delta G = \Delta H - T\Delta S = -30 - (300)(-0.075) = -30 + 22.5 = -7.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \] Since \(\Delta G < 0\), adsorption is spontaneous at \(300\,\mathrm{K}\).

Equilibrium (\(\Delta G = 0\)) occurs at \(T_{\mathrm{eq}} = \dfrac{\Delta H}{\Delta S} = \dfrac{-30}{-0.075} = 400\,\mathrm{K}\). Above \(400\,\mathrm{K}\), \(\Delta G > 0\) and adsorption ceases to be spontaneous.

हिन्दी

प्रश्न (काल्पनिक मान): किसी काल्पनिक भौतिक अधिशोषण प्रक्रम के लिए \(\Delta H = -30\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) तथा \(\Delta S = -75\,\mathrm{J\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\) है। \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) पर \(\Delta G\) की गणना कीजिए तथा वह ताप ज्ञात कीजिए जिसके ऊपर अधिशोषण अस्वतः हो जाएगा।

हल:

\(\Delta S = -0.075\,\mathrm{kJ\,K^{-1}\,mol^{-1}}\)। \(T = 300\,\mathrm{K}\) पर: \[ \Delta G = -30 - (300)(-0.075) = -30 + 22.5 = -7.5\,\mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}} \] चूँकि \(\Delta G < 0\), अतः \(300\,\mathrm{K}\) पर अधिशोषण स्वतःप्रवर्तित है।

साम्यावस्था (\(\Delta G = 0\)) पर \(T_{\mathrm{eq}} = \dfrac{-30}{-0.075} = 400\,\mathrm{K}\)। \(400\,\mathrm{K}\) से ऊपर \(\Delta G > 0\) हो जाएगा और अधिशोषण अस्वतः होगा।

Applying the Hardy–Schulze Rule & Coagulating Value

English

Problem (Assumed Trial): A positively charged \(\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}\) sol is coagulated using \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\), and \(\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}\). In a hypothetical test, \(50\,\mathrm{mL}\) of \(0.02\,\mathrm{M}\) \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) is the minimum volume needed to coagulate \(950\,\mathrm{mL}\) of the sol (total volume \(1.0\,\mathrm{L}\)) in \(2\,\mathrm{h}\). Calculate the coagulating value of \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) and arrange the three electrolytes in increasing order of coagulating power.

Solution:

Millimoles of \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) in \(1.0\,\mathrm{L}\) final mixture \(= 50\,\mathrm{mL} \times 0.02\,\mathrm{mmol\,mL^{-1}} = 1.0\,\mathrm{mmol}\). Thus, coagulating value \(= 1.0\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}\).

Because \(\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}\) sol is positively charged, the active coagulating ions are anions: \(\mathrm{Cl^-}\) (\(-1\)), \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\) (\(-2\)), and \(\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}}\) (\(-3\)). By the Hardy–Schulze rule, increasing order of coagulating power is: \[ \mathrm{NaCl < Na_2SO_4 < K_3[Fe(CN)_6]} \]

हिन्दी

प्रश्न (काल्पनिक परीक्षण): एक धनावेशित \(\mathrm{Fe(OH)_3}\) सॉल को \(\mathrm{NaCl}\), \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) तथा \(\mathrm{K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}\) द्वारा स्कंदित किया जाता है। एक काल्पनिक परीक्षण में, \(950\,\mathrm{mL}\) सॉल को 2 घंटे में स्कंदित करने के लिए न्यूनतम \(50\,\mathrm{mL}\) (\(0.02\,\mathrm{M}\)) \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) (कुल आयतन \(1.0\,\mathrm{L}\)) आवश्यक होता है। \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) का स्कंदन मान ज्ञात कीजिए तथा तीनों वैद्युतअपघट्यों को बढ़ती स्कंदन क्षमता के क्रम में लिखिए।

हल:

\(1.0\,\mathrm{L}\) मिश्रण में \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4}\) के मिलीमोल \(= 50 \times 0.02 = 1.0\,\mathrm{mmol}\), अतः स्कंदन मान \(= 1.0\,\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}\)।

धनावेशित सॉल के लिए प्रभावी आयन ऋणायन (\(\mathrm{Cl^-}\), \(\mathrm{SO_4^{2-}}\), \(\mathrm{[Fe(CN)_6]^{3-}}\)) हैं। हार्डी–शुल्ज़ नियम से बढ़ती स्कंदन क्षमता का क्रम है: \(\mathrm{NaCl < Na_2SO_4 < K_3[Fe(CN)_6]}\)।

Surface Area Scaling to Colloidal Dimensions

English

Problem (Assumed Geometry): A hypothetical solid cube of edge \(1.0\,\mathrm{cm}\) is subdivided into identical cubes of edge \(10\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm}\)). Calculate the total surface area after subdivision and the factor of increase.

Solution:

Initial surface area \(A_1 = 6 \times (1.0\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}\); initial volume \(V = 1.0\,\mathrm{cm^3}\).

Volume of one small cube \(v = (1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm})^3 = 1.0 \times 10^{-18}\,\mathrm{cm^3}\), so number of cubes \(N = \dfrac{1.0}{1.0 \times 10^{-18}} = 10^{18}\).

Total new surface area \(A_2 = 10^{18} \times 6 \times (1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 6.0 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 600\,\mathrm{m^2}\). Surface area increases by a factor of \(10^6\).

हिन्दी

प्रश्न (काल्पनिक ज्यामिति): \(1.0\,\mathrm{cm}\) भुजा वाले एक काल्पनिक ठोस घन को \(10\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm}\)) भुजा वाले समान छोटे घनों में विभाजित किया जाता है। विभाजन के पश्चात कुल पृष्ठीय क्षेत्रफल तथा वृद्धि गुणक ज्ञात कीजिए।

हल:

प्रारंभिक पृष्ठीय क्षेत्रफल \(A_1 = 6 \times (1.0\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 6.0\,\mathrm{cm^2}\)। छोटे घनों की संख्या \(N = \dfrac{1.0}{(1.0 \times 10^{-6})^3} = 10^{18}\)।

कुल नया पृष्ठीय क्षेत्रफल \(A_2 = 10^{18} \times 6 \times (1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{cm})^2 = 6.0 \times 10^6\,\mathrm{cm^2} = 600\,\mathrm{m^2}\)। अतः पृष्ठीय क्षेत्रफल \(10^6\) गुना बढ़ जाता है।

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist / सामान्य त्रुटियाँ एवं पुनरावृत्ति सूची

English
  • Pitfall 1 (Sign of \(\Delta S\)): Students sometimes assume \(\Delta S > 0\) during adsorption. In reality, gas freedom decreases on a surface, so \(\Delta S < 0\); spontaneity requires \(\Delta H\) to be sufficiently negative.
  • Pitfall 2 (Coagulating value vs. power): A smaller coagulating value (\(\mathrm{mmol\,L^{-1}}\)) means higher coagulating power.
  • Pitfall 3 (O/W vs. W/O): Milk and vanishing cream are O/W emulsions; butter and cream are W/O emulsions.
  • Checklist: Remember \(\mathrm{AgI/I^-}\) vs. \(\mathrm{AgI/Ag^+}\), Freundlich limits (\(0 < 1/n < 1\), fails at high \(p\)), enzyme optima (\(298\text{–}310\,\mathrm{K}\), pH \(5\text{–}7\)), and \(\mathrm{ZSM\text{-}5}\).

Concise Final Recap / संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

English

Surface chemistry connects thermodynamics, kinetics, and colloidal science. Adsorption is always exothermic (\(\Delta H < 0, \Delta S < 0\)), governed at moderate pressures by the Freundlich isotherm \(x/m = k\,p^{1/n}\). Solid catalysts accelerate reactions via surface adsorption and intermediate formation, with zeolites (\(\mathrm{ZSM\text{-}5}\)) offering pore-size selectivity and enzymes achieving biological specificity. Colloids (\(1\text{–}1000\,\mathrm{nm}\)) exhibit Tyndall scattering, Brownian motion, and double-layer charge stability, where oppositely charged multivalent ions govern coagulation via the Hardy–Schulze rule.
