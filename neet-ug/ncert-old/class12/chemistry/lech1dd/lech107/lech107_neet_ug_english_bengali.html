1. Learning Objectives and Exam Overview

The p-block of the periodic table spans Groups 13 to 18 and features metals, metalloids, and non-metals. While lighter members of Groups 13 and 14 initiate p-block trends, Groups 15 through 18 display extraordinary diversity in oxidation states, multiple bonding, hydrides, oxoacids, and industrial processes that are heavily tested in NEET UG.

  • Master periodic trends across Groups 15–18: atomic radii, ionisation enthalpy, electron gain enthalpy, electronegativity, and the inert pair effect.
  • Explain the anomalous behaviour of second-period elements (N, O, F) arising from small size, high electronegativity, and absence of valence d-orbitals.
  • Understand the preparation, properties, structures, and reactions of key compounds: \(\mathrm{N_2}\), \(\mathrm{NH_3}\), \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\), \(\mathrm{PH_3}\), \(\mathrm{PCl_3}\), \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\), \(\mathrm{O_2}\), \(\mathrm{O_3}\), \(\mathrm{SO_2}\), \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\), \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\), \(\mathrm{HCl}\), interhalogens, and xenon compounds.
  • Determine basicity, reducing character, and VSEPR geometries of oxoacids, halides, and noble gas compounds.

2. General Periodic Principles Across Groups 15–18

The valence shell electronic configuration of p-block elements is \(ns^2np^{1\text{–}6}\) (except helium, \(1s^2\)). Moving down any group adds a new principal shell, increasing atomic and ionic radii while decreasing ionisation enthalpy and electronegativity.

  • Inert Pair Effect: Down a group, the reluctance of valence \(ns^2\) electrons to participate in bonding (due to poor shielding by intervening \(d\) and \(f\) electrons) makes the lower oxidation state (group oxidation state minus 2) progressively more stable. Thus, \(\mathrm{Bi(+3)}\) is much more stable than \(\mathrm{Bi(+5)}\), and \(\mathrm{Po(+4)}\) is favoured over \(\mathrm{Po(+6)}\).
  • Anomalous Second-Period Behaviour: Nitrogen, oxygen, and fluorine possess only one \(2s\) and three \(2p\) orbitals, restricting their maximum covalency to 4. They form strong \(p\pi\text{–}p\pi\) multiple bonds, whereas heavier congeners (from period 3 onward) have vacant \(d\)-orbitals, allowing expanded octets (e.g., \(\mathrm{PF_6^-}\), \(\mathrm{SF_6}\), \(\mathrm{IF_7}\)) and \(d\pi\text{–}p\pi\) bonding.
  • Shielding Anomaly in Heavy Elements: Between Period 3 and Period 4/5/6, the presence of completely filled \(d^{10}\) and \(f^{14}\) subshells causes only a modest increase in covalent radius from As to Bi compared to the large jump from N to P.

২. শ্রেণি ১৫–১৮ বরাবর সাধারণ পর্যায়বৃত্ত নীতিসমূহ

p-ব্লক মৌলগুলির যোজ্যতা কক্ষের সাধারণ ইলেকট্রন বিন্যাস হলো \(ns^2np^{1\text{–}6}\) (হিলিয়াম ব্যতীত, যার বিন্যাস \(1s^2\))। যেকোনো শ্রেণি বরাবর নিচে নামলে নতুন প্রধান শক্তিস্তর যুক্ত হয়, ফলে পারমাণবিক ও আয়নীয় ব্যাসার্ধ বৃদ্ধি পায় এবং আয়নায়ন এনথ্যালপি ও তড়িৎ-ঋণাত্মকতা হ্রাস পায়।

  • নিষ্ক্রিয় জোড় প্রভাব (Inert Pair Effect): শ্রেণি বরাবর নিচে নামলে মধ্যবর্তী \(d\) ও \(f\) ইলেকট্রনের দুর্বল আবরণী প্রভাবের কারণে যোজ্যতা কক্ষের \(ns^2\) ইলেকট্রন জোড় বন্ধনে অংশগ্রহণে অনীহা দেখায়। ফলে নিম্ন জারণ অবস্থা (শ্রেণির সর্বোচ্চ জারণ অবস্থা বিয়োগ ২) ক্রমশ বেশি স্থায়ী হয়। যেমন, \(\mathrm{Bi(+5)}\) অপেক্ষা \(\mathrm{Bi(+3)}\) অনেক বেশি স্থায়ী।
  • দ্বিতীয় পর্যায়ের মৌলের ব্যতিক্রমী আচরণ: N, O এবং F-এর যোজ্যতা কক্ষে কেবল একটি \(2s\) ও তিনটি \(2p\) অরবিটাল থাকায় এদের সর্বোচ্চ সমযোজ্যতা ৪-এ সীমাবদ্ধ। এরা শক্তিশালী \(p\pi\text{–}p\pi\) বহু-বন্ধন গঠন করে; অন্যদিকে ভারী মৌলগুলির শূন্য \(d\)-অরবিটাল থাকায় এরা অষ্টক প্রসারণ (\(\mathrm{PF_6^-}\), \(\mathrm{SF_6}\), \(\mathrm{IF_7}\)) এবং \(d\pi\text{–}p\pi\) বন্ধন গঠন করতে পারে।
  • ভারী মৌলে আবরণী ব্যতিক্রম: সম্পূর্ণ পূর্ণ \(d^{10}\) ও \(f^{14}\) উপকক্ষের উপস্থিতির কারণে N থেকে P-তে সমযোজী ব্যাসার্ধের বৃদ্ধি যতটা বেশি হয়, As থেকে Bi পর্যন্ত বৃদ্ধি ততটা প্রকট নয়।

3. Group 15: Nitrogen, Phosphorus, and Key Compounds

Group 15 comprises nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), arsenic (As), antimony (Sb), bismuth (Bi), and synthetic moscovium (Mc), with valence configuration \(ns^2np^3\). Metallic character increases down the group: N and P are non-metals, As and Sb are metalloids, and Bi is a metal.

  • Ionisation Enthalpy: Due to extra-stable half-filled \(p^3\) orbitals, Group 15 elements have markedly higher first ionisation enthalpies than corresponding Group 14 (and even Group 16) elements.
  • Bonding Contrast (N vs P): Nitrogen forms a diatomic gas \(\mathrm{N\equiv N}\) with a very high bond enthalpy (\(941.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)), making \(\mathrm{N_2}\) inert at room temperature. However, the single \(\mathrm{N-N}\) bond is weaker than \(\mathrm{P-P}\) because lone pairs on adjacent small N atoms repel strongly. Thus, catenation is weaker in N than in P.
  • Hydrides (\(\mathrm{EH_3}\)): Moving from \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) to \(\mathrm{BiH_3}\):
    • Thermal stability decreases (bond dissociation enthalpy falls from \(389\) to \(255\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\) up to \(\mathrm{SbH_3}\)).
    • Reducing character increases (\(\mathrm{BiH_3}\) is the strongest reducing agent).
    • Basicity decreases: \(\mathrm{NH_3 > PH_3 > AsH_3 > SbH_3 \ge BiH_3}\).
    • Boiling point: \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (\(238.5\ \mathrm{K}\)) > \(\mathrm{PH_3}\) (\(185.5\ \mathrm{K}\)) due to intermolecular H-bonding in \(\mathrm{NH_3}\).
    • Bond angle: \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) (\(107.8^\circ\)) > \(\mathrm{PH_3}\) (\(93.6^\circ\)) > \(\mathrm{AsH_3}\) (\(91.8^\circ\)) > \(\mathrm{SbH_3}\) (\(91.3^\circ\)).
  • Oxides and Halides: Acidic nature of \(\mathrm{E_2O_3}\) decreases down the group (\(\mathrm{N_2O_3, P_4O_6}\) acidic; \(\mathrm{As_2O_3, Sb_2O_3}\) amphoteric; \(\mathrm{Bi_2O_3}\) basic). Pentahalides (\(\mathrm{EX_5}\)) are more covalent than trihalides (\(\mathrm{EX_3}\)) due to higher polarising power of the \(+5\) state (Fajans' rules). Nitrogen forms no pentahalide because it lacks \(d\)-orbitals.

Dinitrogen, Ammonia, and Nitric Acid

Dinitrogen (\(\mathrm{N_2}\)): Prepared in the laboratory by heating aqueous \(\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) with \(\mathrm{NaNO_2}\), or by thermal decomposition of \((\mathrm{NH_4)_2Cr_2O_7}\). Ultra-pure \(\mathrm{N_2}\) is obtained by heating barium or sodium azide: \(\mathrm{Ba(N_3)_2 \rightarrow Ba + 3N_2}\).

Ammonia (\(\mathrm{NH_3}\)): Manufactured by Haber's process:

\[\mathrm{N_2(g) + 3H_2(g) \rightleftharpoons 2NH_3(g)} \quad (\Delta_f H^\circ = -46.1\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})\]

Optimum conditions: ~\(200\ \mathrm{atm}\) (\(20\ \mathrm{MPa}\)), ~\(700\ \mathrm{K}\), and iron oxide catalyst promoted with \(\mathrm{K_2O}\) and \(\mathrm{Al_2O_3}\). \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) acts as a Lewis base, turning \(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}(aq)}\) deep blue (\([\mathrm{Cu(NH_3)_4}]^{2+}\)) and dissolving \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) as \([\mathrm{Ag(NH_3)_2}]\mathrm{Cl}\).

Nitric Acid (\(\mathrm{HNO_3}\)): Manufactured by Ostwald's process: catalytic oxidation of \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) over Pt/Rh gauze at \(500\ \mathrm{K}\) and \(9\ \mathrm{bar}\) gives \(\mathrm{NO}\), which oxidises to \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) and dissolves in water to yield \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\). Concentrated \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) oxidises \(\mathrm{Cu}\) to \(\mathrm{NO_2}\) (whereas dilute \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) gives \(\mathrm{NO}\) with \(\mathrm{Cu}\) and \(\mathrm{N_2O}\) with \(\mathrm{Zn}\)). Metals like \(\mathrm{Cr}\) and \(\mathrm{Al}\) become passive due to a protective oxide film. In the brown ring test for \(\mathrm{NO_3^-}\), \(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) reduces \(\mathrm{NO_3^-}\) to \(\mathrm{NO}\), forming the brown complex \([\mathrm{Fe(H_2O)_5(NO)}]^{2+}\).

Phosphorus Allotropes, Halides, and Oxoacids

  • Allotropes: White phosphorus (\(\mathrm{P_4}\)) consists of discrete tetrahedral molecules with \(60^\circ\) bond angles, creating severe angular strain that makes it highly reactive, waxy, poisonous, chemiluminescent, and soluble in \(\mathrm{CS_2}\). Red phosphorus is polymeric chains of \(\mathrm{P_4}\) tetrahedra (obtained at \(573\ \mathrm{K}\)), non-poisonous, and insoluble in \(\mathrm{CS_2}\). Black phosphorus (\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\)) is the most thermodynamically stable form.
  • Phosphine (\(\mathrm{PH_3}\)): Prepared by heating white \(\mathrm{P_4}\) with concentrated \(\mathrm{NaOH}\) in an inert \(\mathrm{CO_2}\) atmosphere: \(\mathrm{P_4 + 3NaOH + 3H_2O \rightarrow PH_3 + 3NaH_2PO_2}\). Used in Holme's signals and smoke screens.
  • Halides: \(\mathrm{PCl_3}\) is pyramidal (\(sp^3\)) and hydrolyses to \(\mathrm{H_3PO_3}\). \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\) is trigonal bipyramidal (\(sp^3d\)) in gas/liquid phases, with two longer axial bonds (\(240\ \mathrm{pm}\)) and three shorter equatorial bonds (\(202\ \mathrm{pm}\)) due to greater axial-equatorial repulsion.
  • Oxoacids of Phosphorus: Only \(\mathrm{H}\) atoms in \(\mathrm{P-OH}\) bonds are ionisable (determining basicity); \(\mathrm{P-H}\) bonds impart reducing power.
    • \(\mathrm{H_3PO_2}\) (Hypophosphorous acid, \(+1\)): 1 \(\mathrm{P-OH}\), 2 \(\mathrm{P-H}\), 1 \(\mathrm{P=O}\) \(\rightarrow\) monobasic, strong reducing agent.
    • \(\mathrm{H_3PO_3}\) (Orthophosphorous acid, \(+3\)): 2 \(\mathrm{P-OH}\), 1 \(\mathrm{P-H}\), 1 \(\mathrm{P=O}\) \(\rightarrow\) dibasic, reducing; disproportionates on heating: \(4\mathrm{H_3PO_3 \rightarrow 3H_3PO_4 + PH_3}\).
    • \(\mathrm{H_3PO_4}\) (Orthophosphoric acid, \(+5\)): 3 \(\mathrm{P-OH}\), 1 \(\mathrm{P=O}\) \(\rightarrow\) tribasic, non-reducing.

৩. শ্রেণি ১৫: নাইট্রোজেন, ফসফরাস এবং প্রধান যৌগসমূহ

শ্রেণি ১৫-তে নাইট্রোজেন (N), ফসফরাস (P), আর্সেনিক (As), অ্যান্টিমনি (Sb), বিসমাথ (Bi) এবং কৃত্রিম মস্কোভিয়াম (Mc) রয়েছে, যাদের যোজ্যতা বিন্যাস \(ns^2np^3\)। শ্রেণি বরাবর নিচে নামলে ধাতব ধর্ম বৃদ্ধি পায়: N ও P অধাতু, As ও Sb ধাতুকল্প এবং Bi ধাতু।

  • আয়নায়ন এনথ্যালপি: অর্ধপূর্ণ \(p^3\) অরবিটালের অতিরিক্ত স্থায়িত্বের কারণে শ্রেণি ১৫-র প্রথম আয়নায়ন এনথ্যালপি সংশ্লিষ্ট শ্রেণি ১৪ (এমনকি শ্রেণি ১৬) মৌলগুলির চেয়ে বেশি।
  • বন্ধনের পার্থক্য (N বনাম P): নাইট্রোজেন উচ্চ বন্ধন এনথ্যালপিযুক্ত (\(941.4\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) দ্বি-পরমাণুক গ্যাস \(\mathrm{N\equiv N}\) গঠন করে। কিন্তু ক্ষুদ্র N পরমাণুতে নিঃসঙ্গ ইলেকট্রন জোড়ের তীব্র বিকর্ষণের কারণে একক \(\mathrm{N-N}\) বন্ধন \(\mathrm{P-P}\) বন্ধনের চেয়ে দুর্বল; তাই N-এর ক্যাটিনেশন ধর্ম P অপেক্ষা কম।
  • হাইড্রাইড (\(\mathrm{EH_3}\)): \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) থেকে \(\mathrm{BiH_3}\) পর্যন্ত তাপীয় স্থায়িত্ব ও ক্ষারকীয়তা হ্রাস পায় (\(\mathrm{NH_3 > PH_3 > AsH_3 > SbH_3 \ge BiH_3}\)), কিন্তু বিজারণ ক্ষমতা বৃদ্ধি পায় (\(\mathrm{BiH_3}\) তীব্রতম বিজারক)। আন্তঃআণবিক H-বন্ধনের কারণে \(\mathrm{NH_3}\)-এর স্ফুটনাঙ্ক (\(238.5\ \mathrm{K}\)) \(\mathrm{PH_3}\) (\(185.5\ \mathrm{K}\)) অপেক্ষা বেশি।
  • অক্সাইড ও হ্যালাইড: \(\mathrm{E_2O_3}\)-এর আম্লিক ধর্ম নিচে নামলে কমে। ফাজানের নিয়ম অনুযায়ী \(+5\) জারণ অবস্থায় পোলারাইজিং ক্ষমতা বেশি হওয়ায় পেন্টাহ্যালাইড (\(\mathrm{EX_5}\)) ট্রাইহ্যালাইড (\(\mathrm{EX_3}\)) অপেক্ষা বেশি সমযোজী। d-অরবিটাল না থাকায় N পেন্টাহ্যালাইড গঠন করে না।

ডাইনাইট্রোজেন, অ্যামোনিয়া ও নাইট্রিক অ্যাসিড

ডাইনাইট্রোজেন (\(\mathrm{N_2}\)): পরীক্ষাগারে জলীয় \(\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) ও \(\mathrm{NaNO_2}\)-এর বিক্রিয়ায় বা \((\mathrm{NH_4)_2Cr_2O_7}\)-এর তাপীয় বিয়োজনে প্রস্তুত করা হয়। বিশুদ্ধ \(\mathrm{N_2}\) পেতে \(\mathrm{Ba(N_3)_2}\) বা \(\mathrm{NaN_3}\)-কে উত্তপ্ত করা হয়।

অ্যামোনিয়া (\(\mathrm{NH_3}\)): হেবার পদ্ধতিতে ~\(200\ \mathrm{atm}\) চাপে, ~\(700\ \mathrm{K}\) উষ্ণতায় এবং \(\mathrm{K_2O}\) ও \(\mathrm{Al_2O_3}\) উদ্দীপকযুক্ত আয়রন অক্সাইড অনুঘটকের উপস্থিতিতে উৎপাদিত হয়। লুইস ক্ষারক হিসেবে \(\mathrm{NH_3}\) নীল \(\mathrm{Cu^{2+}}\) দ্রবণে গাঢ় নীল \([\mathrm{Cu(NH_3)_4}]^{2+}\) গঠন করে।

4. Group 16: Chalcogens, Ozone, and Sulphur Compounds

Group 16 (oxygen, sulphur, selenium, tellurium, polonium, livermorium) has valence configuration \(ns^2np^4\). Oxygen and sulphur are non-metals, Se and Te are metalloids, and Po is a radioactive metal.

  • Electron Gain Enthalpy Anomaly: Oxygen has a less negative electron gain enthalpy (\(-141\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) than sulphur (\(-200\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) because the compact \(2p\) subshell of oxygen experiences significant interelectronic repulsion toward an incoming electron.
  • Hydrides (\(\mathrm{H_2E}\)): From \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) to \(\mathrm{H_2Te}\), the \(\mathrm{H-E}\) bond dissociation enthalpy decreases; hence acidic strength and reducing power increase, while thermal stability decreases. \(\mathrm{H_2O}\) is a liquid due to strong hydrogen bonding, whereas \(\mathrm{H_2S}\) is a gas.
  • Dioxygen (\(\mathrm{O_2}\)) and Ozone (\(\mathrm{O_3}\)): \(\mathrm{O_2}\) is paramagnetic despite having even electrons (two unpaired electrons in \(\pi^*\) orbitals). \(\mathrm{O_3}\) is prepared by passing dry \(\mathrm{O_2}\) through a silent electric discharge (\(3\mathrm{O_2 \rightarrow 2O_3}\), \(\Delta H^\circ = +142\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)). \(\mathrm{O_3}\) is angular (\(117^\circ\), equal \(\mathrm{O-O}\) bonds of \(128\ \mathrm{pm}\)) and a strong oxidising agent (oxidises \(\mathrm{PbS \rightarrow PbSO_4}\) and \(\mathrm{I^- \rightarrow I_2}\)).
  • Allotropes of Sulphur: Yellow rhombic (\(\alpha\)) sulphur is stable below \(369\ \mathrm{K}\); needle-shaped monoclinic (\(\beta\)) sulphur is stable above \(369\ \mathrm{K}\). Both contain puckered crown-shaped \(\mathrm{S_8}\) rings. At ~\(1000\ \mathrm{K}\), sulphur vapour exists predominantly as paramagnetic \(\mathrm{S_2}\).
  • Sulphur Dioxide (\(\mathrm{SO_2}\)) and Sulphuric Acid (\(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\)): Moist \(\mathrm{SO_2}\) acts as a reducing agent, converting \(\mathrm{Fe^{3+}}\) to \(\mathrm{Fe^{2+}}\) and decolourising acidified \(\mathrm{KMnO_4}\). \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) is manufactured by the Contact process:
\[2\mathrm{SO_2(g) + O_2(g) \xrightarrow{V_2O_5,\ 720\ K,\ 2\ bar} 2SO_3(g)} \quad (\Delta_r H^\circ = -196.6\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}})\] \[\mathrm{SO_3 + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow H_2S_2O_7\ (Oleum)} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{H_2O}} 2\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\]

In water, \(K_{a_1} \gg K_{a_2}\) (\(K_{a_2} = 1.2 \times 10^{-2}\)). Concentrated \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) is a strong dehydrating agent (chars carbohydrates) and hot oxidising agent (oxidises \(\mathrm{Cu, S, C}\) while reducing to \(\mathrm{SO_2}\)).

  • Key Sulphur Oxoacids: \(\mathrm{H_2SO_3}\) (sulphurous acid, \(+4\)), \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) (sulphuric acid, \(+6\)), \(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_7}\) (oleum/pyrosulphuric acid, \(+6\), contains \(\mathrm{S-O-S}\) bridge), and \(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_8}\) (peroxodisulphuric acid, \(+6\), contains \(\mathrm{S-O-O-S}\) peroxide linkage).

5. Group 17: Halogens, Interhalogens, and Oxoacids

Halogens (F, Cl, Br, I, At, Ts) have \(ns^2np^5\) configuration and possess the highest negative electron gain enthalpy in their respective periods.

  • Key Anomalies of Fluorine:
    • Electron gain enthalpy: \(\mathrm{Cl}\) (\(-349\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) > \(\mathrm{F}\) (\(-333\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) due to interelectronic repulsion in F's compact \(2p\) subshell.
    • Bond dissociation enthalpy: \(\mathrm{Cl_2 > Br_2 > F_2 > I_2}\). \(\mathrm{F_2}\) (\(158.8\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) is unusually weak due to lone-pair–lone-pair repulsion at short \(\mathrm{F-F}\) distance.
    • Oxidising power: Despite lower electron gain enthalpy, \(\mathrm{F_2}\) is the strongest oxidising agent (\(E^\circ = +2.87\ \mathrm{V}\)) due to low \(\mathrm{F-F}\) bond dissociation enthalpy and high hydration enthalpy of \(\mathrm{F^-}\).
    • Oxidation state: Fluorine shows only \(-1\), whereas Cl, Br, I exhibit \(-1, +1, +3, +5, +7\) using vacant \(d\)-orbitals.
  • Hydrogen Halides (\(\mathrm{HX}\)): Acid strength increases down the group: \(\mathrm{HF < HCl < HBr < HI}\) (since \(\mathrm{H-X}\) bond dissociation enthalpy decreases). \(\mathrm{HF}\) is a liquid (b.p. \(293\ \mathrm{K}\)) due to H-bonding.
  • Chlorine (\(\mathrm{Cl_2}\)) and \(\mathrm{HCl}\): Manufactured by Deacon's process (\(4\mathrm{HCl + O_2 \xrightarrow{CuCl_2,\ 723\ K} 2Cl_2 + 2H_2O}\)) or electrolysis of brine. With cold dilute \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\) gives \(\mathrm{NaCl + NaOCl}\); with hot concentrated \(\mathrm{NaOH}\), it disproportionates to \(\mathrm{NaCl + NaClO_3}\). Bleaching action of \(\mathrm{Cl_2}\) is permanent because it proceeds via oxidation by nascent oxygen from \(\mathrm{HOCl}\). A \(3:1\) mixture of conc. \(\mathrm{HCl}\) and conc. \(\mathrm{HNO_3}\) forms aqua regia, dissolving Au and Pt.
  • Halogen Oxoacids & Interhalogens: Fluorine forms only \(\mathrm{HOF}\). Chlorine forms \(\mathrm{HOCl}\) (\(+1\)), \(\mathrm{HOClO}\) (\(+3\)), \(\mathrm{HOClO_2}\) (\(+5\)), and \(\mathrm{HOClO_3}\) (\(+7\)). Interhalogens (\(\mathrm{XX'}, \mathrm{XX'_3}, \mathrm{XX'_5}, \mathrm{XX'_7}\), where X is larger) are covalent, diamagnetic, and more reactive than parent halogens (except \(\mathrm{F_2}\)) because \(\mathrm{X-X'}\) bonds are weaker than \(\mathrm{X-X}\) bonds.

6. Group 18: Noble Gases and Xenon Chemistry

Noble gases (He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn, Og) have closed \(ns^2np^6\) valence shells (He: \(1s^2\)), giving them very high ionisation enthalpies and positive electron gain enthalpies. Because they are monoatomic with only weak dispersion forces, they exhibit extremely low boiling points (He has the lowest at \(4.2\ \mathrm{K}\)).

  • Discovery of Noble Gas Compounds: Neil Bartlett observed that \(\mathrm{O_2}\) (\(\Delta_i H_1 = 1175\ \mathrm{kJ\,mol^{-1}}\)) reacts with \(\mathrm{PtF_6}\) to form red \(\mathrm{O_2^+PtF_6^-}\). Example 2: Predicting VSEPR Geometry and Hydrolysis Type of Xenon Compounds

  • (A) \(-1\) এবং \(+1\)
  • (B) \(-1\) এবং \(+5\)
  • (C) \(+1\) এবং \(+5\)
  • (D) \(-1\) এবং \(+7\)

উত্তর: (B)

Practice Question 4 (Matching / Structure Identification)

Which of the following pairs of species are isostructural (possess the same molecular geometry)?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{XeF_4}\) and \(\mathrm{SF_4}\)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{XeF_2}\) and \(\mathrm{IBr_2^-}\)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{BrF_3}\) and \(\mathrm{PCl_3}\)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{XeO_3}\) and \(\mathrm{SO_3}\)

Answer: (B)

Solution: Both \(\mathrm{XeF_2}\) and \(\mathrm{IBr_2^-}\) have 2 bond pairs and 3 lone pairs around the central atom (\(sp^3d\) hybridisation), giving both a linear geometry. In contrast, \(\mathrm{XeF_4}\) is square planar while \(\mathrm{SF_4}\) is see-saw; \(\mathrm{BrF_3}\) is bent T-shaped while \(\mathrm{PCl_3}\) is pyramidal; \(\mathrm{XeO_3}\) is pyramidal while \(\mathrm{SO_3}\) is trigonal planar.

Practice Question 5 (Single-Correct MCQ)

Which of the following oxoacids of sulphur contains a peroxide (\(\mathrm{-O-O-}\)) linkage?

  • (A) \(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_7}\) (Pyrosulphuric acid)
  • (B) \(\mathrm{H_2SO_3}\) (Sulphurous acid)
  • (C) \(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_8}\) (Peroxodisulphuric acid)
  • (D) \(\mathrm{H_2SO_4}\) (Sulphuric acid)

Answer: (C)

Solution: Peroxodisulphuric acid (\(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_8}\), Marshall's acid) has the structure \(\mathrm{HO-SO_2-O-O-SO_2-OH}\), containing one \(\mathrm{-O-O-}\) peroxide bond, whereas \(\mathrm{H_2S_2O_7}\) (oleum) has a \(\mathrm{S-O-S}\) oxo-bridge.

Practice Question 6 (Statement-Based MCQ)

Consider the following two statements regarding Group 15 and Group 17 chemistry:

Statement I: \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\) has three shorter equatorial bonds and two longer axial bonds in the gaseous phase.

Statement II: \(\mathrm{ICl}\) is less reactive than \(\mathrm{I_2}\) because \(\mathrm{ICl}\) is a polar molecule.

  • (A) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
  • (B) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
  • (C) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
  • (D) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct.

Answer: (C)

Solution: Statement I is correct: in trigonal bipyramidal \(\mathrm{PCl_5}\), axial \(\mathrm{P-Cl}\) bonds (\(240\ \mathrm{pm}\)) suffer greater bond-pair repulsion from the three equatorial bonds (\(202\ \mathrm{pm}\)) and are longer. Statement II is incorrect: interhalogen compounds like \(\mathrm{ICl}\) are more reactive than \(\mathrm{I_2}\) because the \(\mathrm{I-Cl}\) bond is weaker than the \(\mathrm{I-I}\) bond.

7. Common Pitfalls, Revision Checklist, and Final Recap

Common NEET UG Pitfalls to Avoid:
  • Do not confuse the basicity of \(\mathrm{H_3PO_2}\) (monobasic) and \(\mathrm{H_3PO_3}\) (dibasic) with the total number of H atoms in their formulas.
  • Remember the bond dissociation enthalpy order of halogens is \(\mathrm{Cl_2 > Br_2 > F_2 > I_2}\) (not \(\mathrm{F_2}\) at the top), and electron gain enthalpy is \(\mathrm{Cl > F > Br > I}\).
  • \(\mathrm{XeF_6}\) hydrolysis to \(\mathrm{XeO_3}\) or \(\mathrm{XeOF_4}\) is not a redox reaction, whereas \(\mathrm{XeF_2}\) and \(\mathrm{XeF_4}\) hydrolyses are redox reactions.
  • Quick Recap: Across Groups 15–18, second-period elements (N, O, F) show unique \(p\pi\text{–}p\pi\) bonding and a max covalency of 4; heavier elements expand octets using \(d\)-orbitals and favour lower oxidation states down the group due to the inert pair effect. Mastering VSEPR geometries, oxoacid structures, and industrial processes (Haber's, Ostwald's, Contact) covers the core NEET UG weightage of this unit.
