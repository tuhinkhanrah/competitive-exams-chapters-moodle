Coordination Compounds: Structure, Bonding, Isomerism, and Applications

A comprehensive, exam-focused tutorial covering Werner's theory, IUPAC nomenclature, stereochemistry, Valence Bond Theory, Crystal Field Theory, metal carbonyls, and real-world applications.

Learning Objectives and Chapter Overview

Transition metals readily form complex species in which a central metal atom or ion binds to surrounding anions or neutral molecules through coordinate covalent (donor–acceptor) interactions. Unlike simple salts, these coordination entities retain their identity in aqueous solution and exhibit distinctive colours, magnetic moments, and biological functions.

What You Will Master for NEET UG

  • Applying IUPAC rules to write systematic names and formulas of mononuclear coordination compounds.
  • Identifying structural isomers (linkage, coordination, ionisation, solvate) and stereoisomers (geometrical and optical).
  • Predicting hybridisation, geometry, and spin-only magnetic moments using Valence Bond Theory (VBT).
  • Explaining d-orbital splitting, spin states, colour, and spectrochemical trends using Crystal Field Theory (CFT).
  • Understanding synergic bonding in metal carbonyls and key biological, medicinal, and industrial applications.

1. Werner's Coordination Theory and Double Salts vs. Complexes

Alfred Werner (1898) proposed the first systematic model to explain why transition metal salts combine with neutral molecules such as ammonia to form stable compounds with varying ionisation behaviour. According to Werner, a metal ion exhibits two distinct types of valency:

  • Primary Valency (Ionisable): Corresponds to the oxidation state of the central metal ion. It is satisfied exclusively by negative ions (anions) and is non-directional. When dissolved in water, ions satisfying only the primary valency outside the coordination sphere dissociate freely.
  • Secondary Valency (Non-ionisable): Corresponds to the coordination number (CN) of the metal ion and is fixed for a given metal in a particular oxidation state. It can be satisfied by neutral molecules or negative ions. Secondary valencies are directional, giving rise to definite spatial arrangements called coordination polyhedra.

Double Salts vs. Coordination Complexes

Both double salts and coordination complexes form by combining two or more stable stoichiometric compounds, but their behaviour in aqueous solution is fundamentally different:

Property Double Salt Coordination Complex
Dissociation in water Dissociates completely into simple constituent ions. Complex entity remains intact; only counter ions dissociate.
Ion tests in solution Gives positive chemical tests for all individual ions. Does not give tests for ions bound inside the coordination sphere.
Representative examples Carnallite (\(\mathrm{KCl}\cdot\mathrm{MgCl}_2\cdot 6\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), Mohr's salt (\(\mathrm{FeSO}_4\cdot(\mathrm{NH}_4)_2\mathrm{SO}_4\cdot 6\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), Potash alum (\(\mathrm{KAl(SO_4)}_2\cdot 12\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)) Potassium ferrocyanide (\(\mathrm{K}_4[\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{CN})_6]\)), Tetraamminecopper(II) sulphate (\([\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4]\mathrm{SO}_4\))

2. Essential Terminology and Ligand Classification

  • Coordination Entity: The central metal atom or ion bonded to a fixed number of ions or molecules, written inside square brackets, such as \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_3\mathrm{Cl}_3]\) or \([\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{4-}\).
  • Central Atom/Ion: The metal atom or ion that accepts electron pairs from ligands and acts as a Lewis acid (electron-pair acceptor).
  • Ligands: Ions or molecules that donate at least one lone pair of electrons to the central metal atom/ion, acting as Lewis bases.
  • Coordination Sphere and Counter Ions: The central metal and its directly attached ligands enclosed in square brackets form the coordination sphere. Ionisable ions outside the brackets are counter ions.
  • Coordination Number (CN): The total number of ligand donor atoms forming \(\sigma\)-bonds directly with the central metal atom/ion. Pi (\(\pi\)) bonds between the metal and ligand are never counted in the coordination number.
  • Oxidation Number: The residual charge left on the central atom if all ligands are removed along with the electron pairs shared with the metal.
  • Homoleptic vs. Heteroleptic Complexes: Complexes with only one kind of donor group (e.g., \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}\)) are homoleptic; those with more than one kind (e.g., \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4\mathrm{Cl}_2]^+\)) are heteroleptic.

Classification of Ligands by Denticity and Binding Mode

Ligand Type Definition Key Examples
Unidentate (Monodentate) Binds through a single donor atom. \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\), \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\), \(\mathrm{NH}_3\), \(\mathrm{CO}\), \(\mathrm{CN}^-\)
Didentate (Bidentate) Binds simultaneously through two donor atoms. Ethane-1,2-diamine (\(\mathrm{en}\): \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{NCH}_2\mathrm{CH}_2\mathrm{NH}_2\)), Oxalate (\(\mathrm{ox}^{2-}\): \(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\))

Chelate Ligand A di- or polydentate ligand that forms a ring with the central metal ion, increasing complex stability (chelate effect). \(\mathrm{en}\), \(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\), \(\mathrm{EDTA}^{4-}\)
Ambidentate Ligand A unidentate ligand possessing two different donor atoms, capable of coordinating through either one at a time. \(\mathrm{NO}_2^-\) (nitrito-N via N or nitrito-O via O); \(\mathrm{SCN}^-\) (thiocyanato-S via S or thiocyanato-N via N)

3. IUPAC Nomenclature of Mononuclear Coordination Compounds

Rules for Writing Formulas

  1. Write the central metal atom first, followed by ligands in alphabetical order of their symbols/abbreviations (irrespective of ligand charge).
  2. Enclose polyatomic ligands and ligand abbreviations (such as \(\mathrm{en}\), \(\mathrm{ox}\)) in parentheses.
  3. Enclose the entire coordination entity in square brackets with no spaces inside.
  4. For a charged coordination ion written without counter ions, place the net charge as a right superscript outside the brackets with the number before the sign, e.g., \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]^{3+}\).

Rules for Systematic Naming

  1. Cation First: Name the cation before the anion, regardless of whether the complex is cationic or anionic. Never indicate the number of counter ions with prefixes (e.g., write potassium, not tripotassium).
  2. Alphabetical Ligand Order: Name all ligands alphabetically before the central metal name. Ignore multiplicative prefixes when alphabetising.
  3. Ligand Endings: Anionic ligands end in -ido (e.g., chlorido for \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\), cyanido for \(\mathrm{CN}^-\), hydroxido for \(\mathrm{OH}^-\), oxalato for \(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\)). Neutral ligands retain their molecular name except aqua (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)), ammine (\(\mathrm{NH}_3\)), carbonyl (\(\mathrm{CO}\)), and nitrosyl (\(\mathrm{NO}\)).
  4. Multiplicative Prefixes: Use di-, tri-, tetra- for simple ligands. When a ligand name already contains a numerical prefix (e.g., ethane-1,2-diamine, triphenylphosphine), use bis-, tris-, tetrakis- and place the ligand name in parentheses.
  5. Central Metal Suffix and Oxidation State: If the coordination entity is a cation or neutral molecule, use the element's standard name. If it is an anion, add the suffix -ate (using Latin roots where standard, such as ferrate for Fe, cuprate for Cu, argentate for Ag, plumbate for Pb). Follow the metal name immediately with its oxidation state in Roman numerals in parentheses, e.g., (III) or (0).

4. Isomerism in Coordination Compounds

Compounds sharing the same chemical formula but differing in atom connectivity or spatial arrangement are isomers. They fall into two broad categories:

A. Structural Isomerism (Different Bond Connectivity)

  • Linkage Isomerism: Shown by complexes with ambidentate ligands that can coordinate through different donor atoms, e.g., \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5(\mathrm{NO}_2)]\mathrm{Cl}_2\) (yellow, N-bound) and \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5(\mathrm{ONO})]\mathrm{Cl}_2\) (red, O-bound).
  • Coordination Isomerism: Occurs in salts where both cation and anion are complex ions, by exchanging ligands between the two metal centres, e.g., \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6][\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{CN})_6]\) and \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6][\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{CN})_6]\).
  • Ionisation Isomerism: Arises when a counter ion is itself a potential ligand and swaps places with a coordinated ligand, yielding different ions in solution, e.g., \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5(\mathrm{SO}_4)]\mathrm{Br}\) (gives \(\mathrm{AgBr}\) precipitate with \(\mathrm{AgNO}_3\)) and \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5\mathrm{Br}]\mathrm{SO}_4\) (gives \(\mathrm{BaSO}_4\) precipitate with \(\mathrm{BaCl}_2\)).
  • Solvate (Hydrate) Isomerism: Differs in whether solvent molecules are directly coordinated to the metal or occupy crystal lattice sites, e.g., \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]\mathrm{Cl}_3\) (violet) and \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_5\mathrm{Cl}]\mathrm{Cl}_2\cdot\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) (grey-green).

B. Stereoisomerism (Same Bonds, Different Spatial Arrangement)

  • Geometrical Isomerism:
    • Tetrahedral complexes (\([\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{B}_2]\), etc.) do not show geometrical isomerism because all four ligand positions are mutually adjacent and equivalent.
    • Square planar complexes of type \([\mathrm{MX}_2\mathrm{L}_2]\) show cis (adjacent) and trans (opposite) isomers. Type \([\mathrm{MABXL}]\) (four different unidentate ligands) forms three geometrical isomers (two cis and one trans).
    • Octahedral complexes of type \([\mathrm{MX}_2\mathrm{L}_4]\) or \([\mathrm{M(AA)}_2\mathrm{X}_2]\) show cis and trans isomers. Type \([\mathrm{MA}_3\mathrm{B}_3]\) shows facial (fac) isomers (three identical ligands at corners of one triangular face) and meridional (mer) isomers (three identical ligands along a meridian).
  • Optical Isomerism: Shown by chiral complexes that lack a plane or centre of symmetry and form non-superimposable mirror images (dextro and laevo enantiomers). Common in octahedral complexes with didentate ligands, such as \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{en})_3]^{3+}\), cis-\([\mathrm{PtCl}_2(\mathrm{en})_2]^{2+}\), and cis-\([\mathrm{CrCl}_2(\mathrm{ox})_2]^{3-}\). Note that the trans isomers of \([\mathrm{M(AA)}_2\mathrm{X}_2]\) possess a plane of symmetry and are optically inactive.

5. Bonding in Coordination Compounds: VBT and CFT

Valence Bond Theory (VBT)

Under the influence of approaching ligands, the central metal atom/ion hybridises vacant \((n-1)d\), \(ns\), \(np\) (or \(ns\), \(np\), \(nd\)) orbitals to generate equivalent hybrid orbitals of definite geometry, which overlap with filled ligand orbitals.

Coordination Number Hybridisation Geometry Complex Type
4 \(sp^3\) Tetrahedral Outer/high-spin (e.g., \([\mathrm{NiCl}_4]^{2-}\)) or \(d^{10}\) (e.g., \([\mathrm{Ni(CO)}_4]\))
4 \(dsp^2\) Square planar Inner/low-spin (e.g., \([\mathrm{Ni(CN)}_4]^{2-}\), \([\mathrm{PtCl}_4]^{2-}\))
5 \(sp^3d\) Trigonal bipyramidal e.g., \([\mathrm{Fe(CO)}_5]\)
6 \(d^2sp^3\) Octahedral Inner orbital / low-spin / spin-paired (uses \((n-1)d\))
6 \(sp^3d^2\) Octahedral Outer orbital / high-spin / spin-free (uses \(nd\))
Spin-Only Magnetic Moment: \[\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mathrm{BM}\] where \(n\) is the number of unpaired electrons and \(\mathrm{BM}\) denotes Bohr Magneton.

Limitations of VBT: It relies on several assumptions, does not distinguish quantitatively between weak and strong ligands, fails to explain the colours and absorption spectra of complexes, and cannot predict thermodynamic or kinetic stabilities.

Crystal Field Theory (CFT)

CFT treats metal–ligand bonding as purely electrostatic: anionic ligands are point charges and neutral dipolar ligands are point dipoles. In an isolated gaseous metal ion, all five \(d\) orbitals are degenerate. An asymmetrical ligand field lifts this degeneracy.

  • Octahedral Crystal Field: Six ligands approach along the \(\pm x, \pm y, \pm z\) axes. The \(d_{x^2-y^2}\) and \(d_{z^2}\) orbitals (pointing directly at the ligands) experience stronger repulsion and rise in energy by \(+\frac{3}{5}\Delta_o\) (\(+0.6\Delta_o\)), forming the doubly degenerate \(e_g\) set. The \(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}\) orbitals (lying between the axes) drop in energy by \(-\frac{2}{5}\Delta_o\) (\(-0.4\Delta_o\)), forming the triply degenerate \(t_{2g}\) set.
  • Tetrahedral Crystal Field: Four ligands approach between the coordinate axes. The splitting pattern is inverted: the \(t_2\) set (\(d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}\)) rises by \(+\frac{2}{5}\Delta_t\), while the \(e\) set (\(d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}\)) drops by \(-\frac{3}{5}\Delta_t\). Because tetrahedral complexes lack a centre of symmetry, the subscript g (gerade) is omitted. For identical metal, ligands, and bond lengths: \[\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o\] Since \(\Delta_t\) is much smaller than the pairing energy \(P\), tetrahedral complexes are almost always high-spin.
Diagram 3: Splitting of degenerate d orbitals in octahedral (\(\Delta_o\)) and tetrahedral (\(\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o\)) crystal fields.

Spectrochemical Series and Spin-State Selection

Ligands arranged in order of increasing crystal field splitting strength (\(\Delta_o\)) form the experimentally determined spectrochemical series:

\[\mathrm{I}^- < \mathrm{Br}^- < \mathrm{SCN}^- < \mathrm{Cl}^- < \mathrm{S}^{2-} < \mathrm{F}^- < \mathrm{OH}^- < \mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-} < \mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} < \mathrm{NCS}^- < \mathrm{edta}^{4-} < \mathrm{NH}_3 < \mathrm{en} < \mathrm{CN}^- < \mathrm{CO}\]
  • Weak-Field Ligands (\(\Delta_o < P\)): For \(d^4\) to \(d^7\) ions, the fourth electron enters the higher-energy \(e_g\) level to avoid paying pairing energy \(P\), giving high-spin complexes (e.g., \(d^4 \rightarrow t_{2g}^3 e_g^1\)).
  • Strong-Field Ligands (\(\Delta_o > P\)): Pairing in \(t_{2g}\) is energetically favoured over promoting electrons to \(e_g\), giving low-spin complexes (e.g., \(d^4 \rightarrow t_{2g}^4 e_g^0\)).

Colour in Coordination Compounds

When white light passes through a transition metal complex, an electron absorbs a photon of frequency \(\nu\) matching \(\Delta_o\) and undergoes a \(d\text{-}d\) transition (e.g., \(t_{2g}^1 e_g^0 \rightarrow t_{2g}^0 e_g^1\) in violet \([\mathrm{Ti}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]^{3+}\)). The observed colour is the complementary colour of the absorbed wavelength. Without ligands, no splitting occurs and the salt is colourless (for example, anhydrous \(\mathrm{CuSO}_4\) is white, whereas hydrated \(\mathrm{CuSO}_4\cdot 5\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) is blue). Gemstones such as ruby (\(\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}\) in \(\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{O}_3\)) and emerald (\(\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}\) in beryl, \(\mathrm{Be}_3\mathrm{Al}_2\mathrm{Si}_6\mathrm{O}_{18}\)) owe their colours to \(d\text{-}d\) transitions of \(\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}\) ions in octahedral sites.

Limitations of CFT: Because it treats ligands strictly as point charges, CFT erroneously predicts that anionic ligands should cause the largest splitting (whereas in reality they lie at the weak-field end of the spectrochemical series) and ignores covalent character in metal–ligand bonds.

6. Bonding in Metal Carbonyls and Key Applications

Homoleptic metal carbonyls such as \(\mathrm{Ni(CO)}_4\) (tetrahedral), \(\mathrm{Fe(CO)}_5\) (trigonal bipyramidal), and \(\mathrm{Cr(CO)}_6\) (octahedral) contain metal in zero oxidation state. Dinuclear carbonyls include \(\mathrm{Mn}_2(\mathrm{CO})_{10}\) (two \(\mathrm{Mn(CO)}_5\) square pyramids joined by a \(\mathrm{Mn–Mn}\) bond) and \(\mathrm{Co}_2(\mathrm{CO})_8\) (a \(\mathrm{Co–Co}\) bond bridged by two \(\mathrm{CO}\) groups).

The metal–carbon bond in carbonyls exhibits synergic bonding:

  • \(\sigma\)-bond: Donation of a lone pair from the carbonyl carbon into a vacant metal orbital.
  • \(\pi\)-bond (back-bonding): Donation of an electron pair from a filled metal \(d\)-orbital into the vacant antibonding \(\pi^*\) orbital of \(\mathrm{CO}\). This reinforces the \(\mathrm{M–C}\) bond (increasing its bond order) while slightly weakening the \(\mathrm{C–O}\) bond.

Worked Examples

Determining Primary and Secondary Valency from Precipitation Data

Problem: A hypothetical platinum(IV) coordination compound with empirical composition \(\mathrm{PtCl}_4\cdot 5\mathrm{NH}_3\) (assumed test sample) is dissolved in water. On adding excess aqueous \(\mathrm{AgNO}_3\) in the cold, \(3\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) precipitate per mole of the compound. Write its coordination formula, determine the primary and secondary valencies of platinum, and state the electrolyte ratio.

Solution:

  1. Precipitation of \(3\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) per mole of complex indicates that \(3\,\mathrm{Cl}^-\) ions are ionisable counter ions outside the coordination sphere.
  2. The remaining \(1\,\mathrm{Cl}^-\) ion and all \(5\,\mathrm{NH}_3\) molecules are directly bonded to \(\mathrm{Pt}\) inside the coordination sphere: \([\mathrm{Pt}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5\mathrm{Cl}]\mathrm{Cl}_3\).
  3. Primary valency equals the oxidation state of platinum: \(x + 5(0) + 1(-1) = +3 \Rightarrow x = +4\). So primary valency is 4.
  4. Secondary valency equals the number of groups bound directly to \(\mathrm{Pt}\): \(5 + 1 = 6\).
  5. Dissociation produces one \([\mathrm{Pt}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_5\mathrm{Cl}]^{3+}\) cation and three \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) anions (4 ions total), corresponding to a 1:3 electrolyte.

Applying IUPAC Rules to Names and Formulas

Problem: (a) Write the IUPAC name of \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4\mathrm{Cl}(\mathrm{NO}_2)]\mathrm{NO}_3\) (assuming nitro binds through nitrogen). (b) Write the formula for potassium diaquadioxalatocobaltate(III).

Solution:

  1. Part (a): The cation is \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4\mathrm{Cl}(\mathrm{NO}_2)]^+\) and the counter anion is \(\mathrm{NO}_3^-\). Let oxidation state of \(\mathrm{Cr}\) be \(x\): \(x + 4(0) + (-1) + (-1) = +1 \Rightarrow x = +3\). Listing ligands alphabetically (ammine, chlorido, nitrito-N):
    Name: Tetraamminechloridonitrito-N-chromium(III) nitrate.
  2. Part (b): "Cobaltate(III)" indicates \(\mathrm{Co}^{3+}\) in an anionic complex. Ligands are two aqua (\(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\), charge \(0\)) and two oxalato (\(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\), charge \(-2\) each). Net charge on the complex ion \(= +3 + 2(0) + 2(-2) = -1\). One \(\mathrm{K}^+\) counter ion balances this charge. Alphabetical ligand symbols inside brackets (\(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4\) before \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\)):
    Formula: \(\mathrm{K}[\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4)_2(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_2]\).

Predicting Hybridisation, Geometry, and Magnetic Moment via VBT

Problem: Compare the geometry, hybridisation, and spin-only magnetic moment of \([\mathrm{NiBr}_4]^{2-}\) and \([\mathrm{Ni}(\mathrm{CN})_4]^{2-}\).

Solution:

  1. In both complexes, nickel is in the \(+2\) oxidation state with valence configuration \(3d^8\) (two unpaired electrons in the free \(\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}\) ion).
  2. In \([\mathrm{NiBr}_4]^{2-}\), \(\mathrm{Br}^-\) is a weak-field ligand and does not force pairing of the \(3d\) electrons. Nickel uses one \(4s\) and three \(4p\) orbitals to form \(sp^3\) hybrid orbitals, giving a tetrahedral geometry with \(n = 2\) unpaired electrons. \[\mu = \sqrt{2(2+2)} = \sqrt{8} \approx 2.83\,\mathrm{BM}\quad\text{(paramagnetic)}\]
  3. In \([\mathrm{Ni}(\mathrm{CN})_4]^{2-}\), \(\mathrm{CN}^-\) is a strong-field ligand that pairs the two unpaired \(3d\) electrons, vacating one \(3d\) orbital. Nickel undergoes \(dsp^2\) hybridisation, giving a square planar geometry with \(n = 0\) unpaired electrons (\(\mu = 0\,\mathrm{BM}\), diamagnetic).

Evaluating Spin State and Crystal Field Stabilisation Energy (CFSE)

Problem: Consider a hypothetical octahedral \(d^5\) metal complex with an assumed pairing energy \(P = 22{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\). Determine the \(t_{2g}^x e_g^y\) configuration, number of unpaired electrons, and CFSE (in \(\Delta_o\) and \(P\)) when the crystal field splitting is (i) \(\Delta_o = 15{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) and (ii) \(\Delta_o = 29{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\).

Solution:

  1. Case (i): \(\Delta_o = 15{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1} < P = 22{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) (weak-field, high-spin). Electrons occupy all five orbitals singly: \(t_{2g}^3 e_g^2\), giving 5 unpaired electrons. \[\mathrm{CFSE} = 3(-0.4\Delta_o) + 2(+0.6\Delta_o) = -1.2\Delta_o + 1.2\Delta_o = 0\]
  2. Case (ii): \(\Delta_o = 29{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1} > P = 22{,}000\,\mathrm{cm}^{-1}\) (strong-field, low-spin). All five electrons enter the \(t_{2g}\) level: \(t_{2g}^5 e_g^0\), leaving 1 unpaired electron (with two extra electron pairs formed compared to the high-spin state). \[\mathrm{CFSE} = 5(-0.4\Delta_o) + 2P = -2.0\Delta_o + 2P\]

NEET UG Practice Questions

Practice Question 1 Single-Correct MCQ

One mole of a hydrated chromium(III) chloride complex of empirical formula \(\mathrm{CrCl}_3\cdot 5\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) reacts with excess cold \(\mathrm{AgNO}_3\) solution to precipitate \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{AgCl}\). What is the secondary valency of chromium and the total number of ions produced per formula unit in aqueous solution?

  • (A) Secondary valency = 5; Total ions = 3
  • (B) Secondary valency = 6; Total ions = 3
  • (C) Secondary valency = 6; Total ions = 4
  • (D) Secondary valency = 5; Total ions = 2

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: Precipitating \(2\,\mathrm{mol}\) of \(\mathrm{AgCl}\) per mole of complex shows two \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) ions are outside the coordination sphere as counter ions, while one \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) and five \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) molecules are coordinated to \(\mathrm{Cr}^{3+}\). Thus, the formula is \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_5\mathrm{Cl}]\mathrm{Cl}_2\). The secondary valency (coordination number) of \(\mathrm{Cr}\) is \(5 + 1 = 6\), and dissociation yields one \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_5\mathrm{Cl}]^{2+}\) ion and two \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) ions, making 3 ions in total.

Practice Question 2 Statement I & II

Read the following two statements carefully:
Statement I: The oxalate ion (\(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\)) is an ambidentate ligand because it contains two oxygen donor atoms.
Statement II: Complexes containing chelating ligands such as ethane-1,2-diamine are generally more stable than analogous complexes with unidentate ligands.

  • (A) Both Statement I and Statement II are correct.
  • (B) Both Statement I and Statement II are incorrect.
  • (C) Statement I is correct, but Statement II is incorrect.
  • (D) Statement I is incorrect, but Statement II is correct.

Correct Answer: (D)

Explanation: Statement I is incorrect because \(\mathrm{C}_2\mathrm{O}_4^{2-}\) is a didentate (chelating) ligand that binds simultaneously through two equivalent oxygen atoms; an ambidentate ligand is a unidentate ligand with two different donor atoms (like \(\mathrm{NO}_2^-\) or \(\mathrm{SCN}^-\)) that binds through only one at a time. Statement II is correct due to the chelate effect.

Practice Question 3 Single-Correct MCQ

What is the correct IUPAC name of the coordination compound \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{en})_2(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})\mathrm{Br}]\mathrm{SO}_4\)?

  • (A) Aquabromidobis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate
  • (B) Bromidoaquabis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(III) sulphate
  • (C) Aquabromidodi(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobalt(II) sulphate
  • (D) Aquabromidobis(ethane-1,2-diamine)cobaltate(III) sulphate

Correct Answer: (A)

Explanation: The sulphate counter ion has a \(-2\) charge, so the complex cation is \([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{en})_2(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})\mathrm{Br}]^{2+}\). Oxidation state of \(\mathrm{Co}\): \(x + 2(0) + 0 + (-1) = +2 \Rightarrow x = +3\). Alphabetical order of ligands: aqua, bromido, ethane-1,2-diamine (using bis because the ligand name contains di). Since the complex is cationic, the metal is named cobalt(III).

Practice Question 4 Single-Correct MCQ

Which of the following coordination entities exhibits optical isomerism?

  • (A) trans-\([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{ox})_2\mathrm{Cl}_2]^{3-}\)
  • (B) cis-\([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4\mathrm{Cl}_2]^+\)
  • (C) cis-\([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{ox})_2\mathrm{Cl}_2]^{3-}\)
  • (D) \([\mathrm{Pt}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_2\mathrm{Cl}_2]\)

Correct Answer: (C)

Explanation: Octahedral complexes of type \([\mathrm{M(AA)}_2\mathrm{X}_2]\) are chiral in their cis form (lacking a plane of symmetry) and exist as a pair of d and l enantiomers. In contrast, trans-\([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{ox})_2\mathrm{Cl}_2]^{3-}\), cis-\([\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_4\mathrm{Cl}_2]^+\), and square planar \([\mathrm{Pt}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_2\mathrm{Cl}_2]\) possess a plane of symmetry and are optically inactive.

Practice Question 5 Single-Correct MCQ

Which of the following complexes has a spin-only magnetic moment closest to \(4.90\,\mathrm{BM}\)?

  • (A) \([\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{4-}\)
  • (B) \([\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]^{2+}\)
  • (C) \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}\)
  • (D) \([\mathrm{Mn}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{4-}\)

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: \(\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)} = 4.90\,\mathrm{BM} \Rightarrow n = 4\) unpaired electrons (\(\sqrt{24} \approx 4.90\)). In \([\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]^{2+}\), \(\mathrm{Fe}^{2+}\) is \(3d^6\) and \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O}\) is a weak-field ligand, giving \(t_{2g}^4 e_g^2\) with 4 unpaired electrons. Meanwhile, \([\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{4-}\) has \(n=0\), \([\mathrm{Cr}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}\) (\(3d^3\)) has \(n=3\), and \([\mathrm{Mn}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{4-}\) (\(3d^5\), low-spin) has \(n=1\).

Practice Question 6 Single-Correct MCQ

Based on the spectrochemical series, what is the correct decreasing order of the wavelength of light absorbed (\(\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}\)) in the visible/UV region by the following octahedral complexes of \(\mathrm{Co}^{3+}\)?

\(\text{I: } [\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{CN})_6]^{3-}, \quad \text{II: } [\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{NH}_3)_6]^{3+}, \quad \text{III: } [\mathrm{Co}(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O})_6]^{3+}\)

  • (A) \(\text{I} > \text{II} > \text{III}\)
  • (B) \(\text{III} > \text{II} > \text{I}\)
  • (C) \(\text{II} > \text{III} > \text{I}\)
  • (D) \(\text{III} > \text{I} > \text{II}\)

Correct Answer: (B)

Explanation: Ligand field strength increases in the order \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{O} < \mathrm{NH}_3 < \mathrm{CN}^-\), so crystal field splitting energy increases as \(\Delta_o(\text{III}) < \Delta_o(\text{II}) < \Delta_o(\text{I})\). Since \(\Delta_o = \frac{hc}{\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}}\), absorbed wavelength is inversely proportional to \(\Delta_o\), giving \(\lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}\): \(\text{III} > \text{II} > \text{I}\).

Practice Question 7 Assertion–Reason

Assertion (A): Tetrahedral complexes rarely adopt low-spin electronic configurations.
Reason (R): For the same metal, ligands, and metal–ligand distances, the tetrahedral crystal field splitting \(\Delta_t\) is \(\frac{4}{9}\Delta_o\), which is smaller than the electron-pairing energy \(P\).

  • (A) Both (A) and (R) are true, and (R) is the correct explanation of (A).
  • (B) Both (A) and (R) are true, but (R) is not the correct explanation of (A).
  • (C) (A) is true, but (R) is false.
  • (D) (A) is false, but (R) is true.

Correct Answer: (A)

Explanation: Because only four ligands surround the metal and none point directly along the orbital axes, \(\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o\). This splitting energy is too small to overcome the pairing energy \(P\), so electrons occupy the upper \(t_2\) orbitals before pairing, resulting in high-spin configurations.

Practice Question 10 Single-Correct MCQ

Among the following iron(III) complexes, which one is expected to be the most stable in aqueous solution?

Common Pitfalls and NEET UG Revision Checklist

  • Counting \(\pi\)-bonds in Coordination Number: Only \(\sigma\)-donor bonds count toward CN; multiply bonded ligands like \(\mathrm{CO}\) still contribute 1 per ligand to CN.
  • Alphabetising Ligands with Prefixes: Always alphabetise by the ligand's base name (e.g., ammine before chlorido, even for triammine or dichloro). Use bis/tris for ligands whose names already contain di/tri (like ethane-1,2-diamine).
  • Tetrahedral Geometrical Isomerism Trap: Tetrahedral \([\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{B}_2]\) never shows cis–trans isomerism; only square planar \([\mathrm{MA}_2\mathrm{B}_2]\) does.
  • Optical Activity in trans-Octahedral Complexes: Remember that cis-\([\mathrm{M(AA)}_2\mathrm{X}_2]\) is optically active, whereas trans-\([\mathrm{M(AA)}_2\mathrm{X}_2]\) is optically inactive due to symmetry.
  • Confusing \(d^8\) Square Planar vs. Tetrahedral: \(\mathrm{Ni}^{2+}\) (\(3d^8\)) with weak-field \(\mathrm{Cl}^-\) or \(\mathrm{Br}^-\) is tetrahedral (\(sp^3\), paramagnetic, \(n=2\)), whereas with strong-field \(\mathrm{CN}^-\) it is square planar (\(dsp^2\), diamagnetic, \(n=0\)).

Concise Final Recap

Werner's theory links primary valency to oxidation state and secondary valency to coordination number and geometry. IUPAC nomenclature provides unambiguous names by listing ligands alphabetically before the central metal (ending in -ate for anionic complexes). Structural and stereoisomerism arise from ligand connectivity and spatial arrangement. While VBT predicts hybridisation and spin-only magnetic moments (\(\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\,\mathrm{BM}\)), CFT explains spin states (\(\Delta_o\) vs. \(P\)), tetrahedral splitting (\(\Delta_t = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_o\)), and \(d\text{-}d\) colour transitions. Finally, synergic bonding stabilises metal carbonyls, and coordination complexes play indispensable roles in biology, metallurgy, analysis, and medicine.
