Molecular Basis of Inheritance

वंशागति का आणविक आधार (Molecular Basis of Inheritance)

English

Learning Objectives & Exam Overview

In classical genetics, heritable traits were attributed to abstract "factors." Molecular biology reveals the physical chemistry of those factors: deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA). For NEET UG, this topic carries high weightage and tests both conceptual mechanisms (replication forks, transcription polarity, lac operon switching) and quantitative reasoning (Chargaff's base ratios, helix length, and Meselson–Stahl generation proportions).

  • Analyze the chemical polarity, base pairing, and nucleosome packaging of DNA.
  • Evaluate classic experiments proving DNA is the genetic material and replicates semiconservatively.
  • Trace information flow from DNA to RNA (transcription & splicing) and RNA to polypeptide (genetic code & translation).
  • Explain prokaryotic gene regulation via the lac operon, plus the principles of the Human Genome Project and DNA fingerprinting.

1. Chemical Architecture & Packaging of DNA

Polynucleotide Chain & DNA vs. RNA

Nucleic acids are linear polymers of nucleotides. Each nucleotide consists of three components: a nitrogenous base, a pentose sugar (2′-deoxyribose in DNA; ribose in RNA), and a phosphate group.

  • Nitrogenous Bases: Purines (double-ring) are Adenine (A) and Guanine (G). Pyrimidines (single-ring) are Cytosine (C), Thymine (T) (exclusive to DNA; chemically 5-methyl uracil), and Uracil (U) (replaces T in RNA).
  • Linkages: The base attaches to the 1′ carbon of the pentose sugar via an N-glycosidic linkage to form a nucleoside (e.g., adenosine, deoxythymidine). Adding a phosphate to the 5′-OH of the sugar via a phosphoester linkage yields a nucleotide. Adjacent nucleotides join through 3′→5′ phosphodiester linkages, creating a sugar-phosphate backbone with a free 5′-phosphate at one end and a free 3′-OH at the other.
  • RNA's 2′-OH Group: Every ribose residue in RNA carries an extra reactive –OH group at the 2′ position, making RNA chemically more labile and catalytically active than DNA.

Watson–Crick Double Helix & Chargaff's Rules

First isolated by Friedrich Meischer in 1869 as acidic "Nuclein," DNA's three-dimensional structure was solved in 1953 by James Watson and Francis Crick using X-ray diffraction data from Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins, alongside Erwin Chargaff's base-equivalence rules.

Chargaff's Equivalence (dsDNA): \(\mathrm{[A]} = \mathrm{[T]}, \quad \mathrm{[G]} = \mathrm{[C]}, \quad \frac{\mathrm{A + G}}{\mathrm{T + C}} = 1\)
  • Antiparallel Polarity: Two polynucleotide strands run in opposite directions—one 5′→3′ and the other 3′→5′.
  • Complementary Base Pairing: A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine inside the helix, keeping the inter-strand diameter uniform. Adenine pairs with Thymine via two hydrogen bonds (\(\mathrm{A=T}\)), and Guanine pairs with Cytosine via three hydrogen bonds (\(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\)).
  • Helical Geometry: Right-handed helix; pitch = \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)); ~\(10\,\mathrm{bp}\) per turn; axial rise per base pair = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\)). Base-pair stacking plus hydrogen bonds stabilize the helix.

Central Dogma & DNA Packaging

Francis Crick's Central Dogma states that genetic information flows unidirectionally: \(\mathrm{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{transcription}} \mathrm{mRNA} \xrightarrow{\text{translation}} \mathrm{Protein}\), while DNA duplicates itself by replication. Certain retroviruses perform reverse transcription (\(\mathrm{RNA} \rightarrow \mathrm{DNA}\)).

Because a diploid human cell contains \(6.6 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{bp}\) (length \(\approx 6.6 \times 10^{9} \times 0.34 \times 10^{-9}\,\mathrm{m} \approx 2.2\,\mathrm{m}\)) inside a nucleus of ~\(10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\), tight packaging is essential:

  • Prokaryotes (e.g., E. coli, \(4.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{bp}\)): Negatively charged DNA is organized in large loops held by positively charged proteins in a nuclear region called the nucleoid.
  • Eukaryotes: Negatively charged DNA wraps around a positively charged histone octamer (rich in basic amino acids lysine and arginine) to form a nucleosome (~200 bp of DNA per nucleosome), sealed by H1 histone. Repeating nucleosomes form the "beads-on-string" chromatin fiber. Further condensation at metaphase requires Non-Histone Chromosomal (NHC) proteins.
  • Euchromatin vs. Heterochromatin: Loosely packed, lightly staining, transcriptionally active regions are euchromatin; densely packed, darkly staining, transcriptionally inactive regions are heterochromatin.

1. DNA की रासायनिक संरचना एवं पैकेजिंग

पॉलीन्यूक्लियोटाइड श्रृंखला तथा DNA बनाम RNA

न्यूक्लिक अम्ल न्यूक्लियोटाइड के रेखीय बहुलक हैं। प्रत्येक न्यूक्लियोटाइड के तीन घटक होते हैं: एक नाइट्रोजनी क्षार, एक पेंटोज शर्करा (DNA में 2′-डीऑक्सीराइबोज; RNA में राइबोज) और एक फॉस्फेट समूह।

  • नाइट्रोजनी क्षार: प्यूरीन (द्वि-वलय) में एडिनीन (A) व ग्वानीन (G) आते हैं। पिरिमिडीन (एकल-वलय) में साइटोसीन (C), थाइमीन (T) (केवल DNA में; रासायनिक रूप से 5-मेथिल यूरेसिल), तथा यूरेसिल (U) (RNA में T के स्थान पर) आते हैं।
  • बंध: क्षार शर्करा के 1′ कार्बन से N-ग्लाइकोसिडिक बंध द्वारा जुड़कर न्यूक्लियोसाइड बनाता है। शर्करा के 5′-OH से फॉस्फोएस्टर बंध द्वारा फॉस्फेट जुड़ने पर न्यूक्लियोटाइड बनता है। दो न्यूक्लियोटाइड 3′→5′ फॉस्फोडाइएस्टर बंध द्वारा जुड़ते हैं, जिससे 5′-फॉस्फेट सिरा और 3′-OH सिरा बनता है।
  • RNA का 2′-OH समूह: RNA में प्रत्येक राइबोज शर्करा के 2′ स्थान पर एक अतिरिक्त क्रियाशील –OH समूह होता है, जो RNA को DNA की तुलना में अधिक अस्थायी व उत्प्रेरक बनाता है।

वाटसन–क्रिक द्विकुंडली मॉडल एवं चारगाफ नियम

1869 में फ्रेडरिक मीशर ने नाभिक से अम्लीय 'न्यूक्लिन' की खोज की। 1953 में जेम्स वाटसन और फ्रांसिस क्रिक ने रोज़ालिंड फ्रैंकलिन व मॉरिस विल्किंस के एक्स-रे विवर्तन डेटा तथा इरविन चारगाफ के प्रेक्षणों के आधार पर DNA की द्विकुंडली संरचना प्रस्तुत की।

चारगाफ समतुल्यता (dsDNA): \(\mathrm{[A]} = \mathrm{[T]}, \quad \mathrm{[G]} = \mathrm{[C]}, \quad \frac{\mathrm{A + G}}{\mathrm{T + C}} = 1\)
  • प्रतिसमानांतर ध्रुवता: दोनों श्रृंखलाएँ विपरीत दिशाओं में होती हैं—एक 5′→3′ तथा दूसरी 3′→5′।
  • पूरक क्षार युग्मन: प्यूरीन सदैव पिरिमिडीन से जुड़ता है, जिससे कुंडली का व्यास समान रहता है। A और T के बीच दो हाइड्रोजन बंध (\(\mathrm{A=T}\)) तथा G और C के बीच तीन हाइड्रोजन बंध (\(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\)) होते हैं।
  • कुंडली ज्यामिति: दक्षिणावर्त कुंडली; पिच = \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)); प्रति मोड़ ~\(10\,\mathrm{bp}\); दो क्षार युग्मों के बीच दूरी = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\))।

सेंट्रल डोग्मा एवं DNA की पैकेजिंग

फ्रांसिस क्रिक के सेंट्रल डोग्मा के अनुसार आनुवंशिक सूचना एकदिशीय रूप से प्रवाहित होती है: \(\mathrm{DNA} \rightarrow \mathrm{mRNA} \rightarrow \mathrm{Protein}\) (प्रोटीन)। कुछ विषाणुओं में उत्क्रम अनुलेखन (\(\mathrm{RNA} \rightarrow \mathrm{DNA}\)) होता है।

द्विगुणित मानव कोशिका में \(6.6 \times 10^{9}\,\mathrm{bp}\) (~2.2 मीटर लंबा DNA) लगभग \(10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\) केंद्रक में पैक रहता है:

  • प्रोकैरियोट्स (जैसे E. coli, \(4.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{bp}\)): ऋणावेशित DNA धनावेशित प्रोटीनों के साथ बड़े लूपों में न्यूक्लियॉइड क्षेत्र में व्यवस्थित रहता है।
  • यूकैरियोट्स: ऋणावेशित DNA धनावेशित हिस्टोन अष्टक (क्षारीय अमीनो अम्ल लाइसीन व आर्जीनीन से समृद्ध) के चारों ओर लिपटकर न्यूक्लियोसोम (~200 bp) बनाता है, जिसे H1 हिस्टोन स्थिर करता है। ये क्रोमैटिन के 'माला के मोती' बनाते हैं। उच्च स्तरीय संघनन हेतु गैर-हिस्टोन गुणसूत्रीय (NHC) प्रोटीन आवश्यक होते हैं।
  • यूक्रेमैटिन बनाम हेटरोक्रोमैटिन: ढीला बंधा, हल्का अभिरंजित व अनुलेखन रूप से सक्रिय भाग यूक्रेमैटिन है; सघन, गहरा अभिरंजित व निष्क्रिय भाग हेटरोक्रोमैटिन है।

2. Experimental Proof of Genetic Material & RNA World

Experiment (Year) Organism / System Key Observation & Conclusion
Frederick Griffith (1928) Streptococcus pneumoniae (S virulent vs. R non-virulent) in mice Heat-killed S + live R killed mice; live S recovered. A "transforming principle" converted R to S, though its chemical nature remained unknown.
Avery, MacLeod & McCarty (1933–44) Purified fractions of heat-killed S + live R Proteases and RNases did not block transformation, whereas DNase inhibited transformation—proving DNA is the transforming principle.
Hershey & Chase (1952) Bacteriophage & E. coli using \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) (DNA) and \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) (protein) After infection, blending, and centrifugation: \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) appeared in bacterial pellet; \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) stayed in supernatant. Unequivocal proof that DNA is the genetic material.

Criteria for Genetic Material & RNA World

A genetic molecule must: (1) replicate, (2) be chemically and structurally stable, (3) permit slow mutations for evolution, and (4) express Mendelian characters. Both DNA and RNA satisfy these (some viruses like TMV and QB bacteriophage use RNA), but DNA is preferred for storage because it lacks the reactive 2′-OH group and contains thymine instead of uracil, conferring superior stability and repair capacity. RNA was the first genetic material (the RNA World), serving both as genetic code and catalyst (ribozymes) before DNA evolved for safer storage.

3. DNA Replication: Semiconservative Proof & Enzymatic Fork

Watson and Crick proposed semiconservative replication: each parental strand acts as a template so every daughter duplex retains one parental and one newly synthesized strand.

  • Meselson & Stahl (1958): Grew E. coli in heavy nitrogen \(^{15}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) (note: \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) is a heavy, non-radioactive isotope) and shifted them to normal \(^{14}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\), separating DNA by CsCl density-gradient centrifugation. After Generation I (\(20\,\mathrm{min}\)): 100% hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}{}^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA. After Generation II (\(40\,\mathrm{min}\)): 50% hybrid + 50% light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}{}^{14}\mathrm{N}\)). Taylor et al. (1958) confirmed this in eukaryotes (Vicia faba) using radioactive thymidine.
  • Enzymes & Energetics: The main enzyme is DNA-dependent DNA polymerase, which polymerizes ~\(2000\,\mathrm{bp/s}\) in E. coli (completing \(4.6 \times 10^{6}\,\mathrm{bp}\) in \(18\,\mathrm{min}\)) with high accuracy. Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates (dNTPs) serve a dual role: they act as substrates and provide energy via their two terminal high-energy phosphates.
  • Replication Fork Polarity: DNA polymerase synthesizes only in the 5′→3′ direction. Thus, on the 3′→5′ template strand, synthesis is continuous (leading strand). On the 5′→3′ template strand, synthesis is discontinuous (lagging strand fragments), later joined by DNA ligase.
  • Origin of Replication (ori) & Cell Cycle: Replication starts at a specific ori site (essential for cloning vectors). In eukaryotes, DNA replication occurs during S-phase; failure of cell division after replication causes polyploidy.

3. DNA प्रतिकृति: अर्ध-संरक्षी प्रमाण एवं एंजाइम तंत्र

वाटसन व क्रिक ने अर्ध-संरक्षी प्रतिकृति का प्रस्ताव दिया: प्रत्येक पैतृक रज्जुक साँचे का कार्य करता है, जिससे प्रत्येक संतति DNA अणु में एक पैतृक और एक नव-संश्लेषित रज्जुक होता है।

4. Transcription & Post-Transcriptional Processing

Transcription copies one strand of a DNA segment into RNA using complementarity (A pairs with U). Only one strand is copied because: (1) two complementary templates would yield two different proteins from one locus, and (2) simultaneous complementary RNAs would hybridize into double-stranded RNA, blocking translation.

  • Transcription Unit: Comprises a Promoter, Structural gene, and Terminator. Because DNA-dependent RNA polymerase polymerizes 5′→3′, the 3′→5′ strand is the Template strand, while the 5′→3′ strand is the Coding strand (same sequence as RNA except T for U). Promoter lies upstream (5′-end of coding strand); Terminator lies downstream (3′-end of coding strand).
  • Gene Organization: A cistron is a DNA segment coding for a polypeptide. Prokaryotes typically have polycistronic units; eukaryotes have monocistronic split genes containing expressed exons interrupted by non-coding introns.
  • Bacterial Transcription: One RNA polymerase transcribes mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA. It requires transient association with sigma (\(\sigma\)) factor for initiation and rho (\(\rho\)) factor for termination. Because there is no nuclear membrane and mRNA needs no processing, transcription and translation are coupled in bacteria.
  • Eukaryotic Complexity: Three nuclear RNA polymerases divide labor:
    • RNA Pol I: rRNAs (28S, 18S, 5.8S)
    • RNA Pol II: precursor of mRNA (hnRNA)
    • RNA Pol III: tRNA, 5S rRNA, snRNAs
    Primary hnRNA undergoes capping (methyl guanosine triphosphate, \(^{m}\mathrm{Gppp}\), added at 5′-end), splicing (introns removed, exons joined), and tailing/polyadenylation (200–300 adenylate residues added at 3′-end independently of template) before export to the cytoplasm.

5. Genetic Code, Mutations & Translation

  • Deciphering the Code: George Gamow deduced a triplet code (\(4^{3} = 64\) codons) for 20 amino acids. Har Gobind Khorana synthesized defined RNA homopolymers/copolymers; Marshall Nirenberg developed the cell-free protein synthesis system; Severo Ochoa's enzyme (polynucleotide phosphorylase) polymerized RNA without a template.
  • Salient Features:
    1. Triplet: 61 sense codons specify amino acids; 3 stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) terminate translation.
    2. Degenerate: Most amino acids have multiple codons (e.g., UUU and UUC both code for Phe).
    3. Comma-less: Read contiguously without punctuation.
    4. Nearly Universal: Exceptions occur in some mitochondrial and protozoan codons.
    5. Dual Role of AUG: Codes for Methionine (Met) and acts as the initiator codon.
  • Mutations: A single base-pair substitution in the \(\beta\)-globin gene (glutamate → valine) causes sickle cell anemia. Insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases causes a frameshift mutation altering the entire downstream reading frame, whereas insertion/deletion of 3 (or multiples of 3) bases adds/removes whole amino acids without shifting the frame.
  • tRNA (Adapter Molecule): Cloverleaf in 2D, inverted-L in 3D. Bears an anticodon loop complementary to mRNA codon and a 3′ amino acid acceptor end. No tRNAs exist for stop codons.
  • Translation Mechanism: (1) Aminoacylation (charging) of tRNA requires ATP. (2) Ribosome (small + large subunit; ~80 proteins + rRNAs) acts as a factory and catalyst: 23S rRNA in bacteria is the ribozyme (peptidyl transferase) forming peptide bonds. (3) mRNA contains Untranslated Regions (UTRs) at both 5′ and 3′ ends for efficient translation. (4) A release factor binds the stop codon to terminate synthesis.

6. Regulation of Gene Expression & The lac Operon

In eukaryotes, gene expression is regulated at four levels: transcriptional, processing (splicing), mRNA transport, and translational. In prokaryotes, transcriptional initiation is the predominant control point, organized in operons (polycistronic structural genes under a common promoter and operator).

Elucidated by François Jacob and Jacques Monod, the lac operon regulates lactose metabolism via negative regulation:

  • Regulatory Gene (i): Derived from inhibitor (not inducer); constitutively produces the repressor protein.
  • Operator (o): Adjacent to promoter; binds the active repressor to block RNA polymerase.
  • Structural Genes: z codes for \(\beta\)-galactosidase (hydrolyzes lactose → galactose + glucose); y codes for permease (increases cell permeability to \(\beta\)-galactosides); a codes for transacetylase.
  • Switching Mechanism: Without an inducer, the repressor binds o (operon OFF; a basal low level of expression always persists so permease can import lactose). When lactose (or allolactose) enters, it binds and inactivates the repressor, freeing the promoter for RNA polymerase (operon ON). Note: Glucose or galactose cannot act as inducers.

7. Human Genome Project (HGP) & DNA Fingerprinting

  • HGP Overview (1990–2003): A 13-year international mega-project (~$9 billion estimated cost; ~3 × 10⁹ bp) that catalyzed Bioinformatics and addressed Ethical, Legal, and Social Issues (ELSI).
  • Methodologies: Two approaches: Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs) (sequencing only genes expressed as RNA) and Sequence Annotation (sequencing the entire genome—coding + non-coding—and assigning functions). DNA fragments were cloned in bacteria/yeast using BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosomes) and YAC (Yeast Artificial Chromosomes) vectors and sequenced via automated Sanger sequencers. Chromosome 1 was finished last (May 2006).
  • Salient Features: Human genome = 3164.7 million bp; ~30,000 genes; average gene = 3000 bases; largest gene = dystrophin (2.4 million bases); <2% codes for proteins; 99.9% base sequence is identical across humans; Chromosome 1 has the most genes (2968) and Y has the fewest (231); ~1.4 million SNPs (Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms) identified.
  • DNA Fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys): Exploits non-coding repetitive DNA separated as satellite DNA peaks during density-gradient centrifugation. Polymorphism (heritable variation at >0.01 allele frequency) is highest in non-coding regions. Using VNTR (Variable Number of Tandem Repeats, a mini-satellite of 0.1–20 kb) as a radiolabeled probe in Southern blot hybridization (DNA isolation → restriction digestion → electrophoresis → blotting → probe hybridization → autoradiography), unique banding patterns are obtained (identical only in monozygotic twins). PCR amplification allows analysis from a single cell for forensics and paternity testing.

Example 1: Applying Chargaff's Rule & Helix Geometry

Problem (Assumed Data): A hypothetical double-stranded DNA molecule of a bacterium has a total length of \(680\,\mathrm{nm}\). If \(18\%\) of its total nitrogenous bases are Adenine, calculate: (a) the total number of base pairs, (b) the percentage of Cytosine, and (c) the total number of hydrogen bonds.

Solution:

(a) Distance between consecutive base pairs = \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\). Therefore:

\[ \text{Total bp} = \frac{680\,\mathrm{nm}}{0.34\,\mathrm{nm/bp}} = 2000\,\mathrm{bp} \]

(b) By Chargaff's rule, \(\mathrm{[A]} = \mathrm{[T]} = 18\%\), so \(\mathrm{A + T} = 36\%\). Remaining \(\mathrm{G + C} = 100\% - 36\% = 64\%\). Since \(\mathrm{[G]} = \mathrm{[C]}\), Cytosine = \(32\%\).

(c) Number of \(\mathrm{A=T}\) pairs = \(18\%\) of \(2000 = 360\) (Wait: \(36\%\) of \(2000\,\mathrm{bp} = 720\,\mathrm{bp}\), because \(18\%\) of \(4000\) bases = \(720\) A bases, each paired with one T!). Thus: \(\mathrm{A=T}\) pairs = \(720\), and \(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\) pairs = \(2000 - 720 = 1280\).

\[ \text{Total H-bonds} = (720 \times 2) + (1280 \times 3) = 1440 + 3840 = 5280 \]
Example 2: Predicting Meselson–Stahl Generation Proportions

Problem (Assumed Data): An E. coli culture grown for many generations in \(^{15}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) is transferred to \(^{14}\mathrm{NH_4Cl}\) medium and allowed to divide for \(60\,\mathrm{minutes}\) (assuming a \(20\,\mathrm{minute}\) generation time). What is the ratio of hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}{}^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) to light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}{}^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA molecules?

Solution:

Number of generations in \(60\,\mathrm{min}\) = \(\frac{60}{20} = 3\) generations. Starting with \(1\) heavy (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}{}^{15}\mathrm{N}\)) molecule, total DNA molecules after \(3\) generations = \(2^{3} = 8\). Because replication is semiconservative, the original \(2\) heavy strands remain in exactly \(2\) hybrid molecules, and the remaining \(8 - 2 = 6\) molecules are light. Ratio of hybrid : light = \(2 : 6 = 1 : 3\) (or \(25\%\) hybrid, \(75\%\) light).

Problem (Assumed Data): A hypothetical coding strand of a prokaryotic gene is: 5′-ATG GGC TTT TGA CCA-3′. Determine the template strand polarity and sequence, the transcribed mRNA sequence, and the number of amino acids in the resulting polypeptide.

Solution:

The template strand is antiparallel and complementary to the coding strand:

3′-TAC CCG AAA ACT GGT-5′ (Template strand)

The mRNA matches the coding strand with U replacing T:

5′-AUG GGC UUU UGA CCA-3′

Practice Question 8 (Single-Correct MCQ)

During protein synthesis in bacteria, which molecule acts directly as the catalytic enzyme (ribozyme) for peptide bond formation between adjacent amino acids?

  • (1) Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase
  • (2) 23S rRNA
  • (3) 16S rRNA
  • (4) Release factor

Answer: (2) 23S rRNA

Worked Solution: In bacteria, the large ribosomal subunit contains 23S rRNA, which acts as a ribozyme (peptidyl transferase) to catalyze peptide bond formation. Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase charges tRNA, and release factors terminate translation at stop codons.

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist

  • \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) vs. \(^{32}\mathrm{P}/^{35}\mathrm{S}\): \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) in Meselson–Stahl is a heavy, non-radioactive isotope separated by CsCl density; \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) and \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) in Hershey–Chase are radioactive isotopes.
  • Coding Strand Confusion: The coding strand does not act as the template; it is named so because its 5′→3′ sequence matches the transcribed mRNA (with T instead of U).
  • i Gene Meaning: In the lac operon, i stands for inhibitor (repressor), not inducer.
  • 5′ vs. 3′ Processing: Capping (\(^{m}\mathrm{Gppp}\)) occurs at the 5′-end of hnRNA; poly-A tailing (200–300 A residues) occurs at the 3′-end.

Concise Final Recap

DNA is a right-handed antiparallel double helix (\(0.34\,\mathrm{nm/bp}\), \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm/turn}\)) stabilized by H-bonds (\(\mathrm{A=T}\), \(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\)) and base stacking, packaged around positively charged histone octamers into nucleosomes (~200 bp). Proven as the genetic material by Avery et al. and Hershey–Chase, DNA replicates semiconservatively (Meselson–Stahl) in the 5′→3′ direction (continuous leading, discontinuous lagging). Transcription copies the 3′→5′ template into RNA; eukaryotic hnRNA undergoes 5′-capping, splicing, and 3′-polyadenylation. The triplet, degenerate, nearly universal genetic code is read by tRNA adapters on ribosomes (23S rRNA ribozyme) to build polypeptides. Prokaryotic genes are regulated via operons (e.g., lactose-inducible lac operon), while HGP (3164.7 Mbp, ~30,000 genes) and VNTR-based DNA fingerprinting anchor modern genomics.

संक्षिप्त अंतिम सारांश

DNA एक दक्षिणावर्त प्रतिसमानांतर द्विकुंडली है (\(0.34\,\mathrm{nm/bp}\), \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm/turn}\)), जो हाइड्रोजन बंधों (\(\mathrm{A=T}\), \(\mathrm{G\equiv C}\)) से स्थायी होती है और हिस्टोन अष्टकों पर लिपटकर न्यूक्लियोसोम (~200 bp) बनाती है। एवरी व हर्षे–चेज़ द्वारा आनुवंशिक पदार्थ सिद्ध होने के बाद, DNA अर्ध-संरक्षी विधि से 5′→3′ दिशा में प्रतिकृति करता है। अनुलेखन में 3′→5′ साँचे से RNA बनता है; यूकैरियोटिक hnRNA में 5′-आच्छादन, समबंधन और 3′-पॉलीएडिनाइलेशन होता है। त्रिक व अपह्रासित आनुवंशिक कूट को tRNA तथा राइबोसोम (23S rRNA राइबोजाइम) पॉलीपेप्टाइड में बदलते हैं। जीवाणु में जीन नियमन ऑपेरॉन (लैक ऑपेरॉन) से होता है, तथा HGP (3164.7 Mbp, ~30,000 जीन) और VNTR-आधारित DNA अंगुलिछापन आधुनिक जीनोमिक्स के आधार हैं।
