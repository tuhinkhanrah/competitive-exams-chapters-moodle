2. Chemical Architecture of Nucleic Acids & DNA Packaging

2.1 Monomers to Polynucleotide Chains

Nucleic acids—deoxyribonucleic acid (DNA) and ribonucleic acid (RNA)—are polymers of nucleotides. Each nucleotide consists of three components:

  1. A Nitrogenous Base: Purines (Adenine, A; Guanine, G) have a double-ring structure, whereas Pyrimidines (Cytosine, C; Thymine, T; Uracil, U) have a single-ring structure. Thymine (5-methyl uracil) is characteristic of DNA, while Uracil replaces Thymine in RNA.
  2. A Pentose Sugar: Deoxyribose in DNA lacks an oxygen atom at the 2' carbon, whereas ribose in RNA possesses a reactive 2'-OH group.
  3. A Phosphate Group: Linked to the 5'-OH of the sugar via a phosphoester bond.

A nitrogenous base attaches to the 1' carbon of the pentose sugar via an N-glycosidic linkage to form a nucleoside (e.g., adenosine, deoxythymidine). Adding a phosphate group at the 5' carbon creates a nucleotide. Adjacent nucleotides join through 3'–5' phosphodiester linkages, forming a sugar-phosphate backbone with a free 5'-phosphate at one end and a free 3'-OH at the other.

2.2 The Double Helix & Chargaff's Rules

Friedrich Meischer first isolated DNA as an acidic nuclear substance ("nuclein") in 1869. In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick integrated X-ray diffraction data from Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins with Erwin Chargaff's chemical equivalence rules to propose the double-helix model:

  • Antiparallel Polarity: Two polynucleotide strands run in opposite directions—one 5'→3' and the other 3'→5'.
  • Complementary Hydrogen Bonding: Purines pair specifically with pyrimidines: Adenine pairs with Thymine via 2 hydrogen bonds (\(A = T\)), and Guanine pairs with Cytosine via 3 hydrogen bonds (\(G \equiv C\)). This maintains a uniform helix diameter.
  • Chargaff's Equivalence: In double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), \([\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}]\) and \([\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{C}]\), so \(\frac{[\mathrm{A}]+[\mathrm{G}]}{[\mathrm{T}]+[\mathrm{C}]} = 1\). However, the \((\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{T})/(\mathrm{G}+\mathrm{C})\) ratio varies by species.
  • Helical Dimensions: Right-handed coil with a pitch of \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)), approximately \(10\,\mathrm{bp}\) per turn, and an axial rise of \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\)) between consecutive base pairs. Base-pair stacking adds structural stability.

2.3 Central Dogma & DNA Packaging

Francis Crick formulated the Central Dogma of molecular biology: genetic information flows from \(\mathrm{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{transcription}} \mathrm{mRNA} \xrightarrow{\text{translation}} \mathrm{Protein}\). In retroviruses, information flows in reverse from RNA to DNA via reverse transcription.

A diploid human cell contains \(6.6 \times 10^9\,\mathrm{bp}\), spanning approximately \(2.2\,\mathrm{m}\) in length—far exceeding the nuclear diameter (\(\approx 10^{-6}\,\mathrm{m}\)).

  • Prokaryotes (e.g., E. coli): Negatively charged DNA is organized into large loops held by positively charged proteins in a region called the nucleoid.
  • Eukaryotes: Negatively charged DNA wraps around a positively charged histone octamer (rich in basic amino acids lysine and arginine) to form a nucleosome. A typical nucleosome holds \(200\,\mathrm{bp}\) of DNA, sealed by H1 linker histone, appearing as "beads-on-string" chromatin under electron microscopy. Higher-order condensation requires Non-Histone Chromosomal (NHC) proteins.

২.১ মনোমার থেকে পলিনিউক্লিওটাইড শৃঙ্খল

নিউক্লিক অ্যাসিড—ডিঅক্সিরাইবোনিউক্লিক অ্যাসিড (DNA) এবং রাইবোনিউক্লিক অ্যাসিড (RNA)—হলো নিউক্লিওটাইডের পলিমার। প্রতিটি নিউক্লিওটাইডের তিনটি উপাদান থাকে:

  1. নাইট্রোজেনঘটিত ক্ষারক (Nitrogenous Base): পিউরিন (অ্যাডেনিন, A; গুয়ানিন, G) দ্বি-বলয়যুক্ত এবং পিরিমিডিন (সাইটোসিন, C; থাইমিন, T; ইউরাসিল, U) এক-বলয়যুক্ত। থাইমিন (5-মিথাইল ইউরাসিল) DNA-তে থাকে, আর RNA-তে থাইমিনের পরিবর্তে ইউরাসিল থাকে।
  2. পেন্টোজ শর্করা: DNA-এর ডিঅক্সিরাইবোজ শর্করার 2' কার্বনে অক্সিজেন পরমাণু অনুপস্থিত থাকে, কিন্তু RNA-এর রাইবোজ শর্করায় একটি সক্রিয় 2'-OH মূলক থাকে।
  3. ফসফেট মূলক: ফসফোএস্টার বন্ধনীর মাধ্যমে শর্করার 5'-OH-এর সাথে যুক্ত থাকে।

একটি নাইট্রোজেনঘটিত ক্ষারক N-গ্লাইকোসিডিক বন্ধনী দ্বারা পেন্টোজ শর্করার 1' কার্বনের সাথে যুক্ত হয়ে নিউক্লিওসাইড গঠন করে। 5' কার্বনে ফসফেট যুক্ত হলে নিউক্লিওটাইড তৈরি হয়। দুটি নিউক্লিওটাইড 3'–5' ফসফোডাইএস্টার বন্ধনী দ্বারা যুক্ত হয়ে শর্করা-ফসফেট মেরুদণ্ড গঠন করে, যার এক প্রান্তে মুক্ত 5'-ফসফেট এবং অপর প্রান্তে মুক্ত 3'-OH থাকে।

২.২ দ্বি-তন্ত্রী হেলিক্স ও চারগাফের নিয়ম

১৮৬৯ সালে ফ্রেডরিখ মিশার নিউক্লিয়াস থেকে আম্লিক পদার্থ হিসেবে DNA ("নিউক্লিন") আবিষ্কার করেন। ১৯৫৩ সালে জেমস ওয়াটসন ও ফ্রান্সিস ক্রিক, রোজালিন্ড ফ্র্যাঙ্কলিন ও মরিস উইলকিন্সের X-রশ্মি অপবর্তন তথ্য এবং এরউইন চারগাফের নিয়মের ভিত্তিতে DNA-এর দ্বি-তন্ত্রী হেলিক্স মডেল প্রস্তাব করেন:

  • বিপরীত-সমান্তরাল মেরুত্ব (Antiparallel Polarity): দুটি শৃঙ্খল বিপরীত অভিমুখে থাকে—একটি 5'→3' এবং অন্যটি 3'→5'।
  • পরিপূরক হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধনী: অ্যাডেনিন ও থাইমিনের মধ্যে ২টি হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধনী (\(A = T\)) এবং গুয়ানিন ও সাইটোসিনের মধ্যে ৩টি হাইড্রোজেন বন্ধনী (\(G \equiv C\)) থাকে। এর ফলে হেলিক্সের ব্যাস সর্বত্র সমান থাকে।
  • চারগাফের সমতা নিয়ম: দ্বি-তন্ত্রী DNA-তে \([\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}]\) এবং \([\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{C}]\), অর্থাৎ \(\frac{[\mathrm{A}]+[\mathrm{G}]}{[\mathrm{T}]+[\mathrm{C}]} = 1\)। তবে \((\mathrm{A}+\mathrm{T})/(\mathrm{G}+\mathrm{C})\) অনুপাত প্রজাতিভেদে ভিন্ন হয়।
  • হেলিক্সের পরিমাপ: দক্ষিণাবর্তী কুণ্ডলীর পিচ \(3.4\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(34\,\mathrm{Å}\)), প্রতি পূর্ণ পাকে প্রায় \(10\,\mathrm{bp}\) এবং দুটি বেস পেয়ারের মধ্যবর্তী দূরত্ব \(0.34\,\mathrm{nm}\) (\(3.4\,\mathrm{Å}\))।

3. The Quest for Genetic Material & The RNA World

3.1 Key Historical Experiments

  • Griffith's Transformation Principle (1928): Working with Streptococcus pneumoniae, Frederick Griffith observed that virulent S-strain (polysaccharide coat, kills mice) and avirulent R-strain (rough, mice live) behaved unexpectedly when mixed: heat-killed S-strain + live R-strain killed mice, yielding live S-strain bacteria. Some "transforming principle" converted R into S, though its chemical identity remained unknown.
  • Biochemical Characterization (1933–1944): Oswald Avery, Colin MacLeod, and Maclyn McCarty purified fractions from heat-killed S cells. Proteases and RNases did not block transformation, whereas DNase completely inhibited it—proving DNA was the transforming principle.
  • Hershey–Chase Experiment (1952): Alfred Hershey and Martha Chase provided unequivocal proof using bacteriophages infecting E. coli. Phages grown with radioactive phosphorus (\(^{32}\mathrm{P}\)) labeled DNA (since DNA has phosphorus, proteins do not), whereas phages grown with radioactive sulfur (\(^{35}\mathrm{S}\)) labeled the protein coat (proteins contain sulfur, DNA does not). After infection, blending, and centrifugation, \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) radioactivity was detected inside bacterial cells, while \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) remained in the supernatant. Thus, DNA entered the host as the genetic material.

3.2 Criteria for Genetic Material & The RNA World

A genetic material must: (1) replicate, (2) be chemically and structurally stable, (3) allow slow mutations for evolution, and (4) express Mendelian characters. DNA is preferred for information storage because it lacks the reactive 2'-OH group (present in RNA) and contains thymine instead of uracil, making it less labile and amenable to repair. Conversely, RNA—which evolved first in the RNA World—acts as both genetic material (e.g., TMV, Qβ bacteriophage) and catalyst (ribozyme), mutating faster and directly mediating protein synthesis.

4. DNA Replication: Mechanism & Experimental Proof

4.1 Semiconservative Replication

Watson and Crick predicted that each parental strand acts as a template for a new complementary strand, yielding daughter helices composed of one old and one new strand. Matthew Meselson and Franklin Stahl (1958) proved this in E. coli (generation time \(20\,\mathrm{min}\)) by growing cells in heavy \(^{15}\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\) medium and transferring them to normal \(^{14}\mathrm{NH}_4\mathrm{Cl}\) medium, separating DNA by CsCl density-gradient centrifugation (note: \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) is a heavy stable isotope, not radioactive):

  • Generation I (\(20\,\mathrm{min}\)): \(100\%\) intermediate/hybrid density (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA.
  • Generation II (\(40\,\mathrm{min}\)): \(50\%\) hybrid (\(^{15}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) and \(50\%\) light (\(^{14}\mathrm{N}/^{14}\mathrm{N}\)) DNA.

Taylor and colleagues (1958) confirmed semiconservative replication in eukaryotes using radioactive thymidine in Vicia faba (faba beans).

4.2 Enzymatic Machinery & The Replication Fork

  • Origin of Replication (ori): Replication initiates at a specific site (which is why recombinant DNA vectors must carry an ori). In eukaryotes, DNA replicates during the S-phase of the cell cycle; failure of cell division after replication causes polyploidy.
  • DNA-dependent DNA Polymerase: Catalyzes polymerization exclusively in the 5'→3' direction (at \(\approx 2000\,\mathrm{bp/s}\) in E. coli).
  • Dual Role of dNTPs: Deoxyribonucleoside triphosphates act both as substrates and as energy sources (via their two terminal high-energy phosphates).
  • Continuous vs. Discontinuous Synthesis: At the Y-shaped replication fork, synthesis on the 3'→5' template strand is continuous (leading strand), whereas synthesis on the 5'→3' template strand is discontinuous (Okazaki fragments), later joined by DNA ligase.

5. Transcription: From Gene to Functional RNA

Transcription copies genetic information from only one strand of a DNA segment into RNA (with Uracil pairing with Adenine). Copying both strands simultaneously would produce two different proteins from one segment and form double-stranded RNA, preventing translation.

5.1 The Transcription Unit

A transcription unit has three regions: Promoter (5' upstream of coding strand; binds RNA polymerase), Structural Gene, and Terminator (3' downstream of coding strand). Because DNA-dependent RNA polymerase synthesizes RNA only in the 5'→3' direction, the 3'→5' DNA strand acts as the template strand, while the 5'→3' DNA strand (whose sequence matches RNA except T for U) is called the coding strand.

5.2 Prokaryotes vs. Eukaryotes

  • Prokaryotes: Polycistronic genes; a single RNA polymerase synthesizes mRNA, tRNA, and rRNA. It requires transient association with σ (sigma) factor for initiation and ρ (rho) factor for termination, catalyzing elongation on its own. Since mRNA needs no processing and there is no nuclear membrane, transcription and translation are coupled.

    • RNA Pol III: tRNA, 5S rRNA, and snRNAs.

6. Genetic Code, Mutations & Translation

6.1 Features of the Genetic Code

George Gamow proposed a triplet code (\(4^3 = 64\) codons) to specify 20 amino acids. Deciphering was achieved using Har Gobind Khorana's chemical synthesis of defined RNAs, Marshall Nirenberg's cell-free translation system, and Severo Ochoa's enzyme (polynucleotide phosphorylase, template-independent RNA synthesis).

  • Triplet & Stop Codons: 61 codons specify amino acids; 3 are stop/nonsense codons (UAA, UAG, UGA).
  • Dual Role of AUG: Codes for Methionine (Met) and acts as the initiator codon.
  • Degenerate: Most amino acids are coded by more than one codon (exceptions: Met and Trp have 1 each).
  • Commaless & Nearly Universal: Read contiguously without punctuation; UUU codes for Phe from bacteria to humans (minor exceptions in mitochondria and some protozoans).

6.2 Mutations & tRNA Adapter

A point mutation changing a single base pair in the β-globin gene (Glu → Val) causes sickle-cell anemia. Insertion or deletion of 1 or 2 bases shifts the reading frame (frameshift mutation), whereas insertion or deletion of 3 bases (or multiples of 3) adds or removes whole amino acids without altering the downstream reading frame.

Francis Crick proposed tRNA (earlier sRNA) as the adapter molecule. Its secondary structure resembles a clover-leaf, while its actual 3D structure is an inverted L. It has an anticodon loop complementary to the mRNA codon and a 3'-amino acid acceptor end. Stop codons have no tRNAs.

6.3 Translation Mechanism

  1. Charging (Aminoacylation) of tRNA: Amino acids are activated with ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA.
  2. Initiation: The small ribosomal subunit binds mRNA at the start codon (AUG), recognized by initiator tRNA.
  3. Elongation: Charged tRNAs bind sequentially; peptide bond formation is catalyzed by the 23S rRNA ribozyme in the large subunit. mRNA flanks include untranslated regions (UTRs) at 5' and 3' ends for efficiency.
  4. Termination: A release factor binds the stop codon, releasing the completed polypeptide.

7. Regulation of Gene Expression: The lac Operon

In eukaryotes, gene expression is regulated at transcriptional, processing (splicing), mRNA transport, and translational levels. In prokaryotes, transcriptional initiation is the primary control point. Francois Jacob and Jacques Monod elucidated the lac operon as a model of negative transcriptional regulation:

  • i gene (inhibitor): Constitutively produces the lac repressor protein.
  • z gene: Codes for β-galactosidase (hydrolyzes lactose into galactose + glucose).
  • y gene: Codes for permease (increases cell permeability to β-galactosides).
  • a gene: Codes for transacetylase.

Without Inducer (Lactose absent): Active repressor binds the operator (o), blocking RNA polymerase. With Inducer (Lactose/Allolactose present): Inducer binds and inactivates the repressor, allowing RNA polymerase to transcribe lac mRNA. Note that glucose or galactose cannot act as inducers, and a very low basal level of permease is always present so lactose can enter the cell initially.

8. Human Genome Project (HGP) & DNA Fingerprinting

8.1 Human Genome Project (1990–2003)

HGP used two approaches: Expressed Sequence Tags (ESTs) (identifying all genes expressed as RNA) and Sequence Annotation (sequencing the entire genome—coding and non-coding—and assigning functions). DNA fragments were cloned in bacterial/yeast hosts using BAC and YAC vectors and sequenced via automated Sanger sequencers. Chromosome 1 was the last chromosome sequenced (May 2006).

  • Total size: \(3164.7\,\text{million bp}\); estimated genes: \(\approx 30{,}000\); average gene size: \(3000\,\text{bases}\) (largest: Dystrophin at \(2.4\,\text{million bases}\)).
  • 99.9% of nucleotide bases are identical across all humans; <2% of the genome codes for proteins.
  • Chromosome 1 has the most genes (2968); Y chromosome has the fewest (231).
  • About 1.4 million SNPs (single nucleotide polymorphisms) were identified.

8.2 DNA Fingerprinting

Developed by Alec Jeffreys, DNA fingerprinting exploits high polymorphism in repetitive satellite DNA, specifically VNTRs (Variable Number of Tandem Repeats, a class of mini-satellite ranging from \(0.1\) to \(20\,\mathrm{kb}\)). A DNA polymorphism is an inheritable mutation occurring at a locus with frequency \(>0.01\). Steps in Southern blot hybridization include: (1) DNA isolation, (2) restriction digestion, (3) gel electrophoresis, (4) blotting onto nitrocellulose/nylon membrane, (5) hybridization with radiolabeled VNTR probe, and (6) autoradiography. PCR amplification allows analysis from a single cell. All tissues from an individual yield the same pattern, differing only between individuals (except monozygotic twins).

Applying Chargaff's Equivalence Rule

Problem: Assume a hypothetical double-stranded DNA molecule contains \(18\%\) Guanine. Calculate the percentage of Adenine in this DNA molecule.

Solution: According to Chargaff's rule for dsDNA, \([\mathrm{G}] = [\mathrm{C}]\) and \([\mathrm{A}] = [\mathrm{T}]\). Given \([\mathrm{G}] = 18\%\), we have \([\mathrm{C}] = 18\%\). Therefore:

\[ [\mathrm{G}] + [\mathrm{C}] = 18\% + 18\% = 36\% \] \[ [\mathrm{A}] + [\mathrm{T}] = 100\% - 36\% = 64\% \implies [\mathrm{A}] = \frac{64\%}{2} = 32\% \]

11. Common Pitfalls, Revision Checklist & Final Recap

High-Yield NEET Traps to Avoid:
  • \(^{15}\mathrm{N}\) in Meselson–Stahl is a heavy isotope, NOT radioactive (unlike \(^{32}\mathrm{P}\) and \(^{35}\mathrm{S}\) in Hershey–Chase).
  • In a transcription unit, the coding strand does NOT code for RNA; the template strand (3'→5') directs RNA synthesis.
  • The i gene in the lac operon stands for inhibitor, NOT inducer.
  • Stop codons (UAA, UAG, UGA) have no tRNAs; release factors terminate translation.

Final Recap: DNA is the stable, double-stranded hereditary blueprint packaged via histones into chromatin. It replicates semiconservatively in the 5'→3' direction, transcribes into RNA (processed via capping, splicing, and tailing in eukaryotes), and translates via the triplet genetic code and tRNA adapters on ribosomes. Gene expression is tightly regulated (e.g., lac operon), while HGP and VNTR-based DNA fingerprinting have revolutionized genomics and forensics.

সংক্ষিপ্ত সারসংক্ষেপ: DNA হলো স্থিতিশীল বংশগতীয় বস্তু যা হিস্টোনের সাহায্যে ক্রোমাটিনে সজ্জিত থাকে। এটি 5'→3' অভিমুখে অর্ধ-সংরক্ষণশীলভাবে প্রতিলিপিত হয়, RNA-তে ট্রান্সক্রাইব হয় এবং রাইবোজোমে tRNA ও ট্রিপলেট জেনেটিক কোডের মাধ্যমে প্রোটিনে অনূদিত হয়। জিন প্রকাশের নিয়ন্ত্রণ (lac অপেরন), HGP এবং VNTR-ভিত্তিক DNA ফিঙ্গারপ্রিন্টিং আধুনিক আণবিক জীববিজ্ঞানের ভিত্তি।
