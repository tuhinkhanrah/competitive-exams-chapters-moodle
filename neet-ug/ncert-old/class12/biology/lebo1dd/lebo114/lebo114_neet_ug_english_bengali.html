Ecosystem Architecture: Components, Stratification & the Pond Model | বাস্তুতন্ত্রের গঠন: উপাদান, স্তরবিন্যাস ও পুকুরের উদাহরণ

English

An ecosystem is the fundamental functional unit of nature in which living organisms (biotic components) interact with one another and with their surrounding physical environment (abiotic components). Ecosystems range in scale from a small temporary pond to a vast tropical forest or ocean, and the entire biosphere may be viewed as a global composite of all local ecosystems.

For systematic study, ecosystems are broadly classified into:

  • Terrestrial ecosystems: Forests, grasslands, and deserts.
  • Aquatic ecosystems: Ponds, lakes, wetlands, rivers, and estuaries.
  • Man-made (anthropogenic) ecosystems: Crop fields and aquaria.

Structural Features

The interaction of biotic and abiotic factors produces a characteristic physical structure defined by two primary attributes:

  • Species Composition: The identification and enumeration of all plant, animal, and microbial species present in an ecosystem.
  • Stratification: The vertical distribution of different species occupying distinct vertical layers. For instance, in a forest ecosystem, tall trees occupy the uppermost canopy stratum, shrubs form the middle layer, and herbs and grasses occupy the bottom layer.

The Pond as a Self-Sustaining Aquatic Model

A shallow pond clearly demonstrates how the four functional pillars—productivity, decomposition, energy flow, and nutrient cycling—operate together:

  • Abiotic component: Water containing dissolved inorganic and organic substances, plus nutrient-rich bottom sediment. Solar radiation, temperature cycles, and day-length regulate overall system rates.
  • Autotrophs (Producers): Phytoplankton, algae, and floating, submerged, and marginal plants along the edges.
  • Consumers (Heterotrophs): Zooplankton, free-swimming forms (nekton), and bottom-dwelling organisms (benthos).
  • Decomposers: Fungi, bacteria, and flagellates, especially abundant in the bottom mud, which mineralise dead organic matter for reuse by autotrophs.

Ecosystem Productivity: GPP, NPP & Secondary Productivity | বাস্তুতন্ত্রের উৎপাদনশীলতা: GPP, NPP ও গৌণ উৎপাদনশীলতা

English

Primary production is the total amount of biomass or organic matter synthesized per unit area over a given time period by plants during photosynthesis. It is expressed in terms of dry weight (\(\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}}\)) or energy (\(\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}}\)).

The rate of biomass production is called productivity, expressed as \(\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\) or \(\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\) so that different ecosystems can be compared directly.

  • Gross Primary Productivity (GPP): The total rate of organic matter production during photosynthesis.
  • Net Primary Productivity (NPP): The remaining biomass after plants utilize a portion of GPP for cellular respiration (\(R\)). NPP represents the biomass actually available for consumption by heterotrophs (herbivores and decomposers).
  • Secondary Productivity: The rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers (heterotrophs).
\[\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R\]
Global Productivity Benchmarks (High-Yield NEET Facts):
  • Annual NPP of the entire biosphere is approximately 170 billion tons (dry weight) of organic matter.
  • Although oceans cover nearly 70% of Earth's surface, their annual productivity is only 55 billion tons (limited mainly by light penetration in deeper waters and nutrient scarcity such as nitrogen). The remaining ~115 billion tons comes from terrestrial ecosystems.
  • Primary productivity varies across ecosystems depending on resident plant species, nutrient availability, environmental factors, and photosynthetic capacity.

Decomposition Dynamics: Steps & Regulating Factors | বিয়োজন প্রক্রিয়া: ধাপসমূহ ও নিয়ন্ত্রক উপাদান

English

Decomposition is the breakdown of complex organic matter into simple inorganic substances such as carbon dioxide (\(\mathrm{CO_2}\)), water, and mineral nutrients by decomposers. Dead plant remains (leaves, bark, flowers) and dead animal remains, including fecal matter, constitute detritus, the raw material for decomposition.

Five key processes operate simultaneously on detritus:

  1. Fragmentation: Detritivores (such as earthworms) break down coarse detritus into smaller particles, increasing surface area for microbial enzymes.
  2. Leaching: Water-soluble inorganic nutrients percolate down into lower soil horizons and precipitate as unavailable salts.
  3. Catabolism: Bacterial and fungal extracellular enzymes degrade detritus into simpler inorganic substances.
  4. Humification: Accumulation of a dark-colored, amorphous, colloidal substance called humus, which is highly resistant to microbial action, decomposes very slowly, and serves as a nutrient reservoir.
  5. Mineralisation: Further slow degradation of humus by specific microbes to release inorganic nutrients back into the soil.
Tutorial Diagram 2: Core processes of terrestrial decomposition from detritus to mineral release.

Factors Controlling Decomposition Rate

Decomposition is largely an oxygen-requiring (aerobic) process regulated by:

  • Chemical Composition of Detritus: Under identical climatic conditions, decay is slower when detritus is rich in lignin and chitin, and faster when rich in nitrogen and water-soluble sugars.
  • Climatic Factors: Temperature and soil moisture are the most critical climatic regulators. A warm and moist environment accelerates microbial decomposition, whereas low temperature and anaerobiosis (oxygen deficiency) inhibit it, causing organic matter to accumulate.

Energy Flow, Food Chains & the 10% Law | শক্তি প্রবাহ, খাদ্যশৃঙ্খল ও ১০% সূত্র

English

Except for deep-sea hydrothermal ecosystems, the Sun is the sole energy source for all ecosystems on Earth. Of total incident solar radiation, less than 50% is Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR). Plants and photosynthetic bacteria capture only 2–10% of PAR (roughly 1–5% of total incident sunlight), yet this small fraction sustains the entire biosphere.

Energy flow is unidirectional (Sun → Producers → Consumers) and obeys both laws of thermodynamics: ecosystems require a continuous energy supply to synthesize ordered biomolecules and counteract increasing entropy (disorder), while heat is dissipated at every transfer.

Grazing Food Chain (GFC) vs. Detritus Food Chain (DFC)

Feature Grazing Food Chain (GFC) Detritus Food Chain (DFC)
Starting Point Living green plants / phytoplankton (Producers) Dead organic matter (Detritus)
Key Organisms Producers → Herbivores → Primary & Secondary Carnivores Decomposers / Saprotrophs (Fungi, Bacteria)
Major Conduit Dominant conduit of energy flow in aquatic ecosystems Larger fraction of energy flows through DFC in terrestrial ecosystems

Because DFC organisms often serve as prey for GFC animals, and omnivores (such as cockroaches, crows, and humans) feed across chains, natural food chains interconnect to form a food web.

Trophic Levels, Standing Crop & the 10% Law

  • Trophic Level: A functional feeding position in a food chain—Producers (1st trophic level), Herbivores / Primary consumers (2nd), Primary carnivores / Secondary consumers (3rd), Secondary carnivores / Tertiary consumers (4th).
  • Standing Crop: The mass of living material (biomass) or number of organisms at a trophic level at a particular time. Measuring biomass as dry weight is more accurate than fresh weight because water content fluctuates widely.
  • 10% Law: Only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next higher level; the remaining ~90% is lost as respiratory heat or unassimilated waste. This restricts the number of trophic levels in a GFC (typically 3 to 5).

Ecological Pyramids: Upright, Inverted & Limitations | বাস্তুতান্ত্রিক পিরামিড: ঊর্ধ্বমুখী, উল্টানো ও সীমাবদ্ধতা

English

Ecological pyramids graphically represent trophic relationships in terms of number, biomass, or energy, with producers forming the base and top carnivores at the apex.

Pyramid Type Typical Shape Key Exceptions & Explanation
Pyramid of Number Upright (e.g., grassland) Inverted or Spindle-shaped in a single big tree ecosystem (1 tree supports hundreds of insects, fewer small birds, and very few predatory birds).
Pyramid of Biomass Upright (terrestrial forests/grasslands) Inverted in the sea/ocean: Biomass of zooplankton/fishes far exceeds the standing crop of phytoplankton because phytoplankton reproduce and turn over rapidly.
Pyramid of Energy Always Upright Never inverted, because energy is always lost as heat at every trophic transfer.

Limitations of Ecological Pyramids

  • They do not account for the same species occupying two or more trophic levels simultaneously (e.g., a sparrow is a primary consumer when eating seeds, and a secondary consumer when eating insects).
  • They assume a simple linear food chain rather than a realistic food web.
  • Saprophytes (decomposers) are not given any place in ecological pyramids despite their vital ecosystem role.

Ecological Succession: Primary, Secondary, Hydrarch & Xerarch | বাস্তুতান্ত্রিক অনুক্রম: প্রাথমিক, গৌণ, হাইড্রার্ক ও জেরার্ক

English

Ecological succession is the gradual, orderly, and fairly predictable change in the species composition of a given area over time, parallel with changes in the physical environment, culminating in a stable climax community in near equilibrium with the environment.

  • Sere: The entire sequence of communities that successively replace one another in a given area. Individual transitional communities are seral stages. During succession, species diversity, number of organisms, and total biomass increase.
  • Pioneer Species: The first hardy species to invade a bare area.

Primary vs. Secondary Succession

  • Primary Succession: Occurs where no living organisms ever existed (e.g., bare rock, newly cooled lava, newly created pond). It is very slow (hundreds to thousands of years) because fertile soil must first be formed.
  • Secondary Succession: Occurs in areas that lost their previous biotic communities due to fire, flooding, deforestation, or abandoned farmlands. Because soil or sediment is already present, secondary succession is much faster.

Hydrarch and Xerarch Succession

Both hydrarch succession (in wet/aquatic habitats, progressing from hydric to mesic) and xerarch succession (in dry habitats, progressing from xeric to mesic) converge toward a similar mesic (medium-water) climax forest community:

  • Hydrarch Sequence: Phytoplankton (pioneers) → Rooted-submerged plants → Rooted-floating angiosperms / Free-floating plants → Reed-swamp stage → Marsh-meadow stage → Scrub stage → Forest (climax).
  • Xerarch Sequence (on bare rock): Lichens (pioneers; secrete acids to weather rock and form thin soil) → Bryophytes (mosses) → Herbs and shrubs → Climax forest.

English

Example 1: Calculating Net Primary Productivity and Respiration Loss

Scenario (Assumed Hypothetical Values): In an experimental grassland plot, autotrophs fix solar energy to produce a Gross Primary Productivity (\(\mathrm{GPP}\)) of \(1,400\,\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\). If plant cellular respiration (\(R\)) consumes \(35\%\) of this gross production, calculate the Net Primary Productivity (\(\mathrm{NPP}\)) available to herbivores and decomposers.

Solution:

First, calculate the respiratory loss \(R\):

\[R = 0.35 \times 1,400\,\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}} = 490\,\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\]

Using the productivity relation \(\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R\):

\[\mathrm{NPP} = 1,400 - 490 = 910\,\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\]

Thus, \(910\,\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\) of organic energy is available to heterotrophs.

Example 2: Applying the 10% Law from Incident Sunlight to Tertiary Consumers

Scenario (Assumed Hypothetical Values): Suppose \(2,000,000\,\mathrm{J}\) of total sunlight falls on a crop field. Assume that \(50\%\) of this incident radiation is PAR, and crop plants convert \(2\%\) of PAR into Net Primary Productivity (\(\mathrm{NPP}\)). Following the 10% law, how much energy reaches a tertiary consumer (4th trophic level) in a grazing food chain?

Solution:

  1. Photosynthetically Active Radiation (PAR): \(0.50 \times 2,000,000\,\mathrm{J} = 1,000,000\,\mathrm{J}\).
  2. Energy fixed as \(\mathrm{NPP}\) by producers (1st trophic level): \(0.02 \times 1,000,000\,\mathrm{J} = 20,000\,\mathrm{J}\).
  3. Primary consumer (2nd trophic level): \(10\%\) of \(20,000\,\mathrm{J} = 2,000\,\mathrm{J}\).
  4. Secondary consumer (3rd trophic level): \(10\%\) of \(2,000\,\mathrm{J} = 200\,\mathrm{J}\).
  5. Tertiary consumer (4th trophic level): \(10\%\) of \(200\,\mathrm{J} = 20\,\mathrm{J}\).

Answer: \(20\,\mathrm{J}\) reaches the tertiary consumer.

Example 3: Interpreting Biomass Inversion and Trophic Flexibility

Scenario (Assumed Hypothetical Values): A marine survey records a phytoplankton standing crop of \(5\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}}\) and a zooplankton standing crop of \(24\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}}\) at a single sampling instant. Why does this not violate the laws of thermodynamics?

Solution:

Standing crop measures biomass at a single point in time, not the rate of energy production over a year. Phytoplankton have a very short lifespan and rapid reproductive turnover, so their annual productivity (\(\mathrm{kcal\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)) is still much higher than that of zooplankton. Hence, the pyramid of biomass is inverted, whereas the pyramid of energy remains strictly upright, fully obeying thermodynamics.

In a hypothetical forest ecosystem, the Gross Primary Productivity (\(\mathrm{GPP}\)) is \(2,500\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\) and respiratory loss (\(R\)) by producers is \(1,000\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\). Assuming \(10\%\) trophic transfer efficiency, what is the secondary productivity rate at the herbivore (2nd trophic) level?

  • (A) \(250\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)
  • (B) \(150\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)
  • (C) \(100\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)
  • (D) \(15\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)

Answer: (B)

Solution: First compute \(\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R = 2,500 - 1,000 = 1,500\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\). Herbivores receive \(10\%\) of \(\mathrm{NPP}\): \(0.10 \times 1,500 = 150\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\).

সমাধান: প্রথমে \(\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R = 2,500 - 1,000 = 1,500\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\) নির্ণয় করি। তৃণভোজী স্তরে \(\mathrm{NPP}\)-এর \(10\%\) স্থানান্তরিত হয়: \(0.10 \times 1,500 = 150\,\mathrm{g\,m^{-2}\,yr^{-1}}\)।

Common Pitfalls & Revision Checklist | সাধারণ ভুলভ্রান্তি ও পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

English
  • PAR vs. Captured Energy: PAR is less than 50% of total incident sunlight; plants capture 2–10% of PAR (not 2–10% of total sunlight).
  • Standing Crop vs. Standing State: Standing crop = living biomass/number at a trophic level; Standing state = inorganic nutrients present in soil at a given time.
  • Humus vs. Mineralisation: Humification forms colloidal, decay-resistant humus; mineralisation is the subsequent slow release of inorganic nutrients from humus.
  • Dry Weight vs. Fresh Weight: Always prefer dry weight for biomass pyramids and productivity comparisons.
  • Costanza's Ecosystem Services: Soil formation alone accounts for ~50% of the US $33 trillion/yr total; climate regulation and habitat are ~6% each.

Concise Final Recap | সংক্ষিপ্ত সারসংক্ষেপ

English

An ecosystem integrates abiotic and biotic components through productivity (\(\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R\)), decomposition (simultaneous fragmentation, leaching, catabolism, humification, and mineralisation), unidirectional energy flow (~10% trophic transfer), and biogeochemical cycling (gaseous carbon vs. sedimentary phosphorus). Ecological pyramids of energy are always upright, whereas marine biomass pyramids are inverted due to rapid phytoplankton turnover. Both hydrarch and xerarch successions converge toward a mesic forest climax, sustaining vital ecosystem services.

বাংলা (Bengali)

বাস্তুতন্ত্র উৎপাদনশীলতা (\(\mathrm{NPP} = \mathrm{GPP} - R\)), বিয়োজন (খণ্ডীকরণ, লিচিং, ক্যাটাবলিজম, হিউমিফিকেশন ও খনিজীকরণ), একমুখী শক্তি প্রবাহ (~১০% স্থানান্তর) এবং জৈব-ভূ-রাসায়নিক চক্রের (গ্যাসীয় কার্বন বনাম পাললিক ফসফরাস) মাধ্যমে জৈব ও অজৈব উপাদানকে যুক্ত করে। শক্তির পিরামিড সর্বদা ঊর্ধ্বমুখী, তবে দ্রুত আবর্তনের কারণে সমুদ্রের জীবভরের পিরামিড উল্টানো হয়। হাইড্রার্ক ও জেরার্ক উভয় অনুক্রমই মেসিক অরণ্য ক্লাইম্যাক্সে উপনীত হয় এবং অমূল্য বাস্তুতান্ত্রিক পরিষেবা প্রদান করে।
