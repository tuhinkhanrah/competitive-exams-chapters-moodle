2.1 Concept of Health and Disease

Early medical traditions—including Greek medicine (Hippocrates) and Indian Ayurveda—proposed the 'good humor' hypothesis, claiming that health depended on a balance of bodily fluids ('humors') and that excess 'black bile' caused fever and a hot temperament. This hypothesis was overturned by William Harvey's experimental demonstration of blood circulation and thermometer measurements showing normal body temperature in individuals previously thought to possess 'black bile'.

Modern biology defines health not merely as the absence of disease or physical fitness, but as a state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being. The neural and endocrine systems interact closely with the immune system, meaning mental state directly influences physical immunity. Health is shaped by three broad determinants:

  1. Genetic disorders: Inherited defects or deficiencies present from birth.
  2. Infections: Invasion and multiplication of pathogenic organisms.
  3. Lifestyle: Diet, water quality, rest, exercise, and personal habits.

When organ or system function is impaired with characteristic signs and symptoms, a disease occurs. Infectious diseases spread easily from person to person (e.g., typhoid, common cold, AIDS), whereas non-infectious diseases do not transmit between individuals (e.g., cancer, the leading non-infectious cause of mortality).

প্রাচীন গ্রিক চিকিৎসাশাস্ত্র (হিপোক্রেটিস) এবং ভারতীয় আয়ুর্বেদ শাস্ত্রে ‘গুড হিউমর’ (good humor) অনুকল্প প্রচলিত ছিল, যেখানে মনে করা হতো দেহের তরল পদার্থের ভারসাম্যই স্বাস্থ্য এবং ‘ব্ল্যাক বাইল’ (black bile) নামক উপাদানের আধিক্য জ্বরের কারণ। উইলিয়াম হার্ভে পরীক্ষামূলকভাবে রক্ত সংবহন আবিষ্কার করেন এবং থার্মোমিটারের সাহায্যে প্রমাণ করেন যে ওই ধরনের ব্যক্তিদের দেহের তাপমাত্রা স্বাভাবিক থাকে; ফলে এই অনুকল্পটি ভুল প্রমাণিত হয়।

আধুনিক জীববিজ্ঞানে স্বাস্থ্য বলতে কেবল রোগের অনুপস্থিতি বা শারীরিক সক্ষমতা বোঝায় না; এটি হলো সম্পূর্ণ শারীরিক, মানসিক ও সামাজিক সুস্থতার অবস্থা। স্নায়ুতন্ত্র ও অন্তঃক্ষরা তন্ত্রের মাধ্যমে মন আমাদের অনাক্রম্যতন্ত্রকে প্রভাবিত করে। স্বাস্থ্য প্রধানত তিনটি বিষয়ের ওপর নির্ভর করে:

  1. জিনগত ব্যাধি: জন্মগত বা পিতামাতা থেকে উত্তরাধিকারসূত্রে প্রাপ্ত ত্রুটি।
  2. সংক্রমণ: রোগসৃষ্টিকারী জীবাণুর আক্রমণ।
  3. জীবনযাত্রা: খাদ্য, পানীয় জল, বিশ্রাম, ব্যায়াম ও অভ্যাস।

এক বা একাধিক অঙ্গের স্বাভাবিক কাজ ব্যাহত হয়ে নির্দিষ্ট লক্ষণ প্রকাশ পেলে তাকে রোগ বলে। যে রোগ সহজেই এক ব্যক্তি থেকে অন্য ব্যক্তিতে ছড়ায় তাকে সংক্রামক রোগ (যেমন—টাইফয়েড, সাধারণ সর্দি, AIDS) এবং যে রোগ ছড়ায় না তাকে অসংক্রামক রোগ (যেমন—ক্যান্সার) বলে।

2.2 Major Pathogenic Diseases in Humans

Disease-causing organisms—called pathogens—include bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoans, and helminths. Gut pathogens must survive low stomach pH and resist digestive enzymes. The essential NEET UG profile of major human diseases is summarised below:

Disease (Category) Pathogen Transmission / Vector Target & Key Symptoms Diagnostic / Key Fact
Typhoid (Bacterial) Salmonella typhi Contaminated food & water (faecal-oral) Small intestine → blood; sustained high fever (39°C–40°C), weakness, stomach pain, constipation, headache, loss of appetite; intestinal perforation in severe cases Confirmed by Widal test; classic carrier: Mary Mallon ("Typhoid Mary")
Pneumonia (Bacterial) Streptococcus pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae Inhaling droplets/aerosols; sharing glasses/utensils Alveoli of lungs fill with fluid; fever, chills, cough, headache; lips & fingernails turn grey-to-bluish in severe cases Affects gas exchange in alveoli
Common Cold (Viral) Rhino viruses Respiratory droplets; fomites (pens, books, doorknobs, keyboards) Nose and respiratory passage (NOT lungs); nasal congestion/discharge, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache, tiredness Usually lasts 3–7 days
Malaria (Protozoan) Plasmodium (P. vivax, P. malaria, P. falciparum) Bite of infected female Anopheles mosquito Liver cells → RBCs; cyclic chills & high fever every 3–4 days due to haemozoin release upon RBC rupture P. falciparum causes malignant (potentially fatal) malaria
Amoebiasis (Protozoan) Entamoeba histolytica Contaminated food/water; houseflies act as mechanical carriers Large intestine; constipation, abdominal pain, cramps, stools with excess mucus and blood clots Also called amoebic dysentery
Ascariasis (Helminthic) Ascaris (roundworm) Soil, water, fruits/vegetables contaminated with eggs from faeces Intestine; internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anaemia, blockage of intestinal passage Eggs excreted in faeces
Elephantiasis / Filariasis (Helminthic) Wuchereria bancrofti, W. malayi Bite of female mosquito vectors Chronic inflammation of lymphatic vessels of lower limbs & genital organs causing gross deformities Slowly developing chronic condition
Ringworms (Fungal) Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton Soil; sharing towels, clothes, or combs Dry, scaly lesions on skin, nails, scalp with intense itching; thrives in heat & moisture (groin, between toes) One of the most common fungal infections

2.3 Prevention and Vector Control

  • Food- and water-borne diseases (typhoid, amoebiasis, ascariasis): Maintain personal hygiene, clean drinking water, proper waste/excreta disposal, and periodic disinfection of water reservoirs.
  • Air-borne diseases (pneumonia, common cold): Avoid close contact with infected individuals and their belongings.
  • Vector-borne diseases (malaria, filariasis, dengue, chikungunya): Eliminate mosquito breeding sites (stagnant water), clean coolers regularly, use mosquito nets and wire-mesh screens, spray insecticides, and introduce larvivorous fishes such as Gambusia in ponds. Note that Aedes mosquitoes transmit dengue and chikungunya.

3.1 Innate Immunity (Non-specific, Present at Birth)

Immunity is the overall ability of the host to fight disease-causing organisms. Innate immunity provides non-specific defence from birth through four barrier types:

  1. Physical barriers: Skin (main barrier preventing microbial entry) and mucus coating of the respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urogenital epithelia that traps microbes.
  2. Physiological barriers: Gastric acid in the stomach, saliva in the mouth, and tears from the eyes—all inhibit microbial growth.
  3. Cellular barriers: Leukocytes such as polymorpho-nuclear leukocytes (PMNL-neutrophils), monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells (a type of lymphocyte) in blood, and tissue macrophages that phagocytose and destroy microbes.
  4. Cytokine barriers: Virus-infected cells secrete proteins called interferons that protect neighbouring non-infected cells from further viral infection.

3.2 Acquired Immunity, Antibody Structure, and Graft Rejection

Acquired immunity is pathogen-specific and characterised by immunological memory:

  • Primary response: First encounter with a pathogen elicits a low-intensity response.
  • Secondary (anamnestic) response: Subsequent encounter with the same pathogen produces a rapid, highly intensified response due to memory B- and T-cells.

Two lymphocyte lineages execute acquired immunity:

  • B-lymphocytes (Humoral Immune Response): Secrete an army of proteins called antibodies into the blood. Each antibody molecule consists of four peptide chains—two small light chains and two longer heavy chains—represented as \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{L}_2\). Major classes include IgA, IgM, IgE, and IgG.
  • T-lymphocytes (Cell-Mediated Immunity, CMI): Do not secrete antibodies themselves, but helper T-cells assist B-cells in antibody production, while CMI mediates direct cellular defence. CMI is responsible for graft rejection because the body distinguishes 'self' from 'non-self'. Therefore, tissue matching and blood group matching are mandatory before transplantation, and recipients require lifelong immunosuppressants.

3.3 Active vs Passive Immunity, Vaccination, Allergies, and Auto-Immunity

  • Active immunity: Host produces its own antibodies upon exposure to living/dead microbes or antigenic proteins (natural infection or vaccination). It is slow to reach full effect but generates long-lasting memory.
  • Passive immunity: Ready-made (preformed) antibodies are directly administered for immediate protection. Examples: Colostrum (yellowish initial breast milk rich in IgA antibodies), maternal IgG crossing the placenta to the foetus, tetanus antitoxin, and anti-venom injections for snakebites.
  • Vaccination & Recombinant Vaccines: Introduces antigenic proteins or inactivated/weakened pathogens to generate memory B- and T-cells. Recombinant DNA technology enables large-scale production of antigenic polypeptides in bacteria or yeast, such as the Hepatitis B vaccine produced from yeast.
  • Allergies: Exaggerated immune response to environmental antigens (allergens such as dust mites, pollen, animal dander). Mediated by IgE antibodies and release of histamine and serotonin from mast cells, causing sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, and breathing difficulty. Quickly relieved by anti-histamines, adrenalin, and steroids.
  • Auto-immunity: Genetic or unknown breakdown of self-tolerance causing the immune system to attack self-cells (e.g., Rheumatoid arthritis).

3.4 Lymphoid Organs

  • Primary lymphoid organs (Bone marrow & Thymus): Sites where immature lymphocytes differentiate into antigen-sensitive lymphocytes. Bone marrow produces all blood cells including lymphocytes; the thymus (lobed organ near the heart beneath the breastbone) is large at birth and shrinks to a very small size by puberty. Both provide micro-environments for T-lymphocyte maturation.
  • Secondary lymphoid organs (Spleen, Lymph nodes, Tonsils, Peyer's patches of small intestine, Appendix): Sites where mature lymphocytes interact with antigens and proliferate into effector cells. The spleen (large bean-shaped organ) filters blood-borne microbes and stores erythrocytes. Lymph nodes trap antigens from lymph and tissue fluid.
  • MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue): Located in the lining of respiratory, digestive, and urogenital tracts; constitutes about 50% of total lymphoid tissue in the human body.

4.1 AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome)

First reported in 1981, AIDS is an acquired (non-congenital) deficiency of the immune system caused by the Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), an enveloped retrovirus containing an RNA genome.

  • Transmission routes: Sexual contact with an infected person, transfusion of contaminated blood/blood products, sharing infected needles (intravenous drug users), and transplacental transfer from an infected mother to her child. It does not spread by mere touch or physical contact.
  • Mechanism of pathogenesis: HIV enters macrophages, where viral RNA is converted into viral DNA by reverse transcriptase. This viral DNA integrates into the host genome and directs continuous production of progeny viruses—making macrophages an HIV factory. Simultaneously, HIV infects and destroys helper T-lymphocytes (\(\mathrm{T_H}\)), causing a progressive decline in \(\mathrm{T_H}\) count.
  • Clinical course & diagnosis: Incubation lag ranges from a few months to 5–10 years. Loss of \(\mathrm{T_H}\) cells leads to bouts of fever, diarrhoea, weight loss, and opportunistic infections (e.g., Mycobacterium, viruses, fungi, and Toxoplasma). Widely diagnosed by ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immuno-Sorbent Assay). Anti-retroviral drugs only prolong life; prevention (safe blood banks, disposable needles, safe sex, NACO/WHO awareness) is paramount.

4.2 Cancer Biology: Mechanisms, Detection, and Treatment

Normal cells exhibit contact inhibition—contact with neighbouring cells halts uncontrolled division. Cancer cells lose contact inhibition and divide relentlessly to form cell masses called tumors:

  • Benign tumors: Remain confined to their original site, do not spread, and cause little damage.
  • Malignant tumors: Rapidly proliferating neoplastic cells that invade surrounding tissues, starve normal cells of nutrients, and undergo metastasis—cells slough off, travel via blood to distant sites, and establish secondary tumors (the most feared property).

Carcinogens include physical agents (ionising X-rays and gamma rays; non-ionising UV rays causing DNA damage), chemical agents (tobacco smoke causing lung cancer), and biological agents (oncogenic viruses carrying viral oncogenes). Normal cells also possess cellular oncogenes (c-onc) or proto-oncogenes that trigger neoplastic transformation when activated.

Detection & Treatment: Biopsy and histopathological staining, blood/bone marrow counts (for leukaemias), radiography (X-rays), CT (Computed Tomography) using X-rays for 3D internal imaging, and MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) using strong magnetic fields and non-ionising radiation. Treatment combines surgery, radiotherapy, chemotherapy (side effects: hair loss, anaemia), and immunotherapy using biological response modifiers such as \(\alpha\)-interferon to activate the immune system against tumor cells.

Scenario (Hypothetical): Compare two clinical cases: Case A involves a newborn receiving maternal protection through breast milk during the first 4 days after birth. Case B involves a kidney transplant recipient whose body begins rejecting the donor organ despite ABO blood-group matching. Solution:

Example 3: Tracing Retroviral Enzyme Action and Immune Collapse in HIV | উদাহরণ ৩: HIV সংক্রমণে রেট্রোভাইরাল উৎসেচকের ক্রিয়া ও অনাক্রম্যতা ভেঙে পড়া
English

Scenario (Hypothetical): A patient tests positive on an ELISA screen and shows a sharp drop in helper T-cell (\(\mathrm{T_H}\)) counts over several years, followed by a Mycobacterium infection. Explain the molecular step that allows HIV to permanently commandeer host macrophages and why bacterial infections that are normally controlled become life-threatening.

Solution:

  • Molecular step: HIV is a retrovirus with an RNA genome. Inside host macrophages, the viral enzyme reverse transcriptase synthesises viral DNA from viral RNA. This viral DNA integrates into the host cell's nuclear DNA, turning macrophages into an HIV factory while the cell survives.
  • Reason for opportunistic infections: Progeny virions destroy \(\mathrm{T_H}\) lymphocytes, which are essential to activate B-cells and coordinate immune defences. Severe \(\mathrm{T_H}\) depletion leaves the patient immunodeficient against normally manageable pathogens such as Mycobacterium and Toxoplasma.
বাংলা

সমাধান:

  • আণবিক ধাপ: HIV-এর RNA জিনোম ম্যাক্রোফাজে প্রবেশের পর রিভার্স ট্রান্সক্রিপ্টেজ উৎসেচকের সাহায্যে ভাইরাল DNA তৈরি করে, যা পোষক কোষের DNA-তে যুক্ত হয়ে ক্রমাগত নতুন ভাইরাস তৈরি করে (HIV ফ্যাক্টরি)।
  • সুযোগসন্ধানী সংক্রমণের কারণ: অপত্য ভাইরাসগুলি \(\mathrm{T_H}\) লিম্ফোসাইট ধ্বংস করে দেয়। ফলে অনাক্রম্যতন্ত্র ভেঙে পড়ে এবং Mycobacterium ও Toxoplasma-এর মতো সাধারণ জীবাণুও মারাত্মক সংক্রমণ ঘটায়।

Which diagnostic technique uses strong magnetic fields and non-ionising radiations to accurately detect pathological and physiological changes in living tissues?

  • (A) Computed Tomography (CT)
  • (B) Radiography
  • (C) Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)

8. Common Pitfalls and Revision Checklist | সাধারণ ভুল ও পুনরালোচনা তালিকা

English
High-Frequency NEET Traps to Avoid:
  • Common cold vs Pneumonia: Rhino viruses infect only the nose and respiratory passage, not the alveoli of the lungs; pneumonia infects the alveoli.
  • Primary vs Secondary Lymphoid Organs: Bone marrow and thymus are primary; spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches, and appendix are secondary.
  • CT vs MRI: CT uses X-rays (ionising); MRI uses magnetic fields and non-ionising radiations.
  • Coca vs Cannabis vs Poppy: Coca blocks dopamine transport (stimulant); Cannabis acts on brain receptors (cardiovascular effects); Opioids bind CNS & GI receptors (depressant).

Revision Checklist: Verify you can recall Widal test (Typhoid), ELISA (AIDS), \(\mathrm{H}_2\mathrm{L}_2\) (Antibody), IgE (Allergy), IgA (Colostrum), MALT (50%), Gambusia (larvivorous fish), and \(\alpha\)-interferon (cancer immunotherapy).

বাংলা
NEET পরীক্ষায় এড়িয়ে চলার মতো সাধারণ ভুল:
  • সাধারণ সর্দি বনাম নিউমোনিয়া: রাইনো ভাইরাস কেবল নাক ও শ্বাসনালিকে আক্রান্ত করে, ফুসফুসের অ্যালভিওলাইকে নয়; নিউমোনিয়া অ্যালভিওলাইকে আক্রান্ত করে।
  • প্রাথমিক বনাম গৌণ লসিকা অঙ্গ: অস্থিমজ্জা ও থাইমাস হলো প্রাথমিক; প্লীহা, লসিকাগ্রন্থি, টনসিল, পেয়ার্স প্যাচ ও অ্যাপেন্ডিক্স হলো গৌণ।
  • CT বনাম MRI: CT-তে X-ray ব্যবহৃত হয়; MRI-তে চৌম্বক ক্ষেত্র ও অ-আয়নীয় বিকিরণ ব্যবহৃত হয়।
  • কোকা বনাম ক্যানাবিস বনাম পপি: কোকেন ডোপামিন পরিবহনে বাধা দেয় (উদ্দীপক); ক্যানাবিস মস্তিষ্কের গ্রাহকে কাজ করে (সংবহনতন্ত্রে প্রভাব); ওপিওয়েড CNS ও অন্ত্রে কাজ করে (অবসাদক)।
